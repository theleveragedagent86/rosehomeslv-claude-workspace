# CODENAME HISTORY - Project Brief

A faceless, animated history YouTube channel in the style of OverSimplified, focused on **real, documented, lesser-known WWI and WWII operations, battles, and strategies.** No made-up stories. Accuracy is the moat.

---

## THE CHANNEL

**Name:** Codename History

**Niche:** Real but obscure WWI/WWII military history. Battles, strategies, operations, and the strange true stories most people have never heard. The "everyone knows D-Day, nobody knows the tennis star who outran the SS" lane.

**Positioning:** The stories that got left in history's footnotes, told with the humor and pacing of OverSimplified but with a hard rule: every beat is verifiable. Fact-checking is a feature, not overhead. The obscure-story lane is full of half-true internet legends, so verifying before scripting is what protects both credibility and monetization.

**Format tiers:**
- **Standard episodes:** 10-15 minutes. Single battle or story. (Minimum 5 minutes, but 10-15 is the target.)
- **Operation videos (deep dives):** 15-45 minutes. Full end-to-end coverage of a named operation. These are the flagship long-form pieces.

---

## VIDEO TITLE CONVENTION

**Core principle:** Hook first, brand second. This is a deliberate departure from OverSimplified.

OverSimplified uses plain titles ("WW2 - OverSimplified") because his topics are already world-famous, so the topic name alone earns the click. Codename History's entire premise is stories nobody knows, so the topic name carries zero curiosity for a cold scroller. The **hook** has to do the work the famous topic name does for him.

### Standard episode format:
`[Curiosity-driven hook] | Codename History`

Examples:
- `When Americans and Germans Fought TOGETHER | Codename History`
- `The Tennis Star Who Outran the SS | Codename History`
- `Hitler Was Fooled by Inflatable Tanks | Codename History`

Why: The hook earns the click from strangers. The `| Codename History` suffix builds format recognition as the library grows. YouTube truncates titles from the right, so if the suffix gets cut, you lose branding (recoverable) not the hook (not recoverable). That's the correct thing to sacrifice.

### Deep-dive / operation format:
`Codename: [Operation Name] - [Hook or subtitle]`

Examples:
- `Codename: Gunnerside - The Raid That Stopped Hitler's Atomic Bomb`
- `Codename: Greif - The Nazis Who Disguised Themselves as Americans`

**Rule for the "Codename:" prefix:** Only use it when the story is a genuinely codenamed operation. Do NOT force it onto stories that were never codenamed (e.g. Castle Itter was never "Codename: Itter"; the Ghost Army was never "Codename: Ghost Army"). Forcing invented codenames would be exactly the fake-history problem the channel exists to avoid. Reserve the prefix as a **series label for the big operation videos** so that when viewers see "Codename:" they know it's a full deep dive.

## PACKAGING (from the OverSimplified playbook)

- **Thumbnails:** Match the animated style of the video. Repeating (or partially repeating) the title on the thumbnail is fine and can be inherently clickable. Generate 3 variants for A/B testing.
- **Upload tactic:** OverSimplified frequently uploads in **pairs**. Launching two related videos together is a proven tactic for this niche. Castle Itter + Ghost Army is a strong launch pair.
- **Consistency > frequency:** This niche rewards quality and rewatchability over upload volume. Running gags and callbacks across videos build a loyal, binge-prone audience.

- YouTube Partner Program threshold: 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours.
- History/education is a high-retention, wide-demographic niche (far bigger addressable audience than most niches).

- AI-assisted content is NOT auto-demonetized. YouTube penalizes low-value spam, not AI. Original scripts + real editing + fitting voiceover = safe. Codename History's accuracy-first, original-research approach clears this bar easily.

## COLD OPEN (0:00 - 0:45)

**[VISUAL: Black screen. Sound of birds. Fade in on a cartoony Austrian castle on a hill. Peaceful music.]**

**NARRATOR:** May 5th, 1945. Hitler has been dead for five days. Germany surrenders in 48 hours. The war in Europe is basically over. Everyone can finally relax.

**[VISUAL: A cartoon American soldier and a cartoon German soldier standing on the same castle wall. Both holding rifles. They look at each other. Awkward pause.]**

**NARRATOR:** Which is why it's a little weird that on this exact morning, an American tank commander, a decorated German major, a Waffen-SS captain, two former Prime Ministers of France, and a professional tennis player...

**[VISUAL: Each character pops onto the wall as they're named. The tennis player is holding a racket instead of a rifle.]**

**NARRATOR:** ...are all standing on the same castle wall, on the same side, shooting at the same enemy.

**[VISUAL: Zoom out. Hundreds of tiny SS soldiers swarming up the hill. Record scratch. Freeze frame.]**

**NARRATOR:** This is the Battle of Castle Itter. The only time in the entire war that Americans and Germans fought together as allies. And I promise you, every part of this story is real.

**[TITLE CARD: "THE STRANGEST BATTLE OF WWII" in flat animated style]**

## ACT 1: THE WORLD'S FANCIEST PRISON (0:45 - 3:30)

**[VISUAL: Map of Austria. Zoom into the Tyrol mountains. Castle Itter appears with a little "For Rent" sign.]**

**NARRATOR:** Castle Itter sits on a hill in the Austrian Alps. For most of its life it was a private estate, then a hotel. It was doing great. And then 1943 happened, and Heinrich Himmler looked at this lovely alpine getaway and said:

**[VISUAL: Cartoon Himmler with tiny glasses pointing at the castle.]**

**HIMMLER (character voice):** "That would make a GREAT prison."

**NARRATOR:** The SS seized the castle and turned it into a very unusual facility. Administratively, it was part of the Dachau concentration camp system. But Itter wasn't a labor camp. It was a VIP prison. This is where the Nazis kept people who were too famous to quietly get rid of, but too dangerous to let go.

**[VISUAL: A "Who's Who" lineup, each character sliding in with a name card:]**

**NARRATOR:** The guest list was absurd. Édouard Daladier, former Prime Minister of France. Paul Reynaud, ALSO former Prime Minister of France, who by the way hated Daladier's guts.

**[VISUAL: Daladier and Reynaud glaring at each other from across a dinner table. Lightning between their eyes.]**

**NARRATOR:** General Maxime Weygand. General Maurice Gamelin, who ALSO hated each other. Charles de Gaulle's sister. And Jean Borotra, a four-time Grand Slam champion tennis player known as the Bouncing Basque.

**[VISUAL: Borotra bouncing a tennis ball against the castle wall. A guard glares. He keeps bouncing.]**

**NARRATOR:** So for two years, the most important political prisoners in France sat in a castle, ate their meals, and passive-aggressively despised each other while the war raged on outside.

**[VISUAL: Calendar pages flying off. "1943... 1944... 1945"]**

**NARRATOR:** And then, in the spring of 1945, everything fell apart. Hitler was dead. The Reich was collapsing. And on May 2nd, the castle's commander looked out the window, saw which way the wind was blowing, and did what any brave SS officer would do.

**[VISUAL: The commander sprinting out the front gate with a suitcase, leaving a commander-shaped dust cloud.]**

**NARRATOR:** He ran. The guards followed. And suddenly, fourteen elderly French VIPs found themselves in control of a castle in the middle of hostile territory.

**[VISUAL: The prisoners standing in the empty courtyard, holding weapons the guards left behind, looking at each other.]**

**REYNAUD (character voice):** "So... we are free?"

**NARRATOR:** Well. Not exactly. Because the mountains around them were still crawling with units of the 17th SS Panzergrenadier Division. And these were not "the war is over, let's go home" Nazis. These were die-hard fanatics who were executing deserters and anyone who tried to surrender. And word was, they had orders regarding the castle's famous prisoners. Those orders were not "wish them well."

**[VISUAL: Ominous SS silhouettes on the mountain ridge. Dark clouds.]**

**NARRATOR:** The prisoners needed help. Fast.

## ACT 2: THE WORLD'S WEIRDEST RESCUE PARTY (3:30 - 7:00)

**[VISUAL: The handyman character, Zvonimir Čučković, with a toolbox.]**

**NARRATOR:** Enter Zvonimir Čučković, a Yugoslav resistance member forced to work at the castle as a handyman. On May 3rd, he told the commander he had an errand to run in town. This was a lie. What he actually did was walk FORTY MILES through Nazi-held territory to Innsbruck to find the Americans.

**[VISUAL: Čučković casually strolling past confused German patrols, whistling, holding a wrench like it's a hall pass.]**

**NARRATOR:** He made it. He told the US 103rd Infantry Division about the castle. They sent a rescue column, which got shelled, and then recalled, because it wandered into another unit's sector, and the US Army was VERY serious about paperwork.

**[VISUAL: American tanks turning around while a staff officer holds up a giant form labeled "WRONG SECTOR."]**

**NARRATOR:** Meanwhile, back at the castle, the prisoners had no idea if their handyman was even alive. So they launched Plan B: the cook.

**[VISUAL: Cook character, Andreas Krobot, mounting a bicycle heroically. Epic music that immediately cuts to sad bicycle bell sounds.]**

**NARRATOR:** Andreas Krobot pedaled to the nearby town of Wörgl carrying a letter begging for help. And in Wörgl, he found something nobody expected. A German officer... who wanted to help.

**[VISUAL: Major Josef Gangl, drawn with a kind face and a very tired expression.]**

**NARRATOR:** This is Major Josef "Sepp" Gangl. Decorated Wehrmacht officer. Served on the Eastern AND Western fronts. And by May 1945, completely done with the Nazis. Instead of retreating with the SS, Gangl had stayed behind, joined up with the Austrian resistance, and was using his remaining men to protect local civilians from SS reprisals.

**NARRATOR:** Gangl wanted to save the castle prisoners. Problem: he had about twenty men. The SS had hundreds. So Gangl did something almost unthinkable for a German major. He grabbed a white flag, drove down the road, and surrendered to the nearest American unit... so he could ask them for a favor.

**[VISUAL: Gangl's car pulls up to a Sherman tank. Gangl waves a white flag.]**

**GANGL (character voice):** "Hello. I surrender. Also, I need to borrow your tank."

**[VISUAL: Cut to Captain Jack Lee, chewing a cigar, unimpressed expression, leaning on a Sherman tank labeled "Besotten Jenny."]**

**NARRATOR:** The American he surrendered to was Captain John "Jack" Lee of the 23rd Tank Battalion, 12th Armored Division. A cigar-chewing, rough-talking tanker from New York. Lee heard Gangl's story: castle, VIPs, SS death squads, clock ticking. And Lee said the most American thing possible:

**LEE (character voice):** "Sure. Why not."

**NARRATOR:** Lee got approval from headquarters, grabbed a small force with his tank, Besotten Jenny, and set off for the castle. With the German major riding along. In the same convoy. Two days before the end of the war.

**[VISUAL: The convoy driving up a mountain road. American soldiers and German soldiers in the same trucks, side-eyeing each other.]**

**NARRATOR:** When they arrived on May 4th, it got weirder. The French prisoners had already organized their own defense, and the man they'd put in charge was Kurt-Siegfried Schrader... a Waffen-SS captain.

**[VISUAL: Record scratch. Schrader waves awkwardly.]**

**NARRATOR:** Schrader had been recovering from wounds near the castle, had befriended the prisoners, had completely rejected Nazism, and had even moved his own family into the castle to protect them. So the defense of Castle Itter now consisted of: about a dozen American soldiers, one Sherman tank, roughly a dozen anti-Nazi German soldiers, one reformed SS officer, a handful of Austrian resistance fighters, and fourteen French politicians and celebrities who all hated each other.

**[VISUAL: Team lineup shot, superhero-movie style, but everyone looks confused about why the others are there.]**

**NARRATOR:** Total defenders: around three dozen. Coming up the hill: over a hundred and fifty Waffen-SS troops with machine guns, mortars, and an 88 millimeter gun.

**LEE (character voice):** "...we're gonna need a bigger castle."

## ACT 3: THE LAST BATTLE (7:00 - 11:00)

**[VISUAL: Dawn, May 5th. Quiet. Then muzzle flashes erupt from the treeline.]**

**NARRATOR:** At dawn on May 5th, the SS attacked. Machine gun fire raked the walls. Mortar rounds dropped into the courtyard. And that 88, one of the most feared guns of the entire war, started hammering the castle directly.

**NARRATOR:** Lee had positioned Besotten Jenny at the front gate, where its machine guns broke up the first SS assaults. Gangl's Germans and Lee's Americans fought side by side on the walls. And the French VIPs? Lee ordered them to hide in the cellar.

**[VISUAL: Lee pointing at the cellar door. Cut to the elderly Frenchmen crossing their arms.]**

**NARRATOR:** They said no. These men, some in their seventies, grabbed rifles left behind by the guards and joined the fight. Two former Prime Ministers of France, who could not agree on literally anything else, agreed on this.

**[VISUAL: Daladier and Reynaud back to back on the wall, firing, still refusing to make eye contact.]**

**NARRATOR:** For six hours, the defenders held. But it was going badly. The 88 found its mark and Besotten Jenny was knocked out. Ammunition ran low. The SS pushed closer to the walls. And then came the moment that turned Josef Gangl into a hero of two nations.

**[VISUAL: Music drops out. The animation slows down.]**

**NARRATOR:** Former Prime Minister Paul Reynaud was exposed in the line of fire. Gangl moved to pull him to safety. An SS sniper's bullet found Gangl first. A German officer, in the last days of the war, died protecting a French Prime Minister from other Germans. He was the only defender killed that day. Today, there is a street in Wörgl named after him, and he is honored as an Austrian national hero.

**[VISUAL: Brief, respectful still frame of Gangl. Then back to the chaos.]**

**NARRATOR:** The castle was in trouble. Ammo nearly gone. Walls crumbling. Radio damaged. Somebody had to get word to the American reinforcements. So who volunteers? The tennis player.

**[VISUAL: Jean Borotra stretching like he's about to play a Wimbledon final.]**

**NARRATOR:** Jean Borotra, 46 years old, hopped over the castle wall and RAN through the SS lines. Through actual gunfire. This man dodged an entire SS assault force on foot, reached the American 142nd Infantry Regiment, and delivered the message. He then asked for a uniform and a weapon so he could go BACK and keep fighting. Legend.

**[VISUAL: Borotra sprinting downhill while SS soldiers fire, tennis "out!" line-call graphics popping up as bullets miss him.]**

**NARRATOR:** Just as the SS launched what would have been their final assault, the relief column from the 142nd came roaring up the road. Tanks, infantry, the works. The SS attack collapsed instantly. Over a hundred SS soldiers were captured. The prisoners were safe. The strangest battle of World War II was over.

**[VISUAL: The defenders on the battered wall watching the SS scatter. The Frenchmen cheer. Lee lights a new cigar.]**

## ACT 4: AFTERMATH (11:00 - 12:30)

**NARRATOR:** Two days later, Germany surrendered. The war in Europe was over.

**NARRATOR:** Jack Lee received the Distinguished Service Cross. The French VIPs went home on May 10th, where several of them went right back into French politics. Historian Stephen Harding, who wrote the definitive book on the battle, points out that if the SS had gotten into that castle, the men who shaped postwar France would have been erased in a single afternoon. This tiny battle of a few dozen defenders quietly protected the future of an entire country.

**NARRATOR:** Schrader, the SS officer who switched sides, was arrested by the Americans for his party affiliation, but his sentence was reduced because of what he did at Itter. And Sepp Gangl is remembered as proof that even in the final hours of the most destructive war in human history, one person's choice still mattered.

**[VISUAL: The castle at sunset, restored to peaceful. The little "hotel" sign reappears.]**

**NARRATOR:** So the next time someone tells you they know everything about World War II, ask them about the time an American tanker, a Wehrmacht major, an SS defector, two rival Prime Ministers, and a tennis champion defended a castle together. Because the strangest stories in history are usually the true ones.

**[VISUAL: End card with subscribe animation and two video thumbnails for the next episodes.]**

**NARRATOR:** If you want more real operations nobody talks about, subscribe. Next up: the ghost army that fooled Hitler with inflatable tanks.

**[END]**
