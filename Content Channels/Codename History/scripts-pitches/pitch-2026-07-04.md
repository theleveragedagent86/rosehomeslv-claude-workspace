# Codename History - Story Pitch (World War 2) - 2026-07-04

Five vetted, real, source-gated WW2 candidates. Mixed theatre and type: a bloodless heist raid, the strangest last stand of the war, a Pacific intelligence coup, an Eastern Front escape, and a naval logistics epic. Reply with the number you want and it runs the full research + write + fact-check + package.

---

## 1. Operation Postmaster: The Pirate Raid Britain Denied Ever Happening
- **Hook:** A handful of British irregulars threw a dinner party to distract the crew, then literally stole three Axis ships out of a neutral harbor in under half an hour.
- **Why it works / how obscure:** January 1942, neutral Spanish Fernando Po. The Small Scale Raiding Force under Gus March-Phillipps seized the liner Duchessa d'Aosta and two smaller vessels in a near-bloodless snatch. Comedy is the diplomatic scramble to pretend the ships "defected"; Britain publicly denied all involvement. Still a footnote despite the 2024 "Ministry of Ungentlemanly Warfare" film.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / deep-dive Operation / ~18 min.
- **Sources (verified real):** Imperial War Museums (https://www.iwm.org.uk/history/operation-postmaster-the-most-daring-mission-of-the-second-world-war); Thomas Harder, Anders Lassen biographer (https://thomasharder.dk/en/anders-lassens-war-operation-postmaster-comacchio); Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Postmaster).
- **Accuracy caveat:** Neutral-territory legality was the whole point of the cover story. Do not overstate it as a clean commando battle. It was a snatch, then denied.

## 2. The Battle of Castle Itter: The Strangest Fight of the War
- **Hook:** For one day in May 1945, American GIs and Wehrmacht soldiers stood shoulder to shoulder on a castle wall, shooting at the SS, while a French tennis champion loaded their rifles.
- **Why it works / how obscure:** A genuinely bizarre last stand where sworn enemies became battlefield allies to defend imprisoned French VIPs (Daladier, Reynaud, Weygand, Borotra). The only defender killed was the German major, honored afterward as an Austrian hero. Well documented, still a footnote to most viewers.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / deep-dive Operation / ~20 min.
- **Sources (verified real):** Britannica, "Battle for Castle Itter" (https://www.britannica.com/event/Battle-for-Castle-Itter); Task & Purpose (https://taskandpurpose.com/history/ww2-battle-of-castle-itter/); Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Castle_Itter).
- **Accuracy caveat:** "Strangest battle" is popular framing, not a formal designation. Some accounts overstate the number of ex-POW combatants. Keep the prisoner roster accurate and the fight itself straight.

## 3. The Z Plan: How Filipino Guerrillas Fished Japan's Battle Plan Out of the Sea
- **Hook:** Japan's entire secret naval strategy washed ashore in a red leather portfolio, was retrieved by a shopkeeper, then smuggled out of the Philippines inside mortar shells.
- **Why it works / how obscure:** April 1944. Admiral Fukudome's plane crash-landed off Cebu, the "Z Plan" documents floated free, and Lt. Col. James Cushing's guerrillas recovered them and shipped them to Australia by submarine, directly shaping the Battle of the Philippine Sea. Pacific theatre, truly obscure, Tier-1 archive documented.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / deep-dive Operation / ~22 min.
- **Sources (verified real):** U.S. National Archives, Prologue magazine (https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2005/fall/z-plan); Dirk Jan Barreveld, "Cushing's Coup" (https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/26805751); Wikipedia, Philippine resistance against Japan (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_resistance_against_Japan).
- **Accuracy caveat:** Cushing's hostage negotiation (returning Fukudome and survivors on April 10, tied to halting civilian reprisals) is real and must be played grave, not comic.

## 4. Devyatayev's Flight: The Prisoner Who Stole a Nazi Bomber Off a V-2 Rocket Base
- **Hook:** A starving Soviet POW at a top-secret missile island killed a guard, walked onto the runway in a stolen uniform, and flew a Heinkel bomber full of prisoners home.
- **Why it works / how obscure:** February 1945, Usedom island (the Peenemunde V-weapon complex). Pilot Mikhail Devyatayev and nine POWs hijacked an He-111 and reached Soviet lines. The aircraft carried V-rocket telemetry gear, so the escape doubled as an intelligence windfall. Eastern Front, almost unknown to Western audiences.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / Standard / ~14 min.
- **Sources (verified real):** HistoryNet (https://www.historynet.com/devyatayev.htm); Wikipedia, Mikhail Devyatayev (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Devyataev); Aircrew Remembered (https://aircrewremembered.com/devyatayev-mikhail.html).
- **Accuracy caveat:** His postwar years under NKVD suspicion (treated as a possible collaborator until cleared in 1957) are documented and played straight. Some Soviet-era retellings inflate the intelligence value; anchor that to sourced accounts, not legend.

## 5. The Tanker That Refused to Die: SS Ohio and the Convoy That Saved Malta
- **Hook:** Malta had days of fuel left, and its survival came down to one bomb-shattered tanker dragged into harbor lashed between two destroyers, decks awash, settling on the bottom the moment the last oil was pumped out.
- **Why it works / how obscure:** Operation Pedestal (August 1942) is logistics-as-drama: a besieged island, a gauntlet of air and submarine attack, and a single American-built tanker whose delivery decided a campaign. Known to enthusiasts, but the Ohio's ordeal is a footnote to general audiences.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / deep-dive Operation / ~22 min.
- **Sources (verified real):** Imperial War Museums, "Operation Pedestal and the Siege of Malta" (https://www.iwm.org.uk/collections/item/object/205194917); SS Ohio (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Ohio); Dudley Mason, George Cross (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Mason).
- **Accuracy caveat:** Play the human cost straight. Pedestal lost nine of fourteen merchant ships and hundreds of lives to deliver the fuel. The Ohio was effectively finished after the run.

**Manager's pick:** Operation Postmaster first. Best comedy-to-stakes ratio (a bloodless heist masked by a literal dinner party), a clean deep-dive runtime, rising recognition from the recent film while still a footnote, and anchored by an IWM Tier-1 source. Castle Itter is the close second for pure "this cannot be real, but it is."

**Strong runners-up (not in the 5, available on request):** The Great Panjandrum (Britain's runaway rocket wheel, pure-comedy Standard, ~11 min); Last Stand of Taffy 3 off Samar (destroyers charge a battleship fleet, ~20 min); HMS Glowworm (destroyer rams a cruiser, first VC of the war, ~12 min); The Wooden Horse escape from Stalag Luft III (~13 min); Operation Mincemeat (the corpse that fooled Hitler, semi-famous, ~20 min).
