## 1. A Toilet Sank This Nazi Submarine
- **Hook:** Nine days into its first patrol, a brand-new U-boat was doomed by its own high-tech toilet, which flooded the sub with seawater and released poison gas.
- **Why lesser-known:** It is a punchline, not a battle, so serious histories skip it. Most people have never heard a warship was lost to plumbing.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / Standard / ~9 min.
- **Sources (verified real):** Wikipedia, "German submarine U-1206" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_submarine_U-1206); U.S. Naval Institute post on U-1206's loss; War History Online, "The Plumbing Disaster That Led to the Sinking of U-1206" (https://www.warhistoryonline.com/world-war-ii/plumbing-disaster-u-1206.html).
- **Accuracy caveat:** Core facts (14 April 1945, off Peterhead, a mis-operated pressurized toilet valve flooded the boat, chlorine gas from the batteries, forced surfacing, scuttled) are documented. The "captain could not flush and called a specialist" detail is widely repeated but partly anecdotal, so frame it as the commonly reported account. Crew losses small, played straight.
- --- viral + cost ---
- **PACKAGE:** title above + THUMBNAIL: cutaway U-boat interior, a comically ominous toilet glowing, green gas curling up, panicked crew + COLD OPEN: a sailor triumphantly pulls a flush lever, then the whole compartment erupts with seawater.
- **VIRAL SCORE:** 5/5 (curiosity: no way that's real; image: the glowing toilet; bridge: everyone has feared a bathroom disaster; shareability: instant "you HAVE to hear this").
- **BUILD COST:** Lean (single interior set, tiny cast, one big flooding gag, almost entirely narration and reactions).

## 2. The Bear the Army Made a Soldier
- **Hook:** To smuggle their pet bear onto a troop ship, Polish soldiers enlisted him with a rank, a paybook, and a serial number. Then he carried live artillery shells at Monte Cassino.
- **Why lesser-known:** People know "a bear helped at Monte Cassino" as a meme, but not that he was a legally enlisted private, later promoted to corporal.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / Standard / ~10 min.
- **Sources (verified real):** TIME, "Wojtek the Bear: Surprising WWII Animal Hero" (https://time.com/4731787/wojtek-the-bear-history/); Snopes, "Wojtek the Bear: A Real Polish WWII Soldier" (https://www.snopes.com/articles/464800/wojtek-the-bear-wwii-polish-soldier/); Polish History, "Wojtek: A Bear Who Became A Soldier" (https://polishhistory.pl/wojtek-a-bear-who-became-a-soldier/).
- **Accuracy caveat:** He genuinely hauled crates of shells at Monte Cassino, but the tidy "never dropped one" line is unit lore, so frame it as what his comrades reported. Keep it charming, not exaggerated. Research stage should anchor the enlistment/promotion on a Tier-1 source (e.g. Imperial War Museums / Polish Institute).
- --- viral + cost ---
- **PACKAGE:** title above + THUMBNAIL: a brown bear upright in a Polish army setting, cradling an artillery shell like a football + COLD OPEN: a bear on two legs receiving a wooden crate of shells from a soldier and lumbering off with it.
- **VIRAL SCORE:** 5/5 (curiosity: wait, real?; image: unbeatable bear-with-shell thumbnail; bridge: the animal-and-soldiers bond; shareability: screenshot-and-send).
- **BUILD COST:** Standard (a couple of settings and the bear as a recurring character, but mostly hold-frames, reactions, and one supply-line map).

## 3. One Soldier Captured an Entire City
- **Hook:** A one-eyed, back-broken Canadian private lost his best friend at dusk, then took a Dutch city from the Germans alone by dawn.
- **Why lesser-known:** He refused a medal from Field Marshal Montgomery and stayed out of the spotlight. Most Canadians still cannot name him.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / Standard / ~11 min.
- **Sources (verified real):** Veterans Affairs Canada, "Leo Major" (https://www.veterans.gc.ca/en/remembrance/people-and-stories/leo-major); Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Leo Major" (https://www.britannica.com/biography/Leo-Major); Library and Archives Canada Blog, "Have you heard of Leo Major" (https://thediscoverblog.com/2022/11/10/have-you-heard-of-leo-major-the-liberator-of-zwolle/).
- **Accuracy caveat:** He ran a loud one-man bluff (grenades, captured machine guns, fires) so the Zwolle garrison thought a full force had arrived. The Dutch resistance and a retreating garrison shaped the outcome. Play it as a bluff that worked, not a lone kill-count fantasy.
- --- viral + cost ---
- **PACKAGE:** title above + THUMBNAIL: one eyepatched soldier standing alone on an empty dark street, the whole city black behind him + COLD OPEN: a single man sprinting down a black street hurling grenades and shouting orders to imaginary troops on both sides.
- **VIRAL SCORE:** 5/5 (curiosity: how, alone?; image: iconic eyepatch-and-empty-city frame; bridge: underdog grief and defiance; shareability: "he did WHAT alone").
- **BUILD COST:** Lean (one town at night, tiny cast, carried by narration, reaction shots, and a simple map of Zwolle).

## 4. Hitler Gave This Fake Spy A Medal
- **Hook:** A Spanish chicken farmer invented 27 imaginary spies, fooled Berlin into ignoring the real D-Day beaches, and earned the Iron Cross from Hitler and a British honor for the same lie.
- **Why lesser-known:** Deception work stayed classified for decades. The beaches get the credit, not the one man who kept German panzers waiting for an invasion that never came.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / deep-dive Operation / ~13 min.
- **Sources (verified real):** MI5 (The Security Service) official history, "Agent GARBO" (https://www.mi5.gov.uk/history/world-war-ii/agent-garbo); The National WWII Museum, "Secret Agents, Secret Armies: The D-Day Misfit Spies" (https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/d-day-spies); Wikipedia, "Juan Pujol Garcia" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Pujol_Garc%C3%ADa).
- **Accuracy caveat:** Clean and strongly sourced (MI5 confirms the fictional network, the Iron Cross, and the Fortitude role). He received the MBE (a British honor), sometimes loosely reported as OBE, so state "a British honor" or specify MBE to stay exact.
- --- viral + cost ---
- **PACKAGE:** title above + THUMBNAIL: a mild farmer holding an Iron Cross in one hand and a British medal in the other, ghostly outlines of 27 fake agents behind him + COLD OPEN: Pujol at a typewriter inventing spies out of thin air, each one puffing into existence as a see-through cartoon.
- **VIRAL SCORE:** 5/5 (curiosity: how did both sides decorate him; image: two enemy medals, one man; bridge: the ultimate bluff; shareability: "he faked 27 spies and Hitler thanked him").
- **BUILD COST:** Lean (one recurring lead, a few desks and offices, phantom agents as reusable ghost cards, map beats for the deception, narration-driven).

## 5. 720 Poles Held Off an Entire German Corps
- **Hook:** A Polish captain and roughly 720 conscripts stalled Guderian's advance for three days, then he blew himself up rather than watch the position fall.
- **Why lesser-known:** Overshadowed by the fall of Poland itself. The West remembers the invasion, not the tiny garrison that briefly broke its timetable.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / Standard / ~12 min.
- **Sources (verified real):** Wikipedia, "Battle of Wizna" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Wizna); Wikipedia, "Wladyslaw Raginis" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_Raginis); War History Online, "The Polish Thermopylae: Wizna 1939" (https://www.warhistoryonline.com/instant-articles/the-polish-thermopylae-wizna-1939.html).
- **Accuracy caveat:** Force ratios vary widely by source (720 defenders vs a German corps of tens of thousands; the popular "42,000" is an upper figure, hence the softened title). Use the defended-crossing framing, not a hard headcount. Captain Raginis's suicide after ordering surrender is documented. Human cost (few survivors) played straight. Research stage should pull a Tier-1 Polish source (IPN / Polish military archives) to anchor the numbers.
- --- viral + cost ---
- **PACKAGE:** title above + THUMBNAIL: a lone helmeted soldier in a concrete bunker slit, endless gray German tanks filling the valley beyond, red arrow marking the mismatch + COLD OPEN: the captain calmly lighting a grenade against his own chest as the bunker door buckles.
- **VIRAL SCORE:** 5/5 (curiosity: how, for three days?; image: one-vs-a-horde frame; bridge: everyone has faced a hopeless fight; shareability: the 720-vs-a-corps number).
- **BUILD COST:** Lean (one location of two river bunkers, small cast, carried by maps, hold-frames, and a single marquee last-stand beat).
