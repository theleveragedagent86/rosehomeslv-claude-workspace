# Codename History - Story Pitch (WW2) - 2026-09-01

Two Idea-Scouts ran parallel lanes: European land / deception / raids / escapes, and naval / air /
Pacific / engineering. Ten candidates researched, five shipped below, ordered best-first. Every
candidate passed the source-availability gate (2 to 3 named reputable sources confirming it is real
and documented). Demand verdicts come from the YouTube outlier check, measured against each
channel's own baseline, never raw view counts.

Exclusion list applied: all 17 built or scripted episodes, the 7 previously pitched holds, and the
channel-bible backlog.

## 1. The Only Man to Bomb America, and the Sword He Brought Back | Codename History

- **Hook:** Japan's plan to set the American West on fire came down to one man, one folding
  floatplane bolted to a submarine, and two incendiary bombs dropped on an Oregon forest that had
  been rained on the night before.
- **Why it works / how obscure:** Warrant Officer Nobuo Fujita launched from submarine I-25 on
  9 September 1942 and became the only person ever to conduct a manned aerial bombing of the
  American mainland. The bomb started a small fire the US Forest Service walked over and put out.
  Then the turn: in 1962 the town he bombed invited him back as a parade guest, and he brought his
  family's 400-year-old katana, reportedly intending to kill himself with it if the town wanted him
  tried. Instead he gave it to them, spent thirty years raising money for their library, was made
  an honorary citizen days before he died in 1997, and in 1998 his daughter buried part of his
  ashes at the bomb site. Comedy in the setup, complete sincerity in the ending.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / Standard single-person story / ~9 min.
- **Sources (verified real):** Naval History and Heritage Command, "H-010-6 Attacks on the U.S.
  Mainland" (https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-010/h-010-6.html);
  City of Brookings, Oregon official "Fujita Sword" page (https://www.brookings.or.us/291/Fujita-Sword);
  Nicholas D. Kristof, The New York Times, 3 October 1997 (Fujita obituary).
- **Demand:** PROVEN, and the cleanest signal in the batch. Best example: Thomas Potter (~2.11K
  subs), "OREGON COAST BOMBED BY THE JAPANESE DURING WORLD WAR II" 47,995 views vs ~180 typical
  (median of 8 other uploads, which are Bigfoot and eclipse videos) = roughly 270x outlier. Only
  about 5 real channels have covered it and they are local outlets: Redwood Voice 24,108, CBS
  Sunday Morning 19,479, Oregon Public Broadcasting 8,954, Caliban Rising 3,704. No major history
  channel has taken it.
- **Accuracy caveat:** Say "the only manned aerial bombing of the US mainland", because the
  mainland was also shelled by submarines and later hit by Fu-Go balloon bombs. The seppuku intent
  rests on the 1997 NYT obituary, so present it as what Fujita said he had intended, not as
  established fact.

## 2. Popski's Private Army: The Smallest Army in the British Army | Codename History

- **Hook:** A Belgian sugar-factory engineer was so bad at taking orders that the British Army gave
  up and let him start his own army, 23 men, its own cap badge, and a name the War Office had to
  write on official paperwork: "No. 1 Demolition Squadron, PPA."
- **Why it works / how obscure:** Vladimir Peniakoff wanted to join the LRDG, got told no, and
  instead talked Cairo into chartering him a personal private army with a hand-engraved silver
  astrolabe as its badge. It is the SAS story with the seriousness surgically removed, and almost
  nobody outside British special-forces circles knows it exists.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / Standard, or deep-dive Operation if Ryan wants it end to end /
  ~9 min Standard.
- **Sources (verified real):** Vladimir Peniakoff, Private Army (Jonathan Cape, 1950), his own
  memoir; Imperial War Museums, headdress badge, No. 1 Demolition Squadron
  (https://www.iwm.org.uk/collections/item/object/30070991); Imperial War Museums film, "Popski's
  Private Army in Italy in 1945" (https://www.iwm.org.uk/collections/item/object/205087096).
- **Demand:** PROVEN. Best example: Travelbag Photography (~2.84K subs), "The Search for Popski's
  Lost Patrol" 388,528 views vs ~1,000 typical = ~389x outlier. Corroborated on a real WW2 channel:
  The Ulysses Files (~48.1K subs) 74,943 views vs ~22K median = 3.4x. Roughly 10 to 14 other
  channels cover it, nearly all under 2,000 views, so the lane is open.
- **Accuracy caveat:** Peniakoff is his own primary source and he is a charming, self-serving
  narrator. Unit size claims drift (23 all ranks at formation, never above about 120 in Italy).
  Cross-check every raid tally against IWM and unit records, not the memoir.

## 3. The Six Men Who Volunteered to Be Bricked Into a Rock | Codename History

- **Hook:** Britain sealed six volunteers inside the Rock of Gibraltar with seven years of food,
  two doctors, a radio, and no exit, then forgot about them so thoroughly that the chamber was not
  found again until 1997.
- **Why it works / how obscure:** Operation Tracer. If Gibraltar fell, the men would already be
  inside the mountain watching the harbour through a slit. There was no way out. The medical
  officer's actual job description included dealing with anyone who died in there. It never
  happened, which is the twist, and the cave is real and listed by the Gibraltar government today.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / Standard contingency-plan story / ~9 min.
- **Sources (verified real):** Government of Gibraltar, Ministry for Heritage, "Stay Behind Cave
  complex," listing HLMP2/036
  (https://www.ministryforheritage.gi/heritage-and-antiquities/stay-behind-cave-complex-1072);
  Surgeon Lt-Cdr Bruce Cooper's 2008 confirmation of the site, recorded in that listing; BFBS
  Forces News reporting on the chamber.
- **Demand:** PROVEN, though the multiple is 2.4x rather than the 5x bar, so read it as solid
  rather than explosive. Best example: Calum / @CalumRaasay (~345K subs), "Buried Alive: The Secret
  WWII Bunker Hidden Inside Gibraltar's Rock" 1,924,394 views vs ~789K median across his last 30 =
  2.4x. About 5 other real channels cover it: Tom Finlayson 33,157, Science Channel 27,860, BFBS
  19,743, Get Up 2 Date 2,113.
- **Accuracy caveat:** Two real gaps. The claim that TNA file ADM 223/464 is "the single official
  document" mentioning Tracer comes from secondary sources and is NOT VERIFIED in TNA Discovery.
  Three of the six men's names are still NOT FOUND, so do not state a full roster. The BFBS
  citation above needs a stable URL pinned during Mode 2 research.

## 4. The Torpedo That Refused to Explode | Codename History

- **Hook:** For twenty months the US submarine force fired torpedoes that ran too deep, blew up
  early, or bounced off Japanese hulls with a clang, and the bureau that built them insisted the
  problem was that the submariners were aiming wrong.
- **Why it works / how obscure:** Three separate defects stacked on top of each other, each one
  hiding the next, and the fix only came when Admiral Lockwood started firing live torpedoes into
  fishing nets and cliffs because Washington would not test its own weapon. The absurd centerpiece:
  the magnetic exploder was kept so secret for sixteen years that enlisted torpedomen were not told
  it existed, so nobody who handled the thing daily could report it was broken. Ends dead straight
  with USS Tang, sunk in October 1944 by her own circular-running torpedo, 78 dead, 9 survivors,
  the captain in the water watching his boat go down.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / Standard systems-failure story, or deep-dive if taken end to end
  / ~9 min Standard.
- **Sources (verified real):** Naval History and Heritage Command, "Navy's Use of Torpedoes"
  (https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/exploration-and-innovation/navy-torpedoes.html);
  US Naval Institute, Naval History Magazine, Dec 1992, "Technical Report: The Mk-XIV Torpedo:
  Lessons for Today"
  (https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/1992/december/technical-report-mk-xiv-torpedo-lessons-today);
  NHHC, US Submarine Losses, "Tang (SS 306)"
  (https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/u/united-states-submarine-losses/tang-ss-306.html).
- **Demand:** PROVEN, but the lane is busy. Best example: Drachinifel (~596K subs), 1,416,521 views
  vs ~40,000 typical (median of 10 recent uploads) = ~35x outlier. Roughly 10 other channels cover
  it, several of them large. The differentiator would be the OverSimplified treatment, since every
  existing version is a technical lecture and none of them play the Bureau of Ordnance as the
  antagonist.
- **Accuracy caveat:** Three defects were found in sequence (depth control 1942, magnetic exploder
  mid-1943, contact firing pin Aug to Sep 1943), not one. Do not fold the Tang beat in as a Mark 14
  failure: Tang's fatal weapon was a Mark 18 electric torpedo. Totals for wasted torpedoes vary
  wildly between secondary sources, so avoid a hard number.

## 5. The Only Woman in the Foreign Legion Drove the Breakout | Codename History

- **Hook:** 3,703 Free French held Rommel off for sixteen days in an empty patch of Libyan desert,
  and when they finally broke out through their own minefield at night, the car leading the entire
  column was driven by an English admiral's daughter who had refused the order to evacuate.
- **Why it works / how obscure:** Bir Hakeim. Susan Travers is the only woman ever officially in
  the French Foreign Legion, she was General Koenig's driver, and her car came out of the breakout
  full of holes. In France the battle is national memory. In English it is a footnote, and Travers
  is essentially unknown. A siege story with a single-person spine.
- **War / tier / runtime:** WW2 / Standard, or deep-dive Battle if Ryan wants the full siege /
  ~9 min Standard.
- **Sources (verified real):** Ministere des Armees, Chemins de memoire, "26 mai - 11 juin 1942.
  La bataille de Bir Hakeim"
  (https://www.cheminsdememoire.gouv.fr/fr/26-mai-11-juin-1942-la-bataille-de-bir-hakeim);
  Ministere des Armees, "La bataille de Bir-Hakeim: la guerre du desert"
  (https://www.defense.gouv.fr/terre/actualites/bataille-bir-hakeim-guerre-du-desert);
  Susan Travers with Wendy Holden, Tomorrow to Be Brave (Simon and Schuster, 2000).
- **Demand:** UNTAPPED in English, and that is a genuine gamble, not a free win. French appetite is
  real and measurable: Nota Bene (~2.77M subs) 573,045 views vs ~288K median = 2.0x, plus the
  French Ministry's own channel at 192,688 and 126,693. English coverage tops out at Nettempereur
  34,972 and House of History 32,824. On Travers herself the best video is kepiblancrm at 8,165
  views from 16 years ago, everything else under 3,200. Roughly 15 channels on the battle, nearly
  all French, and effectively none on Travers.
- **Accuracy caveat:** Casualty figures conflict. French official sources give roughly 3,703 at the
  start, about 2,500 out, and more than 1,000 killed, wounded, captured or missing, while other
  passages in the same material cite about 900 French losses and about 3,300 German. Present the
  official figure and name the disagreement. Travers's memoir is late-life recollection, so anchor
  breakout details to the ministry sources.

## Manager's pick

**#1, Nobuo Fujita.** It is the only candidate where a real outlier sits on a channel with
essentially no audience, which means the demand lives in the story rather than in the channel, and
no serious history channel has claimed the lane. It is also the most on-brand shape the channel
has: a documented absurdity for two acts, then an ending that earns silence. Fujita's return to
Brookings is a `TONE: STRAIGHT` close that does not need a joke to land.

If Ryan wants the funniest one instead, take **#2 Popski**, which has the strongest raw outlier
(~389x) and the single best true setup on the list.

## Runners-up (verified, available on request)

- **The Battle Against Nobody (Kiska, August 1943).** 34,000 Americans and 5,300 Canadians stormed
  an island the Japanese had evacuated 2.5 weeks earlier, then fought each other in the fog.
  PROVEN at ~20x on Simple History, but genuinely CROWDED: Simple History 4,383,002, Kings and
  Generals 434,302, Dark Docs 692,047, Clear and Present History 708,368. Great premise, bad lane
  for a small channel.
- **Alexandria, December 1941, told from the British side.** Six Italians on human torpedoes mined
  two battleships, and de la Penne warned the captain of the ship he had mined, from inside it.
  CROWDED at 1.2x. The only differentiated angle is the cover-up: the Royal Navy kept up colours
  and routine on two battleships sitting on the harbour bottom.
- **Harry Ree and the Peugeot works.** SOE agent talks the Peugeot family into sabotaging their own
  factory after an RAF raid missed and killed the town. UNTAPPED at 17 views on the only dedicated
  video, so no demand evidence at all, but the adjacent sabotage lane sells (Timeline 435,702).
  Three source conflicts to resolve first: bombing date, civilian casualties, and the Peugeot
  contact's first name.
- **Vincigliata, the castle full of generals who tunnelled out with kitchen knives.** CROWDED on
  Carton de Wiart as a listicle subject (1.5M plus), but zero dedicated videos on the escape
  itself. Sell the ensemble, not the unkillable-soldier framing.
- **Off-theme bonus, WW1:** Zeppelin L 59, "the Africa Ship", built to be dismantled and eaten by
  the crew it was resupplying in German East Africa. Roughly 4,200 miles and 95 hours aloft. Almost
  no English coverage. Worth holding for the next WW1 round. Carries a strong myth-bust: the
  popular "British faked the recall signal" story traces to Richard Meinertzhagen, whose diaries
  are now known to be substantially fabricated.
