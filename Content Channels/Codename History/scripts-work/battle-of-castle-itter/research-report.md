# Research Report: The Battle for Castle Itter (5 May 1945)

## One-paragraph summary

In the final days of the war in Europe, five days after Hitler killed himself and two days before Germany's surrender, American tankers and defecting Wehrmacht soldiers fought SIDE BY SIDE against attacking Waffen-SS troops to defend a group of high-profile French prisoners held at Schloss Itter in the Austrian Tyrol. It is one of the only known WW2 actions where American and German soldiers fought as allies. The American commander was Lieutenant John C. "Jack" Lee Jr. of the 23rd Tank Battalion, 12th Armored Division. The German defenders were led by Major Josef "Sepp" Gangl, who had already defected to the Austrian resistance. Gangl was killed protecting former French PM Paul Reynaud, the only defender to die. Relief arrived that afternoon. Popular accounts call it "the strangest battle of World War II," which is popular framing, not a formal designation.

## Verified timeline
- Late April / early May 1945 - Major Josef "Sepp" Gangl, stationed near Wörgl, contacts the local Austrian resistance under Alois Mayr and provides them information and weapons. [Wikipedia "Josef Gangl"; GunsAmerica/Will Dabbs]
- Early May 1945 - The German garrison abandons/loosens control of Schloss Itter, a castle prison holding French VIPs; the prisoners and a sympathetic SS officer seek outside help. [Wikipedia "Battle of Castle Itter"; Task & Purpose]
- 5 May 1945 - The Battle of Castle Itter: a combined force of American tankers, Gangl's Wehrmacht men, SS-Hauptsturmführer Kurt-Siegfried Schrader, and armed French prisoners defends the castle against roughly 100 to 150 Waffen-SS attackers. [Wikipedia; Task & Purpose; Britannica per search]
- 5 May 1945, during the battle - Jean Borotra vaults the castle wall and runs through SS positions to reach American lines and bring relief. [Wikipedia; Task & Purpose; HistoryNet]
- 5 May 1945, ~16:00 - Relief force (2nd Battalion, 142nd Infantry Regiment, 36th Division) arrives and breaks the siege. [Wikipedia; HistoryNet]
- 7 May 1945 - Germany signs its unconditional surrender (two days after the battle). [Wikipedia, general record]

Note: HistoryNet dates the action to "May 4," but Wikipedia, Task & Purpose, Britannica, and the standard record date the battle 5 May 1945; the 4th refers to the preceding buildup. Using 5 May.

## Key figures
- **Lieutenant John C. "Jack" Lee Jr.**, US Army, 23rd Tank Battalion, 12th Armored Division, US XXI Corps - commanded the American armored relief that occupied and defended the castle. Later promoted to captain; awarded the Distinguished Service Cross. From Norwich, New York. [Wikipedia; HistoryNet; Task & Purpose] (Note: some sources call him "Captain" reflecting his later promotion; his rank at the battle was 1st Lieutenant.)
- **Major Josef "Sepp" Gangl**, Wehrmacht - defected and joined the Austrian resistance under Alois Mayr near Wörgl; led the German soldiers defending the castle alongside the Americans; killed in the battle protecting Paul Reynaud. Honored as an Austrian resistance hero; Sepp-Gangl-Straße in Wörgl is named for him. [Wikipedia "Josef Gangl"; Wikipedia "Battle of Castle Itter"; GunsAmerica]
- **SS-Hauptsturmführer Kurt-Siegfried Schrader** - an SS officer who sided with the prisoners and helped defend the castle; received a 2-year sentence postwar; died 1995. [Wikipedia]
- **SS-Oberführer Georg Bochmann**, 17th SS Panzergrenadier Division "Götz von Berlichingen" - commanded the attacking SS force. [Wikipedia]
- **Alois Mayr** - leader of the local Austrian resistance group Gangl contacted. [Wikipedia "Josef Gangl"]

## French VIP prisoners (roster)
Held at Schloss Itter, transferred there as high-value prisoners. Confirmed across sources:
- **Édouard Daladier** - former French Prime Minister. [Wikipedia; Britannica]
- **Paul Reynaud** - former French Prime Minister (the man Gangl died protecting). [Wikipedia; Task & Purpose]
- **General Maxime Weygand** - former commander-in-chief. [Wikipedia; Britannica]
- **General Maurice Gamelin** - former commander-in-chief. [Wikipedia; Britannica]
- **Jean Borotra** - tennis champion (a "Four Musketeers" Wimbledon star); ran for help during the battle. [Wikipedia; Task & Purpose; HistoryNet]
- **Léon Jouhaux** - labor/trade union leader (later Nobel Peace Prize laureate). [Wikipedia; search]
- **Michel Clemenceau** - politician, son of WW1-era PM Georges Clemenceau; fired at attackers. [HistoryNet; search]
- **François de La Rocque** - right-wing political leader and later resistance figure; fired at attackers. [Wikipedia; HistoryNet]
- **Marie-Agnès Cailliau** (née de Gaulle) - elder sister of Charles de Gaulle. [Wikipedia; Britannica]
- **André François-Poncet** - diplomat/politician. [search/Britannica]

Also present: Eastern European prisoners detached from Dachau used for castle labor. [search/Britannica]

## Numbers (high-risk, source each)
- **Battle date:** 5 May 1945. [Wikipedia; Task & Purpose; Britannica]
- **American contingent:** Lee set out with 4 Sherman tanks and ~14 soldiers; during the actual battle the American force was "just over a dozen men and two Sherman tanks." [Wikipedia via search] Wikipedia infobox lists ~36 total defenders (all groups) with 4 tanks, 1 engaged. [Wikipedia]
- **German defenders under Gangl:** roughly a dozen to two dozen Wehrmacht soldiers. [Task & Purpose: "roughly two dozen under Gangl"]
- **Attacking SS force:** ~100 to 150 men of the 17th SS Panzergrenadier Division "Götz von Berlichingen," with flak/88mm guns. [Wikipedia; Wikipedia "Josef Gangl"; Task & Purpose: "more than 100"]
- **Defender casualties:** 1 killed (Gangl) and 4 wounded. [Wikipedia; Task & Purpose]
- **Tank:** the M4 Sherman "Besotten Jenny" was knocked out by an 88mm round (destroyed; gas tanks caught fire per HistoryNet). [Wikipedia; HistoryNet; Task & Purpose]
- **Relief arrival:** ~16:00, 2nd Battalion, 142nd Infantry Regiment (36th Division). [Wikipedia; HistoryNet]
- **Context:** five days after Hitler's suicide (30 April 1945), two days before German surrender (7 May 1945). [Wikipedia, general record]

## Absurd / comedic beats (all real)
- American tankers and German Wehrmacht soldiers fighting as allies, on the same wall, against the SS. Genuinely one of the only such WW2 actions. [Wikipedia; Task & Purpose]
- The defenders included a defecting Wehrmacht major, a turncoat SS captain, American GIs, and French politicians and generals who hated each other in peacetime, all sharing a firing line. [Wikipedia; HistoryNet]
- Former prime ministers and generals of France personally grabbing rifles and shooting back (Reynaud, Clemenceau, La Rocque, Borotra all fired). [HistoryNet; search]
- A world-famous tennis champion (Borotra) vaulting the castle wall and sprinting through SS positions to fetch the cavalry, then borrowing a US uniform to guide them back. [Wikipedia; Task & Purpose]
- The whole thing happens after Hitler is already dead, days before the war ends. The SS attacked a castle to recapture prisoners with the war already lost. [Wikipedia]

## Human-cost beats (play straight)
- **Major Josef "Sepp" Gangl killed:** fatally hit by a sniper round (7.92mm Mauser) while trying to move former PM Paul Reynaud out of the line of fire. He was the ONLY defender to die. A German officer died fighting alongside his former enemies to save French prisoners. Honored as an Austrian resistance hero; a Wörgl street bears his name. Play completely straight. [Wikipedia; Wikipedia "Josef Gangl"; Task & Purpose]
- Four other defenders wounded. [Wikipedia; Task & Purpose]
- These were high-value hostages the SS intended to recapture or kill in the war's final chaos; the stakes for the prisoners were execution. Play straight. [general record]

## Myth vs record (corrections)
- **"The strangest battle of World War II"** - Popular telling: an official / definitive title. Actual: this is popular framing used by many outlets and by Stephen Harding's book, NOT a formal historical designation. State it as "popular accounts call it." [Wikipedia: "Popular accounts have called it the strangest battle of World War II"; Task & Purpose; History Guild]
- **Number of prisoner-combatants overstated:** Some retellings imply all the prisoners fought. Record: a handful took up arms (Reynaud, Clemenceau, La Rocque, Borotra named as firing); keep it to the named few rather than "all the VIPs fought." [HistoryNet]
- **Lee's rank:** Some sources call him "Captain." At the battle he was a 1st Lieutenant; the captaincy and Distinguished Service Cross came after. Say Lieutenant (promoted to captain). [Wikipedia; HistoryNet]
- **Gangl's death, cause detail:** Sources agree he was killed by a sniper/rifle round while shielding Reynaud. Minor variation: Wikipedia says while moving Reynaud out of harm's way; Task & Purpose says he stood to wave Reynaud away; HistoryNet says a sniper hit him at a rooftop observation post. All agree: sniper, protecting Reynaud, sole fatality. Anchor to "killed by a sniper protecting Paul Reynaud."
