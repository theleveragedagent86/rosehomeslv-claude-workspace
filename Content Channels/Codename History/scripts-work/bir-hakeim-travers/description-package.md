## YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION (paste-ready)

Night, the tenth of June 1942. A Ford staff car waits at a narrow gap cut through a minefield in the Libyan desert. Behind it, what is left of a brigade surrounded for two weeks. Ahead of it, three lines of German and Italian positions. The driver is a thirty two year old Englishwoman named Susan Travers.

Three years later she became the first woman ever enrolled in the French Foreign Legion. She was not in it that night, and she never refused an order to leave.

This is Bir Hakeim, a dry well and an old Italian camel corps post seventy kilometres inland from the Mediterranean, held from 26 May to 11 June 1942, and the two national myths built on top of it. The French telling overclaims the battle. The English telling overclaims the driver. We go through the record instead: a garrison of about 3,700 men that every retelling calls French, though two thirds came from France's colonies and overseas territories, plus a British anti aircraft battery sitting inside the position. The first attack was Italian, not German, and the Italian armoured division that made it lost about half its tanks in under an hour. Koenig ordered every woman out at the end of May and Travers went, then attached herself to a resupply convoy and asked Koenig for permission to come back, which he granted. That is a stranger and better story than the refusal everybody repeats. On the night of 10 June the brigade broke out through a lane the sappers never finished clearing, and her car came out riddled with bullets. One account says eleven holes. She was formally enrolled in the Legion as an adjudant chef in June 1945.

And on what the sixteen days actually bought, we take the French defence ministry's own answer rather than the national one. The ministry claims three things: the battle slowed the German advance, delayed the fall of Tobruk, and let the British pull back. It does not claim the battle made El Alamein possible. A historian of the Afrika Korps calls that part ridiculous, and the calendar agrees with him, because Bir Hakeim fell on the eleventh of June and Tobruk did not fall until the twenty first.

## TITLE OPTIONS (working titles)

**The `Codename:` form is not available for this episode.** Bir Hakeim is a place, an old well and an Italian camel corps post, not a codenamed operation. All three options use the Standard form.

**What I did with the script's options:** the script supplied exactly one title and I **kept it verbatim as option 1 and as the recommendation**. Options 2 and 3 are new, because there was nothing else to refine. Both were written against the same constraint the recommended title already satisfies: they must not place Susan Travers in the Legion at Bir Hakeim, and they must not claim anything strategic for the battle that the French defence ministry does not claim for it.

1. **`She Drove the Breakout, Then Joined the Foreign Legion | Codename History`** (RECOMMENDED, and the script's own title, unchanged. **The word "then" is load-bearing and must survive any title pass.** It is the entire accuracy of the line: she drove the breakout in June 1942 as a Free French volunteer driver, and she enrolled in the Legion in June 1945. Any rewrite that collapses the two into "the Foreign Legion driver who drove the breakout" inverts the episode.)
2. `Sixteen Days, One Narrow Lane, and Two National Myths | Codename History` (the structure of the episode rather than its personality. Sixteen days is 26 May to 11 June, the lane is the breakout, and the two myths are the French overclaim of the battle and the English overclaim of the driver, which is the spine.)
3. `She Left the Siege, Then Talked Her Way Back In | Codename History` (the corrective beat itself as the hook. It is what actually happened, it is stranger than the refusal version, and it makes the correction the reason to click rather than a thing the video has to walk back.)

## PACKAGE NOTES

- The hook is two lines and both sit above the "...more" fold. Line one is the cold open verbatim in substance, the car at the gap, which is the curiosity gap. Line two is the payoff and carries **both** corrections at once: she was not in the Legion that night, and she never refused an order to leave. Putting the corrections in the fold is deliberate, because the description is what gets scraped and quoted, and this episode exists to refuse two myths rather than to retell them.
- **Susan Travers is never given Legion membership or a rank at Bir Hakeim anywhere in this file.** The word "then" in the recommended title, the "three years later" in the hook, and the "June 1945" in the body all do the same job, and at least one of them has to survive whatever the build's title pass does.
- **She did not refuse the evacuation order, and the description says so in the fold and again in the body.** The body gives the full sequence, Koenig ordered the women out, she went, she attached herself to a resupply convoy, she asked permission to return and got it. The word "refused" appears only in the negative.
- **The strategic claim is limited to the French defence ministry's own three items and nothing else:** slowed the German advance, delayed Tobruk, let the British pull back. The description states outright that the ministry does not claim the battle made El Alamein possible, attributes "ridiculous" to a historian of the Afrika Korps as the script does, and backs it with the dates, 11 June and 21 June, which need nobody's opinion. **"Saved the Eighth Army" and "made El Alamein possible" appear nowhere in this file in the channel's own voice.**
- **The 3,300 figure is not in this file at all.** It is the Axis loss figure, it is the number the original pitch mistook for a French count, and a description is exactly the place where a bare number floats free of the sentence that qualifies it. It stays on screen inside the narration's "Axis losses at Bir Hakeim" sentence and does not travel.
- **No French casualty number appears here either**, for the same reason. The script states more than a thousand, notes the rival figure of around nine hundred, and says nobody has reconciled them. That is a three-sentence problem and it does not compress, so the description does not attempt it.
- **The eleven bullet holes carry the script's exact hedge**, "One account says eleven holes." It traces to one origin and there is a competing figure of roughly twenty in circulation. The unhedged version must not appear in the description, the title, the thumbnail or the pinned comment.
- **No Hitler order about shooting captured Free French.** That sweeping version is NOT FOUND and is absent from the script. The documented narrow version, the order on German political refugees that Rommel ignored, is in the script but is deliberately left out of the description, because at description length it cannot be kept narrow.
- **Churchill does not appear, and neither does the Fighting French renaming.** That beat was cut at QA for wrong attribution. The change from la France libre to la France combattante was decided by the Comite national francais on 13 July 1942.
- Koenig is named because he is load-bearing for the evacuation-and-return sequence. The mistress beat, which the script attributes to the French defence ministry's own magazine, is not in the description, because it only works with its attribution attached and it is not what the episode is about.
- Nobody else is named. Amilakvari, Dewey, the burned pennant captain and the one-handed gunner all stay on screen where their hedges live. The 620 Indian soldiers, the prisoner deaths and the torpedoed prison ship are likewise not in the description, because each is single-sourced and each carries a hedge that does not survive compression.
- The garrison composition is in, because it is the episode's second-strongest correction after Travers and it is Tier 1: about 3,700 men, two thirds from the colonies and overseas, plus the British battery inside the position. No individual contingent is enumerated here.
- Chapter titles are lifted from the script's act names and its current post-QA timecodes, nine chapters, cold open through outro. The cold open and outro chapters are worded from their own narration. **These are the re-timed codes from the QA pass in which a beat was cut, not the first draft's set.**
