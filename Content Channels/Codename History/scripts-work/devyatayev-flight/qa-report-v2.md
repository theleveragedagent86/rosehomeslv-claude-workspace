## Issues to fix

### F-1. The headline hook is single-sourced and unfootnoted. BLOCKING.

**Claim as spoken (sc. 52, 55):** "He willingly demonstrated every procedure and hand movement needed to start the aircraft." ... **"That's in the record."**

I pulled the raw wikitext of the German Wikipedia article. The passage reads:

> "Wichtig während dieser Vorbereitungen war, dass Dewjatajew einem deutschen Piloten dabei zusehen konnte, wie dieser eine zweimotorige Heinkel He 111 zum Start vorbereitete. Der Pilot zeigte dabei Dewjatajew bereitwillig alle dafür notwendigen Abläufe und Handgriffe."

**Neither sentence carries a footnote.** The sentence immediately *before* it is footnoted `<ref name="Schilling155" />` and the paragraph above is footnoted to Schilling 154, but the two sentences that carry this claim are unreferenced editorial prose. The research report's citation, "[de.wikipedia citing Schilling 1978]", is not what the source says.

Corroboration search, all negative:
- **Russian Wikipedia**, by far the most detailed account of the escape: **absent.** It gives the scrap-heap panels and the translator, and nothing about a German pilot demonstrating anything.
- **English Wikipedia**: absent.
- **Historisch-Technisches Museum Peenemünde**, the museum on the actual site, on its own Dewjatajew page: absent. It covers the escape, the guard, the He 111, Hobohm and the hero myth, and says nothing about a start-up lesson.
- Every other web hit (dewiki.de, de-academic.com, timenote.info, googl-info, flugrevue) is a German Wikipedia mirror or downstream retelling. **These are not independent sources.** This is precisely the v1 trap.

So: one Tier 2 source, unfootnoted within that source, contradicted by silence in the three other best sources including the site's own museum. Under the standing rule ("a significant claim is single-sourced or unsourced" and "a fabricated specific anywhere"), this fails. It is made worse by three escalations the script adds on top:
- **"That's in the record."** (sc. 55) asserts documentary status for a claim with no citation. This line must go regardless of what happens to the rest.
- **"Because he was being polite."** (sc. 53) invents a motive no source gives.
- The **title, thumbnail and description** all rest entirely on it, and the description escalates further to "because he asked politely", which is not even in the script.

**You asked me to tell you plainly if this one is weak. It is weak, and the whole package needs rethinking.** My honest confidence that a German pilot deliberately demonstrated the start-up sequence to Devyatayev: **low, roughly 30 percent.** It is not disproved and it is not absurd, he was demonstrably on the airfield detail and demonstrably watched German start-up preparations, which *is* corroborated (de.wikipedia: "So beobachteten sie die Startvorbereitungen der deutschen Piloten"; ru.wikipedia agrees on observation). The *observation* is solid. The **willing demonstration to him personally** is the part that rests on one unfootnoted sentence, and it is the part the title sells.

**Fix, choose one:**
- **(a) Retitle and rebuild the hook on what is verified.** The observation-plus-scrapheap-plus-translator chain is multi-sourced and is still an excellent hook: he learned a bomber cockpit from a rubbish dump and by watching German crews start engines. Title option 2 or 3 from the production notes both survive intact and need no new verification.
- **(b) Keep the beat, demote it to attributed.** Rewrite sc. 52-55 so the narration says out loud that one German account, and only one, says the pilot showed him the sequence, and that the museum on the site does not carry it. Cut "That's in the record" and "Because he was being polite." This is honest, but a hedged claim cannot carry a title card, so the title still has to change.
- **(c) Ship as written.** Not available. This is the exact error class that killed v1.

### F-2. Bunkerbau death figure is misread, and contradicts the script's own Tier 1 number. BLOCKING.

**Claim as spoken (sc. 34):** "Four hundred men built one concrete shelter. Two hundred ninety five died on it."

Raw wikitext, de.wikipedia/Peenemünde-West, citing Kanetzki:

> "Das Kommando „Bunkerbau" bestand aus 400 Häftlingen. Der Einsatz dieses Kommandos wird von Zeitzeugen als der brutalste beschrieben. **Insgesamt 295 Tote sind dokumentiert, einschließlich deutscher Bauarbeiter und Häftlinge.**"

Two problems:
1. The 295 is a **total documented death figure that explicitly includes German civilian construction workers** ("einschließlich deutscher Bauarbeiter und Häftlinge"). The script silently drops that qualifier and converts a mixed figure into 295 dead prisoners.
2. Read the script's way it means 295 of 400 men died on one work detail, a 74 percent death rate, while **three scenes earlier the script states, correctly and from Tier 1, that 248 deaths total are documented at Karlshagen I** (sc. 31). 295 cannot be nested inside 248. The script contradicts itself inside forty seconds, on a TONE: STRAIGHT human-cost beat, where accuracy matters most.

**Fix:** cut the number, or rewrite to the source. Something like "Four hundred men built one concrete shelter. Witnesses called it the worst detail on the island." keeps the beat, keeps the weight, and asserts nothing unsupported.

### F-3. "Nine surfaces" is not in the Tier 1 source. BLOCKING.

**Claim as spoken (sc. 20):** "Devyatayev was put on the shoe running detail. A track of nine surfaces."

The Sachsenhausen Memorial's own history page says only "**a shoe testing path with various different surfaces**". It gives no count. The "nine surfaces" and "700 m" specifics circulate in visitor guides and blogs, all plausibly downstream of one guidebook, and the research report itself flagged that they "come from secondary guides". A specific integer on a Holocaust human-cost beat needs a Tier 1 anchor and does not have one.

**Fix:** "A track of nine different surfaces" becomes "A track laid in different surfaces." Costs one word, loses nothing, and the memorial's own wording backs it.

### F-4. Devyatayev's own disposition is an unresolved source conflict, stated flat. BLOCKING.

**Claim as spoken (sc. 99):** "Devyatayev was held and interrogated until September, partly inside Special Camp Number Seven."

- **ru.wikipedia**: NKVD "Спецлагерь № 7", the former Sachsenhausen.
- **de.wikipedia**, raw: "Dewjatajew wurde verdächtigt, deutscher Spion zu sein, **und in eine Strafeinheit der Armee versetzt.** So blieb Dewjatajew bis September 1945 in Haft und wurde immer wieder verhört."

German Wikipedia says he was put in an **army penal unit**. The research report lists this as an **UNRESOLVED CONFLICT** and the script's own fact-check log admits it ("Note German Wikipedia instead says a penal unit"). The narration then states the Russian version flat, and Act 6 builds three scenes, its emotional centrepiece and its act title, "Same camp, different jailers", on top of it.

Note the irony: v2's proudest correction is cutting v1's "penal battalions". That correction is **right for the seven enlisted men** (the 215th / 397th trail is documented and I confirmed it). It is **not settled for Devyatayev himself**, and the script has quietly taken the opposite side of a live dispute in order to land a better act.

Both sources agree he was in custody, repeatedly interrogated, until September 1945. That much ships.

**Fix:** either hedge in the narration ("Russian records put him inside Special Camp Number Seven"), or drop the camp identification and keep the custody-until-September fact, which is unanimous. Do not state Special Camp No. 7 flat while a comparable source says something else.

### F-5. "He was never on the Army site" overstates the source and contradicts the scene before it. BLOCKING.

**Claim as spoken (sc. 107):** "He was never on the Army site. He knew the ground. That's all."

The source (de.wikipedia, footnoted to the Förderverein Peenemünde Infoblatt) says the escapers were used **only for auxiliary work at the airfield** and **could not have learned anything about V-2 development** at the neighbouring Army establishment. That is a claim about what they could have known. "Never on the Army site" is an absolute negative about where a man physically was, and no source supports it.

It also contradicts the script two scenes earlier: sc. 105-107 has Korolev walking him around the ruined site while he points out **launch positions among concrete ruins**, which are the Army/V-2 installations. The script asserts he was never there and then draws him there.

**Fix:** "He was never inside the rocket programme. He worked the Luftwaffe airfield. He knew the ground. That's all." Keeps the correction, drops the unsupported absolute, removes the self-contradiction.

### Non-blocking issues, fix before build

**I-1. Citation integrity in SOURCES and FACT-CHECK LOG.** The log attributes the flagship intelligence-value correction to "[Raketenspuren, Ch. Links Verlag 2008, pp. 128 ff.]". In de.wikipedia's raw wikitext that sentence is footnoted to **"Vor 70 Jahren: Flucht aus Peenemünde, Infoblatt des Fördervereins Peenemünde, Nr. 1/2015"**, a local support-association newsletter. *Raketenspuren* appears only in the article's **Literatur** further-reading list, never as an inline citation for this claim. The author string is also wrong: de.wikipedia gives **"Volkhard Bode, Christian Thiel"** for the 2008 6th edition, and "Volkhard Bode, Gerhard Kaiser" for a 2011 printing; the script's SOURCES block invents a three-author 2008 edition with page numbers, presented as if consulted. The correction itself survives on structural grounds (the West/East split is independently verified) so the narration does not need to change, but **the SOURCES block and log must cite what was actually read.** This is the same disease as v1's aircrewremembered citation.

**I-2. The 12:36 clock conflicts with Devyatayev's own book.** ru.wikipedia carries a footnote the research report missed: **"В книге Девятаева указывается 11:45, в более поздних воспоминаниях появляется 12:36"**, his *book* says 11:45; 12:36 appears only in later memoirs. The narration is correctly attributed ("By his own account"), so it does not assert. But visual 82 burns **12:36** onto a cockpit clock as though it were a fact, and the fact-check log does not mention the 11:45 variant at all. Either drop the clock from the visual or have the narration note his accounts differ.

**I-3. "He told that story for fifty years" (sc. 25).** de.wikipedia sources the "Angeblich ... Nikitenko" claim to **Schilling 1978**, not to Devyatayev. I found nothing establishing that Devyatayev personally told the identity-swap story, let alone for fifty years. It is an unverified attribution of a specific act to a named real person. Reword to something like "The legend ran for fifty years" unless someone can source him telling it. Everything else in the sc. 23-25 myth beat is correctly calibrated and should not be touched.

**I-4. "The dead were driven to Greifswald" (sc. 35) erases the on-site burials.** The source says at least 171 were cremated at Greifswald between Nov 1943 and Sept 1944 and "**andere Leichen wurden vor Ort verscharrt**", other bodies were buried on site. The museum records a mass grave of 56 found in the mid-1960s. The no-crematorium half of the line is right; the second half overstates. On a TONE: STRAIGHT beat, prefer "Some were driven to Greifswald. Others were buried on the island."

**I-5. Visual 15 asserts an aircraft type the narration deliberately avoided.** Narration at sc. 15 correctly does not name the type. Visual 15 draws "A P-39 Airacobra with red stars burning". de.wikipedia (Schilling) says he bailed out of a **La-5**. The image makes an unhedged claim the narration was written to dodge. Use a neutral silhouette or a text-only map card.

**I-6. "Gefreiter Alfred Johnen" is unfootnoted.** In the raw wikitext the guard's name sits in the same unreferenced paragraph as F-1, and I found no corroboration outside German Wikipedia and its mirrors. Lower stakes than the title claim, and the museum independently confirms a guard was killed, but the name is spoken (sc. 61) and burned onto a memorial-style card (visual 67). Given that visual 67 is a straight in-memoriam card for a named real person, it should rest on more than one unfootnoted sentence. Consider "the guard" in narration and no name card, or accept the risk knowingly.

Correct the notes so the next editor is not recomputing against a wrong baseline.
