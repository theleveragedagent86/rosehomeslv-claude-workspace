# Research Report: The Escape of Mikhail Devyatayev (Heinkel He 111, 8 February 1945)

## One-paragraph summary

On 8 February 1945, ten Soviet prisoners of war led by fighter pilot Senior Lieutenant Mikhail Devyatayev escaped from a Nazi labor camp on the Baltic island of Usedom, home of the Peenemünde V-weapon research and test complex, by seizing a German Heinkel He 111 bomber on the airfield. The prisoners killed their guard, Devyatayev got the unfamiliar bomber airborne on a second attempt, and they flew west-to-east across the front under fire from both German fighters and Soviet anti-aircraft guns, landing in Soviet-held territory. Devyatayev, a decorated ace shot down in July 1944, had survived by swapping his identity with that of a dead prisoner after being condemned to death for an earlier escape attempt. Rather than a hero's welcome, the returning POWs were interrogated as suspected enemy agents by SMERSH/NKVD counterintelligence. Devyatayev was cleared and named a Hero of the Soviet Union only in 1957, reportedly after rocket designer Sergei Korolev vouched for him. Some Soviet-era details (precise intelligence value, the Korolev connection) rest largely on Russian-language accounts and should be attributed, not stated as settled fact.

## Verified timeline

- 13 July 1944 - Senior Lieutenant Devyatayev, flying a Bell P-39 Airacobra, is shot down near Lvov (Lwow) over German-held territory and captured. [aircrewremembered; Wikipedia; Russia Beyond]
- Summer/autumn 1944 - Held first at a camp near Lodz, Poland; attempts escape, is caught, and is condemned to death; transferred to Sachsenhausen. [Wikipedia; ASAP History; bazovo.ru summary]
- At Sachsenhausen - With help from a camp barber, swaps his identity/number tag with that of a dead prisoner (a man named Nikitenko), converting his status from condemned prisoner to ordinary penal prisoner. [Wikipedia; Russia Beyond]
- End of October 1944 - Sent with a large group of prisoners to the camp on Usedom serving the Peenemunde test site (Karlshagen sub-camp area). [Russia Beyond; aircrewremembered]
- 8 February 1945, around midday - While a work gang is on the airfield, prisoner Ivan Krivonogov kills the guard with a crowbar and takes his rifle; the ten prisoners seize a Heinkel He 111. [Wikipedia; aircrewremembered; Russia Beyond; HistoryNet]
- 8 February 1945 - First takeoff attempt fails; on the second attempt Devyatayev gets the bomber airborne. It is fired on by German fighters and then by Soviet anti-aircraft guns. The group lands in Soviet-held territory that same day. [Russia Beyond; aircrewremembered; ASAP History]
- Late March 1945 - After hospitalization, the seven enlisted escapees are sent to penal (shtrafbat) units. [Wikipedia]
- 15 August 1957 - Devyatayev is made a Hero of the Soviet Union. [Wikipedia; aircrewremembered]
- 24 November 2002 - Devyatayev dies in Kazan, aged 85. [Wikipedia; aircrewremembered]

## Key figures

- **Mikhail Petrovich Devyatayev**, Senior Lieutenant, 104th Guards Fighter Aviation Regiment (flight commander), fighter ace credited with 9 kills - shot down and captured July 1944, led and piloted the escape. [aircrewremembered; Wikipedia]
- **Ivan Krivonogov**, prisoner - killed the guard with a crowbar and took his rifle. [Wikipedia; aircrewremembered; HistoryNet]
- **Pyotr (Peter) Kutergin**, prisoner - stripped the dead guard's uniform and put it on as a disguise. [HistoryNet]
- **Sokolov, Nemchenko** - among the prisoners who helped organize/convince the group. [aircrewremembered]
- **Sergei Korolev**, head of the Soviet space/rocket program - reportedly interviewed Devyatayev about Peenemunde and later vouched for him, helping clear his name in 1957. [Wikipedia; Russia Beyond] (Soviet-era account; attribute.)
- **Colonel Mandralsky**, SMERSH counterintelligence, 61st Army - reportedly filed a "Top Secret" note ordering the returned escapees interrogated as suspected enemy agents. [Russian-language accounts via search] (Attribute; not independently confirmed in Tier 1 Western source.)

## Numbers (high-risk, source each)

- **Escapers: 10 total** (Devyatayev plus 9). [Wikipedia; aircrewremembered; HistoryNet; Russia Beyond; ASAP History - all agree]
- **Aircraft: Heinkel He 111 H22.** [Wikipedia; aircrewremembered agree on H22 variant; other sources say He 111 generally]
- **Shot down 13 July 1944**, flying a Bell P-39 Airacobra, near Lvov. [aircrewremembered; Wikipedia; Russia Beyond]
- **9 confirmed kills** as an ace. [aircrewremembered]
- **Age 27 at capture** (born 1917). [aircrewremembered]
- **Hero of the Soviet Union: 15 August 1957.** [Wikipedia; aircrewremembered]
- **Died 2002, aged 85** (24 November 2002, Kazan). [Wikipedia; aircrewremembered]
- **Casualties of the ten afterward:** Wikipedia states "five died in action over the following months" among the seven sent to penal battalions; it also cites a source saying only three survived the war. Sources agree most of the enlisted men were killed at the front after return; exact survivor count varies. Present as a range and note the disagreement. [Wikipedia; HistoryNet]

## Absurd / comedic beats (all real)

- A starving POW who had never flown a twin-engine German bomber took off in one on his second try and flew it across the front. [aircrewremembered; ASAP History]
- The escape happened at midday, in the open, on the airfield of one of the most secret sites in the Reich. Devyatayev recalled that "no-one at the airfield paid any attention to the roar of our plane." [Russia Beyond]
- One pursuing German fighter reportedly could not shoot them down because it had no ammunition left. [Russia Beyond]
- To fool the airfield, one prisoner (Kutergin) put on the dead guard's uniform as a disguise. [HistoryNet]
- The men escaped a place engineers considered escape-proof, where the only "way out" for prisoners was said to be the crematorium chimney - and they left by air. [Russia Beyond]
- After pulling off one of the war's most daring escapes and bringing home enemy hardware, the reward was being interrogated as a possible spy. (Irony, played with a light touch; the interrogation itself is a straight beat.)

## Human-cost beats (play straight)

- Devyatayev was condemned to death for an earlier escape attempt and survived only by taking a dead man's identity; the tag-swap meant the "Devyatayev" on the death list was cremated while he lived on under another name. [Wikipedia; Russia Beyond] TONE: STRAIGHT.
- Conditions at the Usedom/Peenemunde camp were lethal; starvation, disease, and the crematorium were the norm. [Russia Beyond] TONE: STRAIGHT.
- The guard was killed during the escape. [multiple] State plainly, no joke. TONE: STRAIGHT.
- After returning, most of the escapees were sent to penal battalions and the enlisted men were largely killed at the front within months; sources agree only a few survived the war. [Wikipedia; HistoryNet] TONE: STRAIGHT.
- The SMERSH/NKVD suspicion of returned POWs reflected a real, systemic Soviet policy that treated former prisoners as suspected traitors. TONE: STRAIGHT.

## Myth vs record (corrections)

- **Popular telling:** Devyatayev single-handedly stole rocket secrets that launched the Soviet space program. / **Actual:** He and the group did provide information about the German missile program (V-1 and V-2), and Russian accounts say he later helped Korolev locate test sites and identify equipment; but the specific, decisive intelligence value and the Korolev role rest largely on Soviet-era and Russian-language sources. Attribute these claims; do not state them as independently settled fact. [Wikipedia notes limited Western corroboration; Russia Beyond]
- **Popular telling:** The plane carried V-2 rocket secrets. / **Actual:** Russia Beyond says the captured Heinkel "contained secret communications equipment and instruments" and that Devyatayev could later point out V-2 launcher coordinates. The stronger, better-sourced claim is that the escapees provided information about the missile program, not that the airframe itself was full of rocket blueprints. Frame carefully. [Russia Beyond; Wikipedia]
- **Popular telling:** He got a hero's welcome. / **Actual:** He was interrogated as a suspected enemy agent and not recognized as a Hero of the Soviet Union until 1957. [Wikipedia; aircrewremembered - well corroborated]
