# EPISODE SCRIPT: A GERMAN PILOT TAUGHT HIM TO STEAL THE PLANE

**Target runtime:** 8:45 to 9:15 (~1,350 narration words at ~150 wpm)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake dialogue. Every scene is ONE still image, held under 5 seconds, hard cut. No zoom, no pan, no Ken Burns.
**Story type:** Single true escape (real, documented). Ten prisoners, one guard, one stolen bomber.
**Title (recommended):** `A German Pilot Taught Him to Steal the Plane | Codename History`
**Scene / image count:** 108
**Unique generations after reuse:** 98 (10 marked REUSE)

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: This version replaces script-v1 entirely. Do not merge them. **The identity-swap act from v1 has been cut.** The Karlshagen I prisoner register lists him as no. 11024 under his own name, and both German and English Wikipedia hedge the Nikitenko story with "allegedly", so it now appears once, in Act 2, as a legend the paperwork contradicts. Also cut from v1: the aircraft variant "H22" (say only "a Heinkel He 111"), the "only way out is the chimney" line (there was no crematorium on the site), "penal battalions" as a blanket fact, "five died in action" (it was six), and the name "Colonel Mandralsky" (unsourced).
>
> **Attributed, not asserted. Do not upgrade these into hard facts in the edit, the thumbnail, or the description:** (1) the pursuing German aircraft and whether it was out of ammunition, marked in narration as a story the pilots told afterwards; (2) Kutergin wearing the dead guard's uniform, marked in narration as contested; (3) the "21 minutes" and the 12:36 clock reading, which are Devyatayev's own testimony; (4) the intelligence value of the escape, which the site's own historians dispute. Göring's execution order and Hitler naming him a personal enemy are NOT in this script at all. Leave them out.
>
> VOICE NOTE: Sarcasm is the default register. The narrator treats a suicidal feat as a mild scheduling inconvenience and a bureaucratic detail as a catastrophe. Roast the Reich's documented incompetence hard: the most secret airfield in Germany, guarded at lunchtime by one second-line home-guard private, where a German pilot cheerfully gave a Soviet prisoner a free start-up lesson. **The comedy stops dead at every beat marked TONE: STRAIGHT.** No jokes, no wink, no music sting there. Narration goes quiet and factual. That boundary is what makes everything else land.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Voice choices:**
- Sarcasm is the default register throughout Acts 1, 3, 4 and 5. Understate the insane, overstate the trivial. "The password to Germany's most secret airfield was mild plausibility." "Defeated by a flat battery, which is also how most mornings begin."
- Second-person "you" used once, hard, at the Soviet anti-aircraft beat, so the friendly-fire irony lands as the viewer's own decision.
- Fake dialogue blocks: the SS officer handing over the island, the Luftwaffe on its fence, the German pilot offering the tour, and Devyatayev's flat thank you. Keep all four short, stupid, and dry. Do not give Gefreiter Johnen a comic voice at any point.
- **Callbacks planted and paid:** "lunchtime" from the cold open pays off at scene 68. The scrap heap "free tuition" line at 55 sets up the "free start-up sequence" card at 62. The Peenemünde East versus West diagram at scene 30 is the whole payoff of the intelligence correction at scene 124. "One guard" from scene 2 is reused at scene 45.
- **The comedy stops dead** at the Sachsenhausen block (19 to 24), the Karlshagen block (34 to 41), the killing of the guard (75 to 77), and everything from scene 107 to 126. Those are marked TONE: STRAIGHT in the narration for the build to tag. No music sting, no reaction shot, no gag card inside those ranges.

**Accuracy corrections from v1 (see fact-check log):**
- Identity swap cut as a factual spine. Now one three-scene myth-versus-record beat (25 to 27) resolved against the prisoner register.
- "He 111 H22" cut. The script says "a Heinkel He 111" and nothing more.
- "Only way out is the chimney" cut entirely. No crematorium is drawn anywhere on the island. The Greifswald beat replaces it.
- "Penal battalions" cut. Replaced with the actual unit trail, 215th Reserve Rifle Regiment then the 397th Rifle Division.
- Survivor count corrected. Six dead, four survived, named dates.
- Pursuing fighter's ammunition demoted to a signposted "the story goes" beat at 97 and 98, with the contradiction stated out loud instead of a punchline.
- Kutergin's uniform explicitly presented as contested at 76 and 77.
- "Colonel Mandralsky" cut. Göring's execution order and the Hitler personal-enemy line cut.
- Devyatayev's U-2 ambulance years restored, which makes him far more interesting than "ace".

**Title options:**
1. `A German Pilot Taught Him to Steal the Plane | Codename History` **(recommended.** It is the strongest verified hook in the story, it is unbelievable, and it is true, which is exactly the channel's promise.)
2. `The Nazis Left One Guard on Their Most Secret Airfield | Codename History`
3. `Ten Prisoners Stole a Nazi Bomber at Lunchtime | Codename History`

**Channel placement:** Classic single-operation Codename History episode. Pairs with other escape and evasion episodes. It is also the channel's best myth-correction episode so far, because the museum on the actual site has published a page specifically about the hero cult, which means the correction is not the channel being contrarian, it is the channel agreeing with the site's own historians. Lean on that in the pinned comment.

## FACT-CHECK LOG

- **Born 1917, thirteenth child of a peasant family, Torbeyevo, Mordovia.** Verified. [de.wikipedia citing Schilling 1978]
- **River navigator qualification, then Orenburg flying school.** Verified. [de.wikipedia citing Schilling]
- **First kill a Ju 87 on 24 June 1941, two days into the war.** Verified. [de.wikipedia citing Schilling]
- **Shot down over Tula 1942, thigh wound, transferred to U-2 biplanes on supply, bombing and casualty evacuation.** Verified. **Corrects v1**, which presented him only as a pure ace. [de.wikipedia citing Schilling]
- **Returned to fighters under Pokryshkin, May 1944. Nine kills, roughly 150 sorties.** Verified, consistent across German, English and Russian sources. [de/en/ru wikipedia]
- **Shot down 13 July 1944 near Lvov on his 185th sortie.** Verified. Aircraft type P-39 Airacobra is the majority view and fits the unit, but German Wikipedia citing Schilling says La-5. Script deliberately does not name the aircraft type in narration for this reason; the visual shows a P-39.
- **Failed tunnel escape, August 1944, camp near Lodz. Sent to Sachsenhausen end September 1944.** Verified. [de.wikipedia; ru.wikipedia]
- **Sachsenhausen: over 200,000 interned 1936 to 1945; at least 10,000 Soviet POWs murdered autumn 1941.** Tier 1. [Sachsenhausen Memorial and Museum]
- **Shoe-testing track, nine surfaces, established 1940, prisoners marching for days with full packs to test sole materials for industry.** Tier 1 for existence and function. [Sachsenhausen Memorial and Museum]
- **Identity swap. CORRECTED FROM v1.** The Karlshagen I prisoner list has him as no. 11024 "Dewjatajew, Michail" under his own name. German Wikipedia states this contradicts the legend handed down in the USSR and the GDR; both German and English Wikipedia hedge the Nikitenko story with "allegedly". Script presents it as legend versus register, which is the honest frame. [HTM Peenemünde Archiv EC/45/17, cited in de.wikipedia]
- **Shipped to Usedom autumn 1944.** Verified. Russian sources say October, German sources say November. Script says "that autumn" to avoid the conflict.
- **Peenemünde-East (Army, V-2) and Peenemünde-West (Luftwaffe test station) were separate establishments, split administratively 1 April 1938.** Verified and load-bearing for the intelligence correction. [de.wikipedia/Peenemünde-West]
- **April 1943, Major Otto Stahms proposed removing roughly 3,000 foreign workers as a secrecy risk and replacing them with concentration camp labour; Milch agreed.** Verified. Played as bitter irony, not as a joke. [de.wikipedia citing Kanetzki]
- **Karlshagen I: ~1,500 prisoners total, 248 deaths documented by name.** Tier 1. [HTM Peenemünde]
- **Prisoners hand-carried 150 kg Fi 103 warheads from the reed belt to the launch pad and dug out unexploded bombs after raids.** Tier 1. [HTM Peenemünde; memorialmuseums.org]
- **Bunkerbau commando: 400 prisoners, 295 documented deaths.** Verified. [de.wikipedia citing Kanetzki]
- **No crematorium on site; at least 171 dead taken to the municipal crematorium at Greifswald. CORRECTS v1's chimney imagery.** Verified. [de.wikipedia citing Kanetzki; Arolsen Archives ref. 1-1-35-1_2267002]
- **Operation Hydra, August 1943: markers fell on the Trassenheide forced labour camp, several hundred labourers killed, only two significant German technical personnel died.** Verified. [en.wikipedia Operation Hydra; IWM; Defense Media Network]
- **Autumn 1943, camp guard duty passed from the SS to Landesschützenzug 308/XI, a second-line Luftwaffe home guard unit.** Verified. [de.wikipedia/Peenemünde-West citing Kanetzki]
- **A German fellow prisoner got Devyatayev onto the airfield work detail.** Verified. [de.wikipedia]
- **He learned the cockpit from wrecked instrument panels on the scrap heap beside the airfield; another prisoner translated the German labels.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]
- **A German pilot willingly demonstrated the entire He 111 start-up procedure to him, every necessary step and hand movement.** Verified. Source is Schilling 1978, an East German military publisher, so treat the surrounding colour as period-flavoured, but the detail itself is what German Wikipedia carries. **This is the episode's centrepiece and v1 missed it entirely.** [de.wikipedia citing Schilling]
- **Aircraft selected about a month in advance, early January 1945.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia]
- **A sympathetic German anti-aircraft gunner was invited on the escape and declined, fearing for his family.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia]
- **"Tsygan", the assistant foreman, was framed with a staged theft to free a place; Nemchenko took it.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia]
- **8 February 1945, around midday, ten prisoners covering He 111s with camouflage nets, guarded by one man.** Verified. [HTM Peenemünde; de.wikipedia]
- **The campfire, built so the guard and prisoners could warm up and heat the lunch due to be brought out.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia]
- **The revetment cover story, told by Sokolov, and accepted.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia]
- **The guard, Gefreiter Alfred Johnen, killed by Ivan Krivonogov. TONE: STRAIGHT.** Verified. Note the method is not specified in the v2 sources, so the script does not name a weapon. v1's "crowbar" has been removed. [de.wikipedia; ru.wikipedia]
- **Kutergin wearing the dead guard's greatcoat. CONTESTED, presented as contested.** Russian sources carry directly competing accounts, one saying he wore it, one saying it was blood-soaked and unusable. v1 stated it flat. [ru.wikipedia]
- **No battery in the aircraft; a battery cart had to be fetched.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia; HistoryNet]
- **First takeoff failed, control column would not come back, ran off the runway near the shore, aircraft hit the ground, panic, one of the group threatened Devyatayev with the rifle.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia]
- **German ground personnel gathered on the runway; he taxied the bomber at them and they scattered; he returned to the start line.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia]
- **After the second takeoff the aircraft pitched up, lost speed and dived; several men hauled on the yoke; he stabilised it by finding the elevator trim wheel.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia]
- **12:36 clock reading and the "21 minutes" figure.** Devyatayev's own account. Script says "by his own account". Do not assert. [ru.wikipedia citing Devyatayev]
- **The pursuit. TIER 3, DELIBERATELY UNRESOLVED IN NARRATION.** Two named pursuers in two aircraft types, both problematic: Hobohm in a Ju 88, disputed by Russian Wikipedia as unreliable because he flew bombers; Dahl in an Fw 190, whose own memoir says he fired his last rounds and broke off for fuel, and who was posted to Me 262 duties in that period. The script states out loud that the accounts do not agree. **Do not let the edit resolve this into "the fighter had no ammunition".** [ru.wikipedia; en.wikipedia Walther Dahl; Mathews and Foreman]
- **Hit and set on fire by Soviet anti-aircraft artillery; sideslipped to blow the flames out; belly-landed near Woldenberg, now Dobiegniew, about 8 km behind the front in the 61st Army's sector.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]
- **They tried to hide in a wood, were too weak, sat by the aircraft, and the Soviet soldiers who found them took them for Germans.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia]
- **22 February 1945, seven sent to the 215th Army Reserve Rifle Regiment; 19 and 29 March to the 397th Rifle Division and its 447th and 448th Rifle Regiments. NOT penal battalions. CORRECTS v1.** Verified from the Soviet personnel record trail. [ru.wikipedia]
- **16 April 1945: Kutergin, Serdyukov, Sokolov and Urbanovich killed forcing the Oder. Oleynik killed 21 April near Tarnow. Nemchenko, who had lost an eye and served as a company medical orderly, killed 24 April. Six of ten dead within eleven weeks. Four survived.** Verified with named dates. **Corrects v1's "five died in action".** [ru.wikipedia]
- **Devyatayev held and interrogated until September 1945, part of it in NKVD Special Camp No. 7 on the site of the former Sachsenhausen concentration camp. TONE: STRAIGHT.** Verified. **v1 did not have this.** Note German Wikipedia instead says a penal unit; both agree he was in custody until roughly September 1945. [ru.wikipedia]
- **Over 4 million returnees, including about 1.5 million surviving POWs, processed through NKVD filtration. Policy, not one officer's decision.** Verified. [academic literature on filtration; NKVD Orders 001735 and 00100]
- **A Luftflottenkommando 6 interrogation protocol, together with statements from former fellow prisoners, contributed to his exoneration.** Verified. [de.wikipedia citing Bode/Thiel]
- **September 1945, "Colonel Sergeyev" summoned him to Usedom for about three days as guide and consultant. Sergeyev was Sergei Korolev; the pseudonym is independently corroborated.** Verified. [ru.wikipedia; ODKB-CSTO; Korolev's later "Professor K. Sergeyev" byline]
- **Intelligence value. CORRECTED FROM v1.** Bode, Kaiser and Thiel argue the escapers worked only on auxiliary tasks at the Luftwaffe airfield and could not have learned anything about V-2 development at the separate Army establishment. The script says plainly that he did not steal the rocket programme. [Raketenspuren, Ch. Links Verlag 2008, pp. 128 ff.]
- **Hero of the Soviet Union, 15 August 1957, on Korolev's initiative.** Verified. [de/ru/en wikipedia; HTM Peenemünde]
- **The hero cult.** The Peenemünde museum's own page is titled "Der Heldenmythos um Dewjatajew" and records that after 1957 he was staged by the Soviet press as an ideal Soviet citizen and supported the propaganda with the story of his escape. The museum does not dispute the escape. It disputes the cult. Script reflects this in one line at scene 127. [HTM Peenemünde]
- **1961, first captain of a Meteor hydrofoil on the Volga; never flew an aircraft again. Met Günter Hobohm at Peenemünde in June 1999. Died 24 November 2002, aged 85.** Verified. [de.wikipedia; HTM Peenemünde]
- **NOT USED, deliberately:** Göring's 13 February visit and execution order; Hitler naming him a personal enemy; "Colonel Mandralsky"; the He 111 "H22" variant; the "Gustav Anton" code letters; the chimney line. All are unverified, unsourced outside the Soviet-derived chain, or overturned.
- **Em-dashes:** zero. Checked.
