## Description

At 10:59 a.m. on November 11, 1918, an American private charged a German machine gun. The Germans stood up and waved him back, because every man on that field knew the war ended in sixty seconds.

The legend says the guns simply fell silent at the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month. The record says the armistice was signed a little after 5 a.m., the exact minute of peace was wired to every headquarters, and commanders ordered attacks anyway, including one for a town's bathing facilities. The final morning cost roughly 11,000 casualties on all sides, more casualties than D-Day, and this is the true, documented story of the last six hours of World War 1 and the last four men killed.

## Thumbnail concepts

### Concept A (locked package, refined)

**Text:** "1 MINUTE LEFT" (3 words)
**Composition:** 16:9. A colossal semi-translucent clock face dominates the upper two-thirds of the frame, tilted slightly toward the viewer, hands frozen at 10:59 with the minute hand one visible tick from twelve. The clock glows bone-white through a wall of grey-blue fog. Lower third: churned no-man's-land in silhouette. One lone WW1 American infantryman, full silhouette, charges left-to-right in mid-stride, rifle leveled with fixed bayonet, coat flaring. At the far right edge, a single machine-gun muzzle flash, the only warm light in the image, rim-lights the soldier's front edge in orange.
**Palette:** Desaturated slate blue and fog grey field; clock face in pale bone-gold; one saturated orange flash accent; soldier in pure black silhouette. High contrast between clock glow and fog.
**Lighting:** Diffuse cold light from the clock above, hard warm rim light from the muzzle flash at right. No other light sources.
**Text placement:** "1 MINUTE LEFT" stacked in two lines ("1 MINUTE" / "LEFT"), upper left quadrant, tall condensed extra-bold white letters with a heavy black outline and slight drop shadow, angled 2-3 degrees. The "1" oversized. Nothing overlaps the clock hands or the soldier.
**Curiosity tie:** the clock supplies the deadline, the charge supplies the man, the flash supplies the death the title promises.

### Concept B

**Text:** "GO BACK!" (2 words)
**Composition:** 16:9, shot from the charging soldier's point of view. One German machine gunner is the single focal subject, standing upright behind his MG 08 on its sled mount, centered slightly right, both arms raised high and mid-wave, palms out, mouth open in a shout. Fog erases everything behind him to a flat grey field. In the extreme lower foreground, out of focus, the blurred dark edge of a shoulder and bayonet tip entering the frame, small enough to read as "you," not as a second subject.
**Palette:** Near-monochrome fog grey and cold green-grey field uniform; the gunner's face and palms the brightest values in frame; black gun silhouette below him.
**Lighting:** Flat overcast diffusion with a soft top light picking out the raised arms and face, so the wave reads instantly at feed size.
**Text placement:** "GO BACK!" in huge distressed white block letters with black outline across the top fifth of the frame, centered. Nothing else in frame.
**Curiosity tie:** the enemy is begging you to stop, which is exactly the wrongness the title teases: why is the machine gunner the one saying go back?

### Concept C

**Text:** "60 SECONDS LEFT" (3 words)
**Composition:** 16:9 extreme macro. One battered brass pocket watch, the single focal subject, half-sunk in dark churned mud, filling the right two-thirds of the frame at a slight tilt. Its cracked glass reads 10:59, with a blood-red second hand one tick from the top. A single strand of barbed wire crosses the lower left corner, out of focus. Faint fog haze over the whole scene.
**Palette:** Dark umber mud, tarnished brass and cream watch face, one saturated red accent on the second hand and a thin red crack in the glass. Everything else desaturated.
**Lighting:** One hard directional key from upper left, as if a flare, throwing a long shadow off the watch and a bright specular hit on the cracked glass. Deep vignette on all four corners.
**Text placement:** "60 SECONDS" / "LEFT" stacked in the open upper-left third, massive white condensed extra-bold with black outline, the "60" in the same red as the second hand. Text never covers the watch face.
**Curiosity tie:** pure countdown, the number does the work; it pairs with the title by asking sixty seconds until what, and for whom.
