## YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION (paste-ready)

Night, the third of November 1943. Six factory workers slip into a guarded German war plant in eastern France, press plastic explosive onto the transformers and the forge compressors, and walk back out. Nobody stops them. In the morning the factory is running exactly as it was, because every single detonator had been fitted the wrong way round.

So they went home and came back two nights later, on Guy Fawkes Night. And the family that owned the factory wanted the whole thing wrecked. They were helping pay for it, and by London's own account they had been considering it already.

This is the Peugeot works at Sochaux, July 1943 to spring 1944, and Harry Ree, the London schoolmaster and former pacifist who ran the SOE circuit called Stockbroker. The story that usually gets told has a British agent talking a reluctant company into sabotage after an RAF raid, and has the RAF coming back later to finish the job. The record is stranger and more uncomfortable than that, and we go through it in order.

Ree's first contact with Peugeot management was in early July 1943, before the raid, and it was about a loan to his circuit repayable by the British Treasury after the war. The raid supplied the argument, not the introduction. On the night of the fifteenth of July, Bomber Command sent 165 Halifaxes, the Pathfinder markers went down 700 yards beyond the factory over streets of houses, about 600 bombs landed on the town and about 30 landed in the plant. A hundred and twenty three people were killed, which is the local French count, and hundreds more were injured. Fifty one people died the same night at Besancon, and those deaths are separate and are often wrongly combined into the Sochaux figure. The RAF's own record assessed the factory at five per cent damaged and noted that production was normal afterwards. The official British history says some hundreds of townspeople were killed, and it is wrong.

Then the part the legend gets backwards. SOE's own 1946 internal report says Peugeot were already toying with the idea of sabotaging their own factory to stop production and prevent another disastrous British bombardment. The family member behind the deal was Rodolphe Peugeot, not Robert, the elderly father who had given up the presidency in 1941. Rodolphe tested the circuit by composing a personal message and asking for it on the BBC, then handed over the plans and the inside men. The postwar verified list records a three month stop after the fifth of November, then three weeks, five weeks, several months, and output cut to forty per cent as replacement machine tools kept being destroyed in the yard before they could be unloaded. In March 1944 Bomber Command scheduled a second raid on Sochaux, the circuit sent London proof of the damage, and London cancelled it. There was no second raid.

And it cost. The Gestapo broke up much of the local network, Ree's sub agent Eric Cauchi was shot and killed in early 1944, several directors were taken straight from a weekly board meeting, and one of them, Auguste Bonal, was deported and shot beside a road in Germany in April 1945, two weeks before the surrender. In the autumn of 1944 the Germans stripped the plant anyway. As for whether sabotage beat bombing, the official historian's own verdict on industrial sabotage in France is that the results were only moderately good.

## TITLE OPTIONS (working titles)

**The `Codename:` form is deliberately not used here.** STOCKBROKER is the name of an SOE circuit, a standing network of people, not a codenamed operation, and the channel reserves the `Codename: X` form for operations. Leading with `Codename: STOCKBROKER` would also promise a spy-network episode and this episode is about a factory, a bombing error and a business decision. All three options use the Standard form.

**What I did with the script's recommendation:** kept it, unchanged, as option 1 and as the recommendation. The word "next" is load-bearing and is not touched. "The Next Air Raid" describes a raid that had not happened and, in the event, never happened, which is exactly what the record supports. Any edit toward "the second raid", "the RAF coming back", or "before the bombers returned" would assert a second bombing that the script spends Act 7 proving was cancelled, so none of the alternates go near that ground either.

1. **`They Wrecked Their Own Factory to Stop the Next Air Raid | Codename History`** (RECOMMENDED. Kept verbatim from the script. It is the whole inversion in eleven words, "their own" carries the curiosity gap, and "the next" cannot be read as a claim that a second raid happened.)
2. `The Car Company That Helped Pay to Have Its Own Factory Wrecked | Codename History` (the money angle, which is the cold open's second beat. "Helped pay" matches the narration exactly and claims no figure, because the funding figures in the sources are not reconcilable and none is spoken.)
3. `Every Detonator Was Backwards. So They Came Back on Guy Fawkes Night. | Codename History` (the cold open as the title. Entirely inside the SOE report, including the joke, and it asserts nothing about outcomes at all.)

## PACKAGE NOTES

- The hook is the first two paragraphs and both sit above the "...more" fold. Paragraph one is the cold open's curiosity gap, the raid that failed on a technicality. Paragraph two is the payoff, the owners wanting it wrecked and having thought of it first.
- **There is no second RAF raid anywhere in this file.** The description states the March 1944 raid was scheduled and cancelled, and then says "There was no second raid" in plain words so that a scraped or quoted excerpt cannot invert it. Ree's threat that the RAF would come back is not quoted in the description at all, because out of its Act 4 quotation marks it reads as an event.
- **The occupation-economy figures are absent, as they are from the script.** No 80 per cent collapse, no fall from 6,000 vehicles a month to 500. Those measure raw material shortage and labour conscription, they mostly predate the sabotage, and attributing them here would be the worst error available. The only effect figures in the description are Foot's Appendix G list: three months, three weeks, five weeks, several months, output cut to forty per cent, and it is named as "the postwar verified list" for that reason. Foot's own p. 287 line about the plant being out of production for much of the rest of the war is not used.
- **Casualties are handled exactly as the narration handles them.** 123 killed, labelled as the local French count. The injured are "hundreds more" with no number, because 336 is Middlebrook's British figure and the French sources give about 250. Besancon's 51 dead are stated as separate and as often wrongly combined, and are never added in. Foot's "some hundreds of townspeople were killed" is named as wrong.
- **Rodolphe Peugeot is named and given no job title**, because the sources call him the manager at Sochaux, the owner, a cadre in the family firm and the head of Aciers et Outillages Peugeot, and they cannot all be right. The Robert Peugeot correction is stated, since it is the most useful single tell in the description text.
- **The sabotage-beat-bombing claim is not made.** No independent non-SOE assessment of it exists. The description closes on Foot's own "only moderately good" verdict on industrial sabotage in France, which is the same load the outro carries.
- **The myth inversion is stated at SOE's own strength and no higher.** Peugeot were "already toying with the idea", which is what the 1946 report says. The description does not go on to say they were only protecting themselves, and it keeps Jean-Pierre Peugeot's double game and the Petainism finding out of the summary rather than flattening either side of the family's record into a verdict.
- **Eric Cauchi gets "early 1944" and "was shot and killed", with no date and no killer named**, because the National Archives file, Foot and Ree's own papers give three different accounts.
- **The four-mile forest crawl is not in this file.** The river swim is real and is in the script, but the Act 6 escape is left to the chapter title in the description rather than summarised, so there is no opportunity to reintroduce it.
- No number is given for the arrests in the October 1943 sweep, for the directors taken in March 1944, for the wagons that stripped the plant in 1944, or for the money Peugeot put into the circuit. Every one of those is single-sourced or contested in the fact-check log.
