# Research Report: Harry Ree and the Peugeot Works

## Read this first: the pitch is mostly right, but three parts of it are wrong

The core story is real and unusually well documented for an SOE episode. Three things in the brief need correcting before the script is written.

1. **"The RAF returned to bomb the works again" is not supported.** No second raid on the Peugeot works at Sochaux is documented anywhere I could find. A second Bomber Command attack was **planned for March 1944 and cancelled** after the sabotage evidence reached London. The tidy version is actually tidier than the brief assumes. What complicates the story is not a second raid, it is that the effect of the sabotage was smaller and more temporary than the legend says.
2. **"Tank turrets" is SOE's word, not the French record's.** Foot says tank turrets. Every French source, including the plant's own local histories, says **patins de chars**, tank track shoes or pads, plus connecting rods and cylinder heads, plus light trucks, plus aircraft parts. Hedge this.
3. **"Talked a member of the Peugeot family into sabotaging their own factory" flattens what happened.** SOE's own 1946 internal report says the Peugeot management were **already toying with the idea themselves** before Ree proposed it, precisely to avoid another RAF raid. Ree did not have to sell it as hard as the legend says.

Also note: the pitch's implied causality, bombing first and contact after, is wrong in sequence. Ree's first contact with Peugeot management was in **early July 1943, before the raid**, and was about money.

## The three flagged conflicts, resolved

### 1. The bombing date: night of 15/16 July 1943

**Resolved.** The raid took off on the evening of **15 July 1943** and bombed in the early hours of **16 July 1943**. RAF Bomber Command, **165 Halifaxes, 134 from No. 4 Group and 31 from No. 8 Group (Pathfinder Force)**, with the target indicators dropped by **No. 35 Squadron**. Stated target: the **Peugeot works at Sochaux**, roughly 3 km east of Montbeliard. Five Halifaxes lost. [Middlebrook and Everitt, *The Bomber Command War Diaries*, quoted in full on RAFCommands; RAF Bomber Command Campaign Diary, July 1943; aircrewremembered.com working from squadron Operations Record Books]

**Where the disagreement comes from, and it is one man.** M. R. D. Foot's official history *SOE in France* dates it to **"14 July"**. Foot is the most-copied source on this story, so his date has propagated into a large share of English-language retellings. It is wrong. French local accounts frequently say **16 July**, which is defensible as the date the bombs actually fell but is not the operation date.

**For the script:** say "the night of 15 July 1943" or "the early hours of 16 July". Do not say 14 July.

### 2. The civilian casualties: about 120 to 135 dead, most credibly 123 or 126, plus roughly 250 to 336 injured

There is no single agreed number. Here is what each figure actually measures.

| Figure | What it measures | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **123 killed, 336 injured** | The contemporary French local report, as recorded by Bomber Command | Middlebrook and Everitt, *The Bomber Command War Diaries*; RAF Bomber Command Campaign Diary |
| **126 civilian victims** | A later archive-based recount for the **commune of Sochaux specifically**, from Sochaux, Montbeliard and Besancon archives and police records | Emmanuel Coris and Alain Duvernoy, *Le bombardement de Sochaux dans la nuit du 15 au 16 juillet 1943* (Cetre) |
| **120 dead, 250 injured, 1,200+ displaced** | Peugeot-adjacent French corporate history, workers' districts | Jean-Louis Loubet, *La Maison Peugeot* (Perrin, 2009), p. 242, via fr.wikipedia |
| **125 dead, 250 injured** | The most commonly repeated French popular figure | Est Republicain; autocult.fr; fr.wikipedia Usine Stellantis de Sochaux |
| **135 dead, 250 injured** | Local historian's count | Andre Chatelot, *Mes Cahiers d'Histoire* Cahier no. 1 |
| **"Almost three hundred killed or wounded"** | A compressed killed-plus-wounded figure. Understates the 123 + 336 = 459 total | Neal Ascherson, *London Review of Books*, 2020 |
| **"Some hundreds of townspeople were killed"** | **Overstated by roughly two to three times.** The single most-copied error in the story | M. R. D. Foot, *SOE in France*, p. 287 |

**One trap to avoid:** the same night, bombs also fell in error on **Besancon**, hitting the Gare Viotte and the Chaprais district, with a reported 51 dead there. Those are **separate** casualties from a separate misdrop and must not be merged into the Sochaux total.

**For the script:** "around a hundred and twenty-five people killed and hundreds more hurt" is safe. If you want a single hard number, use **123 killed and 336 injured** and attribute it to the Bomber Command record. Never say "hundreds killed".

**Bomb distribution, which is the real point:** approximately **30 bombs fell inside the factory and about 600 fell on the town**. The markers were **700 yards beyond the target**. The factory was assessed at **5 per cent damaged** and **"production was normal after the raid"**. Roughly 90 to 100 houses destroyed and two small factories damaged in Sochaux. [Bomber Command War Diaries]

### 3. The Peugeot contact: Rodolphe Peugeot, but more than one Peugeot was involved and one widely-cited account gets it wrong

**Best identification: Rodolphe Peugeot** (1902 to 1979), son of Robert I Peugeot and younger brother of company president Jean-Pierre Peugeot, a senior figure in the firm with existing Resistance contacts. [Imperial War Museums catalogue of its oral history interview with Harry Alfred Ree, which explicitly lists "his contact with Resistance leaders like Rodolphe Peugeot", so this is Ree's own naming; Neal Ascherson in the *LRB* reviewing Ree's papers; fr.wikipedia "Peugeot pendant la Seconde Guerre mondiale", citing Loubet, *La Maison Peugeot*; autocult.fr]

**Who gets it wrong, and why.** The name **"Robert Peugeot"** comes from **Patrick Howarth, *Undercover: The Men and Women of the SOE* (1980)**, and is repeated by Spartacus Educational, which is where most casual retellings pick it up. Robert I Peugeot (1873 to 1945) was the patriarch. He handed the presidency to Jean-Pierre in **July 1941**, was in very poor health after several heart attacks and using a wheelchair, and died in 1945. He was not "one of the directors" Ree could call on in 1943. Almost certainly a confusion between father and son.

**Foot never gives a first name at all.** He writes only "M. Peugeot" and "one of the directors of the firm". Anyone who cites Foot for a first name is citing something Foot did not say.

**The complication the script should keep.** SOE's own contemporary internal history names Ree's **first** management contact as **Paul Sire, "Director of Co-ordination for all Peugeot factories"** [Major R. A. Bourne-Paterson, *SOE in France 1941-1945*, written 1946, published Pen and Sword 2016]. French sources call the same man **Pierre Sire**, describing him as head of the coordination services created by Jean-Pierre Peugeot, who made contact with the OCM resistance organisation in early 1943 [Chatelot]. So Ree's way into the firm was through a **senior executive, not a family member**. Separately, **Jean-Pierre Peugeot**, the president, is named as the man who handed Ree 10,000 francs on arrival, who helped saboteurs move inside the plant and identified which machines to hit.

**For the script:** the family member behind the deal is **Rodolphe Peugeot**. Say so, and if you want one line of honesty, note that the door into the company was opened by an executive named Sire and that the president's money was already flowing to Ree before any of it started.

## Verified timeline

- **1937** - Harry Alfred Ree, a language master, teaching at Bradford Grammar School, later at Beckenham and Penge County School for Boys. [en.wikipedia; AIM25 archival record for RÉE, Harry Alfred (1914-1991)]
- **1940** - Ree registers as a **conscientious objector**, conditional on serving in the National Fire Service. [en.wikipedia; Ascherson, *LRB*]
- **1941** - Ree re-registers for military service and is called up. Recruited into SOE. Commissioned captain, Intelligence Corps. Codename **"Cesar"**, field name **"Henri" / "Henry"**. [en.wikipedia; Ascherson; Foot]
- **Early 1943** - Berlin places the Peugeot works under direct **Volkswagen** control. Ferdinand Porsche and Anton Piech take the plant over from Adler, chiefly for its foundry. [Chatelot; driventowrite; fr.wikipedia]
- **April 1943** - Ree parachutes into France, received by Southgate near Tarbes, jumping with Maingard. Southgate is alarmed by Ree's French accent and passes him to HEADMASTER, which collapses. Ree moves to **ACROBAT** under John Starr, distrusts Starr's security, and is sent off on his own mission toward Belfort. [Foot, p. 286]
- **Spring / early summer 1943** - Ree's circuit becomes independent as **STOCKBROKER**, in Franche-Comte, around the Doubs and the Jura. [Foot]
- **Early July 1943** - **Before the raid.** Ree makes first contact with Peugeot management through **Paul (or Pierre) Sire**, coordination director. [Bourne-Paterson]
- **July 1943** - London acknowledges receipt of Peugeot money over the BBC. [Chatelot]
- **Night of 15/16 July 1943** - **The raid.** 165 Halifaxes. Markers 700 yards long. About 30 bombs in the factory, 600 in the town. 123 killed, 336 injured. Factory 5 per cent damaged, production normal afterwards. 5 Halifaxes lost. [Bomber Command War Diaries; aircrewremembered]
- **Early August 1943** - Sire meets "Henry" and passes on the first sketch of the **V-1 flying bomb**, obtained by Jean-Pierre Peugeot from a Peugeot engineer summoned to the Volkswagen plant at Fallersleben. Ree routes it to the British legation at Berne. [Chatelot, single source, hedge]
- **Autumn 1943** - The deal. Ree proposes internal sabotage instead of bombing. Peugeot asks for proof of bona fides. **Peugeot composes a brief personal message which the BBC broadcasts a few nights later.** Convinced, he supplies factory plans and inside contacts, including the foreman of the machine shop. Ree makes **one** personal reconnaissance of the plant and never enters it again. [Foot, p. 287, Tier 1]
- **Early autumn 1943** - An attempt on 22 high-tension transformers fails; charges on the **forge compressors and the foundry sand skip** succeed. Ball bearings appear in rear axles and emery powder in the petrol of trucks bound for Germany. [Chatelot; cancoillotte]
- **27 October 1943** - Gestapo sweep: **24 resistants arrested, including Captain Roger Fouillette** of the OCM. [Chatelot]
- **Night of 3 November 1943** - **First sabotage attempt fails.** Six workmen under "Andre" get inside and place their charges on transformers and compressors. **The detonators had been put in the wrong way round.** [Bourne-Paterson, Tier 1 SOE record; corroborated by cancoillotte and Chatelot]
- **5 November 1943** - **The attack succeeds.** SOE's own report notes the date wryly: "(Guy Fawkes no doubt providing the inspiration)". Foot's verified list records the result as a **3 months' stop** and labels it **"First 'blackmail' operation"**. [Bourne-Paterson; Foot, Appendix G]
- **19 November 1943** - Auxiliary compressor destroyed with a magnetic charge. [cancoillotte]
- **20 November 1943** - Foundry sand bins destroyed. [cancoillotte]
- **c. 28 November 1943** - **The fight.** Ree, calling at the house of M. Hauger, a schoolmaster in Sochaux, opens the door onto a **Feldgendarme** with a pistol. Foot: "a fantastic fist fight with a Feldgendarme who intended to arrest him." Ree breaks a bottle over the German's head; the German empties his magazine into Ree's torso; Ree feels nothing and assumes blanks; the German shouts "Sortez" and props himself against the wall. Ree gets out across the garden, over a wall, across a field, over **a small stream**, and to a house 3 km away. [Bourne-Paterson; Foot]
- **December 1943** - Ree is smuggled into **Switzerland** to recover, reportedly with the help of two cars lent by the Peugeot company. [Ascherson; Chatelot]
- **9 December 1943** - Sabotage blocks fake-ambulance parts production; tank track output cut from 250 to 150 pieces. [cancoillotte]
- **c. 15 January 1944** - Aircraft-parts sabotage. Foot's verified result: **3 weeks' stop**. [Foot, Appendix G]
- **6 February 1944** - Eric Cauchi ("Pedro"), Ree's parachuted sub-agent, **shot by the Gestapo in a cafe brawl**. [Foot, p. 371]
- **10 February 1944** - Two entries in Foot's verified list: **several months' stop** on the tank work and a **5 weeks' stop** on aircraft parts, both with the note **"Replacement machine tools destroyed on arrival from Germany"**. [Foot, Appendix G]
- **13 March 1944** - Main transformer destroyed. Plant stopped 5 days. [cancoillotte]
- **15 March 1944** - Foot's verified list: **"Output cut to 40 per cent"**, again with replacement machine tools destroyed on arrival from Germany. [Foot, Appendix G]
- **March 1944** - Bomber Command schedules a **second raid** on Sochaux, believing the Germans will start V-1 fuselage production there. The circuit cuts the plant's entire electrical supply and photographs of the sabotage go to London. **London is convinced and cancels the raid.** [fr.wikipedia citing Loubet; cancoillotte; corroborated in kind by Bourne-Paterson: "the R.A.F. were successfully dissuaded from making another attack"]
- **23 March 1944** - After roughly fourteen sabotages in five months, **Meurer**, the German director of the plant, asks Berlin to finish with the "terrorists". The Gestapo arrest **seven or eight Sochaux directors** at a directors' meeting, including **Auguste Bonal**, director of stamping and sports director of FC Sochaux, plus **Robert Godard**, **Louis Margaine**, Delattre, Fabrizio and **Ernest Mattern**. [fr.wikipedia Auguste Bonal, citing Defence Ministry archives; Chatelot; toutmontbeliard.com]
- **29 March 1944** - Jean-Pierre Peugeot goes to Gestapo regional headquarters at Dijon to ask for his directors' release, is accused of "making eyes at occult forces", and afterwards judges it safer to cross into Switzerland. [Chatelot, single source]
- **22 August 1944** - Bonal deported: Dijon, then Struthof, then the **Schomberg** labour camp in Wurttemberg. [fr.wikipedia Auguste Bonal]
- **Spring 1944** - Ree returns to Britain through Spain, in time to serve on EMFFI staff. [Foot, p. 372]
- **July 1944** - **Diana Rowden**, courier to the neighbouring ACROBAT circuit, murdered at **Natzweiler** with Vera Leigh, Andree Borrel and Sonia Olschanesky. [Foot, p. 429]
- **September to 17 November 1944** - Germans strip the Sochaux plant, loading machines, electric motors, transformers and the vehicle park onto a reported **2,500 wagons** for Germany. [Chatelot]
- **23 April 1945** - **Auguste Bonal shot dead by retreating German troops near Bad Waldsee**, five days after being marched out of Schomberg. The FC Sochaux stadium is renamed for him in July 1945. [fr.wikipedia Auguste Bonal; fcsochaux.fr; toutmontbeliard.com]
- **17 May 1991** - Harry Ree dies, aged 76. [en.wikipedia; AIM25]

## Key figures

**Harry Alfred Ree, DSO, OBE** (15 October 1914 to 17 May 1991). British. Son of Dr Alfred Ree, a chemist from a Danish Jewish family, and Lavinia Elisabeth Dimmick, American-born great-granddaughter of the chemist and industrialist Eleuthere Irenee du Pont. Schoolmaster. **Registered as a conscientious objector in 1940, then re-registered for military service in 1941**, which is verifiable and is the strongest character beat available. Captain, Intelligence Corps. SOE F Section. Codename **"Cesar"**, field name **"Henri"**. Organiser of the **STOCKBROKER** circuit in Franche-Comte. Postwar: headmaster of Watford Grammar School for Boys from 1951, first Professor of Education at the University of York 1961 to 1974, first Provost of Derwent College, then gave up the chair in 1974 to go back to classroom teaching at Woodberry Down Comprehensive in London until 1980. Note the shape of that: he left a professorship to teach children again. [en.wikipedia; AIM25; Ascherson, *LRB*; Foot's index]

**Rodolphe Peugeot** (2 April 1902 to 17 June 1979). Son of Robert I, younger brother of company president Jean-Pierre. Resistance contacts including, per French accounts, Jean Moulin. Named by Ree in his own IWM oral history as his Peugeot contact. Financed Ree's circuit. Won a Croix de Guerre in 1939. [IWM; fr.wikipedia; autocult.fr]

**Jean-Pierre Peugeot**. President of Automobiles Peugeot from **July 1941**, when Robert I stood down. Ran the "double jeu": falsified reports, invented procedures to slow production, hid hundreds of STO evaders on company farms and forestry sites, funded the maquis. Also, in 1940 to 1941, on the Petainist side of the ledger. [Francois Marcot, "La direction de Peugeot sous l'Occupation: petainisme, reticence, opposition et resistance", *Le Mouvement social* no. 189, 1999, pp. 27-46; fr.wikipedia]

**Robert I Peugeot** (1873 to 1945). The patriarch. Handed the presidency to Jean-Pierre in July 1941. Chatelot describes him by then as badly weakened by several heart attacks and moving about in a wheelchair (single source). **Not** Ree's contact, despite Howarth and Spartacus. [fr.wikipedia, Robert Peugeot (1873-1945); Marcot; Chatelot]

**Paul Sire** (French sources: **Pierre Sire**). Director of Coordination for all Peugeot factories. Ree's first management contact, early July 1943. [Bourne-Paterson; Chatelot]

**Pierre Lucas**. Chief electrician of Peugeot, introduced to Ree by Roger Fouillette. Named in the SOE report as the inside technical man. cancoillotte calls him "Lieutenant Lucas", a reserve officer and electrical engineer, and names a 21-man team under him. [Bourne-Paterson; cancoillotte]

**Roger Fouillette**. Alsatian artillery captain, OCM organiser in the region. Arrested 27 October 1943. [Bourne-Paterson; Chatelot]

**Auguste Bonal**. Director of stamping at Sochaux and sports director of FC Sochaux-Montbeliard. Arrested by the SS in October 1943, released, re-arrested by the Gestapo 23 March 1944, deported, **shot dead near Bad Waldsee on 23 April 1945**. Stade Auguste-Bonal is named for him. [fr.wikipedia; fcsochaux.fr]

**Ernest Mattern**. Newly appointed administrator of Automobiles Peugeot. Argued to the German command that the production programme could not be met while 1,200 skilled men were away on STO, and that building Focke-Wulf parts a few kilometres from Switzerland was reckless. Imprisoned several weeks in 1944. His son Jean-Paul was deported to Dachau. [Chatelot, single source]

**Meurer**. The German director of the Sochaux plant, who asked Berlin in March 1944 to deal with the "terrorists". [Chatelot, single source]

**M. R. D. Foot**. Official historian of SOE in France. **Interested party**, and the source of two of the biggest errors in circulation (the 14 July date and "some hundreds killed"), but also the source of the most useful corrective, below.

**Major R. A. Bourne-Paterson**. Wrote SOE F Section's own internal account in 1946 from the surviving records. Declassified and published 2016. The closest thing to a contemporary SOE document available in print.

## Absurd and comedic beats (all real, with a note on how strong each is)

There is genuinely good comic material here and, unusually, most of it is in the primary sources rather than the retellings. Ranked by how safe it is to build a joke on.

1. **The detonators were in backwards.** On the night of **3 November 1943**, six workmen got inside a guarded German war plant, placed their charges on the transformers and compressors, got back out, and nothing happened, because the detonators had been fitted the wrong way round. They came back two nights later. **Tier 1, SOE's own report, corroborated in two French sources. Strongest beat in the file.**
2. **They came back on Guy Fawkes Night.** SOE's own 1946 report puts the joke in writing: "On the 5th November (Guy Fawkes no doubt providing the inspiration) the attack was repeated, this time with success." **The official record makes the joke for you. Tier 1.**
3. **Blowing up the replacement while it was still on the truck.** Foot: the Germans spent months getting a replacement machine tool built and shipped from Germany, and STOCKBROKER destroyed it **while it was waiting to be unloaded from a lorry in the factory yard**, so the whole replacement process had to start again. Foot's Appendix G shows the note "Replacement machine tools destroyed on arrival from Germany" against **three separate dates**. So it was not a one-off gag, it became a routine. **Tier 1, official history plus its verified appendix. Very strong.**
4. **Ree thought the bullets were blanks.** In the fight, the Feldgendarme emptied his pistol magazine into Ree's torso and Ree, feeling nothing, assumed the magazine was loaded with blanks and carried on fighting. Then the German shouted "Sortez" and leaned against the wall. **Tier 1 via the 1946 SOE report.**
5. **The Michelin family said no.** Foot, deadpan, in the official history: "the Michelin family refused to follow the Peugeots' example and assist in dislocating their tyre factory at Clermont-Ferrand, and the RAF consequently damaged it severely." **Tier 1, black comedy, and it makes the argument better than any statistic.**
6. **Sand, ball bearings and emery powder.** Ball bearings dropped into rear axles, emery powder into the petrol of trucks about to be delivered to the Germans, bad alloys, porous head gaskets and weakened clutches deliberately built in. Slow-motion sabotage as factory-floor comedy. **Medium sourcing, fr.wikipedia and Chatelot, use as a montage rather than a specific claim.**
7. **The Swiss radio excuse.** Ernest Mattern told the German command that building Focke-Wulf aircraft parts a few kilometres from the Swiss border was extremely risky, because Swiss radio's René Payot was broadcasting anti-Axis commentary that everybody was listening to. Bureaucratic sandbagging dressed as concern. **Single source, Chatelot. Hedge or skip.**
8. **The football match and the bomb in the pocket.** The saboteurs, waiting outside the factory gate for someone to fetch a key, played football against the uniformed German guards, and when a home-made bakelite bomb fell out of a saboteur's pocket a German guard politely handed it back. **This is the single most quotable beat in the whole story and it is the one I trust least.** It appears only in **Patrick Howarth, *Undercover* (1980)**. It is not in Foot, not in Bourne-Paterson's SOE report, and not in any French source I found. **If you use it, attribute it out loud as "one account says", or cut it.**

## Human-cost beats (play these completely straight)

- **123 civilians killed and 336 injured** in Sochaux and the neighbouring communes of Vieux-Charmont, Nommay, Etupes and Allenjoie, in a raid where **about 30 bombs out of roughly 630 hit the factory**. Around 90 to 100 houses destroyed, more left uninhabitable, over a thousand people displaced. The factory carried on working normally the next day. **This is the moral centre of the episode. Do not undercut it.**
- **Five Halifax crews** lost the same night, on a raid that achieved 5 per cent damage.
- **Separately, 51 dead at Besancon**, hit by mistake the same night.
- **27 October 1943**: 24 resistants arrested in one sweep, including Captain Roger Fouillette.
- **Diana Rowden**, courier to the neighbouring circuit, arrested in November 1943 after an SOE courier broke under interrogation and the Germans sent an impersonator. Murdered at **Natzweiler concentration camp in July 1944** with three other women. She was in Ree's world, not his circuit, but the arrests came out of the same collapse.
- **Eric Cauchi**, Ree's parachuted sub-agent, shot by the Gestapo in a cafe brawl, 6 February 1944.
- **23 March 1944**: the Gestapo arrested seven or eight Sochaux directors at a directors' meeting. **Auguste Bonal** went Dijon, Struthof, Schomberg, and was **shot dead by retreating Germans on a roadside near Bad Waldsee on 23 April 1945**, two weeks before the German surrender. His name is on the FC Sochaux stadium. **This is the beat the audience will remember. The man who sabotaged the plant to stop the bombing did not survive it.**
- Ernest Mattern's son **Jean-Paul deported to Dachau**.
- Reported **44 Peugeot workers shot and 200 deported** (single source, hedge the numbers but not the fact of reprisals).
- **Ree himself never came back to the plant.** After one reconnaissance he never set foot inside it again. Everyone who kept doing it was French, local, and staying.

## Myth vs record (corrections, each sourced)

**Myth: the RAF came back and bombed the works again.**
**Record: no second raid happened.** A March 1944 attack was planned and then **cancelled** when SOE sent London photographs of the sabotage damage. SOE's own report: "the R.A.F. were successfully dissuaded from making another attack." [Bourne-Paterson; fr.wikipedia citing Loubet; cancoillotte] Foot's line about "the damage that would ensue when the RAF returned to do the job properly" is Ree's **threat** to Peugeot, not a description of something that happened. The pitch has read a threat as an event.

**Myth: Ree had to talk a reluctant industrialist into it.**
**Record: SOE's own contemporary report says Peugeot were already thinking about it.** "The Peugeot people themselves were toying with the idea of sabotaging their own factory, in order to stop production and thereby prevent a repetition of the disastrous bombardment by the R.A.F." [Bourne-Paterson, 1946] This is SOE conceding the idea was already in the room, which is a striking thing for an interested party to write down. It makes the story better, not worse.

**Myth: pure heroism on the Peugeot side.**
**Record: motive and self-interest were the same act, and the historians say so.** The standard academic treatment is Francois Marcot's, and its title is the argument: "petainisme, reticence, opposition et resistance". In May 1940 the leadership was Robert I as president, Jean-Pierre as general director and Maurice Jordan as deputy. Deputy **Francois Peugeot voted to grant full powers to Petain**, and declarations in favour of the Marshal multiplied. The firm then moved, over years, toward obstruction and then resistance. Jean-Pierre's "double jeu" was aimed at three things at once: slowing German production, **keeping the plant in France rather than seeing it transferred to Germany**, and keeping workers out of the STO. A family firm sabotaging its own plant to stop the Allies flattening it is a rational business decision as well as a brave one. [Marcot, *Le Mouvement social* no. 189, 1999; fr.wikipedia; Chatelot]

**Myth: the factory made tank turrets.**
**Record: probably not, and this is an SOE-side word.** Foot says "tank turrets" and "the foreman of the tank turret machine shop", and his Appendix G column simply says "Peugeot tanks". Every French source instead lists **patins de chars** (tank track shoes or pads), connecting rods and cylinder heads, plus 202 saloons and vans and DMA light trucks, plus **aircraft landing gear and later Focke-Wulf Ta 154 fuselage parts**, plus, after Porsche and Volkswagen took the plant over in early 1943, parts for the **Kubelwagen and Schwimmwagen**. Foot separately says "Focke-Wulf engine parts". A V-1 fuselage programme was **proposed and never ran at Sochaux**. [Foot; Chatelot; cancoillotte; fr.wikipedia; driventowrite] **Say "parts for German tanks and German aircraft". Do not say turrets as a flat fact.**

**Myth: "some hundreds of townspeople were killed."**
**Record: about 123 to 126.** Foot's phrase is the origin of the inflation and it appears verbatim in retelling after retelling. [Foot, p. 287, versus Bomber Command War Diaries and Coris]

**Myth: the sabotage put the factory out of action for the rest of the war.**
**Record: Foot's own verified appendix contradicts Foot's own narrative.** Page 287 says "out of production for much of the rest of the war". Appendix G, built from Brooks's postwar on-the-ground verification tour of France, records for Sochaux: 3 months' stop, several months' stop, 3 weeks, 5 weeks, output cut to 40 per cent. That is serious, cumulative, repeated disruption. It is not "out of action". **Use Appendix G's numbers and drop the narrative claim.**

**Myth: this proved sabotage beats bombing.**
**Record: SOE tried to scale it and could not, and the official historian's own verdict is hedged.** Foot: Baker Street "tried to make much use of it elsewhere; but of course it was by now too late in the war to catch on properly. A joint Air Ministry and SOE committee had a dozen meetings during the winter, hunting for targets" and got almost nowhere because Bomber Command was committed to POINTBLANK. Only **one** further success is recorded, and it is a negative one: **Michelin refused and Clermont-Ferrand was bombed.** And then Foot's own summary judgment, at p. 438: **"The results of industrial sabotage in France were only moderately good in terms of damage and delay to enemy war production."** The strongest honest version of the claim is Foot's cost comparison, not an effectiveness comparison: **all** confirmed F Section industrial sabotage in France used about **3,000 lb of plastic**, less than one Mosquito's load and a quarter of one Tallboy, and Bomber Command "frequently lost in a night more men than F section lost in the entire war". That is a real and defensible argument about **cost and civilian harm**. It is not proof that sabotage beat bombing in output terms.

**Myth: the 80 per cent production collapse shows what the sabotage did.**
**Record: it does not.** Peugeot's productivity was down about 80 per cent against 1939 and vehicle output was down from roughly 6,000 a month to 500, but that is the occupation economy, raw-material shortage and labour conscription, and most of it predates the sabotage. **Attributing it to Ree would be the worst single error the script could make.**

**Myth: the sabotage started because of the bombing.**
**Record: partly. The relationship started before it.** Ree's first contact with Peugeot management was in **early July 1943**, before the raid, and Foot records that a Peugeot director was already negotiating a **loan to STOCKBROKER repayable by the British Treasury after the war** when Ree made the sabotage proposal. The raid supplied the argument, not the introduction. And the popular line that the Allies "offered compensation after the war" is a garbling of that pre-existing loan-repayment arrangement.

## Memoir-attributed or single-sourced claims (the writer must hedge these in narration)

**Hedge as "by his own account" or cut:**
- **That Ree witnessed the raid.** Widely repeated. Not established in any primary source I found. Foot does not say it. **Treat as unverified.**
- **"Shot four times: lung, arm, shoulder, side."** From Howarth via Spartacus. SOE's 1946 report says the German "emptied the magazine of his automatic pistol" into Ree's torso without a count of hits.
- **"Swam across a river and crawled four miles through forest to Switzerland."** The SOE report describes crossing a garden, a wall, a field and **a small stream**, to a house **3 km away**, and then being smuggled into Switzerland later to recover. The swim-and-crawl version is the embellished one. **This is exactly the beat the brief warned about, and the warning was correct.**
- **The football match and the returned bakelite bomb.** Single source, Howarth (1980).
- **The exact BBC message wording.** Foot confirms **that** Peugeot composed a personal message and the BBC broadcast it. The wording "La vallee du Doubs est bien belle en ete" is given by three French sources, but **Chatelot attributes that phrase to London acknowledging a Peugeot payment in July 1943, not to the sabotage bona fides**, and Chatelot separately gives Rodolphe Peugeot's own later BBC arrival signal as "Les peupliers de l'oncle Jules ont besoin d'etre elagues". There may be two or three different BBC messages being conflated. **Use "a message he chose himself" unless you are willing to say "one account gives the phrase as".**
- **The money figures.** 150,000 francs plus 50,000 a month (autocult, fr.wikipedia) versus 10,000 francs on arrival (Chatelot). Unreconciled.
- **The V-1 sketch passed to Berne.** Chatelot only.
- **44 workers shot and 200 deported; 2,500 wagons; Jean-Pierre Peugeot's Dijon confrontation and his "occult forces" line; Meurer's appeal to Berlin; Mattern's Swiss-radio argument.** All Chatelot only. Vivid, plausible, locally specific, and unconfirmed elsewhere.
- **Ferdinand Porsche's intervention** to stop the Gestapo arresting all the directors, quoted as "Si vous arretez tous les directeurs, ca ira encore plus mal!" Comes to fr.wikipedia via an autocult.fr article. Single chain.
- **The detailed 21-man saboteur roster** on cancoillotte.net (Fischer, Menegaux, Bernard, Charmois, Schorpp, Wilhelm and others) and the fine-grained dates of 19 and 20 November and 9 December 1943. Regional heritage site, no footnotes given. Names are probably right but I could not verify them individually.

**Note on the whole story's evidence base.** This episode survives largely through (a) Foot's official history, written for SOE from SOE's records, (b) Bourne-Paterson's internal SOE report, written by SOE about SOE, (c) Ree's own postwar memoir, talks and interviews, published by his son as *A Schoolmaster's War* (Yale, 2020), and (d) French local and company-linked histories with their own reasons to stress resistance over collaboration. **Every strand has an interest.** The two places where the sources cut against their own interest are the most trustworthy things in the file: SOE admitting Peugeot thought of it first, and Foot admitting industrial sabotage in France was "only moderately good".

## Gaps

- **NOT FOUND:** Any evidence of a second RAF or USAAF raid on the Peugeot works after 15/16 July 1943. The March 1944 raid was cancelled. **The brief's claim on this point should be treated as false unless the script writer finds something I did not.**
- **NOT FOUND:** Direct access to the text of *A Schoolmaster's War* (Yale, 2020), so **Ree's own wording** on the Peugeot approach, the contact's name and the escape is unquoted here. Every full-text copy I could reach was paywalled or 403. Its introduction does confirm "At the end of November, he got into a fight with an armed Feldgendarme... He was badly wounded but managed to get away." **If the writer wants a Ree quote, this book is the place, and the IWM sound interview (object 80008516) is the other.**
- **NOT FOUND:** The full text of Marcot's 1999 article (Cairn and JSTOR paywalled) and Loubet's *La Maison Peugeot*, so the academic verdict on Peugeot's motives is summarised from the article's abstract, its title, and fr.wikipedia's use of it rather than read directly.
- **NOT FOUND:** German-side documentation of the production loss at Sochaux. Nothing from Speer ministry records, Volkswagen archives or captured German documents. The RAF Air Historical Branch's translated captured-German-documents page on Allied bombing of French targets returned 403. **The production-loss claims therefore rest entirely on Allied and French sources.**
- **NOT FOUND:** An independent, non-SOE, non-Foot academic assessment specifically weighing the Peugeot case as evidence that sabotage beat bombing. The nearest thing to an independent corrective available is **Foot's own caveat on p. 438**, which is inside the interested source. **Treat "this proved sabotage beat bombing" as an SOE claim that has never been independently tested, and say so.**
- **NOT FOUND:** Whether the British Treasury actually repaid the Peugeot loan after the war.
- **UNRESOLVED:** Whether Ree's decisive meeting was with Rodolphe Peugeot in person or was brokered through Paul/Pierre Sire, and whether Rodolphe was continuously in France through the sabotage campaign (Chatelot has him leaving for Morocco and London at some point, without a date; other French sources have him running the sabotage with "Cesar" through spring 1944). **The name Rodolphe is solid; his exact presence in each scene is not.**
- **UNRESOLVED:** Whether the man SOE called "Paul Sire" and the French call "Pierre Sire" is the same person under two first names, which he almost certainly is, or two people.
- **NOT FOUND:** No meaningful existing video coverage of this story, which the brief anticipated. That means there is no consensus retelling to sanity-check against, and it also means the errors above (14 July, "hundreds killed", "Robert Peugeot", "out of action for the rest of the war") have never been publicly corrected. **If this episode gets the dates and numbers right, it becomes the reference version by default. That is worth the extra care.**
