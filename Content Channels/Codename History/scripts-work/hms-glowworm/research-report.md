# Research Report: HMS Glowworm and the First Victoria Cross of WW2

## One-paragraph summary

On 8 April 1940, during the opening of the German invasion of Norway (Operation Weserübung), the British G-class destroyer HMS Glowworm (H92), commanded by Lieutenant Commander Gerard Broadmead Roope, became separated from the battlecruiser HMS Renown's screening force in heavy weather while searching for a sailor washed overboard. Alone, Glowworm ran into two German destroyers (Z11 Bernd von Arnim and Z18 Hans Lüdemann), which fell back on the far larger German heavy cruiser Admiral Hipper. Hopelessly outgunned, Roope laid a smoke screen, traded fire, was badly hit, and then deliberately rammed the Hipper, tearing open roughly 100 feet of the cruiser's starboard side, destroying a torpedo mounting, and letting in around 500 tonnes of water, before Glowworm, ablaze, blew up when her boilers exploded at 10:24. Roope survived the sinking but drowned from exhaustion, slipping off a rope while being hauled up the Hipper's side. Of a crew of 149, about 40 were pulled from the sea by the Hipper; at least six died of wounds aboard, and 31 ultimately survived, meaning roughly 118 men died. The Hipper's captain, Kapitän zur See Hellmuth Heye, later sent word to the British via the International Red Cross praising Roope's gallantry, which contributed to Roope's posthumous Victoria Cross, gazetted 6 July 1945, the first VC awarded for an action of the Second World War.

## Verified timeline

- 6 April 1940 - Glowworm loses a man overboard in heavy weather and is detached from the task force (HMS Renown's group) to search for him. [Wikipedia "HMS Glowworm (H92)"; Naval Historical Society of Australia] (Wikipedia states detachment "on 7 April"; NHSA says the man was lost 6 April. Both agree the cause was a man overboard in heavy weather; presented as "6-7 April" in script.)
- 8 April 1940, morning - Alone and still searching, Glowworm encounters German destroyers Z11 Bernd von Arnim and Z18 Hans Lüdemann, part of the Weserübung invasion force. [Wikipedia; War History Online]
- 8 April 1940 - The German destroyers disengage toward the heavy cruiser Admiral Hipper; Glowworm engages Hipper, lays smoke, is badly hit, then rams the cruiser. [Wikipedia; worldwar1-2.com; navalhistoria.com]
- 8 April 1940, 10:24 - Glowworm's boilers explode; she sinks, taking about 109 of her crew with her. [Wikipedia]
- 8 April 1940 - Admiral Hipper heaves to and rescues about 40 men from the sea; Roope drowns slipping from a rope. [Wikipedia; Naval Historical Society of Australia]
- 6 July 1945 - Roope's posthumous Victoria Cross announced in the London Gazette (Supplement), the first VC for a WW2 action. [London Gazette via Wikipedia "Gerard Broadmead Roope"; victoriacrossonline.co.uk; Forces War Records]

## Key figures

- Lieutenant Commander Gerard Broadmead Roope, RN (13 March 1905 - 8 April 1940) - commanding officer of HMS Glowworm; fought the ship to the end, ordered the ramming, helped his crew into lifejackets, and drowned from exhaustion. Posthumous VC. [Wikipedia "Gerard Broadmead Roope"; CWGC]
- Kapitän zur See Hellmuth Heye (full name Hellmuth Guido Alexander Heye) - captain of the heavy cruiser Admiral Hipper; held his ship downwind to rescue Glowworm's survivors, then recommended Roope for the VC via the International Red Cross. [Wikipedia "Hellmuth Heye"; Naval Historical Society of Australia]

## Absurd / comedic beats (all real)

- A single small destroyer (Glowworm, ~1,350 tons) deliberately ramming a heavy cruiser (Admiral Hipper, ~14,000 tons standard) roughly ten times its size. The size mismatch is the whole joke, and it is real.
- The reason Glowworm was alone at all: it wandered off to look for one man who fell overboard, and stumbled straight into a German invasion fleet.
- Glowworm hit the Hipper so hard it tore off a chunk of the cruiser's plating and sheared off her own bow, then scraped down the side gouging holes the whole way.
- The enemy captain later personally recommended the British commander he had just sunk for Britain's highest medal. One of only a tiny handful of VCs in the whole war owed partly to the opponent's testimony.

## Human-cost beats (play straight) - TONE: STRAIGHT

- Of 149 men aboard Glowworm, roughly 118 died. Only 31 ultimately survived. [Wikipedia; NHSA]
- About 109 men went down with the ship when the boilers exploded at 10:24. [Wikipedia]
- Roope survived the sinking, helped his men into lifejackets and to the rescue ropes, took hold of a rope himself, was pulled part-way up the Hipper's side, and then, from exhaustion and the heavy seas, could no longer hold on and drowned. [Wikipedia "Gerard Broadmead Roope"; NHSA]
- The German crew and some soldiers hauled exhausted, freezing British sailors out of the sea; at least six of those rescued still died of their wounds and were buried at sea. [Wikipedia; NHSA]

## Myth vs record (corrections)

- Popular telling: sometimes framed as Glowworm "accidentally" colliding with the Hipper. / Actual: the ram was deliberate. Roope ordered a hard turn to starboard to ram; the London Gazette citation credits "finally ramming the enemy with supreme coolness and skill." [Wikipedia; London Gazette citation via Forces War Records]
- Popular telling: the VC was a purely British decision. / Actual: it was significantly prompted by the enemy captain, Heye, sending a statement of the action to the British via the Red Cross recommending recognition; one of only a very small number of WW2 VCs owed in part to an opponent's testimony. [Wikipedia "Gerard Broadmead Roope"; NHSA]
- Spelling note: some sources write "Helmuth Heye"; the fuller, more authoritative form is "Hellmuth Heye" (Hellmuth Guido Alexander Heye). Use "Hellmuth Heye." [Wikipedia "Hellmuth Heye"]
- Date-of-detachment note: Wikipedia says Glowworm was detached "on 7 April" to search for the man overboard; NHSA says the man went overboard on 6 April. Both agree on the cause (man overboard, heavy weather) and that the action was 8 April. Script says "the day before" without pinning an exact detachment date.
