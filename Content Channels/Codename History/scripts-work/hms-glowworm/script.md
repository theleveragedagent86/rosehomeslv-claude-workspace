# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE DESTROYER THAT RAMMED A CRUISER TEN TIMES ITS SIZE

**Target runtime:** 11-13 minutes (~1,900 narration words at ~150 wpm)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake country/ship dialogue.
**Story type:** Single obscure naval action (real, documented). HMS Glowworm and the first Victoria Cross of the Second World War.
**Title (standard tier):** `The Destroyer That Rammed a Cruiser Ten Times Its Size | Codename History`

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: Every event here is documented. The size mismatch, the ram, and the enemy captain recommending his own opponent for a medal are all real. A couple of beats correct the popular telling (the ram was deliberate, not accidental; the VC was partly prompted by the enemy). Keep the jokes on the absurdity. Do not re-simplify the corrected beats back into myths.
>
> VOICE NOTE: Comedy lives in the NARRATION, not just the visuals. Deadpan delivery. The narrator explains a suicidal naval charge like it is the world's worst idea, and then makes you feel it when the tone turns. Ships get fake dialogue. The human cost (the crew, Roope's death) is played completely straight, no jokes, marked TONE: STRAIGHT.

## COLD OPEN (0:00 - 0:40)

**[VISUAL: A tiny cartoon destroyer bobbing on a grey, furious sea. Next to it, sliding into frame, an enormous warship that dwarfs it entirely.]**

**NARRATOR:** This little ship is a British destroyer. This absolute wall of steel next to it is a German heavy cruiser roughly ten times its size.

**[VISUAL: The tiny destroyer looks up at the giant. A dramatic gulp.]**

**NARRATOR:** By every rule of naval warfare, the small one is about to die. And it knows it.

**[VISUAL: The destroyer's wheel spins hard. It turns and points its nose straight at the cruiser.]**

**NARRATOR:** So instead of running, it did the most insane thing a warship can do. It aimed itself at the giant, and it hit the gas.

**[VISUAL: Freeze frame, arrow pointing at the destroyer.]**

**NARRATOR:** And the captain of the giant was so impressed, he would spend the rest of the war trying to get his own enemy a medal.

## ACT 1: ONE SHIP, ONE MAN OVERBOARD (0:40 - 2:45)

**[VISUAL: A calendar flipping to April 1940. A map of Norway with British ships steaming toward it.]**

**NARRATOR:** April 1940. The war is seven months old and mostly quiet. Everyone is waiting for something to actually happen. And it is about to happen in Norway.

**[VISUAL: British ships dropping mines into Norwegian coastal waters, labeled "OPERATION WILFRED."]**

**NARRATOR:** The British were up here running Operation Wilfred, sneaking around Norwegian waters laying mines. What they did not know was that Germany had picked the exact same week to invade the entire country.

**[VISUAL: A German invasion fleet steaming north from the other direction, unseen.]**

**NARRATOR:** So two big fleets were heading for the same stretch of freezing sea, and neither one had any idea the other was there.

**[VISUAL: A small destroyer, HMS Glowworm, steaming with a larger group of ships in a rising storm.]**

**NARRATOR:** Our star is one small British destroyer. This is HMS Glowworm, pennant number H92, a G-class destroyer of a little over a thousand tons. She is part of the screen for the big battlecruiser Renown. She is not large. She is not famous. She is about to become both.

**[VISUAL: The Renown steaming ahead, huge, while Glowworm bobs along beside it in worsening weather.]**

**NARRATOR:** For a destroyer, the Renown was the popular kid, and Glowworm was the small friend tagging along. The plan was simple. Stay with the group, guard the big ship, do not get lost. Glowworm was about to fail step three spectacularly.

**[VISUAL: A huge wave crashes over Glowworm's deck. A single tiny sailor tumbles overboard into the sea.]**

**NARRATOR:** Then, in heavy seas, a sailor was washed overboard. And Glowworm did the decent thing. She peeled away from the rest of the fleet, all alone, to circle back and search for one man in a storm.

**[VISUAL: The rest of the fleet vanishing over the horizon while Glowworm turns back into the weather alone.]**

**NARRATOR:** Think about how ordinary that decision was. Not heroic yet. Just a captain refusing to leave a man behind, turning his little ship around into the worst of the weather to go look.

**[VISUAL: Glowworm turning in slow circles in an empty grey ocean, separated from everyone.]**

**NARRATOR:** She did not find him. What she found instead was much, much bigger.

---

## ACT 2: THE WRONG NEIGHBORS (2:45 - 4:45)

**[VISUAL: Glowworm alone in fog and spray. Two enemy shapes materialize out of the murk.]**

**NARRATOR:** On the morning of April 8th, still alone and still searching, Glowworm ran straight into two German destroyers. Which was already a bad matchup. But these two were not the problem.

**[VISUAL: The two German destroyers, Bernd von Arnim and Hans Lüdemann, immediately turning and running away.]**

**NARRATOR:** These are the destroyers Bernd von Arnim and Hans Lüdemann. And instead of fighting a lone British ship, they turned and ran. Which felt like a win. It was not a win.

**[VISUAL: A ship-to-ship phone call. The fleeing destroyers dial a number.]**

**BERND VON ARNIM (character voice):** "Hi, yes, big scary boss? There's a British ship being annoying. Could you come deal with it?"

**NARRATOR:** They were running toward their big friend. And their big friend was the heavy cruiser Admiral Hipper.

**[VISUAL: The Admiral Hipper emerging from the fog, colossal, guns already swinging around. Glowworm is a toy in front of it.]**

**NARRATOR:** The Hipper was a monster. Fourteen thousand tons of armored warship with eight-inch guns, up against a destroyer of barely more than a thousand. This was not a fight. This was a formality.

**[VISUAL: Side-by-side scale comparison. Glowworm a sliver next to the towering Hipper. Numbers ticking up: 1,300 tons versus 14,000.]**

**NARRATOR:** To put that in perspective, the Hipper weighed something like ten times what Glowworm did, and her main guns could reach out and hit you long before your little pop guns could reach her. On paper, Glowworm had already lost.

**[VISUAL: Glowworm's tiny commander looking up at the cliff of grey steel.]**

**NARRATOR:** In command of Glowworm was Lieutenant Commander Gerard Broadmead Roope. He knew exactly what he was looking at, a cruiser that could end him in seconds. And he had a choice to make.

**[VISUAL: A thought bubble over Roope. Option one: turn and flee. Option two: a tiny ship pointed at a huge one.]**

**NARRATOR:** He could turn and run and radio a warning, which was the sensible move, the move the manual would tell him to make. Or he could stay and fight a ship that could sink him with a single salvo.

**[VISUAL: Roope at the wheel, jaw set.]**

**NARRATOR:** He chose to stay. He chose to do something much stupider and much braver. Hold that thought.

---

## ACT 3: DAVID PICKS THE DUMBEST OPTION (4:45 - 6:45)

**[VISUAL: The Hipper's guns firing. Enormous shell splashes erupt around the tiny Glowworm.]**

**NARRATOR:** The Hipper opened fire. And when a cruiser that size hits a destroyer, the destroyer does not usually get a second chapter.

**[VISUAL: Glowworm belching thick black clouds of smoke behind her, weaving.]**

**NARRATOR:** So Roope did the only clever thing left. He laid a smoke screen, this thick wall of black fog, to hide his ship and confuse the giant's gunners while he darted in and out.

**[VISUAL: The smoke rolling across the water. The Hipper firing blind into the cloud, shells landing wide.]**

**NARRATOR:** And for a while, it worked. Hiding a whole warship inside its own smoke sounds ridiculous, but a destroyer's one advantage is that it is small and fast, and inside that fog the giant's gunners could not get a clean shot.

**[VISUAL: Glowworm darting out of the smoke, launching torpedoes at the cruiser, then ducking back in.]**

**NARRATOR:** Glowworm used the cover to dash out, fire torpedoes at the cruiser, and vanish back into the black. She was a mouse throwing punches at a bear and, for a few minutes, actually landing some.

**[VISUAL: Both ships firing through the smoke. A hit slams into Glowworm. Her mast topples.]**

**NARRATOR:** But a mouse cannot win this. The Hipper's shells started finding her. Hits tore into the little ship, her mast came crashing down, and there was only ever going to be one ending to this.

**[VISUAL: Glowworm battered, listing, on fire in places, but still moving.]**

**NARRATOR:** Glowworm was dying. And here is where a normal story ends with a small ship going down bravely. This is not that story.

**[VISUAL: Roope pointing dead ahead at the Hipper. The wheel spinning hard to starboard.]**

**NARRATOR:** Roope turned his crippled, burning destroyer directly at the cruiser and ordered a hard turn to ram. Not by accident. On purpose. He was going to use his sinking ship as the weapon.

**[VISUAL: Glowworm accelerating straight at the Hipper's side. The Hipper trying to turn away, too slow.]**

**NARRATOR:** The Hipper saw it coming and tried to swing away. She was too big and too slow. And Glowworm came in like a spear.

**[VISUAL: Impact. A huge crunch. Sparks and torn metal.]**

**NARRATOR:** She hit.

---

## ACT 4: THE HIT (6:45 - 8:30)

**[VISUAL: Slow motion. Glowworm's bow crumpling and shearing clean off against the cruiser's hull.]**

**NARRATOR:** The collision tore Glowworm's own bow completely off. And then the wreck of the little destroyer scraped all the way down the giant's side.

**[VISUAL: A long gash opening down the Hipper's starboard hull, roughly a hundred feet of torn plating.]**

**NARRATOR:** It ripped open around a hundred feet of the Hipper's starboard side. It destroyed one of her torpedo mountings. And it let something like five hundred tonnes of freezing seawater pour into the biggest warship in the fight.

**[VISUAL: The Hipper listing slightly, alarms flashing on her bridge, crew scrambling to seal off flooded compartments.]**

**NARRATOR:** A destroyer that was already sinking had just seriously wounded a heavy cruiser. Let that sink in. The toy hurt the monster.

**[VISUAL: A German damage-control party fighting the flooding deep inside the Hipper's hull.]**

**NARRATOR:** This was not a scratch. A warship taking on five hundred tonnes of water has a real problem, and the Hipper's crew spent the next stretch of the invasion fighting to keep their own ship in the fight. All of it done to them by a vessel a fraction of their size, on its way to the bottom.

**[VISUAL: Glowworm drifting clear, dead in the water, burning from end to end.]**

**NARRATOR:** But it cost Glowworm everything. She drifted away from the Hipper wrecked, without a bow, and on fire.

**[VISUAL: Clock ticking to 10:24. Then a massive explosion as the ship blows apart.]**

**NARRATOR:** At twenty-four minutes past ten in the morning, the fire reached her boilers, and Glowworm blew up.

**[VISUAL: The tone shifts. Muted colors. Debris and men in the black water. No jokes now.]**

**NARRATOR:** TONE: STRAIGHT. The explosion took around a hundred and nine of her crew down with the ship. Men who had been fighting a battle they could not win, gone in a moment, in freezing water, off the coast of Norway.

---

## ACT 5: THE ENEMY STOPS (8:30 - 10:30)

**[VISUAL: The damaged Hipper, still huge, slowing to a halt near the wreckage and the men in the water.]**

**NARRATOR:** Now the Hipper could have simply left. She was damaged, she was in a war, and the men in that water had just rammed her. Nobody would have blamed her captain for sailing on.

**[VISUAL: The Hipper's captain, Hellmuth Heye, on the bridge, giving an order to stop.]**

**NARRATOR:** Her captain was Kapitän zur See Hellmuth Heye. And he stopped his ship.

**[VISUAL: The Hipper deliberately positioning herself upwind, lowering ropes and nets down her side toward the survivors.]**

**NARRATOR:** He held the Hipper downwind of the wreckage so the survivors could drift toward him, and he put ropes and nets over the side. German sailors and soldiers reached down to haul freezing British men out of the sea.

**[VISUAL: Straight, grim shots. Exhausted men being pulled up the hull, some barely conscious.]**

**NARRATOR:** TONE: STRAIGHT. Around forty men were pulled from the water. At least six of them died of their wounds and the cold anyway, and were buried at sea. In the end, of a crew of one hundred and forty nine, only thirty one survived.

**[VISUAL: Roope in the water, no longer commanding, just helping. Pushing crewmen toward the ropes, fitting a lifejacket onto another man.]**

**NARRATOR:** TONE: STRAIGHT. Lieutenant Commander Roope had survived the sinking. And in the water, he was seen helping his own men, pushing them toward the ropes, making sure they had their lifejackets, before himself.

**[VISUAL: Roope finally taking hold of a rope. German hands pulling him up the side of the hull.]**

**NARRATOR:** TONE: STRAIGHT. At last he took hold of a rope himself, and the Germans began to pull him up the side of the Hipper.

**[VISUAL: Roope, partway up the towering hull, hands slipping. He slides back down into the dark water. Quiet.]**

**NARRATOR:** TONE: STRAIGHT. But he was exhausted, and the sea was heavy, and partway up he could no longer hold on. He slipped from the rope, back into the water, and drowned. He was thirty five years old.

---

## ACT 6: THE MEDAL FROM THE ENEMY (10:30 - 12:30)

**[VISUAL: The damaged Hipper steaming away into the grey, the rescued survivors aboard.]**

**NARRATOR:** And here is the part that makes this whole story almost impossible to believe.

**[VISUAL: Heye at a desk, writing a letter. An envelope marked with a red cross.]**

**NARRATOR:** Captain Heye, the enemy, the man whose ship Glowworm had just rammed, sat down and wrote to the British. He sent word through the International Red Cross describing exactly how Glowworm had fought, and saying her commander deserved the highest praise.

**[VISUAL: A German officer effectively nominating a British officer for a British medal. The absurdity of it drawn plainly.]**

**NARRATOR:** Read that again. A German captain, in the middle of the war, went out of his way to tell his enemy that one of their dead was a hero, and to nudge them toward giving him a medal.

**[VISUAL: A war raging on across a calendar. Glowworm's story sitting unknown in a file.]**

**NARRATOR:** And that testimony mattered, because for years, nobody on the British side actually knew what had happened out there. The men who survived were prisoners in Germany. The men who could describe the final ram were mostly dead. The full story of Glowworm's last stand was locked away on the far side of the war.

**[VISUAL: Time skip. "1945." A Victoria Cross being placed on velvet.]**

**NARRATOR:** So it took until nineteen forty five, until the war in Europe was ending and the whole picture could finally be pieced together, with Heye's account on the record. And then, at last, they gave Gerard Roope the Victoria Cross.

**[VISUAL: The medal, then a caption: "FIRST VICTORIA CROSS OF THE SECOND WORLD WAR."]**

**NARRATOR:** It was the first Victoria Cross awarded for any action of the Second World War. Britain's highest award for bravery, and the very first one of that entire war, and it was owed in part to the word of the enemy.

**[VISUAL: A small gallery of other VC medals, only two or three glowing, to show how rare this is.]**

**NARRATOR:** That almost never happens. Across the whole of the Second World War, you can count on one hand the times an enemy's testimony helped hand out a Victoria Cross. Roope's is one of them. His enemy helped write the case for his own medal.

**[VISUAL: The London Gazette citation on screen, key words highlighted.]**

**NARRATOR:** The official citation credits Roope for fighting off a superior force, seeking out and reporting a powerful enemy, and then fighting his ship to the end against overwhelming odds, finally ramming the enemy with, in their words, supreme coolness and skill.

**[VISUAL: A quiet shot of the empty sea where Glowworm went down.]**

**NARRATOR:** TONE: STRAIGHT. One hundred and eighteen of her men did not come home. The ship is still down there, off Norway, where she chose to fight.

**NARRATOR:** A destroyer that stopped to save one man, found an invasion, and rammed a cruiser ten times its size. A captain who died helping his crew out of the sea before himself. And an enemy commander who made sure the world eventually knew what they had done.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Voice choices:**
- Cold open leads with the pure size mismatch and the enemy-medal twist, the two most irresistible real hooks, before the title card.
- Fake ship dialogue kept short and dumb (the German destroyers phoning their "big scary boss").
- Deadpan roasting stays on the absurdity of the situation (a toy hurting a monster), never on the dead.
- Every act ends on a hook or cliffhanger fragment ("She hit.", "The toy hurt the monster.", "Hold that thought.").
- The entire back half (crew losses, Roope's death, the 118 dead) is played completely straight and marked TONE: STRAIGHT for the shotlist agent. No jokes anywhere near the casualties.

**Accuracy corrections (see fact-check log):**
- The ram is presented as deliberate, not an accidental collision, matching the London Gazette citation ("finally ramming the enemy with supreme coolness and skill").

- Spelling standardized to "Hellmuth Heye" and "Gerard Broadmead Roope."
- Survivor numbers presented honestly as a range that reconciles: about 40 pulled from the sea, at least 6 died of wounds, 31 ultimately survived, about 118 dead.

## FACT-CHECK LOG

Single well-documented naval action. Key beats verified against 2+ sources; corrections from the popular telling noted.

- **Ship identity:** HMS Glowworm, G-class destroyer, pennant H92. Confirmed [Wikipedia "HMS Glowworm (H92)"; uboat.net].
- **Commander:** Lieutenant Commander Gerard Broadmead Roope, RN (1905-1940). Spelling and rank confirmed [Wikipedia "Gerard Broadmead Roope"; CWGC].
- **Date:** 8 April 1940, during Operation Wilfred (British mining) and the opening of Operation Weserübung (German invasion of Norway). Confirmed [Wikipedia; War History Online].
- **Separation:** detached from the task force to search for a man lost overboard in heavy weather. Confirmed [Wikipedia; NHSA]. (Wikipedia dates detachment 7 April; NHSA dates man overboard 6 April; both agree on cause. Script says "then... a sailor was washed overboard," no exact detachment date pinned.)
- **German destroyers:** Z11 Bernd von Arnim and Z18 Hans Lüdemann. Confirmed [Wikipedia; War History Online].
- **Admiral Hipper:** German heavy cruiser, roughly 14,000 tons vs Glowworm's roughly 1,350, hence "ten times its size." Confirmed [Wikipedia].
- **Smoke screen and fight:** Glowworm laid smoke, fired torpedoes, was hit and dismasted. Confirmed [Wikipedia; navalhistoria.com].
- **The ram - CORRECTION:** deliberate, ordered as a hard turn to ram, not accidental. Confirmed by the London Gazette citation ("finally ramming the enemy with supreme coolness and skill") [Forces War Records; Wikipedia].
- **Damage to Hipper:** roughly 100 feet of starboard hull torn open, forward starboard torpedo mounting destroyed, about 500 tonnes of water taken on. Confirmed [Wikipedia (500 t, torpedo mount); War History Online / NHSA (100 feet)].
- **Boilers exploded 10:24; ~109 lost with the ship.** Confirmed [Wikipedia].
- **Casualties:** crew of 149; about 40 pulled from the sea; at least 6 died of wounds aboard; 31 ultimately survived; about 118 dead total. Numbers reconcile across sources [Wikipedia (149, 40 recovered, 6 died, 109 with ship); NHSA (149, 31 survived, 118 dead)]. TONE: STRAIGHT.
- **Roope's death:** survived the sinking, helped crew with lifejackets and ropes, took a rope, was pulled part-way up the Hipper, then from exhaustion and heavy seas let go and drowned, aged 35. Confirmed [Wikipedia "Gerard Broadmead Roope"; NHSA]. TONE: STRAIGHT.
- **Heye's recommendation - CORRECTION:** Kapitän zur See Hellmuth Heye held the Hipper to rescue survivors, then wrote to the British via the International Red Cross praising Roope and recommending recognition. Confirmed [Wikipedia "Gerard Broadmead Roope"; NHSA]. Spelling "Hellmuth Heye" per [Wikipedia "Hellmuth Heye"].
- **Victoria Cross:** posthumous, gazetted 6 July 1945, the first VC for a WW2 action, one of very few owed in part to enemy testimony. Confirmed [London Gazette Supplement via Wikipedia; victoriacrossonline.co.uk; Forces War Records].
