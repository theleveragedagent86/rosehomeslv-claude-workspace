# RESEARCH REPORT: Juan Pujol Garcia / Agent GARBO

Merged from three Wave 1 research agents (origins & recruitment; network & Fortitude South; D-Day & aftermath). Every load-bearing claim below carries at least two reputable sources unless explicitly flagged. Conflicts are stated, never averaged. Gaps are marked NOT FOUND.

---

## 1. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY

Juan Pujol Garcia was a failed Catalan poultry farmer and hotel manager who came out of the Spanish Civil War having served both sides without firing a shot, hating both totalitarianisms. From January 1941 he offered himself to British intelligence in Madrid and was turned away, three times in total. So he did the opposite: he talked his way into the Abwehr by posing as a fanatical pro-Nazi Spanish government official with a diplomatic route to London, was given secret ink, a codebook, a questionnaire and roughly 600 pounds, and was sent to Britain to build a spy ring. He never went. He set up in Lisbon and invented Britain out of a tourist guide, a map and an out of date railway timetable, filing wholly fabricated reports to Madrid, complete with imaginary sub-agents. Bletchley Park decrypts of Abwehr traffic revealed to the British that some unknown agent was feeding Germany fiction that Germany was acting on. That, plus his wife's approach to the American naval attache in Lisbon, finally got him taken seriously. He reached Britain on 24 April 1942, was codenamed BOVRIL then GARBO, and was paired with MI5 case officer Tomas Harris. Together they grew the fiction into a notional network commonly counted at 27 agents. In 1944 GARBO became one of the main channels of Operation Fortitude South, the deception that invented the First United States Army Group under Patton and aimed it at the Pas de Calais. His 9 June 1944 message argued Normandy was a diversion, and German armour that had been ordered south was held north of the Seine. Hitler personally authorised an Iron Cross Second Class for him on 29 July 1944. Britain appointed him MBE in December 1944. He faked his own death in Angola in 1949, lived obscurely in Venezuela for 35 years, was tracked down in 1984, and died in Caracas on 10 October 1988.

## 2. VERIFIED TIMELINE

**14 February 1912** Born in Barcelona. CONFLICT: Wikipedia/Wikidata give 1914 (citing the Spanish Biographical Dictionary); Talty reports a birth-registration discrepancy. Most sources, and his own headstone at Choroni (14-02-12), give 1912. USE 1912. [Spartacus; IWM; headstone via agentgarbo.com; conflict noted]

**1931** Compulsory military service, 7th Regiment of Light Artillery. [Wikipedia; Espionage&]

**Pre-war** Studied at the Royal Poultry School, Arenys de Mar. Ran a poultry farm north of Barcelona that failed. Also managed a small hotel. [National WWII Museum; Spartacus; IWM]

**1936-1939** Spanish Civil War. Conscripted by the Republicans, escaped on false papers obtained through the monarchist relief group Socorro Blanco, later crossed to the Nationalist side around the Battle of the Ebro (Sept 1938). Served both sides "without firing a single bullet for either." [Wikipedia; Legion Magazine; Espionage&]

**January 1941** First approach to the British Embassy, Madrid. Rejected. Two more approaches follow across Madrid and Lisbon, one made through his wife Araceli. All rejected. Exact dates of approaches 2 and 3 NOT FOUND. [The Past; Spartacus; TheCollector; National WWII Museum; d-dayinfo.org]

**1941** Recruited by the Abwehr in Madrid. Contact: **Friedrich Knappe-Ratey, codename "Federico."** German codename assigned: **ARABEL** (network later ARABAL; the rendering ALARIC also appears). Given a crash espionage course, invisible ink, a codebook, cover addresses, a questionnaire, and roughly **600 pounds**. Told to go to Britain and recruit sub-agents. [Military Wiki; TheCollector; Wikipedia; Spartacus]

**19 July 1941** Signals Madrid that he has "arrived" in Great Britain. He is in Lisbon. TNA KV 2/40 (German-side GARBO agent reports) opens 15 July 1941, consistent. [Wikipedia; TNA Discovery KV 2/40]

**Mid-1941 to early 1942** The Lisbon fabrication period. Based Lisbon/Estoril. Invents reports from public reference material. Also invents his first sub-agents: per the family site, a KLM pilot courier plus a replacement, plus three regional agents covering Glasgow, Liverpool and the west of England (single interested-party source, roughly five).

**February 1942** Bletchley Park decrypts of Abwehr Madrid traffic reveal agent reports of "superb inaccuracy" about British convoys, and that the Germans trust the source completely. MI6 Section V (Iberia) identifies him. [HistoryNet; National WWII Museum]

**Early February 1942** Pujol and/or Araceli walk into the US Embassy in Lisbon and meet **Lieutenant Patrick Demorest** of the naval attache's office, who takes him seriously and alerts the British. CONFLICT: some retellings name an assistant naval attache "Rousseau." Demorest is better attested. [agentgarbo.com; Espionage&]

**2 April 1942** Decrypted Abwehr signal relays Pujol's report of a nonexistent convoy about to sail from Liverpool to Malta. German orders go out to intercept it; German aircraft fly reconnaissance of the route and of Valletta. TNA KV 2/39 (Top Secret ISOS on GARBO) opens on this exact date. [HistoryNet; TNA Discovery KV 2/39; agentgarbo.com for the memoir detail of five ships]

**April 1942** Interviewed in Lisbon by MI6 officer **Gene Risso-Gill**; moved out by convoy to Gibraltar, then by military aircraft to England. [Spartacus; agentgarbo.com]

**24 April 1942** Arrives in Britain (most accounts, Plymouth). Met by MI5's **Cyril Mills** and **Tomas Harris**. Codenamed **BOVRIL**, then renamed **GARBO** at Mills's suggestion, as befitting "the best actor in the world." Housed at 35 Crespigny Road, Hendon. [Wikipedia; HistoryNet; English Heritage; Espionage&]

**16 May 1942** First report from Agent J(3), a notional Ministry of Information contact, described as the first sub-agent created jointly by Pujol and Harris in Britain. (single-source, indicative only)

**November 1942, Operation TORCH** Real convoys sail from the Clyde and Liverpool. GARBO's Glasgow agent reports the Clyde convoy. His Liverpool agent, **Agent No. 2, William Maximilian Gerbers**, cannot plausibly have missed the Liverpool sailing, so he is written as gravely ill and then killed off. A death notice is placed in a local Liverpool newspaper. The Germans send condolences and agree to pay a pension to the notional widow, who is then recruited as an agent in her own right. [Hubbard-Hall, peer-reviewed, citing TNA KV 2/41; Macintyre via LRB; MI5]

**Late June 1943** Araceli threatens to reveal the operation to the Spanish Embassy in order to get home to Spain. MI5 stages Pujol's fake arrest and detention at Camp 020. She attempts suicide by gas. She signs a statement promising not to interfere again. [Hubbard-Hall citing Harris's report, Appendix XXIX, TNA KV 2/42]

**Spring/summer 1943 onward** Network shifts from secret-ink letters to wireless. After August 1943 essentially all intelligence goes by radio.

**1943** Kuhlenthal showcases the ARABEL product to Abwehr chief Wilhelm Canaris as a model operation. (single-thread, flag)

**12 July 1943** MI5 internal memo records that Pujol's sources for the Lisbon period were "a Blue Guide [tourist guide], a map of England, an out-of-date railway timetable." This is the strongest documentary anchor for the reference-book beat. [The Local, reporting the 2016 TNA release]

**May 1944** GARBO and BRUTUS (Roman Czerniawski) tell their German handlers that Patton commands FUSAG. [Howard, Strategic Deception, via Fortitude literature]

**January 1944 to D-Day** Over **500 radio messages** between GARBO and Madrid, up to four transmissions a day, re-transmitted by Madrid direct to Berlin. [MI5 official history]

**Night 5/6 June 1944** MI5 pre-arranges with Madrid that German operators will listen through the night, cover story being that a sub-agent is arriving with urgent news. At **03:00 on 6 June** GARBO transmits the invasion warning, engineered to be too late to act on. It reports his Southampton sub-agent seeing troops issued embarkation kit including **vomit bags**. No German reply. The Madrid operators are off air from **23:30** and do not answer until roughly **08:00 on 6 June**, hours after the landings began. [MI5 official history; corroborated across TNA-derived reporting]

**6 June 1944, later** Because of the delay MI5 lets GARBO add further genuine but now stale operational detail, which raises his standing. He rebukes Madrid: "I cannot accept excuses or negligence. Were it not for my ideals I would abandon the work." (widely attributed to the MI5/TNA record; exact transmission time medium confidence)

**8 June 1944** Long report itemising Allied units identified in Normandy, all accurate, building credibility for what comes next. (medium-high)

**9 June 1944** THE key message. Reports a meeting with his agents; argues Normandy is a "large-scale diversionary operation" to draw reserves and that the real blow will fall on the Pas de Calais by FUSAG. Includes an inflated order of battle, roughly **75 divisions** in Britain against an actual figure near **50**. Asks that it be passed urgently to the German High Command. [MI5; Masterman via Wikipedia; Hesketh]

**07:30, 10 June 1944** Keitel telephones von Rundstedt. The order moving the **1st SS Panzer Division Leibstandarte** and the **Grossdeutschland Panzer Regiment of the 116th Panzer Division** toward Normandy is cancelled. CAUTION: the 07:30 time traces to a single secondary article apparently drawing on Hesketh. The underlying fact that the movement orders were cancelled and the units stayed north of the Seine is well supported. [Hesketh, Fortitude, via Warfare History Network; corroborated in substance by MI5, IWM, English Heritage]

**~11 June 1944** German message states all reports received in the last week from the ARABEL undertaking "have been confirmed without exception." [cited to Masterman]

**13 June 1944** First V-1 strikes London. Germans soon order GARBO to plot impact points on a special map.

**Late June / July 1944** To escape the V-1 reporting demand, Harris stages GARBO's "arrest" by a plainclothes policeman while he surveys bomb damage. He is "released" and produces a forged letter of apology from the Home Secretary. Berlin concludes he is too valuable to risk. Exact date NOT FOUND. [Wikipedia sourced account; Talty]

**29 July 1944** Berlin informs GARBO by radio that Hitler personally awarded him the **Iron Cross Second Class** for "extraordinary services." He cannot be decorated in person and receives the physical medal only after the war, from a former German handler. [MI5 official history]

**June to August 1944** Germans retain roughly **two armoured and nineteen infantry divisions** in the Pas de Calais awaiting a second invasion; MI5 phrases it "more than 20 divisions" for two months. Fifteenth Army stays north of the Seine. 1st SS Panzer's vanguard reaches Normandy only on the night of **27 June**, main body first week of July. [English Heritage; IWM; MI5]

**December 1944** Appointed **MBE**, presented privately by MI5 Director General **Sir David Petrie**, in secret, to protect his cover. CONFLICT: Wikipedia says 25 November 1944 from King George VI. MI5's own history and English Heritage both say December 1944, presented by Petrie. USE the MI5/English Heritage version. [MI5; English Heritage]

**1948-1949** Tells Harris to spread word that he has died. Travels to Portuguese Angola and fakes death from malaria in **1949**. A year later the British ambassador formally reports him dead to Spain. (Nigel West's publisher blurb says "reported dead in 1959"; treat as error or a later confirmation.)

**1950s-1984** Lives in Lagunillas, Venezuela, in anonymity. CONFLICT on occupation: most accounts say he ran a bookshop and gift shop; English Heritage says he worked for Shell as a language teacher. Not necessarily incompatible across 35 years. Do not state one as the whole truth.

**1971 onward** Rupert Allason, writing as Nigel West, hunts for GARBO's identity, roughly 13 years.

**March 1984** A former MI5 officer who served in Spain supplies Pujol's full name. **20 May 1984** West and Pujol meet in New Orleans. **Late May 1984** Reunion with retired intelligence officers in the drawing room of the Special Forces Club, London. **1984** Received by Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, at Buckingham Palace, in an unusually long audience. **6 June 1984** Travels to Normandy on the 40th anniversary to tour the beaches and pay respects to the dead.

**10 October 1988** Dies in Caracas of a stroke, aged 76. Buried at **Choroni**, Aragua state, Venezuela, inside Henri Pittier National Park by the Caribbean. Headstone: Juan Pujol Garcia 14-02-12 10-10-88, remembered by his wife, children and grandchildren.

## 3. KEY FIGURES

- **Juan Pujol Garcia (1912-1988).** GARBO to MI5, ARABEL to the Abwehr, BOVRIL before that. Barcelona poultry-school graduate, failed farmer, failed hotel manager, Civil War veteran of both sides. No intelligence training.
- **Araceli Gonzalez Carballo.** His wife. Harris's own report credits her with securing the January 1941 British Embassy interview that started the case. She made the Lisbon approach to the Americans and helped invent the fictional agents' life stories. Pujol's memoir omits her role.
- **Tomas "Tommy" Harris (1908-1964).** MI5, section B1A. Wealthy London art dealer specialising in Spanish art, with a Spanish mother, hence fluent Spanish, which mattered because Pujol's English was poor. Co-author of every GARBO letter and message. Wrote the roughly 300-page official case history (TNA KV 2/42), published as "GARBO: The Spy Who Saved D-Day." Killed in a car crash in Majorca in 1964. NOTE: Harris was MI5, not MI6.
- **Lt Col T.A. "Tar" Robertson.** Founded and ran MI5 B1A, the double-agent section. GARBO was a B1A case.
- **J.C. Masterman.** Chairman of the Twenty (XX) Committee, created January 1941, met weekly, approved what the double agents were allowed to tell the enemy. Wrote the foundational insider account.
- **Cyril Mills.** MI5 officer, met Pujol on arrival, proposed the codename GARBO. In civilian life a circus proprietor (Bertram Mills Circus).
- **Major Karl-Erich Kuhlenthal (1908-1975).** Senior Abwehr officer in Madrid who ran the ARABEL case and never suspected. A Mischling with a Jewish grandmother, given an Aryan certificate through Canaris in 1941, which made him desperate to prove himself. He is also the officer who swallowed Operation Mincemeat. British assessment: "the ideal spy to pass (dis)information."
- **Friedrich Knappe-Ratey, "Federico."** Abwehr Madrid, Pujol's initial recruiter and trainer.
- **Lieutenant Patrick Demorest, US Navy.** Assistant in the US naval attache's office, Lisbon. Took Pujol seriously in February 1942.
- **Gene Risso-Gill.** MI6 officer in Lisbon who interviewed Pujol and arranged his passage.
- **Roman Czerniawski, BRUTUS.** The other principal Fortitude South channel, a Polish officer working the same FUSAG order of battle.
- **Sir David Petrie.** MI5 Director General; personally presented the MBE in December 1944.
- **Field Marshal Wilhelm Keitel** (Chief of OKW) and **Field Marshal Gerd von Rundstedt** (C-in-C West). Keitel's 10 June call cancelled the panzer movement.
- **Herbert Morrison.** Home Secretary. Uneasy at the V-1 aim-shifting deception, on the grounds that politicians should not decide who lives and dies. Overruled.
- **Nigel West (Rupert Allason).** Historian who tracked Pujol down in 1984 and co-wrote the memoir.
- **Mary Kathryn Barbier.** Historian, "D-Day Deception." The principal published sceptic of the standard GARBO narrative.
- **Roger Hesketh.** Wartime deception officer whose report, published as "Fortitude: The D-Day Deception Campaign," is the closest thing to a primary account of the German reaction.

## 4. THE NOTIONAL NETWORK (confidence flagged, nothing invented)

- **Agent No. 1, "Carvalho."** Portuguese commercial traveller at Newport, south Wales, watching Channel shipping. Written as lazy and haphazard. (moderate)
- **Agent No. 2, William Maximilian Gerbers.** Swiss or Swiss-German businessman at Bootle, Liverpool. Killed off November 1942. TNA KV 2/41. (high)
- **Agent No. 2(1), "the Widow."** Gerbers's notional widow, taken into the network, and the person the Germans paid a pension to. (high)
- **Agent No. 3, "Carlos" / BENEDICT.** University-educated Venezuelan in Glasgow, GARBO's notional deputy. This is the "Venezuelan in Glasgow." (high on existence and description; codename moderate)
- **Agent 3(1).** An RAF **non-commissioned officer** at Glasgow. Popular retellings wrongly call him an officer. (moderate)
- **Agent No. 4, "CHAMILLUS."** Gibraltarian NAAFI waiter, later at Chislehurst, hostile to Britain because he had been evacuated from the Rock. Doubled as a wireless operator. (moderate-high)
- **Agent No. 5, "MOONBEAM."** Venezuelan in Canada, brother of Agent 3, with a cousin in Buffalo, New York. (moderate)
- **Agent No. 6.** A Field Security NCO, notionally died in 1943. A second notional death, distinct from the Gerbers obituary beat. (moderate)
- **Agent No. 7, "Stanley."** Welsh ex-seaman at Swansea running his own sub-ring, including "DAGOBERT" and "DONNY." (moderate on numbering, high that the sub-ring existed)
- **The Welsh fascists.** An underground fascist group notionally in Swansea. MI5's history renders the name "Brothers of the Aryan World Order"; other accounts say "Brothers in the Aryan World Order" or "World Aryan Order." Pick one and attribute.
- **The KLM courier.** A KLM pilot on the Lisbon route unwittingly carrying GARBO's letters, later replaced by a second pilot.
- **"Rags," an Indian poet, and Theresa Jardine, a Wren** notionally posted to Ceylon. Single-source (Macintyre, "Double Cross," via LRB). Ceylon as part of the network's reach is independently confirmed by MI5.
- **A secretary in Whitehall.** CONFLICT: Spartacus says Cabinet Office; Biteback says a War Cabinet secretary who was notionally GARBO's mistress. Do NOT say "a Wren in the War Office."
- **Others documented in outline:** an indiscreet American army sergeant, a soldier in the 9th Armoured Division, a censor, merchant seamen, and a communist in the Venezuelan's sub-ring who believed he was working for Moscow.

Reach: Britain, Canada, the United States, Ceylon.

## 6. ABSURD / COMEDIC BEATS (all real)

1. **He became a German spy as a resume line.** Rejected by Britain, he decided to get hired by the Abwehr first so he would be worth hiring as a double agent. When he pitched exactly that to the British in autumn 1941, they found the story too implausible to believe.
2. **He sold the Abwehr a job and a visa he did not have**, posing as a fanatically pro-Nazi Spanish government official with diplomatic access to London.
3. **He then did not go to Britain at all.** For roughly nine months he reported on a country he had never set foot in.
4. **MI5's own memo of 12 July 1943 lists his sources: a Blue Guide, a map of England, an out-of-date railway timetable.** MI5, in an internal file, describing what the Third Reich was buying.
5. **The Glasgow litre of wine.** His fictional Glasgow informant reported that local dock workers "would do anything for a litre of wine." Wine. In Glasgow. In 1941. The Germans, equally ignorant, believed it.
6. **His wife wrote the biographies of the imaginary spies.**
7. **He invented a convoy and a real navy went looking for it.** German units were ordered to intercept and German aircraft flew reconnaissance of the route and of Valletta harbour, hunting ships that had never existed. (See Myth vs Record: the reaction was German, not British.)
8. **Masterman's verdict on the Lisbon period:** Pujol was "responsible for a great expenditure of useless labour on the part of the enemy."
9. **MI5's first codename for him was BOVRIL.** After the beef extract.
10. **His case officer was a millionaire art dealer** who ran the most important deception agent of the war between sales of Spanish paintings, and left his Goya collection to the British Museum.
11. **The funeral notice.** MI5 placed a death notice for a man who had never existed in a local Liverpool paper. The Germans sent condolences.
12. **The Germans then put the fictional widow on the payroll**, paying a pension to a woman who did not exist for a husband who had never existed, and she was recruited as an agent.
13. **Kuhlenthal used ARABEL as his showpiece for Canaris**, presenting the crown jewel of German intelligence in Britain, which was written in a semi-detached house in Hendon by a Spaniard and an art dealer.
14. **The fake network crowded out real espionage.** The official assessment is that the Germans in Spain were so flooded with ARABEL product they made no further attempt to infiltrate agents into Britain.
15. **The Reich's money was laundered partly through a Jewish theatrical agent in New York.**
16. **The vomit bags.** The great D-Day warning turned partly on a sub-agent reporting that troops at Southampton had been issued embarkation kits containing vomit bags.
17. **Nobody was listening.** The 03:00 warning, engineered at the highest level and timed to the minute, went out to a Madrid station that had been off air since 23:30 and did not answer until about 08:00.
18. **The spy scolded the enemy for unprofessionalism,** and it worked. Missing the transmission made him more credible, not less, because MI5 could then load the retransmission with true but stale detail.
19. **The forged apology from the Home Secretary,** used to get him out of plotting V-1 impacts after a staged arrest.
20. **The Iron Cross by radio.** Hitler personally authorised a decoration normally reserved for front-line troops, for a man in London working for MI5, and it had to be conferred over the wireless. Both his medals had to be secret; the MBE was handed over privately by MI5's chief because an investiture would have blown his cover.
21. **He killed himself off and came back.** Faked malaria death in Angola in 1949, arranged through his own case officer, then ran a shop in Venezuela for 35 years while the intelligence world thought he was in an African grave. In 1984 a room of retired intelligence officers gathered at the Special Forces Club to greet a man they had been told was dead.

## 7. HUMAN-COST BEATS (play completely straight, TONE: STRAIGHT)

- **The Spanish Civil War is the engine of the story and it is not funny.** His mother and sister were arrested by the Republicans. He was conscripted, deserted, escaped on forged papers, then was mistreated by the side he crossed to. He came out hating both. His stated motive was moral: he wanted to fight Nazi Germany "with my imagination."
- **Malta in 1941-42 was under siege and starving.** Convoys to Malta were among the deadliest runs of the war. His convoy was invented; the reason it was plausible is that real ones were sailing and real crews were dying on them. Play only the fiction for laughs, never the setting.
- **Araceli.** She made the approach that started the case and was written out of her husband's memoir. Isolated in a country she did not want to live in, by mid-1943 she was threatening to expose the operation just to get home. MI5 faked her husband's arrest and detention at Camp 020. She tried to kill herself with the gas taps. She then signed a statement promising never to interfere again. MI5's internal description of her, quoted in the academic literature, is contemptuous. This is the state grinding down a woman to protect an operation. It is not a comic beat under any circumstances.
- **Kuhlenthal's position.** The Abwehr officer who swallowed all of it had a Jewish grandmother and needed spectacular successes to stay safe. That is a reason, not a punchline.
- **Gerbers was killed off because the Liverpool TORCH convoys were real** and could not be explained away any other way.
- **The 6 June warning was a calculated gamble with real lives.** It was genuine intelligence, released deliberately.
- **6 June 1944 itself.** At least 4,414 Allied troops confirmed killed, 2,501 of them American, over 10,300 total casualties, roughly 2,400 at Omaha alone. Every hour German armour was held north of the Seine was bought against that ledger. Do not use the casualty figure as a punchline for GARBO's cleverness.
- **The men on the beaches had no idea** that a Spaniard in London and a network of people who did not exist had anything to do with whether a panzer division reached them. Play the contrast quietly.
- **The V-1 campaign and the aim-shifting deception.** 10,492 flying bombs, 24,165 casualties. Feeding false impact locations pushed the barrage short, onto southern suburbs including Croydon, Penge, Beckenham, Dulwich, Streatham and Lewisham. Croydon alone had roughly 57,000 houses damaged. People died in those streets partly because the deception worked. Herbert Morrison, a south Londoner, was uneasy at "playing God" and was overruled. MI5 later destroyed the false reports GARBO and ZIGZAG had sent, understanding how south Londoners would react if they surfaced. Never comedic.

## 8. MYTH VS RECORD (corrections the script must respect)

1. **"GARBO won D-Day single-handedly."** No. Mary Kathryn Barbier argues in "D-Day Deception" that crediting GARBO, or Fortitude as a whole, is too simplistic, and that Allied bombing had so wrecked French rail and road logistics that the Fifteenth Army probably could not have redeployed in time regardless. The Germans were already predisposed to expect the Pas de Calais. Frame him as the decisive voice in an argument the Germans were already having with themselves, not as the man who created the belief. He was also one channel among several: BRUTUS worked the same deception, physical deception supplied the props, and Ultra told the Allies whether it was landing. "The most trusted spy Germany had, and he did not exist" is accurate. "He won the war single-handed" is not.
2. **Britain did not spot his genius and recruit him.** They rejected him at least three times. MI6 Section V identified him from decrypts; MI5's B1A only got the case after an inter-service dispute, once he was in Britain. The Americans took him seriously first.
3. **The convoy reaction was German, not British.** Do not stage a Royal Navy sortie against a phantom convoy. The British reaction was alarm that an unknown and apparently effective German agent was operating inside Britain.
4. **The agent who "died" was Agent No. 2, William Maximilian Gerbers, not "agent 14."** There is no reputable source for a number 14. A second notional death, Agent No. 6, is documented for 1943, but that is a different beat.
5. **Do not name the newspaper.** Every reputable source says only "the local paper." Say "a local Liverpool paper, in November 1942."
6. **The Gibraltarian waiter and the Welsh fascist are two different fictions.** Popular retellings blur them.
7. **The "RAF officer" was a non-commissioned officer.**
8. **Do not say "a Wren in the War Office."** The sources conflict on placement.
9. **Fortitude North vs South.** Fortitude was two halves under the wider Operation Bodyguard umbrella. North threatened Norway using a fictitious British Fourth Army in Scotland. South threatened the Pas de Calais with FUSAG and is where GARBO mattered. Do not credit him with Norway.
10. **Patton did not invent FUSAG and was not its only notional commander.** Omar Bradley held the largely titular command during formation; GARBO and BRUTUS reported the handover to Patton in May 1944. Patton's value was reputational: the German high command rated him the Allies' best general.
11. **"His 3am warning gave the Germans a chance they blew."** More precisely: the Allies engineered it to be unusable, and then the Germans failed even to receive it on time. Both halves are true. Do not imply the Allies gambled recklessly, and do not imply the Germans could have stopped the invasion had they heard it.
12. **Iron Cross Second Class, not First.** And he was informed by radio, not decorated in person.
13. **MBE, not OBE, and not a royal investiture.** Appointed MBE in December 1944, presented privately by MI5's Director General because a public investiture would have destroyed his cover.
14. **"The only person decorated by both sides in WW2"** is unprovable as a universal negative. Eddie Chapman (ZIGZAG) got the Iron Cross from the Germans but no comparable British decoration. Best phrasing: "one of the very few people, and quite possibly the only one, honoured by both Britain and Nazi Germany in the same war."
15. **He was ARABEL to the Germans, GARBO only to the British.**
16. **"Chicken farmer" is true but flattening.** He was a Royal Poultry School graduate whose farm failed, and who also failed as a hotel manager. "Failed at nearly everything he tried" is closer to the record.
17. **Tomas Harris was MI5.** Sources calling him MI6 are wrong.
18. **He died aged 76**, on 10 October 1988. Wikipedia's "aged 74" is arithmetically wrong against a 1912 birth; The Past's "1987" is also wrong.
19. **The memoir is an interested-party source.** "Operation Garbo" (1985, with Nigel West) and agentgarbo.com were written 40+ years after the fact and omit Araceli's role. Much of the "saved D-Day" framing traces back through it. Flag anything that appears only there.
