# EPISODE SCRIPT: HITLER GAVE THIS FAKE SPY A MEDAL

**Target runtime:** 13 to 14 minutes (~2,000 narration words at ~150 wpm)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake country dialogue.
**Story type:** Single-person deception story (real, documented). Juan Pujol Garcia, Agent GARBO.
**Title (standard tier):** `Hitler Gave This Fake Spy A Medal | Codename History`

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: Several beats here are corrected from the popular telling and must not be re-simplified back. The dead notional agent is Agent No. 2, William Maximilian Gerbers, not "agent 14." The newspaper carrying the fake obituary is never named. The Iron Cross was Second Class, awarded by radio. The British honour was an MBE, presented privately by MI5's Director General, not by the King. The phantom convoy was hunted by Germans, not by the Royal Navy. GARBO did not win D-Day single-handedly, and the script says so out loud.
>
> VOICE NOTE: The comedy lives in the NARRATION, not just the visuals. Deadpan delivery. Countries and spy agencies get fake dialogue. Second-person "you" pulls the viewer in. Every human-cost beat is tagged TONE: STRAIGHT and gets zero jokes, zero wink, zero irony. The Spanish Civil War, Araceli, D-Day, and the V-1 section are sacred.

## COLD OPEN (0:00 - 0:30)

**[VISUAL: A cramped Lisbon room, 1941. A man in shirtsleeves hunched over a typewriter, one bare bulb overhead, a stack of paper beside him.]**

**NARRATOR:** This is a failed chicken farmer from Barcelona, typing in a rented room in Lisbon.

**[VISUAL: As he types, a see-through cartoon man puffs into existence beside the desk. A Portuguese travelling salesman, waving.]**

**NARRATOR:** Every time he finishes a paragraph, a spy appears. This one is a travelling salesman in south Wales. He is not real.

**[VISUAL: Two more translucent figures puff in. A businessman in Liverpool. A student in Glasgow. They stand around the desk like coworkers.]**

**NARRATOR:** Neither is this one. Or this one. Nobody in this room exists except the man at the typewriter, and he has never been to Britain.

**[VISUAL: A German officer in Madrid reading the typed report, nodding with total confidence, stamping it as verified.]**

**NARRATOR:** Germany believed all of it. For four years. And then Adolf Hitler gave him a medal.

**[TITLE CARD: "HITLER GAVE THIS FAKE SPY A MEDAL"]**

## ACT 1: THE MAN WHO FAILED AT EVERYTHING (0:30 - 1:45)

**[VISUAL: Barcelona, 1930s. A young Juan Pujol Garcia in a graduation cap, holding a diploma with a chicken on it.]**

**NARRATOR:** Meet Juan Pujol Garcia. Born in Barcelona in nineteen twelve. He went to the Royal Poultry School. Yes, that was a real school. Yes, he graduated.

**[VISUAL: A poultry farm north of Barcelona, chickens escaping in every direction, a "CLOSED" sign nailed to the gate.]**

**NARRATOR:** He then ran a chicken farm, which failed. So he managed a small hotel, which also failed. His entire resume is birds and disappointment.

**[VISUAL: Tone shift. Muted colors. A Spanish street in 1936, soldiers at both ends, civilians pressed against a wall.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** Then came the Spanish Civil War. His mother and his sister were arrested by the Republicans. He was conscripted, deserted on forged papers, and crossed to the Nationalist side. That side mistreated him too.

**[VISUAL: Pujol walking away from two identical armies, both facing away from him. He carries nothing.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** He served both sides and never fired a single bullet for either one. He came out hating both. He said later that he wanted to fight totalitarianism with his imagination.

**[VISUAL: Colors return. Pujol at the British Embassy in Madrid, hat in hand, being shown the door.]**

**NARRATOR:** So in January nineteen forty one, he walked into the British Embassy in Madrid and offered to spy for them. They said no.

**[VISUAL: A revolving door spinning. Pujol enters three times, gets ejected three times, one attempt made through his wife.]**

**NARRATOR:** He tried again. No. He tried a third time, once through his wife Araceli. No. Britain rejected the greatest double agent of the war three separate times.

**BRITAIN (character voice):** "Sir, this is a war, not a hobby."

**NARRATOR:** So he had an idea. If Britain would not hire him as a spy, he would go and get some experience first.

---

## ACT 2: THE WORST JOB APPLICATION IN HISTORY (1:45 - 3:00)

**[VISUAL: Pujol in a slicked-back haircut and a borrowed suit, marching into an Abwehr office in Madrid with enormous confidence.]**

**NARRATOR:** He decided the fastest way to become a British double agent was to first become a German spy. So he applied to the Abwehr. German military intelligence.

**[VISUAL: Pujol shaking hands with Abwehr officer Friedrich Knappe-Ratey, codename Federico, while a fake diplomatic passport glints in his pocket.]**

**NARRATOR:** He told them he was a fanatically pro-Nazi Spanish government official with diplomatic access to London. He was none of those things. He had no job, no passport, and no plan.

**THE ABWEHR (character voice):** "Do you have any espionage training?"

**PUJOL (character voice):** "No, but I have enormous enthusiasm."

**THE ABWEHR (character voice):** "Perfect. Here is six hundred pounds."

**[VISUAL: A little spy starter kit laid out on a table. Invisible ink, a codebook, cover addresses, a questionnaire, a stack of cash.]**

**NARRATOR:** They gave him a crash course, invisible ink, a codebook, cover addresses, a questionnaire, and roughly six hundred pounds. They codenamed him ARABEL and sent him to Britain to build a spy ring.

**[VISUAL: A map. An arrow leaves Madrid, heads toward Britain, then hard-turns and stops in Lisbon with a little screech mark.]**

**NARRATOR:** He did not go to Britain. He went to Lisbon, sat down, and on the nineteenth of July signalled Madrid that he had arrived safely in Great Britain.

**[VISUAL: Pujol on a Lisbon balcony in the sun, sipping coffee, writing a report about London fog.]**

**NARRATOR:** He was in a different country, in nice weather. For the next nine months he reported on a nation he had never visited.

**GERMANY (character voice):** "Any trouble getting in?"

**PUJOL (character voice):** "None at all. Very foggy here."

**NARRATOR:** If you are going to invent an entire country, you need research materials.

## ACT 3: BRITAIN, ACCORDING TO A GUIDEBOOK (3:00 - 4:25)

**[VISUAL: Three objects on a desk, lit like holy relics. A Blue Guide tourist book, a map of England, a railway timetable with a torn corner.]**

**NARRATOR:** In nineteen forty three, MI5 wrote an internal memo naming three of the sources he used. A Blue Guide tourist book. A map of England. And an out of date railway timetable.

**[VISUAL: The three objects again, now with a Nazi eagle stamp slamming down on them marked "TRUSTED SOURCE."]**

**NARRATOR:** That is what the Third Reich was buying. A guidebook, a map, and a train schedule that was already wrong.

**[VISUAL: Pujol flipping the timetable open, squinting, then confidently typing troop movements.]**

**NARRATOR:** The out of date part is my favourite. He was not even lying with current information.

**[VISUAL: A cartoon Glasgow dockworker being handed a bottle of wine by a delighted German officer. Rain. Seagulls. Confusion.]**

**NARRATOR:** His imaginary Glasgow informant reported that the dock workers there would do anything for a litre of wine. Wine. In Glasgow. In nineteen forty one.

**GERMANY (character voice):** "Astonishing detail. This man is inside their culture."

**NARRATOR:** The Germans believed it, because they knew even less about Britain than he did.

**[VISUAL: A fictional convoy drawn in pencil, sailing off a page from Liverpool toward Malta.]**

**NARRATOR:** Then he invented a convoy. He reported ships sailing from Liverpool to Malta. They did not exist.

**[VISUAL: Real German aircraft taking off, real intercept orders going out, planes sweeping empty ocean and photographing Valletta harbour.]**

**NARRATOR:** German units were ordered to intercept it. German aircraft flew reconnaissance of the route and of Valletta harbour. An air force went hunting for ships that had never been built.

**[VISUAL: Bletchley Park. Codebreakers reading decrypted Abwehr traffic, faces slowly falling.]**

**NARRATOR:** And that is how the British found him. Bletchley Park was reading German signals, and in February nineteen forty two they noticed something alarming. Germany had an agent inside Britain feeding back nonsense, and acting on it.

**MI5 (character voice):** "Who is this man?"

**NARRATOR:** They had turned him away three times.

## ACT 4: THE MEN WHO WERE NOT THERE (4:25 - 6:45)

**[VISUAL: Araceli and Pujol walking into the US Embassy in Lisbon and being taken seriously by a naval officer.]**

**NARRATOR:** What cracked it open was the Americans. In early nineteen forty two, Pujol and Araceli walked into the United States naval attache's office in Lisbon. Lieutenant Patrick Demorest listened, believed them, and told the British.

**[VISUAL: Pujol stepping off a military aircraft in England, met by two men in overcoats.]**

**NARRATOR:** On the twenty fourth of April nineteen forty two, he arrived in Britain for the first time. MI5 codenamed him BOVRIL. After the beef extract.

**[VISUAL: A jar of Bovril with a spy hat on it. It is crossed out and replaced with a glamorous film-star silhouette.]**

**NARRATOR:** Someone overruled that and renamed him GARBO, after Greta Garbo, because they thought he was the best actor in the world. To the Germans he stayed ARABEL.

**[VISUAL: A semi-detached suburban house in Hendon, curtains drawn, chimney smoking politely.]**

**NARRATOR:** He was paired with an MI5 case officer named Tomas Harris, a wealthy London art dealer who spoke fluent Spanish. Germany's crown jewel spy network was now written by a Spaniard and an art dealer in a suburban house in Hendon.

**[VISUAL: A wall chart filling up with portrait frames of invented agents, each drawn in ghostly outline.]**

**NARRATOR:** Together they grew the fiction. The network is commonly counted at twenty seven agents, though historians still argue where you draw the line, because some of the fake agents had their own fake agents.

**[VISUAL: Ghost portraits pop up one at a time. A Gibraltarian waiter. A Welsh ex-seaman. A Venezuelan student in Glasgow.]**

**NARRATOR:** There was a Gibraltarian canteen waiter who hated Britain. Separately, an underground Welsh fascist group who thought they were helping the Reich. And a Venezuelan in Glasgow with a man in the Royal Air Force feeding him gossip.

**[VISUAL: Araceli at a kitchen table writing biographies, giving the ghosts names, jobs, and grudges.]**

**NARRATOR:** Araceli helped write their life stories. The Reich's best source inside Britain was a cast of characters invented at a kitchen table.

**[VISUAL: November 1942. Real convoys sailing from Liverpool. A ghost agent in Bootle looking panicked.]**

**NARRATOR:** Then a problem. In November nineteen forty two, real convoys sailed from Liverpool for Operation Torch, and Agent Number Two, a notional businessman named William Maximilian Gerbers, was supposedly standing right there. He could not plausibly have missed them.

**[VISUAL: The ghost of Gerbers being tucked into a bed and then quietly fading out. A small obituary in a newspaper column.]**

**NARRATOR:** So they killed him. They wrote him as gravely ill, then dead, and placed a death notice in a local Liverpool paper, in November nineteen forty two, for a man who had never been born.

**[VISUAL: Germans writing a condolence card and mailing a pension cheque to an empty chair.]**

**NARRATOR:** The Germans sent condolences. Then they agreed to pay a pension to his widow. Who also did not exist. Who they then recruited as an agent.

**GERMANY (character voice):** "She has been so brave. Sign her up."

**NARRATOR:** And while all of this was going brilliantly, it was quietly destroying somebody real.

---

## ACT 5: WHAT IT COST ARACELI (6:45 - 7:45)

**[VISUAL: Muted palette. Araceli alone in the Hendon house, coat on, suitcase by the door, rain on the window.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** Araceli Gonzalez Carballo secured the interview that started the whole case. She approached the Americans. She wrote the biographies of the fictional agents. Her husband's memoir left her out.

**[VISUAL: Araceli at a table, staring at a telephone. Nothing cartoonish in this shot.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** She was isolated in a country she did not want to live in. By late June nineteen forty three she threatened to walk into the Spanish Embassy and expose everything, simply so she could go home.

**[VISUAL: A staged arrest. Pujol led away in handcuffs. A grey interrogation camp gate closing behind him.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** MI5 responded by staging her husband's arrest and detention at Camp 020, their interrogation centre. She was allowed to believe it was real.

**[VISUAL: A closed kitchen door. A gas tap. Hold on the door. Nothing else.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** She attempted suicide by gas. She survived. Afterwards she signed a statement promising never to interfere with the operation again.

**[VISUAL: Her signature on a typed document, filed into a folder, the folder closing.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** MI5's internal descriptions of her are contemptuous. This is the part of the story nobody puts in the trailer.

**[VISUAL: Slow fade from the closed folder to a map of the English Channel, spring 1944.]**

**NARRATOR:** And it was all in service of one thing, which was still a year away.

---

## ACT 6: THE ARMY THAT DID NOT EXIST (7:45 - 11:00)

**[VISUAL: A huge map of southeast England covered in fake unit markers, tents, and inflatable tanks near Dover.]**

**NARRATOR:** Operation Fortitude South. The Allies invented an entire army group, the First United States Army Group, and parked it in southeast England pointing at the Pas de Calais.

**[VISUAL: A cardboard cutout of General Patton being propped up in front of the fake army.]**

**NARRATOR:** In May nineteen forty four, GARBO and another double agent, a Polish officer codenamed BRUTUS, told their handlers that Patton commanded it. The Germans rated Patton the best general the Allies had.

**[VISUAL: A radio set in Hendon transmitting, a counter ticking upward past five hundred.]**

**NARRATOR:** Between January nineteen forty four and D-Day he sent over five hundred radio messages to Madrid, sometimes four a day, forwarded straight on to Berlin.

**[VISUAL: Night of 5 June. A German radio operator in Madrid yawning, switching off his set, and going to bed.]**

**NARRATOR:** MI5 arranged for German operators to stay up all night on the fifth of June, because a sub-agent was supposedly arriving with urgent news.

**[VISUAL: 03:00. GARBO transmitting frantically. On the German end, an empty chair and a dark room.]**

**NARRATOR:** At three in the morning on the sixth of June, GARBO transmitted the warning that the invasion was coming. It was deliberately timed to arrive too late to be useful. Nobody received it. The Germans had gone off air at half past eleven.

**[VISUAL: Troops at Southampton being handed embarkation kits. A close-up on a paper vomit bag.]**

**NARRATOR:** The message included the detail that troops at Southampton had been issued vomit bags. History's most important warning mentioned sick bags, and nobody was awake to read it.

**[VISUAL: TONE SHIFT. Muted. Landing craft ramps dropping on a grey beach. No cartoon exaggeration.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** On the sixth of June nineteen forty four, at least four thousand four hundred and fourteen Allied troops were confirmed killed. Two thousand five hundred and one of them were American. Total Allied casualties exceeded ten thousand three hundred. Roughly two thousand four hundred of those casualties were at Omaha alone.

**[VISUAL: Quiet shot. Men moving up the sand. No music sting. No joke card.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** None of those men knew that a Spaniard in a north London house, and a network of people who did not exist, had anything to do with whether German tanks reached them.

**[VISUAL: 9 June. GARBO at the radio, Harris beside him, drafting the most important message of the war.]**

**NARRATOR:** On the ninth of June, GARBO sent the message everything had been built for. He argued that Normandy was a large-scale diversionary operation to pull German reserves away, and that the real invasion would land at the Pas de Calais.

**[VISUAL: A German staff map with about seventy five division counters stacked on Britain, next to a smaller true stack of about fifty.]**

**NARRATOR:** He claimed roughly seventy five Allied divisions were sitting in Britain. The real number was closer to fifty. He asked that it be passed urgently to the German High Command.

**[VISUAL: 10 June. A telephone ringing in a German headquarters. A hand cancelling an order on a map.]**

**NARRATOR:** On the tenth of June, the order sending the First SS Panzer Division and a panzer regiment of the 116th toward Normandy was cancelled. The armour stayed north of the Seine.

**[VISUAL: Roughly twenty division markers sitting idle around Calais, waiting for an invasion that never comes.]**

**NARRATOR:** For about two months, more than twenty German divisions sat in the Pas de Calais waiting for an attack that never came.

**[VISUAL: A historian's bookshelf, one book pulled forward, a raised eyebrow.]**

**NARRATOR:** Now a caveat, because accuracy is the whole point of this channel. Historians including Mary Kathryn Barbier argue this gets oversold. The Germans already expected the Pas de Calais, and Allied bombing had wrecked French rail and road links so badly the Fifteenth Army might not have moved in time anyway.

**HISTORY (character voice):** "He did not create the German delusion."

**NARRATOR:** He was the loudest and most trusted voice in an argument the Germans were already having with themselves. Eighty six messages in high-level German intelligence summaries came from him, more than from any other agent. Which brings us to the medals.

## ACT 7: THE PRICE OF SOUTH LONDON (11:00 - 11:55)

**[VISUAL: A V-1 flying bomb crossing a grey London sky. Muted colors. No cartoon faces.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** From June nineteen forty four, Germany fired V-1 flying bombs at London. Ten thousand four hundred and ninety two of them over about ten months, causing twenty four thousand one hundred and sixty five casualties.

**[VISUAL: German plotters marking false impact points on a map, shifting the aim short of central London.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** Germany asked GARBO to report where the bombs were landing. The false reports fed back pushed the barrage short, away from central London and onto the southern suburbs.

**[VISUAL: A quiet residential street in Croydon. Damaged rooftops. No narration gag.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** Croydon and neighbouring south London boroughs took the fall. In Croydon alone about fifty four thousand houses were damaged and more than two hundred people were killed. People died in those streets partly because the deception worked.

**[VISUAL: Herbert Morrison at a desk, objecting. He is overruled. The document is signed anyway.]**

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** The Home Secretary, Herbert Morrison, a south Londoner himself, objected to what he saw as playing God with which citizens lived and died. He was overruled.

**[VISUAL: Return to color, gently. A radio message arriving in Hendon in July 1944.]**

**NARRATOR:** And then, in the middle of all of it, Berlin sent him a present.

---

## ACT 8: TWO MEDALS AND A FAKE GRAVE (11:55 - 13:25)

**[VISUAL: An Iron Cross materialising out of a radio speaker as dots and dashes.]**

**NARRATOR:** On the twenty ninth of July nineteen forty four, Berlin informed GARBO by radio that Adolf Hitler had personally authorised an Iron Cross Second Class for him, for extraordinary services.

**[VISUAL: Pujol in an MI5 office, holding a piece of paper, surrounded by British officers trying not to laugh.]**

**NARRATOR:** A decoration normally reserved for men under fire, authorised by Hitler, conveyed over the wireless, to a man in London working for MI5. He received the actual medal only after the war.

**[VISUAL: December 1944. A small private room. Sir David Petrie handing over an MBE with no crowd and no ceremony.]**

**NARRATOR:** In December nineteen forty four, Britain appointed him a Member of the Order of the British Empire. There was no royal investiture, because a public ceremony would have destroyed his cover. MI5's Director General, Sir David Petrie, handed it over privately.

**[VISUAL: Two medals in a drawer, side by side, the drawer sliding shut.]**

**NARRATOR:** Two medals from two enemies, both kept secret. He is one of the very few people, and quite possibly the only one, honoured by both Britain and Nazi Germany in the same war.

**[VISUAL: 1949. A telegram from Angola reporting a death from malaria. A coffin nobody opens.]**

**NARRATOR:** After the war he feared revenge from surviving Nazis. So he did the one trick he was genuinely world class at. In nineteen forty nine he went to Angola and faked his own death from malaria.

**[VISUAL: A quiet street in Venezuela. Pujol, older, walking home with a bag of shopping, entirely unremarkable.]**

**NARRATOR:** He then lived quietly in Venezuela for thirty five years while the intelligence world believed him buried in Africa.

**[VISUAL: 1984. A drawing room in a London club. Elderly officers standing as a supposedly dead man walks in.]**

**NARRATOR:** In nineteen eighty four a historian tracked him down. He came to London, and a room of retired intelligence officers gathered at the Special Forces Club to greet a man they had been told was dead.

**TONE: STRAIGHT**

**NARRATOR:** On the sixth of June nineteen eighty four, the fortieth anniversary, he went to Normandy, walked the beaches, and paid his respects to the dead. He died in Caracas in nineteen eighty eight, aged seventy six.

**[VISUAL: End card. Subscribe button. Two thumbnails for upcoming episodes.]**

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Voice choices:**
- Cold open is the pitch's typewriter image, played literally. Ghost sub-agents puffing into existence is the visual spine of the episode and should recur whenever the network is mentioned.
- Fake dialogue used for THE ABWEHR, GERMANY, BRITAIN, MI5, PUJOL and one HISTORY line. The HISTORY line is deliberate: the Barbier caveat lands better in voice than as narration.
- Second person "you" used sparingly, in the research-materials beat.
- Five straight sections, all tagged: Spanish Civil War, Araceli, D-Day casualties, V-1 aim-shifting, the 1984 Normandy visit. No jokes inside any of them. The Araceli act carries no character-voice line at all, on purpose.

**Accuracy corrections baked in (do not undo):**
- Agent No. 2, William Maximilian Gerbers, is the notional agent killed off. There is no "agent 14."
- The Liverpool newspaper is never named.
- Network size given as twenty seven with an explicit nod that the count is disputed.
- Iron Cross Second Class, 29 July 1944, by radio, physical medal only after the war.
- MBE in December 1944, presented privately by Sir David Petrie, not a royal investiture.
- "One of the very few, and quite possibly the only one" replaces "the only person decorated by both sides."
- The phantom Malta convoy is hunted by German units and German aircraft. No Royal Navy sortie.
- Lisbon reference material is given as three named items from the memo, not as an exhaustive list: Blue Guide, map of England, out of date railway timetable. No vocabulary of military terms.
- ARABEL is the German codename, GARBO the British one, BOVRIL first.
- The Gibraltarian waiter and the Welsh fascist group are stated as separate fictions. The RAF man's rank is deliberately not stated, because sources disagree (non-commissioned officer in some, Pilot Officer in others). No Wren in the War Office.
- Barbier's pushback is in the script, in voice, not as a footnote.
- No number is placed on lives saved anywhere in the episode.

**Title options:**
1. `Hitler Gave This Fake Spy A Medal | Codename History` (recommended, the medal is the most improbable true fact in the story)
2. `The Spy Who Invented 27 People | Codename History`
3. `He Fooled Germany With A Tourist Guidebook | Codename History`

**Placement in the channel:** Strong standalone. Pairs naturally with an Operation Mincemeat episode (teased in the outro) and with a Double Cross System overview. If the channel builds a deception playlist, this is the anchor.

## FACT-CHECK LOG

- **Birth, Barcelona, 1912.** Conflict exists (1914 in Wikipedia and the Spanish Biographical Dictionary) due to a registration discrepancy. Headstone and most sources give 1912. USED 1912.
- **Royal Poultry School, failed poultry farm, failed hotel.** Confirmed (National WWII Museum, Spartacus, IWM).
- **Spanish Civil War, both sides, no bullet fired, mother and sister arrested, escape on forged papers.** Confirmed. Played straight.
- **Three British rejections from January 1941, one via Araceli.** Confirmed. Exact dates of approaches two and three NOT FOUND, so no dates given on screen.
- **Abwehr recruitment 1941, Knappe-Ratey "Federico," codename ARABEL, roughly 600 pounds, ink, codebook, questionnaire.** Confirmed.
- **19 July 1941 signal claiming arrival in Britain while still in Lisbon.** Confirmed (TNA KV 2/40 opens 15 July 1941, consistent).
- **Blue Guide, map of England, out of date railway timetable.** Confirmed via the MI5 internal memo of 12 July 1943, released 2016. No fourth item added, per the NOT FOUND on the military-vocabulary claim.
- **Glasgow "litre of wine" beat.** Confirmed as a documented notional-report absurdity.
- **Phantom Liverpool to Malta convoy, German intercept orders, German recon of the route and Valletta.** Confirmed. CORRECTION APPLIED: the reaction was German, not British. No Royal Navy sortie staged.
- **February 1942 Bletchley decrypts reveal the unknown agent.** Confirmed.
- **US naval attache's office Lisbon, Lieutenant Patrick Demorest, early 1942.** Confirmed; better attested than the "Rousseau" variant.
- **24 April 1942 arrival, BOVRIL then GARBO, Hendon.** Confirmed.
- **Tomas Harris, MI5 B1A, art dealer, fluent Spanish.** Confirmed. Sources calling him MI6 are wrong.
- **Network size 27.** USED with an explicit "historians still argue" hedge. MI5 says 28, National WWII Museum 26, HistoryNet 30 including sub-sub-agents. Not averaged.
- **Gibraltarian NAAFI waiter and Welsh fascist group as separate fictions, plus an unranked RAF sub-agent.** Waiter and group confirmed and kept distinct. Rank NOT FOUND with agreement across sources, so no rank is stated on screen.
- **Araceli's authorship of the agent biographies and her Lisbon approach.** Confirmed (Harris's own report). Memoir omission confirmed.
- **November 1942 TORCH convoys, Agent No. 2 William Maximilian Gerbers written ill then dead, death notice in a local Liverpool paper, German condolences, pension to the notional widow, widow recruited.** Confirmed (Hubbard-Hall citing TNA KV 2/41; MI5). Newspaper deliberately not named. NOT "agent 14."
- **Araceli, late June 1943: threat to expose, staged Camp 020 arrest, suicide attempt by gas, signed statement.** Confirmed (Hubbard-Hall citing Harris's report, TNA KV 2/42). Played completely straight.
- **Fortitude South, FUSAG, Pas de Calais, Patton named by GARBO and BRUTUS in May 1944.** Confirmed. Fortitude North and Norway deliberately not credited to him.
- **Over 500 radio messages January 1944 to D-Day, up to four a day.** Confirmed (MI5 official history). Unsourced lifetime totals of "1,000 plus" deliberately not used.
- **Night of 5/6 June listening watch, 03:00 warning engineered to be too late, Madrid off air from 23:30, vomit bags detail.** Confirmed (MI5 official history).
- **D-Day casualties: at least 4,414 Allied killed, 2,501 American, over 10,300 total casualties, roughly 2,400 of those casualties at Omaha.** Confirmed (National WWII Museum). Played straight, never as a punchline.
- **9 June message: "large-scale diversionary operation," FUSAG and Pas de Calais argument, about 75 divisions claimed against about 50 actual, urgent request to the High Command.** Confirmed in substance. Exact verbatim text NOT FOUND, so paraphrased.
- **10 June cancellation of the 1st SS Panzer and 116th Panzer regiment movement, armour held north of the Seine.** Confirmed in substance. The specific 07:30 Keitel call time is single-sourced, so no time is stated on screen.
- **More than 20 divisions held in the Pas de Calais for about two months.** Confirmed (MI5, English Heritage, IWM).
- **Barbier caveat.** Confirmed as published historiographical pushback, included in voice.
- **86 GARBO messages in high-level German intelligence summaries, the highest of any D-Day double agent.** Confirmed (National WWII Museum).
- **V-1s: 10,492 launched, 24,165 casualties, barrage pushed short onto Croydon and neighbouring south London boroughs, about 54,000 Croydon houses damaged, over 200 killed (English Heritage; Forces War Records), Herbert Morrison objected and was overruled.** Confirmed (IWM for the V-1 figures). Played completely straight.
- **Iron Cross Second Class, 29 July 1944, personally authorised by Hitler, conveyed by radio, physical medal after the war.** Confirmed (MI5). HistoryNet's "First Class" is wrong and was not used.
- **MBE, December 1944, presented privately by Sir David Petrie.** Confirmed (MI5, English Heritage). Wikipedia's 25 November 1944 and King George VI version not used.
- **"Decorated by both sides."** Softened to "one of the very few, and quite possibly the only one," per the unprovable-universal-negative correction.
- **Faked death in Angola 1949, 35 years in Venezuela.** Confirmed. Occupation in Venezuela left unstated because sources conflict (bookshop and gift shop versus Shell language teacher).
- **1984 rediscovery, Special Forces Club reunion, 6 June 1984 Normandy visit.** Confirmed.
- **Death 10 October 1988 in Caracas, aged 76.** Confirmed against a 1912 birth.
- **Lives saved.** NOT FOUND and unquantifiable. No figure appears anywhere in the script.
