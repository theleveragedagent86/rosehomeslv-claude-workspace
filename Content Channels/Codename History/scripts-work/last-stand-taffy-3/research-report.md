# Research Report: The Last Stand of Taffy 3 (Battle off Samar, 25 October 1944)

## One-paragraph summary

On the morning of 25 October 1944, off the island of Samar in the Philippines, the largest surface battle fleet the Japanese Navy could still muster, the Center Force under Vice Admiral Takeo Kurita, blundered out of the San Bernardino Strait and straight onto a tiny American task unit code-named Taffy 3. Taffy 3 was six thin-skinned escort carriers screened by three destroyers and four destroyer escorts, built to hunt submarines and support the Leyte invasion beaches, not to fight battleships. Kurita's force had four battleships including the Yamato, the largest battleship ever built, plus heavy and light cruisers and destroyers. Rather than run, Taffy 3's "small boys" turned and charged. The destroyers and destroyer escorts made suicidal torpedo runs, laid smoke, and traded gunfire with cruisers and battleships while the escort carriers launched every plane they had. Through relentless aggression, air attack, and Kurita's own confusion about who he was actually fighting, the Japanese broke off and retreated when they were on the verge of victory. The cost was severe: the destroyers Johnston and Hoel, the destroyer escort Samuel B. Roberts, and the escort carrier Gambier Bay were sunk, the escort carrier St. Lo was sunk later that morning by a kamikaze, and more than a thousand American sailors were killed, many dying in the water over the days before rescue.

## Verified timeline (25 October 1944, all times local)

- ~03:00 - Kurita's Center Force passes through San Bernardino Strait and steams south along the Samar coast, undetected. [NHHC; Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- 06:37 - A Taffy 3 patrol pilot and lookouts sight the Japanese force; Ensign William Brooks reports pagoda masts and battleships. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; NHHC]
- ~06:45 - Battle begins; Japanese open fire from long range. Yamato's first salvos straddle the escort carriers. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- ~07:00 - Sprague orders the escort carriers to flee, launch aircraft, and make smoke; orders the "small boys" (destroyers and destroyer escorts) to counterattack. [NHHC; Badass of the Week / Warfare History Network corroboration]
- ~07:10-07:20 - USS Johnston (Cdr Ernest Evans) charges first, opens fire on heavy cruiser Kumano, then launches all ten torpedoes; one blows off Kumano's bow. [Wikipedia "USS Johnston"; NHHC]
- ~07:20-08:00 - Hoel, Heermann, and Samuel B. Roberts join the torpedo attacks against battleships and cruisers; heavy Japanese ships turn and maneuver to comb torpedo wakes, losing formation. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- ~08:55 - USS Hoel sinks after 40-plus shell hits. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- ~09:07-09:11 - USS Gambier Bay capsizes and sinks, the first US escort carrier lost to surface gunfire. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; NHHC]
- ~09:20 - Kurita signals a turn to the north; the Center Force begins to break off. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- ~09:35-10:00 - USS Samuel B. Roberts sinks. USS Johnston, dead in the water and surrounded, is abandoned (~09:45) and rolls over and sinks (~10:00-10:10). [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; Wikipedia "USS Johnston"]
- ~10:50-11:05 - Escort-carrier aircraft sink or cripple Japanese heavy cruisers Suzuya and Chikuma; Chokai had already been disabled and scuttled. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- ~11:00 - A kamikaze strikes USS St. Lo; she explodes and sinks about half an hour later, the first US ship sunk by a kamikaze attack. [National WWII Museum; Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- 27 October - Surviving sailors, adrift up to ~2 days, are finally rescued. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; National WWII Museum]

## Key figures

- **Rear Admiral Clifton A. F. "Ziggy" Sprague** - commander of Taffy 3 (Task Unit 77.4.3). Ordered the escort carriers to flee and make smoke and the screen to counterattack. [NHHC; Wikipedia "Clifton Sprague"]
- **Vice Admiral Takeo Kurita** - commander of the Japanese Center Force, aboard Yamato. Broke off and retreated despite being on the verge of victory. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; NHHC]
- **Commander Ernest E. Evans**, USS Johnston (DD-557) - born 13 August 1908 in Pawnee, Oklahoma; of Cherokee and Creek (Muscogee) heritage; nicknamed "Big Chief" at the Naval Academy. Led the first destroyer charge. Killed / lost with his ship. Posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor; commonly described as the first Native American in the US Navy to receive it. [Wikipedia "Ernest E. Evans"; The Hill; War History Online]
- **Lieutenant Commander Robert W. Copeland**, USS Samuel B. Roberts (DE-413) - told his crew over the loudspeaker that they faced overwhelming odds from which survival could not be expected. Survived. [Wikipedia "Robert W. Copeland"; USS Samuel B. Roberts action report via navybook.com]
- **Commander William D. Thomas**, USS Hoel (DD-533) - led Hoel's torpedo attacks; survived. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]

## Numbers (high-risk, sourced)

**Taffy 3 (Task Unit 77.4.3):** 6 escort carriers, 3 destroyers, 4 destroyer escorts. [NHHC; National WWII Museum; Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- Escort carriers (CVE): Fanshaw Bay (flag), St. Lo, White Plains, Kalinin Bay, Kitkun Bay, Gambier Bay.
- Destroyers (DD): Johnston, Hoel, Heermann.
- Destroyer escorts (DE): Dennis, John C. Butler, Raymond, Samuel B. Roberts.

**Japanese Center Force:** 4 battleships (Yamato, Nagato, Kongo, Haruna), 6 heavy cruisers (Chokai, Haguro, Kumano, Suzuya, Chikuma, Tone), 2 light cruisers (Yahagi, Noshiro), and 11 destroyers. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; Warfare History Network] (The National WWII Museum's short summary rounds this to "four battleships, eight cruisers, and twelve destroyers"; the more detailed order of battle gives 8 cruisers total = 6 heavy + 2 light, and 11 destroyers. Note the minor destroyer-count discrepancy: 11 vs 12.)

**Yamato:** largest battleship ever built; 18.1-inch (46 cm) main guns. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; National WWII Museum]

**US losses (ships sunk):** USS Johnston (DD-557), USS Hoel (DD-533), USS Samuel B. Roberts (DE-413), USS Gambier Bay (CVE-73), and USS St. Lo (CVE-63, kamikaze). [NHHC; Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; National WWII Museum] USS Heermann was the only screening destroyer to survive.

**US casualties (Taffy 3 total):** approximately 1,161 killed and missing and 913 wounded, roughly 2,074 total. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- USS Johnston: complement ~327; about 186 killed, about 141 survived (survivors ~50 hours in the water). [Wikipedia "USS Johnston"; NHHC]
- USS Hoel: about 253 killed. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- USS Samuel B. Roberts: about 90 killed. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- USS Gambier Bay: about 147 killed. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- USS St. Lo: about 113 killed (of a crew of ~889). [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; National WWII Museum]

**Japanese losses:** three heavy cruisers sunk in the action or its immediate aftermath, Chokai, Chikuma, and Suzuya. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"] Kumano's bow was blown off by a Johnston torpedo and she was crippled. [Wikipedia "USS Johnston"; Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]

**Wreck depths (modern corroboration):**
- USS Johnston: identified 31 March 2021 at about 21,180 feet (~6,460 m); the deepest shipwreck surveyed at the time. [Wikipedia "USS Johnston"; Smithsonian Magazine]
- USS Samuel B. Roberts: identified June 2022 at about 22,620 feet (~6,890 m); the deepest shipwreck ever identified. [Wikipedia "USS Samuel B. Roberts"; UPI; Washington Post; USNI Naval History]

## Absurd / comedic beats (all real)

- Taffy 3 was built for anti-submarine and ground-support work. Its "screen" was destroyers and destroyer escorts, small ships, going up against the biggest battleship on earth. The mismatch itself is the joke. [NHHC]
- Sprague's own after-action reaction to survival, and Nimitz's line that "nothing short of special dispensation from the Lord Almighty" explained it, underline how absurd the outcome was. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- Kurita, holding a winning hand, talked himself into believing he was fighting Halsey's fleet carriers because the escort carriers ran and the tiny escorts attacked so ferociously. He turned away when he could have finished the job. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; NHHC]
- The rescue punchline: survivor Jack Yusen recalled a rescue ship's crew shouting "Who won the World Series?" and the men in the water yelling back "St. Louis Cardinals!" [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]

## Human-cost beats (play straight, TONE: STRAIGHT)

- USS Johnston: of ~327 aboard, about 186 killed. Evans, wounded, gave the order to abandon ship and was never recovered. [Wikipedia "USS Johnston"; NHHC]
- USS Hoel: about 253 killed. USS Samuel B. Roberts: about 90 killed. USS Gambier Bay: about 147 killed. USS St. Lo: about 113 killed. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; National WWII Museum]
- More than a thousand Taffy 3 sailors killed or missing; hundreds more wounded. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"]
- Survivors floated in shark-infested water for up to about two days before rescue on 27 October; some died awaiting rescue. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; National WWII Museum]

## Myth vs record (corrections)

- **Popular telling: individual ships "single-handedly" turned back the fleet. / Actual:** it was a combined effect: repeated torpedo runs by several ships (Johnston, Hoel, Heermann, Samuel B. Roberts), relentless air strikes and strafing from the escort carriers' aircraft, smoke, rain squalls, and Kurita's own confusion and loss of formation. No single tin can did it alone. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; NHHC]
- **Popular telling: Kurita retreated because he was beaten. / Actual:** Kurita was still numerically dominant when he turned away. He broke off partly because he mistakenly believed he faced fleet carriers and Halsey's main force, had lost tactical control after maneuvering to dodge torpedoes, and had absorbed heavy air and torpedo pressure. [Wikipedia "Battle off Samar"; NHHC]
- **Note on the destroyer count:** sources vary between 11 and 12 Japanese destroyers. The detailed order of battle gives 11; the museum's short summary says 12. Script uses "about a dozen" or "eleven" and flags the range.
