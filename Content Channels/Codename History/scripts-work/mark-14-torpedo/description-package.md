## YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION (paste-ready)

In July 1943 an American submarine found a 19,000 ton tanker stopped dead in flat water, closed to 850 yards, and fired fifteen torpedoes into it. About a dozen hit. Two exploded, and the ship did not sink.

The US Navy's standard submarine torpedo had three separate defects, and each one was hiding the next.

This is the Mark 14 and the Bureau of Ordnance, December 1941 to September 1943. The version you usually get is idiots ashore and heroes at sea. The record is more awkward than that: the German Navy had almost exactly the same three failures earlier and court martialled senior officers over it, while in the American case nobody was held responsible, and the claim that the submariners fixed all three defects themselves is called certainly an overstatement by the best analytical source on the subject. We go through it in order, the way it actually happened. The torpedo ran about ten feet too deep, so the magnetic exploder was never close enough to a hull to work. Fix the depth and the exploders started going off early. Take the magnetic feature out and the contact firing pin turned out to bend under a square hit, which meant the easiest shot in submarine warfare was the one most likely to fail. And Frenchman's Bay, where the 1942 net test happened, is at Albany in Western Australia, not Maine. And the episode ends with USS Tang, lost in October 1944 to her own circling torpedo, which was a Mark 18 electric and not a Mark 14. Circular running was a flaw both marks shared, and it was never completely solved.

## PACKAGE NOTES

- The hook is two lines and both sit above the "...more" fold. Line one is the Tinosa attack as the cold open plays it, which is the curiosity gap. Line two is the payoff, three defects each hiding the next, which is the episode's structural argument rather than a tease.
- **No total for wasted or dud torpedoes appears anywhere in this file, and none is implied.** The script asserts no total because no defensible number exists, and the description does not smuggle one in by arithmetic. The "over eight hundred torpedoes fired" figure from Act 2 was deliberately left out of the description, because outside the narration's explicit "fired, not wasted" framing it reads as a waste total.
- **Tinosa's numbers are the official history's own arithmetic and nothing more.** Fifteen fired, about a dozen hit, two explosions, ship not sunk. No dud count. The tanker is not named, matching the script.
- **Tang is handled exactly as the script handles it and no tighter.** The description states the loss, states the weapon was a Mark 18 electric and not a Mark 14, and gives the connective thread as the shared circular running flaw that was never completely solved. **The Mark 14 must never be shown or implied as Tang's killer**, in the description, the title, the thumbnail or the pinned comment. The chapter title for the outro is "The flaw both marks shared" for the same reason.
- **No count of German officers court-martialled.** The description says senior officers, plural, matching the script, because the number is contested between sources.
- Milford's "almost exactly the same three failures" keeps its "almost". The German parallel is stated as a parallel and the contrast is the American case, where nobody was held responsible.
- **Frenchman's Bay is placed in Australia in the description as well as the script**, because that is the single fastest tell between a researched retelling and a copied one, and description text gets scraped and quoted more than narration does.
- The 70 percent drop-test figure and the 90 foot drop height are in the script but are left out of the description, not because they are wrong, but because they need the physics sentence next to them to be safe from the 900 foot OCR error in circulation. They belong on screen with their justification.
- No individual is named as the villain. Christie, Withers, English and Blandy are not named in the description any more than in the script, because Act 5 exists to refuse the single-villain reading.
- Nimitz, King, Lockwood, Jacobs and Daspit are all in the script but are not named in the description, which keeps it institutional. The 24 June 1943 date and its conflict with the official history stay on screen where the correction is stated out loud.
- SOURCES block lifted verbatim from the script's `## SOURCES (for YouTube description)`, all 18 entries and their four category headings, unedited and unreordered.
- No em-dashes anywhere in this file.
