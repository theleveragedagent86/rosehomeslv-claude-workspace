# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE TORPEDO THAT REFUSED TO EXPLODE

**Target runtime:** 9 minutes (~1,400 narration words at ~158 wpm)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake dialogue. Hard tonal drop to straight at Act 6 and it is never lifted again.
**Story type:** Single documented institutional failure, told as three sequential defects. The Mark 14 torpedo and the Bureau of Ordnance, December 1941 to September 1943, plus the complication that makes the popular version wrong.
**Title (standard tier):** `The Torpedo That Refused to Explode | Codename History`

> **ACCURACY NOTE:** The popular telling of this story is wrong in five specific ways and every correction is on screen. **Do not re-simplify any of them back into the myth because the myth is funnier.** (1) **Three defects in sequence, not one.** Depth through 1942, the magnetic exploder through mid 1943, the contact firing pin in August and September 1943. Each defect hid the next, and that is the actual reason the scandal lasted as long as it did, so the act structure carries it. (2) **Frenchman's Bay is at Albany, Western Australia**, not Maine, and the narration says the town, the state and the country out loud. (3) **Nimitz ordered the magnetic exploders inactivated on 24 June 1943.** The Navy's own official ordnance history dates it July 24, which cannot be right, because the order has to precede the Tinosa attack of 24 July. The script names the disagreement instead of papering over it. (4) **The drop test was from 90 feet, not 900.** The 900 figure is an OCR error in the transcription of the official history; 90 feet produces an impact speed matching the Mark 14's 46 knots. (5) **Tang was sunk by a Mark 18 electric torpedo, not a Mark 14**, and the narration says so in words. Tang is framed as the same unfixed hazard shipped twice, never as the Mark 14's last victim. **No total for torpedoes wasted appears anywhere**, because no defensible number exists; the only figure used is NHHC's over 800 fired by mid 1942. **No single villain is named**, because the record does not support one: the German Navy had almost exactly the same three failures earlier and court martialled senior officers over it, and the "the submariners fixed it all with no help from Washington" version is called certainly an overstatement by the best analytical source on the subject. Both of those are in the episode. **Hedged in the spoken narration and not to be stripped out:** the firing pin aluminium coming from Japanese aircraft, which is fleet story and not record ("the story in the fleet was"); the 1926 two-shot test, which contradicts the official history ("by the better account"); the Nimitz reply, which reaches us through one author quoting the exchange ("by the account that survives"); and the Tinosa hull-strike detail, which is the crew's account rather than the patrol report ("by the crew's account"). No hard dud count is asserted for Tinosa; the official history's arithmetic is used instead.
>
> **VOICE NOTE:** Deadpan sarcasm is the default through the cold open and Acts 1 to 5. The Bureau of Ordnance is the antagonist and gets fake dialogue, and every line it speaks is a comedic compression of a real, documented institutional posture, never an invented failing. The comedy is in the paperwork and the physics, never in the shooting. **It stops at Act 6 and does not resume.** Act 6 and the entire outro, including every Tang beat, are flat, slow and quiet, with no music sting and no lift at the end of a line.

**Voice choices**
- Deadpan sarcasm carries the cold open and Acts 1 to 5. The comedy is in the paperwork and the physics. Nothing funny sits on a shot being fired at a real ship.
- **The Bureau of Ordnance is the antagonist and speaks three times**, at scenes 21, 25 and 56. Each line is a comedic compression of a documented institutional posture and none invents a failing: the secrecy rationale (Rowland and Boyd), the 1938 "poor maintenance or rough handling" dismissal (Rowland and Boyd, near-verbatim), and the 1943 arming-distance-plus-conditions proposal that King rejected (Bowling). The fourth Bureau voice, at scene 45, is the documented objection that nets in a current do not hang straight (Rowland and Boyd, Milford). **NOT FOUND, and therefore never spoken:** any dated BuOrd document telling submariners they were aiming wrong. The blame posture is carried by narration and by the sand beat instead of by an invented quotation.
- Second person and direct address: "Take a second." "There is your seventy percent failure rate." "You are already ahead of me."
- **Forward reference seeded and paid off.** Three cold-open images return: the crosshair, the bouncing torpedo and the still-floating tanker. The audience does not learn until Act 4 that the cold open was the third defect, not the first, which is the whole structural argument of the episode.
- **One tonal drop, at Act 6, and it is never lifted.** Act 6 and the entire outro, Tang included, are flat and quiet. No music sting from "A number of submarine skippers were relieved of command" to "[END]". The teaser line at the very end is read plainly, not brightly.
- **Comedy density check** on the non-straight material (cold open and Acts 1 to 5, which is 1,190 narration words, about seven minutes thirty two seconds): **50 jokes, roasts or sarcastic asides**, which is one every 9.0 seconds. Well inside the every-15-to-20-second bar. Act 6 and the outro total 215 words, about one minute twenty two seconds, and contain none.

**Accuracy corrections honored**
1. **Three defects in sequence, and the structure carries it.** Act 2 is depth, Act 3 is the magnetic exploder, Act 4 is the contact pistol, and each act ends by handing off to the next: "defect two finally had room to appear", "Now the contact pistol had to work", "Each had been hiding the one behind it." That sequencing is the reason the scandal lasted, and it is the episode's spine rather than a detail.
2. **Frenchman's Bay is placed in Australia, twice, in adjacent beats.** Scene 40 puts Lockwood in Australia with Albany on the map, scene 42 says "Frenchman's Bay at Albany, Western Australia. Not Maine. The far side of the planet." The correction is deliberate and audible.
3. **No total for torpedoes wasted.** The only quantity used is NHHC's "over eight hundred torpedoes" fired by Pacific Fleet submarines by mid 1942, and the narration calls that fired, not wasted.
4. **The Nimitz date conflict is named, not averaged.** The order is dated 24 June 1943, and the following beat says the Navy's own official history dates it a month late. The script does not say July 24 was chosen to coincide with Tinosa, because that is the coincidence that makes the official history's date implausible, and stating it would give the error a foothold.
5. **Duration is twenty one months**, December 1941 to September 1943. "Twenty months" from the pitch is not used anywhere.
6. **The drop test is 90 feet**, with the physics stated as the reason it is the right figure: "Ninety feet. That impact matches the Mark Fourteen's forty six knots almost exactly." The 900 foot OCR error is not repeated.
7. **The 1926 test is the two-shot version, hedged on screen.** "By the better account the first ran underneath and did nothing at all." This is the version that disagrees with the Navy's own official history, which is why it carries the hedge.
8. **The firing pin material is Tier 1; its provenance is fleet story and is labelled as such.** "The fix was a lighter firing pin, machined from aluminum instead of steel" is asserted. "The story in the fleet was the aluminum came from downed Japanese planes" is attributed and never stated as record. Hickam Field and the melted-engine version do not appear.
9. **Tinosa uses the official history's arithmetic and no dud count.** "Fifteen torpedoes. About a dozen hits. Two explosions. The ship did not sink." 14. **Cavite was cut for length** and no figure for torpedoes lost there is asserted.
15. **The Southwest Pacific holdout is not in the episode.** The date is an unresolved four-way conflict and the beat needed Christie by name to work, which point 13 rules out.
