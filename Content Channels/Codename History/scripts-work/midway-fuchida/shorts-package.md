## 1. fuchida-didnt-fly.mp4 (~40 s, 101 narration words)
**Title:** The Most Quoted Eyewitness To Midway Never Took Off That Day
**Description:**
Commander Mitsuo Fuchida led the air attack on Pearl Harbor and sent the code words Tora, Tora, Tora. He is the reason people picture Japanese carriers packed with planes five minutes from launching at Midway. He did not fly at Midway. Days before the battle a surgeon aboard Akagi removed his appendix, and he watched the battle from Akagi's bridge, on his feet, days after abdominal surgery.

## 2. ordnance-shuffle.mp4 (~44 s, 111 narration words)
**Title:** Four Aircraft Carriers Spent 65 Minutes Changing Their Minds About Bombs
**Description:**
At 0715 on June 4th, 1942, Nagumo had 107 reserve aircraft armed to kill ships, and ordered them rearmed with land attack bombs for a second strike on Midway. There was nowhere to store the ordnance the crews took off, so it sat on the hangar floor. At 0820 a scout finally confirmed an American carrier was out there, and the whole thing was reversed. An order and then the counter order, 65 minutes, across four ships.

**Title:** The Torpedo Squadrons At Midway
**Description:**
At 0918 on June 4th, 1942, Torpedo Squadron Eight went in at the Japanese fleet with fifteen aircraft, thirty men and no fighter escort. About thirty Zeros were waiting. All fifteen were shot down and one man survived. Torpedo Six and Torpedo Three were cut apart the same way. Counting the crews who flew from the island, ninety nine men flew in the forty two torpedo planes lost that day. Three came back.

## 4. what-was-on-the-decks.mp4 (~71 s, 177 narration words)
**Title:** The Most Famous Scene At Midway Is Contradicted By The Ship's Own Logbook
**Description:**
Mitsuo Fuchida wrote that at 1020 Akagi's planes were all in position with engines warming, five minutes from airborne, caught in the most vulnerable condition possible with decks loaded with planes armed and fueled. The carriers' own air unit records, Japan's official war history and American B-17 photographs taken that morning all say no attack aircraft were on the Japanese flight decks, only fighters. Not empty. Nobody serious says empty. But more empty than occupied, and mostly fighters.

## 5. hangar-deck.mp4 (~50 s, 126 narration words)
**Title:** The Myth Says The Japanese Decks Were Packed. It Gets It Exactly Backwards.
**Description:**
The planes were in the hangars, and that is why they died. American carrier hangars had roll up screens along the sides that let the space breathe. Japanese hangars were fully enclosed with no natural ventilation, and the Japanese fueled their aircraft down there. So the hangars held fueled planes, charged gasoline lines, petrol vapor, and every bomb and torpedo the crews had swapped on and off all morning. Three minutes after Akagi was hit, her hangar began exploding.

## Cropping notes

The episode is a stills-only build at 16:9, and Shorts are 9:16. On previous episodes a center crop could be masked by motion. Here there is none. Only the very first cold-open frame has any movement in the entire episode, so any bad crop reads as a static, obviously chopped image for the full 3 to 7 seconds that frame is held.

Two risk categories, per the brief: frames with ON-SCREEN TEXT near the horizontal edges, and wide frames (maps, full-ship cutaways, side-by-side comparison cards, formations spread across the horizon). Those need regenerating natively at 9:16 rather than cropping.

**Regenerate natively at 9:16, high priority**

- **torpedo-squadrons.** The worst offender of the six. Nearly every frame is composed wide: fifteen TBDs in loose formation over an empty sea, the forward view over a torpedo bomber's nose with four enemy carriers spread across the horizon, roughly thirty Zeros converging seen from a great distance, and forty two vacant parking positions chalked on a flight deck seen from above. A center crop destroys the horizon spread, which is the entire point of every one of those images. Regenerate all of them at 9:16, tighter and taller, and keep them un-stylized and un-graded as the TONE: STRAIGHT tags require.
- **what-was-on-the-decks.** Two frames cannot survive a crop at all. The two-panel MYTH / RECORD comparison card is side by side by construction and must be re-laid out as a stacked vertical, myth on top, record below, with the ON-SCREEN TEXT re-set. The three photographic prints laid out on a lit inspection table is also a horizontal arrangement and needs restacking. The grainy high-altitude reconnaissance frame of a carrier from almost directly above is wide and should be regenerated. The magazine page with the highlighted paragraph and the "Within five minutes" text, and the stopwatch with its six labelled wedges, both carry text that will clip at the sides.
- **hangar-deck.** The full cutaway of a Japanese carrier showing both hangar decks sealed on every side is the most horizontal image in the episode and is unusable cropped. Regenerate as a vertical cutaway, or as a stacked cross-section. The paired hangar interiors, the American hangar with side screens rolled up and the enclosed Japanese hangar, are both wide interiors and should be regenerated at 9:16 so the comparison still reads.

**Regenerate selectively, most frames survive**

- **fuchida-didnt-fly.** Three frames need work: the animated cold-open deck shot (a Zero rolling the length of the deck and lifting off the bow is a horizontal action and does not fit a vertical frame), the straight-down overhead of four carriers in a loose diamond, and the painted packed-deck illustration. The Battleship Row silhouette frame carries "TORA, TORA, TORA" and needs its text re-set inside the safe area. The rest of the short is portraits and interiors, Fuchida's studio portrait, the sick bay, the officer at the bridge window, the writing desk, and all of those center-crop cleanly or even improve vertical.
- **ordnance-shuffle.** One frame must be regenerated: the hangar deck diagram seen from above with torpedo trolleys and bomb carts crossing in opposite directions, which is a wide diagram whose two flows fall outside a center crop. The rest are recoverable, but every ON-SCREEN TEXT timecode in this short, 0715, 0740 and 0820, plus the "OFF. ON. OFF AGAIN." chalkboard card, must be re-positioned into the vertical safe area, and the two SPEECH BUBBLE frames need their bubbles re-anchored so they do not clip.

**Lowest risk**

- **usni-1955.** Almost entirely portrait-shaped source material already: a journal cover, an article title page, a hardback standing on a display stand, a dust jacket author panel. All of these crop to 9:16 well or are natively taller than wide. Only the closing quotation card needs its type re-set for the narrower measure. No regeneration required beyond that card.
