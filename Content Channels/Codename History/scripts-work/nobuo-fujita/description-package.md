## YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION (paste-ready)

In September 1942 a Japanese submarine surfaced off Oregon and the crew bolted together an airplane on its own deck, in the dark, in enemy water. Twenty years later the town it bombed invited the pilot back, and he showed up carrying his family's four hundred year old sword.

This is the Lookout Air Raids, the strangest attack ever flown against the American mainland, and the fifty five year friendship that came out of it. Nobuo Fujita is the only pilot who ever bombed the mainland United States from the air, and almost everything the popular version tells you about him is wrong: the bombs were 168 pounds each, not 340, the submarine's commander was Meiji Tagami, not "Akiji," he was a Warrant Flying Officer in a purpose built reconnaissance floatplane, not a fighter ace in a modified Zero, and the raid did not happen once, he flew twice. We go through the record, the sources, and the parts that are not funny at all.

## PACKAGE NOTES

- Hook carries both halves of the curiosity gap: the absurdity (a submarine that assembles an aircraft on its own deck) and the turn (the town invited him back). It signals the ending without naming what he does with the sword.
- Legend vs record paragraph names the four corrections the script actually makes on screen: bomb weight, commander's name, aircraft and rank, and the two sorties.
- No claim appears here that is not in the QA-passed script. Specifically avoided: "the only manned aerial bombing of America" and "it happened only once," both overturned in QA.
- The human cost material (the Camden, the Larry Doheny, the L-16, the loss of I-25, and the six killed at Bly in 1945) is deliberately not used as a hook or a selling point. It sits behind the plain chapter title "What the submarine actually did."
