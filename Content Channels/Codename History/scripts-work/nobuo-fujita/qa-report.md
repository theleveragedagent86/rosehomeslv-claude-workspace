**Fix 1, cold open, read in full.** The shipped pair is line 30, "An enemy plane with men inside it bombed mainland America," and line 34, "Exactly one pilot ever did it. This is his story." It is defensible on all four tests:

- **Excludes the Fu-Go balloons.** Unmanned. "With men inside it" rules them out on the face of the sentence, and line 34 adds a pilot, which a balloon does not have.
- **Does not contradict Act 3.** Line 270 narrates the 29 September 1942 second sortie. The old wording counted the event and so collided with it. The referent of "it" in "ever did it" is the act described in line 30, and the number now attached to it is a headcount of pilots, not a headcount of raids.

Wikipedia "Lookout Air Raids" attributes the 9 September raid and the 29 September second attack to the same pilot and observer. The New York Times obituary headline is "Only Foe to Bomb America." Okuda flew both sorties but was the observer, navigator and bombardier, not the pilot, so he does not make the count two. Nothing in the research report offers a competing manned aerial bombing of the mainland by anyone else.
