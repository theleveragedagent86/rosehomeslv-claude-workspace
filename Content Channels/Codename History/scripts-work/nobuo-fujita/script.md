# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE ONLY PILOT WHO EVER BOMBED AMERICA

**Target runtime:** 8-9 minutes (~1,400 narration words at ~158 wpm)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake country dialogue. Hard tonal turn to sincere in the final third.
**Story type:** Single obscure operation plus its fifty-five year aftermath. The Lookout Air Raids, Oregon, 1942, and Nobuo Fujita's return to Brookings.
**Title (standard tier):** `The Only Pilot Who Ever Bombed America | Codename History`

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: Several popular versions of this story are wrong and have been corrected here. The bombs were 168 pounds each, not 340. The submarine's commander was Meiji Tagami, not "Akiji." Fujita was a Warrant Flying Officer flying a two-seat reconnaissance floatplane, not a fighter pilot, and the Yokosuka E14Y was NOT a modified Zero. The defensible claim is that this is the only time an enemy plane with men inside it ever bombed mainland America, because Ellwood and Fort Stevens were naval gunfire and the Fu-Go balloon bombs were unmanned. Do not narrow it to "the only manned aerial bombing," because Fujita flew two sorties, on 9 and 29 September 1942. The seppuku intent is ATTRIBUTED to his New York Times obituary, never asserted. Do not re-simplify any of these back into the myth because the myth is punchier.
>
> VOICE NOTE: Deadpan sarcasm through the first three acts. The comedy is in the narration, not just the visuals. The absurdity is all real: an aircraft carrier that is a tube, a hundred mile an hour invasion, a lookout who identified the enemy by comparing it to a car, and a handful of men with shovels defeating Japan's grand strategic firebombing of America. Then Act 4 goes completely straight and stays there. Act 5 starts wry and turns sincere on the sword. Acts 6 and the outro are played entirely straight. Earn the silence. Do not undercut the ending with a gag.

## ACT 4: WHAT THE SUBMARINE ACTUALLY DID (4:54 - 6:09)

**[VISUAL: A calm grey ocean horizon at dawn, no cartoon elements, muted color.]**

**NARRATOR:** Here is the part that is not funny. Nobody was hurt in either raid.

**[VISUAL: The Oregon forest, intact, quiet, still standing.]** (REUSE: scene 23)

**NARRATOR:** No injuries, no deaths, no buildings lost. That is the confirmed record.

**[VISUAL: The armed tanker SS Camden underway at sea, October 1942.]**

**NARRATOR:** But the submarine stayed on patrol. On October fourth it torpedoed the tanker Camden.

**[VISUAL: The tanker SS Larry Doheny burning on a dark sea at night.]**

**NARRATOR:** One crewman was killed. The next day, I-25 torpedoed the tanker Larry Doheny.

**[VISUAL: A torpedo wake crossing dark water toward a distant submarine silhouette.]**

**NARRATOR:** Six men died. On October eleventh, I-25 fired her last torpedo.

**[VISUAL: The Soviet submarine L-16 on the surface, Soviet naval ensign clearly visible.]**

**NARRATOR:** Tagami believed the target was an American submarine. It was the Soviet submarine L-16.

**[VISUAL: An empty patch of ocean, a Soviet naval ensign sinking beneath the surface.]**

**NARRATOR:** Japan and the Soviet Union were not at war. All fifty six aboard died.

**[VISUAL: I-25 underway on the surface in 1943, crew standing on deck, calm sea.]**

**NARRATOR:** In nineteen forty three, I-25 was lost with all one hundred hands.

**[VISUAL: A hundred silhouetted sailors on a submarine deck, fading into grey.]**

**NARRATOR:** Fujita and Okuda had transferred off. Every man they served with there died.

**[VISUAL: A Fu-Go paper balloon drifting silently over an Oregon forest, spring 1945.]**

**NARRATOR:** Three years later Japan reached America again, with a paper balloon carrying a bomb.

**[VISUAL: A Sunday school picnic group walking up a forest trail on Gearhart Mountain.]**

**NARRATOR:** On May fifth, nineteen forty five, near Bly, Oregon, a picnic group found one.

**[VISUAL: A quiet mountain clearing, still and grey, no people.]**

**NARRATOR:** It killed Elsie Mitchell, who was twenty six and pregnant, and five children.

**[VISUAL: Six names carved on a simple stone memorial in a forest clearing.]**

**NARRATOR:** Edward Engen. Jay Gifford. Sherman Shoemaker. Dick Patzke. Joan Patzke.

**[VISUAL: A plain memorial plaque set into a boulder in the forest.]**

**NARRATOR:** They were the only American mainland civilians killed by enemy weapons in the war.

**[VISUAL: The Oregon coastline at dawn, empty, quiet.]**

**NARRATOR:** That is the whole cost of it, and it is why the sword matters.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Narration word count: 1,399.** (Counted on `NARRATOR:` and `(character voice):` lines only, excluding VISUAL tags, headers, and notes.) This is the QA revision pass, so the count moved from the 1,400 of the first draft. At the measured 158 wpm this runs about 8:50, comfortably above the 8:00 mid-roll floor. At the fast end of the observed VO range it still clears 8:04.

**Visual count: 111 `[VISUAL:]` tags. 8 are marked `(REUSE: scene N)`, so projected unique generations: 103.** Every beat is 14 narration words or fewer, with no exceptions. The cold open scope beat originally ran 16 words and was split across two pictures in the final pass, preserving the scope (an enemy plane, with men inside it) while keeping every hold under the 5 second image ceiling. The second half of that split was then reworded in the QA re-check, because "it happened exactly once" reintroduced the overclaim: Fujita flew two sorties, so the count that is actually unique is the pilot, not the event. No beat is under 8 words. No camera moves anywhere in the script, every tag describes a static picture. Reused scenes: 2, 5, 23, 29, 31, 61, 101, and the 1962 Brookings street (scene 6).

**Voice choices:**
- Deadpan sarcasm carries Acts 1 through 3. The comedy is in the narration, not just the visuals.
- Three `(character voice)` exchanges: Japan and I-25 on the flying-submarine concept, Razz Gardner's field telephone report (built directly on his two documented descriptions), and the Oregon rain.
- Second-person and direct-address beats: "Back up." "Remember that name. Hold that thought." "The town, and this will shock you..."
- Forward reference seeded in Act 1 ("Remember that name. Hold that thought.") on Tagami, paid off in Act 3 (the depth-charging) and Act 4 (the L-16).
- **The tonal turn is at the top of Act 4** and it is hard. "Here is the part that is not funny." From that line to `[END]` there is not a single joke. Act 5 opens wry on the town split, then turns permanently sincere at the sword.
- Comedy density check on the non-straight acts (Cold Open, 1, 2, 3, and the first half of 5): 25 jokes, roasts, or sarcastic asides across roughly 5 minutes 30 seconds of narration, which is one every 13 seconds. Above the every-15-to-20-second bar.

**Accuracy corrections honored (these were all live myths in the popular telling):**
1. **Bomb weight is 168 lb (76 kg) each, not 340 lb.** The 340 figure is the combined load mislabeled as per-bomb. Script says "one hundred sixty eight pounds each."
2. **The commander is Meiji Tagami**, not the widely copied "Akiji Tagami."
3. **Fujita was a Warrant Flying Officer**, explicitly not a fighter pilot, and the E14Y was a purpose-built Yokosuka reconnaissance floatplane, explicitly **not** a modified Zero. The script calls both out on screen.
4. **The claim is scoped correctly:** the cold open says "An enemy plane with men inside it bombed mainland America," then "Exactly one pilot ever did it." Ellwood and Fort Stevens were naval gunfire, the Fu-Go balloons were unmanned. Two earlier phrasings were overturned in QA and must not come back. "Only manned aerial bombing" is wrong because Fujita flew two manned sorties and Act 3 narrates the second. "It happened exactly once" is wrong for the same reason. What is singular here is the pilot, not the event.
5. **The seppuku intent is attributed, never asserted:** "According to his New York Times obituary, he had brought that sword for himself," and "he reportedly intended to use it on himself."
6. **The 1962 visit is dated 24 May 1962**, the Azalea Festival, not the September anniversary.
7. **"The town welcomed him" is not the premise.** The 141 signatures, the opposition ad's own argument, and Fujita's own fear of eggs and violence are all in the script before the warm reception.
8. **Razz Gardner's jailing is flagged "reportedly."**
9. **The sword is at the Chetco Community Public Library**, not City Hall.
10. **The peace tree is a redwood seedling**, not an azalea or a cherry tree.

**Title options:**
1. `The Only Pilot Who Ever Bombed America | Codename History` (**recommended**, the claim is scoped accurately and it is still the strongest hook available)
2. `Japan Bombed Oregon. Men With Shovels Stopped It. | Codename History` (reworded, the old version rested on a crew count the sources do not agree on)
3. `The Town That Invited Its Bomber Back | Codename History`

**Channel placement:** A strong standalone Standard episode and a natural pair with a Fu-Go balloon bomb episode, which the outro teases directly. Also sits well beside any "the war reached the mainland" cluster (Ellwood, Fort Stevens). This is the channel's best available example of the comedy-to-sincerity turn, so it is a good candidate for a channel trailer clip.

**Build notes:** Tag Act 4 in full and Acts 5 (from the banquet-door beat forward), 6, and the outro as `TONE: STRAIGHT`. The Bly, Oregon beats and the name list must be delivered slow and flat with no music sting.

**QA revision pass, changes made:** cold open scope widened to cover both sorties; E14Y cruise corrected from 90 mph (a knots figure misread as mph) to a little over 100 mph, speedometer visual updated to 104; the Larry Doheny moved to "the next day" and the L-16 to "On October eleventh"; the 6:24 clock time and the conflicting "around six" both cut, which also fixes the Act 2 chronology inversion; crew counts replaced with "a handful" in both places and in Title Option 2; "twelve pieces," "seven man crew," "fifteen minutes," "520 pellets," "twenty seven hundred degrees," and "fewer than half" all softened to what more than one outlet supports; the I-25 loss month dropped to the year; "Elsye" corrected to "Elsie" per the Mitchell Monument plaque, with "Eddie Engen" moved to "Edward Engen" so the roll call follows one source, and the memorial visual corrected from five names to six; the Bly claim scoped to the war; the Jaycees idea broadened off a single named man. Act 4 through the END was not touched tonally. No gag was added anywhere near the ending.

**Gaps carried from the research report that the script deliberately avoids:** the verbatim NYT obituary text, the verbatim text of Fujita's 1961 *Proceedings* article, the name of the official who received the sword, the exact 1997 proclamation date, and a launch clock time for the second sortie. **Conflicts the QA pass closed by removal rather than by picking a side:** the Mount Emily report time (6:24 vs 6:42), the Larry Doheny date (5 vs 6 October), the I-25 loss month (25 August vs 3 September 1943), the Forest Service crew size (2 vs 4 vs about 8), the floatplane component count and assembly time, and the thermite pellet count.
