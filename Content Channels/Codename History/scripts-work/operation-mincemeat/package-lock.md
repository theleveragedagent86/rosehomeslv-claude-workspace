## RECOMMENDED TITLE

`The Dead Man Who Fooled the Nazis | Codename History`

(7 words before the suffix. Grade-0 vocabulary, active voice, title case, curiosity gap intact.)

**Reasoning (one line):** Lead with the hook, not the codename, because "Mincemeat" pre-resolves the mystery for the Macintyre-book and 2021-film audience and means nothing at all to everyone else, so the operation name earns its keep in the thumbnail title card, the description, and the tags where it captures search, not in the position that has to stop a cold scroller.

**Secondary note:** "Fooled the Nazis" not "Fooled Hitler." The script never claims Hitler personally was deceived, only German intelligence and German commanders, and the title must not out-claim the script.

## THUMBNAIL CONCEPT (LOCKED)

**Concept A, "The Slab."** One image, one focal subject, hard contrast, 2 words of text.

Flat-color animated illustration, 16:9, in the channel's OverSimplified-style house look. A single dead man lies on a steel mortuary slab, shot from a low three-quarter angle so his face fills the left two thirds of the frame. Eyes closed, skin drained pale grey-blue, jaw slack, absolutely still. He wears a British Royal Marines officer's peaked cap and the shoulder of a Royal Marines uniform tunic. His near hand hangs off the edge of the slab, and a black leather briefcase is chained to his wrist, hanging into the bottom of the frame, the chain catching a hard highlight. Background: pure black, with one harsh overhead cone of white light on the body and a deep blood-red glow bleeding in from the right edge, in which the faint silhouette of a coastline map of the Mediterranean is barely readable. No blood, no gore, no comedy in this frame. Text: **IT WORKED** in heavy condensed white uppercase, bottom right, slight red drop shadow, clear of the face and the briefcase.

Why it clicks: one subject, unmistakable at 120px, and the two-word text creates the gap ("what worked? he is dead") that the title answers only halfway.

**Alternate thumbnail B, "The Eyelash."** Extreme macro, flat-animated. A pair of tweezers holding a single eyelash above an open folded letter stamped SECRET in red, wax seal half melted beside it. Black background, one warm desk-lamp pool of light. Text: **ONE EYELASH**. Strongest "wait, what" concept in the episode and the most quotable fact, but it needs the title to carry the corpse.

**Alternate thumbnail C, "Two Names."** A single stone headstone, centered, harsh side light, deep black sky. Chiselled: WILLIAM MARTIN. Beneath it, a second line being cut into the stone by a chisel mid-stroke, glowing white: GLYNDWR MICHAEL. Text: **NOT HIS NAME**. Somber, no comedy, highest emotional pull, likely the weakest cold-click of the three but the best for suggested-video traffic from history audiences.

Ryan or Phase B picks. All three render at 16:9, under 10 MB, four words or fewer of on-image text, one focal subject each.

## COLD OPEN HOOK IMAGE

**The frame the video opens ON, cold, no logo, no intro:**

A dead man in a British officer's uniform, face down and half submerged, drifting in dark pre-dawn water off a Spanish beach. A black briefcase is chained to his coat, dragging just under the surface beside him. The shoreline is a thin grey line in the distance. No music yet, just water.

This is the strangest verifiable image in the story and it is the entire premise in one picture: a body, a chain, a briefcase, and a coastline that is not his own. It is factual to the record (HMS Seraph released the body off Huelva around 4:15 a.m. on 30 April 1943, briefcase secured to the corpse). Hold it for roughly three seconds, then hard cut back in time to the London mortuary.
