# SHORTS CANDIDATES: Operation Mincemeat

Six standalone vertical Shorts, ranked strongest first, modelled on the Red Baron shorts package (`episodes/red-baron/shorts/shorts-package.md` and `build_shorts.py`).

House spec, same as Red Baron:
- 9:16 vertical, 1080x1920 at 30fps, house flat-animated style, same narrator VO, burned ALL-CAPS captions.
- Hook lands in the first two seconds. No logo, no intro.
- 4 to 5 narration beats plus the shared endcard beat ("FULL STORY ON THE CHANNEL" + SUBSCRIBE + Codename History).
- 25 to 40 seconds each.
- Built at near-zero image cost: every frame below reuses a still or a composition already described in `transcripts/operation-mincemeat.md`, rendered via Claude design + Ken Burns, not new video generation.
- Every fact traces to the episode's FACT-CHECK LOG. Nothing new is claimed.
- Post one every 1 to 2 days around the main launch. Pin a comment linking the full video.

Tone rule carried over from the episode: Shorts 2 and 6 are TONE: STRAIGHT end to end. No joke, no wink, no punchy edit over Glyndwr Michael or over the burial.

## SHORT 1 (rank 1) - "They Caught Nazi Spies With an Eyelash"

**Runtime target:** 35s
**Drawn from:** Act 3 (the eyelash placed inside the folded letter) + Act 5 (the eyelash is gone).
**Why it stands alone:** It is a complete setup-and-payoff in two facts and needs zero knowledge of Sicily, the corpse, or the war's strategy. It is the episode's designated "you have to tell someone this" fact, small enough to repeat verbatim at a dinner table.
**Hook line:** "In 1943 British intelligence needed to know if the Germans had secretly read a letter. So they used an eyelash."

Beats:
1. "In 1943, British intelligence needed to know whether the Germans had secretly opened a letter. So they used an eyelash."
2. "They plucked a single eyelash, laid it inside the folded document, and sealed the envelope. If the letter was ever opened, the eyelash would fall out. No wax seal. No code. One eyelash, as counterintelligence."
3. "The letter went out into enemy hands. Weeks later it came back through official channels, returned politely, as if nobody had touched it."
4. "The eyelash was gone. The letters had been opened, read, and resealed. They had caught German intelligence with an eyelash."
5. Endcard.

**Reused visuals:** the eyelash-into-letter frame, the SECRET briefcase letters, the returned open letter close-up. All three are already described in the episode.

## SHORT 2 (rank 2) - "The Grave With Two Names on It"

**Runtime target:** 40s
**Drawn from:** Act 6 (Huelva headstone, Roger Morgan 1996, CWGC line added 1997).
**Why it stands alone:** A single grave with a wrong name and a corrected name is a whole story in one object. It needs no operation, no plot, no Germans. It is the emotional-share Short and the one most likely to travel into non-history feeds.
**Hook line:** "There is a grave in Spain with two names on it, and for fifty-four years only the fake one was there."
**TONE: STRAIGHT throughout. No comedy anywhere in this Short.**

Beats:
1. "There is a grave in Spain with two names on it. For fifty-four years, only the fake one was there."
2. "The headstone said Major William Martin, Royal Marines. Buried with military honours in 1943. Major Martin never existed."
3. "The body was Glyndwr Michael. A homeless Welshman from Aberbargoed who died alone in London at thirty-four, with no money, no home, and no one."
4. "In 1996 a researcher named Roger Morgan found his name in the public records. In 1997 they carved it into the stone. Glyndwr Michael served as Major William Martin."
5. "He never volunteered. He never knew. After fifty-four years, the man who never was, finally, was."
6. Endcard.

**Reused visuals:** the Huelva headstone, the chisel adding the second line, the full headstone with both names, the muted grey London street. All already in Act 2 and Act 6.

## SHORT 3 (rank 3) - "They Built a Whole Fake Life for a Dead Man"

**Runtime target:** 38s
**Drawn from:** Act 3 (wallet litter, ring receipt, overdraft letter, Pam's letters and photo).
**Why it stands alone:** A list Short. It is pure escalating absurd detail, the format Shorts reward most, and the punchline is the exact figures. No context required beyond "British intelligence, 1943."
**Hook line:** "British intelligence gave a dead stranger a fiancee, a bar tab, and a bank problem."

Beats:
1. "In 1943, British intelligence took a dead man and built him an entire fake life. Not a name. A life."
2. "Theatre ticket stubs, so it looked like he had been out on the town. Keys. A book of stamps. A silver medal. A bill from his gentleman's club."
3. "A receipt for a diamond engagement ring. Fifty-three pounds, ten shillings and sixpence. Because Major Martin was in love."
4. "And a letter from his bank complaining about his overdraft, seventy-nine pounds and change, because Major Martin, like all the best officers, was also slightly broke."
5. "They even obsessed over his underwear tags. Nobody was supposed to see them. In case the Germans checked."
6. Endcard.

**Reused visuals:** the prop desk, the ring receipt, the bank overdraft letter, the underwear tag close-up. All already described.

## SHORT 4 (rank 4) - "A Sardine Pun Moved a German Army"

**Runtime target:** 33s
**Drawn from:** Act 3 (the second letter's sardines pun) + Act 5 (German reinforcements shift toward Greece and Sardinia).
**Why it stands alone:** One joke with a body count of divisions. The setup is a pun and the payoff is a map, which is exactly the kind of "wait, that actually worked?" beat that gets shared. It carries its own stakes without the corpse.
**Hook line:** "The British hid a sardine pun in a fake letter, and a German army moved because of it."

Beats:
1. "In 1943 the British wrote a fake letter for the Germans to find. Hidden inside it was a pun about sardines."
2. "The whole point was to nudge German intelligence toward Sardinia, and away from Sicily, which was the real target. Subtle like an elbow to the ribs."
3. "The Germans read it. They studied it hard. And they concluded exactly what the British had manufactured for them to conclude. Greece and Sardinia. Sicily was a bluff."
4. "Reinforcements shifted toward Greece. A panzer division was redirected east. The Sardinia garrison was strengthened. The fate of thousands of soldiers partly rode on a sardine joke."
5. "On the ninth of July, 1943, the Allies invaded Sicily."
6. Endcard.

**Reused visuals:** the sardine-pun letter, the German officers circling GREECE on a map, the units sliding away from Sicily, the tumbleweed sentry. All already described.

## SHORT 5 (rank 5) - "A Real Officer's Face Went to War Without Him"

**Runtime target:** 30s
**Drawn from:** Act 3 (the ID photo problem, the living MI5 officer who resembled the corpse).
**Why it stands alone:** A single strange problem with a single strange solution. "Dead faces do not photograph like sleeping ones" is a fact most people have never considered, which makes it instantly repeatable.
**Hook line:** "You cannot photograph a dead man and have him look alive. This became a British intelligence problem in 1943."

Beats:
1. "You cannot photograph a dead man and have him look alive. Dead faces do not photograph like sleeping ones."
2. "Which was a problem, because in 1943 British intelligence needed a convincing military ID card for a corpse."
3. "So they went looking through their own building for a living man who happened to look like the dead one. They found one. An MI5 officer."
4. "They photographed him instead, and put his face on the dead man's identity card."
5. "Somewhere in Britain, an officer's passport photo went off to war without him."
6. Endcard.

**Reused visuals:** the ID card being stamped, the living-man photo on the card, the tailor's dummy. All already described.

## SHORT 6 (rank 6) - "They Held a Funeral at 4am Before Using Him as a Weapon"

**Runtime target:** 32s
**Drawn from:** Act 4 (HMS Seraph off Huelva, crew sent below, Psalm 39 read over the side).
**Why it stands alone:** One scene, one moment, one decision. It is the quietest beat in the episode and it is complete in itself: men doing an ugly thing, and pausing to do one decent thing first.
**Hook line:** "At quarter past four in the morning, a British submarine surfaced off Spain to release a dead body."
**TONE: STRAIGHT throughout. No comedy anywhere in this Short.**

Beats:
1. "At about a quarter past four in the morning, on the thirtieth of April, 1943, a British submarine surfaced quietly off the coast of Spain."
2. "The commander, Bill Jewell, sent the crew below. Only the officers stayed on deck. They did not need the whole crew knowing they were carrying a body."
3. "They opened a canister packed with dry ice and lifted out a dead man in a Royal Marines uniform, with a briefcase chained to his coat."
4. "And before they let him go, the commander read aloud part of Psalm thirty-nine."
5. "A real burial, for a real dead man, spoken over the side of a submarine in the dark, before setting him adrift as a weapon. Whatever else this was, they did not just dump him."
6. Endcard.

**Reused visuals:** HMS Seraph at night, the canister opening, the body lowered into black water, the cold-open floating shot. All already described.
