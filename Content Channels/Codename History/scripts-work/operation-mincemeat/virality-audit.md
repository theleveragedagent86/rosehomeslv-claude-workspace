But it opens on the *mortuary*, which is the setup, not on the strangest verifiable image, which is the corpse in the water with the briefcase chained to him. It also never lands the channel's credibility beat ("every part of this is real"), which the Castle Itter pilot did land and which the playbook treats as a hook in its own right.

CURRENT:

> **NARRATOR:** In 1943, two British intelligence officers stood over a dead body and made it a plan. Not part of a plan. The plan.

REPLACEMENT:

A dead man in a British officer's uniform drifts face down, a black briefcase chained to his coat and dragging just below the surface. A thin grey shoreline in the distance. Hold three seconds. ## 5. SEEDED LOOPS AND PAYOFFS (playbook section 3, "at least two live loops")

**Finding: one real loop, one fake loop.**

- **Real loop, PASS.** Act 2's "Nobody would, for over fifty years" is seeded at 6:00 and paid off in Act 6 at 15:45 with Roger Morgan's 1996 discovery and the 1997 headstone. That is a 10-minute loop doing exactly what the playbook asks.
- **Fake loop, FAIL.** Act 1's "Hold that thought" is planted at 3:15 and resolved four seconds later at the top of Act 2, which opens on the two men. Nothing is actually held. A "hold that thought" that pays off instantly trains the viewer to stop trusting the narrator's promises, which is the opposite of the intended effect.

Fix: repoint the Act 1 loop at the eyelash, which is genuinely planted in Act 3 (7:50ish) and genuinely paid off in Act 5 (11:35). That gives two live loops running simultaneously across the whole middle of the video.

### Edit 5.1 (Act 1 ending, plant the real loop)

CURRENT:
> **NARRATOR:** For four years, that suggestion sat in the drawer, too grim and too weird for anyone to touch. In 1943, two men finally were desperate enough. Hold that thought.

REPLACEMENT:
> **NARRATOR:** For four years, that suggestion sat in the drawer, too grim and too weird for anyone to touch. In 1943, two men finally were desperate enough. And the smallest object in this entire story, something you could lose in a blink, is what would eventually tell them whether it had worked. Hold that thought.

No new claim: the eyelash tripwire and its disappearance are both already in the script and both VERIFIED.

### Edit 5.2 (Act 3, close the loop out loud)

CURRENT:

REPLACEMENT:
> **NARRATOR:** And here is that smallest object I told you to hold on to. The detail I cannot get over. They slipped a single eyelash inside one folded letter before sealing it.

## 6. THE ONE "YOU HAVE TO TELL SOMEONE THIS" FACT (playbook section 4)

**The fact: they sealed a single eyelash inside the letter so that if it was missing when the documents came back, they would know the Germans had opened and read them. It was missing.**

This is the share engine. It is small, absurd, verifiable, and repeatable in one sentence at a dinner table, the same slot the pigeon-shot-through-the-chest fact fills on the Cher Ami episode. It is currently stated well in Act 3 but its **payoff** in Act 5 is buried in a clause and never given a clean quotable landing.

### Edit 6.1 (Act 5, make the payoff quotable)

CURRENT:
> **NARRATOR:** The documents came back to the British through official channels. Returned politely, as if untouched, with a straight face and everything. But the British checked their tripwire. The eyelash was gone. The letters had been opened, read, and resealed. Somebody had looked. The insane eyelash plan had worked, which had to be a strange thing to feel proud of.

REPLACEMENT:
> **NARRATOR:** The documents came back to the British through official channels. Returned politely, as if untouched, with a straight face and everything. But the British checked their tripwire. The eyelash was gone. The letters had been opened, read, and resealed. Somebody had looked. They had caught German intelligence with an eyelash. Which had to be a strange thing to feel proud of.

"German intelligence" is the wording the script and the fact-check log already use. No escalation to Hitler or the high command.
