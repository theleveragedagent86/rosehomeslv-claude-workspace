## Issues to fix

1. **"The most repeated fact about Operation Tracer is one nobody can source."** (Act 7, final beat) - The seven-year figure is sourced. It is in the Government of Gibraltar's official heritage listing HLMP2/036, cited at Frontline Ulster footnote 10, and Frontline Ulster speculates it came from Cooper's own oral history. The defensible claim is that it cannot be found in the archive. **Replace with:** "The most repeated fact about Operation Tracer is one nobody can find in the archive." (13 words, holds the beat length, holds the cadence, and is exactly true.)

2. **"At the turn of nineteen ninety six into ninety seven, they felt a draught."** (Act 6) - This asserts a specific window, states no disagreement, and picks the weaker side. Frontline Ulster reports the cavers' own words as "undertaken during Christmas 1997" and dates it "Christmas 1997" and "late 1997" citing the Gibraltar Heritage Journal Vol 1 Iss 1 (2002); the Government of Gibraltar, the Feb 2007 Independent account and aboutourrock.com all say 1997. Only Wikipedia's Gibraltar Caving Group article and BFBS say Boxing Day 1996. **Replace with:** "One Christmas, ninety six or ninety seven, nobody agrees which, they felt a draught." (14 words.)

3. **"He found an existing tunnel at Lord Airey's Battery. Thirteen hundred feet up."** (Act 2) - ADM 223/464 sentence [16] reads "It was 1350' above sea-level", and Wikipedia gives 1,350 ft (410 m). 1,300 traces to nothing. **Replace "Thirteen hundred feet up" with "Thirteen hundred and fifty feet up."**

4. **"What got built was one fifteen millimetre slit."** (Act 2) - Unresolved conflict silently resolved. Frontline Ulster's own site survey says "a small slit barely 20mm high"; the Gibraltar listing gives about 15 mm. Two field measurements, no adjudication available. **Replace with:** "What got built was one slit you could barely get a pencil through." The visual already carries the pencil comparison and the number adds nothing the joke needs.

5. **"Levick used to hallucinate about a street of sweet shops, always closed."** (Act 3) - Single Tier 3 source, the defunct and uncited aboutourrock.com. I searched the Antarctic Heritage Trust's Levick notebook coverage, CoolAntarctica, the Bonhams manuscript listing and the Lambert biography material and found no trace of it. An asserted claim about a real man's mental state cannot rest on this. **Cut the beat.** If a replacement beat is wanted at the same length, the documented Northern Party detail is that they lived on seal meat and that Levick's own diary records a "falling off of spirits".

6. **"The slit acted as a pinhole camera and projected the bay onto it."** and the follow-up **"The observation post finally worked. Nobody was even looking through it."** (Act 6) - Asserted as fact, but Frontline Ulster identifies it as "speculation by the group who discovered it" and rebuts it on physics: "The pinhole principle is only valid when the hole is very small (ie; a pinhole) and the material is very thin." A slit cast through thick concrete and rock is neither. This is the one place the script asserts something the best available technical assessment says is probably wrong. **Cut both beats**, or, if the payoff for the reused wedge visual must be kept, attribute and hedge: "The cavers thought the slit had acted as a pinhole camera. The physics says probably not."

7. **"The Admiralty called a sixty four year old surgeon out of retirement."** (Act 3) - Conflation. Levick, born 3 July 1876, did return to the Royal Navy in 1940 at 64, but for the Commando Special Training Centre at Lochailort, not for Tracer. By the time Tracer consulted him, November 1941 to January 1942, he was 65 and already back in uniform. **Replace with:** "The Admiralty had already pulled a sixty four year old surgeon out of retirement." Then the following line still lands.

8. **"Cooper's own obituary says supplies for a year. Not seven."** (Act 7) - The Telegraph obituary of 4 January 2011 is paywalled and absent from the Wayback Machine, and the script's own fact-check log concedes nobody read it. Asserting the contents of an unread document is not permissible, and it is unnecessary because a better and fully readable source exists. The contemporaneous summary of the Independent on Sunday of 4 February 2007 states the team was warned it would be "up to a year, although they were aware that it could be longer". **Replace with:** "Cooper's own account, when he finally told it, said a year. Not seven." Update the SOURCES block to lead with the Independent rather than the Telegraph for this beat.

9. **"In the only account that survives. Nobody has ever corrected it."** (Act 2) - The second sentence is contradicted by the script's own fact-check log, which notes Wikipedia silently prints 5,760. **Replace with:** "In the only account that survives. It was never corrected on the file."

10. **"He recruited a twenty seven year old surgeon lieutenant called Bruce Cooper."** (Act 3) - The age appears in no source I could locate and is not in the research report. It is derived from a birth date of 22 November 1914 plus a recruitment date, and the recruitment date itself is contested: Wikipedia puts it at "shore leave in 1941" (age 26 or 27), the archive timeline puts recruitment at February to April 1942 (age 27). A specific that depends on an unresolved date should not be asserted. **Replace with:** "He recruited a young surgeon lieutenant called Bruce Cooper."

11. **"Charging the batteries halfway would take over seventy two hours of pedalling."** (Act 2) - This is Frontline Ulster's own "rudimentary calculation", run against a Battery Charger No. 2 that he says only "may have been" the type used. It is a modern reconstruction, not a record figure, and the script states it flatly. **Replace with:** "By one modern reconstruction, charging the batteries halfway meant over seventy two hours of pedalling." (14 words.)

12. **"Three men agreed to be sealed inside a mountain, and nobody wrote them down."** (Act 8, final line) - The sources say the names "are unknown", not that they were never recorded. The line as written asserts an absence of record that nobody can demonstrate. It is also the closing line of the episode, so it should be the most defensible sentence in the script rather than the most rhetorical. **Replace with:** "Three men agreed to be sealed inside a mountain, and nobody knows their names." (14 words, same cadence, same weight, and exactly true.)

Items 1, 2 and 6 are the ones that force the FAIL on their own. Items 3, 4, 5, 8, 10 and 11 are sourcing and precision failures under the stated bar. Items 7, 9 and 12 are accuracy repairs that cost the script nothing.
