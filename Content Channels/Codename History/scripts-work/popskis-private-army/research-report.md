# Research Report: Popski's Private Army (No. 1 Demolition Squadron, PPA)

**Wave 1 Research Agent. Compiled for a Standard-tier Codename History episode, 8 to 12 min, ~1,400 narration words.**

> **READ THIS FIRST, WRITER.** This story has an unusual sourcing problem and the whole episode depends on handling it right.
>
> Vladimir Peniakoff is his own primary source. Three of his own accounts survive and **they contradict each other**: the contemporaneous award citations written by other people (1942 and 1944), his own official "Historical Summary" written for King George VI in 1945 (TNA: WO 106/2332), and his 1950 bestseller *Private Army*. The numbers get bigger with each retelling.
>
> The good news: because the contemporaneous documents survive, we can show the inflation happening on camera. **That is the episode.** The comedy is not "look at this liar." It is "look at how a genuinely brave man's story grew in the telling, and here is the paperwork."
>
> Every beat below is tagged:
> - **[DOC]** = contemporaneous document or official history. Assert freely.
> - **[POPSKI-1945]** = his own official summary. Attribute: "in his own official report".
> - **[MEMOIR]** = his 1950 book or a later retelling. Attribute: "by his own account".
> - **[HUMAN COST]** = play completely straight. No jokes in or adjacent to these.

## One-paragraph summary

Vladimir Peniakoff was a 45-year-old Belgian-born engineer of Russian-Jewish parentage who had spent eighteen years running machinery at an Egyptian sugar factory, spoke fluent Arabic, and had never completed a degree in his life. In 1942 he talked the British Army into giving him his own raiding unit in the Western Desert. Its War Establishment, passed on 10 December 1942, was five officers and eighteen other ranks, twenty-three men and six vehicles, which is roughly a large infantry platoon calling itself a squadron. Its official designation was No. 1 Demolition Squadron, PPA, and the "PPA" in the official title stood for a joke: Popski's Private Army, a name coined by staff officers in Cairo mocking him for running a private war at public expense. The unit fought across Libya and Tunisia, then had a far more consequential war in Italy, where it grew to a War Establishment of eighty, fought as jeep-borne raiders and amphibious troops in the flooded canal country around Ravenna and the Po delta, worked alongside Italian partisans, and at Chioggia in April 1945 accepted the surrender of a German garrison of nearly seven hundred men. Peniakoff lost his left hand and took a bullet through his right at Ravenna in December 1944. Twelve of his men died. He wrote a bestseller in 1950, and died of a brain tumour in May 1951, aged fifty-four, having been a British citizen for four years.

## Verified timeline

### The man, before the war

- **30 March 1897** - Vladimir Peniakoff born at **Huy, province of Liège, Belgium**, to wealthy Russian-Jewish parents who had emigrated to Belgium in **1894**. Father Dmitri, a scientist, ran an aluminium factory using his own patented process for extracting the metal from bauxite. [St John's College Cambridge, *The Eagle* vol. 92, article by Dr John Alexander, Fellow of St John's, reproduced by Friends of PPA with the College Development Office's permission; Wikipedia (orientation)]
- **1912 to 1914** - Two years as an undergraduate at the **Université Libre de Bruxelles**, entering at age 15. Awarded the *Diplôme de humanité classique (Latin et Grec)* and the *Diplôme de candidat ingénieur*, which would have let him proceed to a full engineering degree at seventeen. Interrupted by the German invasion of Belgium, August 1914. [*The Eagle* vol. 92]
- **19 January 1915** - Accepted by **St John's College, Cambridge**, for an unspecified course in Physics and Mathematics, sponsored by Dr Cyril Bradley Rootham, Fellow and College organist. College Register number **2707**. His birth certificate never arrived and his mother's maiden name was recorded wrongly as "Brown"; it was **Rosenthal**. [*The Eagle* vol. 92, working from College archives]
- **Lent term 1915 to June 1916** - In residence at St John's. **Seventeen months. He took no degree.** Dr Alexander's article exists specifically to correct the widespread description of him as "educated at St John's College, Cambridge," noting the description "does not fully reflect what we know of his experience in Cambridge." [*The Eagle* vol. 92] **This is a clean, sourced myth-correction.**
- **1917 to 1918** - Volunteered as a **gunner in the French artillery**. He had begun with conscientious objections to the war; by his fourth term at Cambridge his views had changed. Injured in service, invalided out after the Armistice of 11 November 1918. [Wikipedia citing Friends of PPA; *The Eagle*]
- **December 1924** - Emigrated to **Egypt**, working as an engineer for a sugar manufacturer. Over the next fifteen years he learned to sail, to fly, and to navigate a car across open desert, and became a **Fellow of the Royal Geographical Society**. Spoke English, Russian, Italian, German, French and Arabic. [Wikipedia; Friends of PPA]
- **1927** - His friend **Paolo Caccia Dominioni di Sillavengo**, an Italian count, architect and engineer also working in Egypt, drew him an *ex libris* bookplate featuring an astrolabe. **This drawing is the direct origin of the PPA cap badge.** [Roberto Chiarvetto, "Popski's Astrolabe," Friends of PPA, working from Caccia Dominioni's *El Alamein 1933-1962* (Longanesi, Milano, 1962) and from Popski's papers now held by IWM]
- **May 1933** - Peniakoff and Caccia Dominioni drove to Siwa Oasis via Marsa Matruh with other European friends, four Ford cars. They passed El Alamein. [Chiarvetto; Caccia Dominioni, *El Alamein 1933-1962*]
- **10 November 1928** - Married Josephe Louise Colette "Josette" Ceysens, an Egypt-born Belgian. Two daughters, Olga (b. 1930) and Anne (b. 1932). [Wikipedia citing Friends of PPA]

### War, the desert phase

- **3 September 1939** - Britain declares war. Peniakoff is **42**.
- **4 October 1940** - Commissioned **second lieutenant on the General List**, service number **159661**. **[DOC: London Gazette, supplement 35063, 31 January 1941, p. 682 - I read the original page: "4th Oct. 1940: Vladimir Peniakoff (159661)."]** Posted to the **Libyan Arab Force**.
- **March 1941** - Divorced Josephe and sent the family to South Africa. [Wikipedia citing Friends of PPA]
- **22 April to 18 May 1942** - **First trip into the Jebel Akhdar**, transported by the LRDG. Visited Slonta, Giovanni Berta and Chaulan, made contact with local inhabitants to identify sabotage targets, ran a **road watch on the Martuba road** reporting enemy traffic daily by wireless. **[DOC: his own MC recommendation, Army Form W.3121, TNA WO 373/46/37, scan published by Friends of PPA - I read the document image directly]**
- **May 1942** - Given command of the **Libyan Arab Force Commando**, reportedly about **22 Senussi Arabs, one British sergeant and one Arab officer**, with no transport of its own. [Tier 2: Warfare History Network; consistent with the citation. **The exact LAFC strength is not confirmed from a Tier 1 source. Treat "about two dozen" as the safe formulation.**]
- **28 May to 20 August 1942** - **Second and much longer stay behind the lines.** During it he **destroyed 50,000 gallons of petrol at Giovanni Berta on 17 August 1942**, visited and reported on Derna landing ground, organised the escape of **5 British prisoners of war from the Derna cage** (digit slightly ambiguous in the scan, possibly 6), set up a road-watch network run by Libyan Arabs, and did political work among the local population. **[DOC: MC recommendation, WO 373/46/37]** The citation also records, flatly, that he was throughout "in constant danger of being given away by ARABS." **Note that unvarnished line. The relationship with the local population was not the uncomplicated partnership the later retellings imply.**
- **August 1942** - Returns to Cairo after five months to find the **LAF Commando has been disbanded** in his absence, the unit he was borne on has ceased to exist, and the paymaster has apparently forgotten him. **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **1 September 1942** - LRDG raiding force leaves Faiyum for **Barce**: Major J. R. Easonsmith commanding, T1 patrol (Capt. Wilder) and G1 patrol (Capt. Timpson), **47 men in 12 trucks and 5 jeeps**. Outward journey **1,155 miles**. **[DOC: R. L. Kay, *Long Range Desert Group in the Mediterranean*, Official History of New Zealand in the Second World War, Historical Publications Branch, Wellington]**
- **13 September 1942** - **The Barce raid (Operation Caravan).** Easonsmith took "a British agent (Major V. Peniakoff) and two Senussi spies" close to Barce to gather intelligence. Patrols entered the town at midnight. **[DOC: NZ Official History; MC recommendation gives "the LRDG raid on BARCE L.G. on 13/9/42"]**
- **Before dawn, 14 September 1942** - During the withdrawal, near the police post south of Sidi Selim, **150 Tripolitanian troops under three Italian officers** ambushed the column. **Peniakoff was one of three men wounded**, along with Trooper F. W. Jopling. **[DOC: NZ Official History]** He lost **part of the little finger of his left hand**; it was amputated in the desert the next day and shell splinters were removed from a leg. [Tier 2, corroborated by Caccia Dominioni's 1962 book, which describes his friend losing part of his left little finger to Italian machine-gun fire in the raid]
- **September 1942** - Colonel **John Hackett**, newly appointed OC Special Forces Middle East, conceives a unit "operating on the lines of Popski's parties but with bigger means and transport of its own," to harass the German rear during the withdrawal expected after El Alamein. **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **26 November 1942** - MC **gazetted**. Listed as "Captain (temporary Major) Vladimir Peniakoff, **Libyan Arab Force**." **[DOC: London Gazette, supplement 35799, dated 24 November, published 26 November 1942, p. 5142 - read directly. Recommended by the CO of the LRDG, marked "M.C. immediate", signed by General H. R. Alexander, Commander-in-Chief Middle East Forces]** Note: the MC was for the **Libyan Arab Force Commando and LRDG work, not for PPA**, which did not yet exist.
- **26 November 1942** - Peniakoff **sets out from Cairo** with vehicles and equipment complete "but with only **1 officer, 12 O.R.s and 4 Arabs**." **[POPSKI-1945]** (foPPA note: his book says 3 Arabs, the PPA Card Index lists 5.)
- **10 December 1942** - **The War Establishment M.E.W.E. 866/1 for No. 1 Demolition Squadron is passed by the War Establishment Commission. It provides for 5 officers and 18 other ranks, 4 armed jeeps and 2 three-tonners.** **[POPSKI-1945, quoting the establishment document]** **This is the real, documentary origin of the "23 men" figure. Use this date and these numbers.**
- **Early December 1942** - The unit reaches **Kufra**, heads north for the Jebel Akhdar, but the German withdrawal is so fast that Benghazi falls before they arrive. They turn west to chase the retreating enemy instead. **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **January to February 1943** - LRDG and Indian Long Range Squadron reconnoitre southern Tunisia for the route around the Mareth Line. **[DOC: NZ Official History]**
- **12 January 1943** - **T1 patrol under Captain Nick Wilder crosses the frontier, the first Eighth Army troops to enter Tunisia**, and about thirty miles south-west of Foum Tatahouine finds the pass that became **Wilder's Gap**. **[DOC: NZ Official History]** **PPA was not on this patrol. See Myth vs Record.**
- **January 1943** - **T2 patrol under Lieutenant Tinker, accompanied by a party of PPA**, sets up a base camp about twenty miles south of Ksar Rhilane. "Tinker and Peniakoff, each with two jeeps, then went north towards Djebel Tebaga, through country that was found to be suitable for the passage of a force of all arms." **[DOC: NZ Official History]** **This is the Tebaga Gap corridor, and this recce was genuinely joint LRDG-PPA.**
- **January 1943, Ksar Rhilane** - The base camp is strafed and **all vehicles destroyed**. Two New Zealanders (L/Cpls R. A. Ramsay and R. C. Davies) wounded. Thirty-seven men gather at Ksar Rhilane: **16 LRDG, 13 PPA, 6 French parachutists and 2 SAS.** Five jeeps between them and not enough petrol for a hundred miles. Tinker drives out with the wounded; the rest walk. **[DOC: NZ Official History]** Popski's version adds that local Tunisian Arabs had reported the British vehicles to the Germans, contrasting them with the Senussi of Cyrenaica. **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **Same period** - **David Stirling, founder of the SAS, is captured near Gabes.** **[DOC: NZ Official History]**
- **7 February 1943** - PPA reaches **Gafsa**. Popski claims this as the first contact between an Eighth Army unit and First Army; **4,500 miles covered since leaving Cairo**. **[POPSKI-1945]** **foPPA corrects this: a small SAS group beat them by a couple of days. See Myth vs Record.**
- **19 March 1943, Sened** - Nearly **600 Italians** found trapped in a wadi with no exit while trying to escape over the mountains; "the whole crowd surrendered to a Sergeant and two men." **[POPSKI-1945]** Also at Sened they recovered a truck lost to the Germans near Tripoli two months earlier, still bearing the unit sign, and **bartered it to an American unit for a brand-new three-quarter-ton Dodge, because the Americans wanted a war souvenir.** **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **19 April 1943** - Contact made with 1st Armoured Division; PPA reverts to Eighth Army. **7 May 1943** - PPA finishes the North African campaign at **Enfidaville**. **[POPSKI-1945]**

### War, the Italian phase

- **Summer 1943** - Unit reorganised and enlarged, attached to 1st Airborne Division in Tunisia for **glider training**. The glider role was cancelled. **[POPSKI-1945; Wikipedia]**
- **9 September 1943, 2000 hrs** - PPA lands from the sea at **Taranto** with 1st Airborne Division in **Operation Slapstick**, "its jeeps being the first vehicles ashore." **[POPSKI-1945]** The landing was unopposed. **[DOC: US Naval History and Heritage Command, H-Gram 021-2, drawing on Morison vol. IX, records that USS *Boise* (CL-47) was pressed into service alongside British light cruisers to lift 1st Airborne Division, carrying 788 British troops and 60 to 70 jeeps in place of her floatplanes.]** **That PPA specifically sailed aboard *Boise* is [MEMOIR]**: the book says "I landed from U.S.S. *Boise* and drove out of Taranto in the night." No independent record names PPA among her embarked troops, though with 60 to 70 jeeps aboard, PPA's five being among them is entirely plausible.
- **10 September 1943** - **[HUMAN COST]** **Driver William Sylvester Gaskell (RASC, HQ PPA, age 26) and Lieutenant Duncan Baxter MacGillivray (1st Derbyshire Yeomanry, HQ PPA, age 32) are killed when HMS *Abdiel* is mined in Taranto harbour.** These are PPA's first two deaths in Italy and neither man was in combat. *Abdiel* struck ground mines laid hours earlier by German torpedo boats S-54 and S-61 and sank in about three minutes, killing men of the 1st Airborne Division and her own crew. [PPA Roll of Honour, Friends of PPA; TNA Discovery record C13444450 "HMS Abdiel (minelayer): 10 September 1943; sunk by enemy action; hit a mine in Taranto"; Airborne Assault Museum / ParaData] **Casualty totals for *Abdiel* differ between sources: one gives 58 airborne dead and 48 crew, another gives 150 killed and 154 wounded in total. Do not state a single figure; say "with heavy loss of life" or give the range and name the disagreement.**
- **11 to 14 September 1943** - Patrol visits Brindisi by 0600 on the 10th, Bari on the 11th, then strikes inland behind the German forces. **On 14 September they signal back the detailed ration strength of every German unit in Gravina, Altamura and Gioia del Colle, having captured a German quartermaster with his books.** Later a captured German tank officer, rattled, talks and reveals the identity and destination of his Panzer division. Patrol returns to Bari after **20 days** in enemy territory. **[POPSKI-1945]**
  - **The famous version of this, that PPA's scoop revealed the previously unknown weakness of the German 1st Parachute Division, is [MEMOIR] and nothing else.** The book has Popski and Corporal Cameron raid a German office, cosh an officer he calls "Major Schulz" with an SOE rubber truncheon, and find lying open on the desk the ration strength of 1st Parachute Division and attached troops, dated 12 September 1943, from which he concluded "not more than half their small total strength belonged to fighting units." **No corroboration exists. Corporal Cameron was killed on 21 June 1944 and could never confirm it.** **Watch the circularity: Wikipedia's wording comes from the foPPA plaque text, and foPPA's source is Popski's own 1945 Historical Summary. That is Popski's voice three times over, not three sources.** foPPA are blunt about it, saying the Historical Summary "is almost certainly the main source (rather than the later book) of subsequent discomfort at some of Popski's claims."
  - Patrol composition (Popski, Capt Yunnie, 12 other ranks, 3 Libyan Arab Force soldiers, 5 jeeps) is memoir-sourced; foPPA note the PPA Card Index lists 5 Arabs, not 3.
- **End of October 1943** - The front becomes continuous and mountainous; "no more scope for desert tactics." PPA pulls out after **47 days in action**. **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **9 December 1943, reorganisation. This is documented, not memoir.** **War Establishment NA/229/1 (XII/957/1)** takes effect, superseding the desert M.E.W.E. 866/1. **[DOC: Crown Copyright establishment table, transcribed by Friends of PPA]**

  | | HQ | 'B' | 'R' | 'S' | Total |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | All ranks, establishment | 18 | 16 | 16 | 16 | **66** |
  | **All ranks, including attached** | **26** | **18** | **18** | **18** | **80** |
  | **Jeeps (cars, 5-cwt, 4x4)** | **4** | **6** | **6** | **6** | **22** |

  Attached: an RAC gunner mechanic, 7 Royal Signals wireless operators, a signals electrician, an Army Catering Corps cook, and 4 REME vehicle mechanics. Other transport: 3 x 15-cwt GS, 2 x 15-cwt wireless, 4 x 3-ton lorries, 31 vehicles in all.

  **Weapons on establishment: 24 x .30-inch Browning and 24 x .50-inch Browning**, plus 62 x .45 automatic pistols, 42 x .303 rifles, 6 sniper rifles, 25 x .45 machine carbines, 8 x .303 LMG, 3 x 2-inch mortars.

  **This resolves the "16 versus 18 men per patrol" conflict: 16 was the establishment, 18 with attached specialists. Both figures are right.** Twenty-four of each Browning against 22 jeeps supports the standard claim of one .50 and one .30 per jeep plus spares.

  The HQ patrol was **'Blitz'**, four jeeps, the tactical forward HQ and signals relay between the fighting patrols and Base HQ. The three fighting patrols were **'B', 'R' and 'S'**. A fourth, **'P' Patrol**, appears in the November 1944 war diary doing the DUKW work.

  **Nuance worth having:** the earlier Tunisia-era fit was different. The memoir says he swapped the "clumsy, obsolete Vickers-K guns" for belt-fed Brownings and "mounted on alternate jeeps a .30 and a .50 gun." The every-jeep-has-both picture is the 1943 establishment, not the desert practice.
- **Winter 1943 to early 1944** - Actual strength "rose to over one hundred." **[POPSKI-1945; foPPA note: "Actual Establishment was 80 all ranks, but various strays were adopted along the way."]**
  - Popski's own weapons description of a patrol: **six .50 and six .30 machine guns on the jeeps**, 2 Bren guns, one bazooka, one 3-inch mortar, 8 Tommy guns, 8 carbines, 16 .45 pistols, grenades, explosives, mines, and **smoke generators fixed to the rear of the jeep and fired electrically from the dashboard**. **[POPSKI-1945]**
  - **Fighting personnel kept at 65% of total strength** by cutting batmen and clerks. **Average age in 1944 was 28.** All personnel trained parachutists; most learned Italian. Bulk from the UK, high proportion of Scots and northerners; pre-war trades listed as clerks, barmen, bus conductors, commercial travellers, farmers, miners and factory workers. **[POPSKI-1945]**
  - NCOs reverted to private and officers relinquished temporary rank on joining. Only one punishment: immediate dismissal from the unit. **[POPSKI-1945; Wikipedia]**
- **31 January 1944** - **[HUMAN COST]** Private John Hunter (Argyll & Sutherland Highlanders, SAS, 'B' Patrol), age 23, killed. Commemorated on the Cassino Memorial. [PPA Roll of Honour]
- **January to March 1944** - PPA operates with **Fifth Army on the Garigliano front** during the Anzio landings, small-scale operations on foot across the line. **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **June 1944** - A foot party lands behind the lines at the mouth of the **river Tenna**. Twelve jeeps aboard an LCT should have landed with them but **the craft broached to on a shoal and had to be abandoned and destroyed**. Two days later the party set out again overland in 10 jeeps, spent **four days with partisans building a track over the Sibillini mountains, five thousand feet up, one section so steep it could only be tackled in reverse, with the jeep loads manhandled over it.** They surprised the Germans on the far side, took **Camerino** and handed it to the partisans to hold. **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **21 June 1944** - **[HUMAN COST]** Corporal John Ewen Cameron (Lovat Scouts, 2 Commando, 'Blitz' Patrol), age 34, of Rannoch Station. Ancona War Cemetery. [PPA Roll of Honour]
- **End of July 1944** - PPA comes up against the **Gothic Line** and rejoins the main forces. **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **9 September 1944** - **[HUMAN COST]** Sergeant Charles Herbert Curtis (Royal Engineers, 'Blitz' Patrol), age 24, of Oxford. Montecchio War Cemetery. [PPA Roll of Honour]
- **28 October 1944** - **Porterforce operations begin.** Porterforce was a composite battlegroup of over **2,000 all ranks**, formed when most of I Canadian Corps went into Eighth Army reserve, tasked with holding the Adriatic right flank and taking Ravenna. Built around **Lt-Col Andrew Horsbrugh-Porter's 27th Lancers** with their Staghound armoured cars. **[DOC: G. W. L. Nicholson, *The Canadians in Italy 1943-1945*, Official History of the Canadian Army in the Second World War, vol. II, ch. XIX, which contains a section headed "The Operations of Porterforce, 28 October to 30 November"]** Order of battle as named across Canadian sources: 27th Lancers, The Royal Canadian Dragoons, The Governor General's Horse Guards, The Westminster Regiment (Motor), 12th Independent Machine Gun Company (Princess Louise Fusiliers), 1st, 2nd and 3rd Field Regiments RCA, 2nd and 5th Medium Regiments RCA, 12th and 13th Field Companies RCE, **Popski's Private Army**, and the Italian partisans of **Arrigo "Bulow" Boldrini's 28th Garibaldi Brigade**.
- **1 November 1944** - **Canadian corroboration of PPA, independent of Popski.** The Royal Canadian Dragoons' war record notes that they "were delighted to learn that Popski's Private Army was to take over the coastal flank." **[Canadian official-history-derived sources]**
- **20 November 1944** - **Boldrini makes his way to I Canadian Corps headquarters** to coordinate the liberation of Ravenna with the Canadians. He was later awarded the Gold Medal for Military Valour. On **2 December** his partisans attacked German communications and rear installations around Ravenna. **[Canadian official history]**
- **October to December 1944** - PPA held a front sector about four miles long from east of the Rimini-Ravenna road to the Adriatic, in flooded, wooded, canal-cut country, and used its three six-jeep patrols racing along forest tracks in the **Pineta di Classe**, firing mortars and Brownings, to make a very thin line look like a much larger force. **[DOC for the Porterforce command relationship: Peniakoff's DSO recommendation, Army Form W.3121, TNA WO 373/11/10, headed "PORTERFORCE", recommended by "(A.M. HORSEBRUGH-PORTER) Lt-Col, Comd PORTERFORCE", 25 Nov 44 - I read the document image directly. Narrative detail from Friends of PPA]**
- **First fortnight of November 1944** - The actions for which he got the DSO. From the citation, verbatim in substance: the area between Route 16 and the Adriatic, from the Savio to the Fiumi Uniti canal, was intersected by canals, densely wooded in places and flooded marsh for the rest. "Major Peniakoff during the first fortnight in November with three patrols has liquidated all enemy opposition in this area. Every operation was planned by him, and in the majority of cases he actually led the patrols which have resulted in **51 PW** and many enemy cas, and a retreat by the enemy from the SAVIO to the UNITI." When floods made operations seemingly impossible he **personally led a DUKW assault patrol and supervised the building of "a most unorthodox bridge" over the Ghiaia canal in the face of enemy Spandau fire**, which enabled a joint 27th Lancers and PPA raid that **captured 14 enemy in the village of Ghiaia**. The citation also records that "his own personality has so impressed itself upon the Partisans in the coastal sector that their activities under his guidance have proved quite exceptional." **[DOC: WO 373/11/10]**
  - **Two transcription discrepancies to resolve before broadcast.** Reading the scan directly I make the prisoner figure **51**; foPPA's transcription of the same document reads **31**. Likewise I read the recommending signature date as **25 Nov 44**; foPPA read **23 November 1944**. **Both are handwriting-legibility problems on the same sheet. Use "more than thirty prisoners" and "late November 1944" and you are safe either way.**
  - foPPA note that November 1944 "was the only time that all three main fighting patrols operated together."
- **10 November 1944, PPA War Diary, verbatim** - "'P' patrol is evacuated. Three GERMAN prisoners were taken, one of them an Officer. We sustained two casualties killed, Sjt DAVE PORTER and Tpr CROGHAN." **[DOC: PPA War Diary, TNA WO 170/3962B, extract transcribed by foPPA]**
- **5 to 9 November 1944, PPA War Diary** - 5 Nov: "'P' patrol start training with jeeps and DUKW's." 8 Nov: "'P' patrol finished DUKW training and arrived at PPA HQ at SAVIO. 'P' patrol set out on amphibious operation." 9 Nov: "'P' patrol landed on beach at M2767 at 0013hrs." **[DOC: PPA War Diary]** The memoir adds that jeeps were lifted into the DUKWs, one DUKW carried a crane, DUKW speed was "under five knots," and for longer distances the loaded DUKWs were themselves carried inside LCTs.
- **10 November 1944** - **[HUMAN COST]** **Trooper Thomas John Croghan (3rd Hussars, 'R' Patrol), age 23, of Chester, and Sergeant David Porter DCM (Royal Engineers, SAS, 'R' Patrol), age 21, of St Helens, both killed the same day. Both buried in Ravenna War Cemetery, graves V.A.9 and V.A.10, side by side.** Porter's DCM is the single Distinguished Conduct Medal in the unit's honours list. [PPA Roll of Honour]
- **Late November 1944, Caserma dei Fiumi Uniti** - A German-held mediaeval watch tower at the river mouth covering the approaches to Ravenna. A small party watched it from under bushes for two days until they knew the garrison's habits. Then **fifteen men landed at night, hid in a barn forty yards away, and at 0830, the moment the Germans called everyone in for breakfast, walked in behind them and took the entire post without a sound.** Around midday a party from a neighbouring post strolled up and "were gathered in." At dusk a strong patrol led by an officer came cautiously forward, reached the door, and was likewise overpowered. **"These mysterious disappearances disheartened the Germans to such an extent that during the night all remaining enemy posts south of the river were withdrawn."** Next morning jeeps were ferried across in DUKWs and Ravenna was entered at midday. **[POPSKI-1945]** **This is your single best comedic set-piece and it is in his official report, not just the book.**
- **Also in this phase** - **Fossa di Ghiaio was intimidated into surrender by bringing up ten jeeps under cover of morning mist and opening fire at close range with twenty .50 machine guns.** Five other positions yielding **76 prisoners** were taken without a shot by "Cloak and Dagger" methods. **Jeeps were towed by oxen along flooded roads.** **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **The basilica** - Eighth Army intelligence believed the Germans were using the bell tower of the sixth-century **Basilica di Sant'Apollinare in Classe** as an artillery observation post, and heavy guns were to be brought up to demolish it. Lt-Col **John Willett**, an Eighth Army intelligence officer and Popski's friend, tipped him off. **Popski persuaded the gunners to hold off 24 hours and went in at night with partisans to check. They found only locals. The guns were not needed and the basilica, a UNESCO World Heritage site today, survived.** Popski called it his "first act of virtue in a long career of destruction." **He was later made an Honorary Citizen of Ravenna.** [Friends of PPA, "The Wixoe Memorial"; the quote is Popski's own, so **[MEMOIR]**. The honorary citizenship and the 1950 presentation of a Franta Belsky bronze bust of Popski to the City of Ravenna, displayed in the Palazzo Pubblico, are checkable physical facts.]
- **4 December 1944** - **Ravenna liberated. Confirmed by the Canadian official history.** On the night of 3 to 4 December local partisans reported the city undefended. The 4th Princess Louise Dragoon Guards outflanked it, a blown bridge stopped the tanks, Canadian infantry marched in, and the PLDGs, the partisans and the 27th Lancers "all reached the city centre at about the same time." A photograph of 27th Lancers men in Ravenna is dated 5 December 1944. **[Nicholson vol. II, ch. XIX, section "The Capture of Ravenna, 4 December"]** **Ignore any "5 November 1944" liberation date in circulation. It is wrong.**
- **9 December 1944, about 1130 hrs, north of Ravenna, map ref 588480 / M 5847** - **Peniakoff loses his left hand. This is fully documented. The date, place, time and circumstances are all in the record.**
  - **PPA War Diary, 9 December 1944, verbatim: [DOC, TNA WO 170/3962B]**
    > "The Commanding Officer Major V. PENIAKOFF went on a forward recce at 588480 at 1130 hrs he was wounded in the left hand, right hand and ear. One ITALIAN officer also wounded. Both are evacuated under fire by Dvr BURROWS the C.O's driver in a jeep. The same day 'B' patrol gives covering fire over same bridge with 6 jeeps, each jeep firing separately until its ammunition ran out and then withdrawing to be replaced by another jeep. This action against the enemy who were taking cover on the far side of the canal 30 yards away successfully enabled two dismounted troops of 27 LANCERS to withdraw. Cpl GALLOWAY wounded. Major PENIAKOFF'S left hand is amputated. Capt CANERI takes over."
  - **So the "thirty yards" and the "going to the relief of surrounded 27th Lancers" are the war diary's own words, not Popski's embellishment.** Two contemporary award citations corroborate, both signed by Horsbrugh-Porter, both TNA WO 373/11. **Driver E. Burrows, MM** (London Gazette 10 May 1945, issue 37072 p. 2454): "His CO returned from his recce severely wounded, and with perfect coolness Dvr. BURROWS drove him back to safety, together with another casualty." **Corporal D. Galloway, MM** (same Gazette page): "'B' patrol, PPA, were ordered to go to the relief of a dismounted posn of 'D' Sqn 27L in the area M 5847 who were being strongly attacked by the enemy." Galloway left his jeep under mortar and machine-gun fire to pull a Lancer out of the marshes, was shot in the leg, kept firing for ten minutes, then fainted from blood loss.
  - **[MEMOIR] the rifle grenade.** Neither the war diary nor either citation names the weapon. Only the book does: "One of them leant against the parapet, stuck a rifle grenade into the muzzle of his rifle and took a slow aim at us... something that made no noticeable noise squashed and shattered my left wrist." The memoir also records wounds the summaries omit, a bullet hole through the right palm with the fourth finger broken and a split right ear, gives the date as 9 December 1944, and identifies the Germans as "two companies of an S.S. Battalion, arrived the day before."
  - **The other casualty evacuated on Burrows's jeep was Corporal Len Greetham of the 27th Lancers**, himself awarded an MM for the action, who survived the war and later supported the PPA veterans' association. **That makes this one of the very few PPA episodes with a living independent witness.**
  - **The blackly funny detail, still true:** the summary report recorded that "the only casualty of the patrol was one man slightly wounded." **The one man was the commanding officer and he had just lost a hand.** [Allen Parfitt, quoting the report]
  - **The "fifty minutes", "25,000 rounds" and "eighty Germans dead" come from that same summary report, not from the war diary. Treat them as reported claims.**
- **18 December 1944** - PPA withdrawn, "having been continuously in action for three months and twenty six days." Popski's summary of this phase: **"In these operations P.P.A. had never more than 45 men engaged. The forces opposing it were 2 Battalions, 45 square miles of territory were cleared and the enemy lost 40 killed (approx) and 152 prisoners. P.P.A. losses were 3 killed and 5 wounded."** **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **Winter 1944-45** - **Jean Caneri** takes command in Popski's absence. He found the unit a camp in the Apennines, hired Alpine ski instructors and taught them all to ski, and deliberately made sure no transport was available to them. [Parfitt, drawing on the memoir. **[MEMOIR]**]
- **30 December 1944** - The DSO is **announced**. **That is three weeks after he lost his hand, which is the entire source of the confusion. See Myth vs Record.**
- **26 April 1945** - **DSO gazetted**, "in recognition of gallant and distinguished services in Italy": "Major (temporary) Vladimir PENIAKOFF, M.C. (159661), General List." **[DOC: London Gazette, supplement 37051, dated 24 April, published 26 April 1945, p. 2216, under the heading "The Distinguished Service Order", War Office 26th April 1945 - read directly. Recommendation approved by Lt-Gen R. L. McCreery, GOC Eighth Army]**

### The final phase and after

- **21 April 1945** - Operations begin in the **Po di Volano** country; one patrol captures 22 prisoners, and the next day 27 more. **[POPSKI-1945]**
  - **GEOGRAPHY WARNING, do not get this wrong.** The memoir says PPA "fought around Lake Comacchio" for seven days from 21 April, with the 27th Lancers and the 28th Garibaldi Brigade, and everyone repeats it. **But the war diary's own place names for those days are Massa Fiscaglia, Iolanda di Savoia, Mezzo Goro, Ariano Ferrarese and Coccanile, which lie west and north of Lake Comacchio, beyond the Argenta Gap, not on the lake.** "Around Lake Comacchio" is Popski's shorthand for the general watery sector.
  - **And the famous Comacchio fighting was not PPA at all.** **Operation Roast, 1 to 2 April 1945**, was **2 Commando Brigade** on the spit between the lake and the Adriatic. The genuine PPA connection to that area is much narrower: COPP records place one COPP officer, Derrick Freeman, on a night-navigation raid with PPA near the Po mouth in early April 1945. **Never put PPA in Operation Roast.**
- **23 April 1945** - **[HUMAN COST]** Two patrols bridged the Volano canal and chased the enemy to Iolanda di Savoia. **"Another patrol ran into an ambush and lost its C.O. and his gunner, killed by a bazooka"** (foPPA corrects the weapon to the German equivalent, a **Panzerfaust**). Cross-referencing the Roll of Honour identifies them: **Lieutenant Ian Wallace McCallum (27th Lancers, 'B' Patrol), age 21, son of William and Gwendoline McCallum of Buenos Aires, and Sapper Richard MacDowall (Royal Engineers, 'B' Patrol), age 24, of Edinburgh. Both are buried in Argenta Gap War Cemetery, graves II.E.19 and II.E.20, next to each other.** **[POPSKI-1945 for the event; PPA Roll of Honour for the names and graves]**
- **26 April 1945** - Three PPA patrols and Blitz sail from **Porto Corsini** in six **RCLs (Ramped Cargo Lighters)**, landing at the mouth of the Po. **PPA War Diary: "6 RCLs with minesweeper escort sail at 0600 hrs, landing at mouth of PO at 1100 hrs, landing unopposed, local partisans having occupied the area beforehand." [DOC]** The memoir agrees and adds that from that day "P.P.A. operated in its own watery domain, on the canals, river deltas and lagoons which stretch from the mouth of the Po to Venice." **Independently corroborated by IWM oral history 14884, the boat crews' own account.**
  - **"Popski's Private Navy" is NOT Popski's phrase.** It appears **zero times** in the 1950 memoir. It is the veterans' association's coinage. **The substance behind it is real and rather good:** seven Royal Engineers of an Inland Water Transport Company were **all seven Mentioned in Despatches**, which foPPA call "possibly uniquely for an entire attached section," for running PPA's jeeps at night on shallow-draught lighters up the Adriatic and round the Po delta, "disentangling mines from their propellers as they went."
  - **Unit-number discrepancy:** foPPA say **945 Inland Water Transport Company RE**; IWM's record for their officer, **Lieutenant Brian Ewart Thomas**, says **947 IWT Coy RE** for the PPA attachment (March to May 1945). **Popski gets it wrong a third way and calls Thomas RASC**, which foPPA explicitly correct. **Say "an Inland Water Transport company of the Royal Engineers" and move on.**
- **27 April 1945** - The Adige crossed; **292 prisoners** captured. **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **27 to 29 April 1945, Chioggia** - The Brenta crossed and the German garrison of Chioggia **bluffed into surrendering**. In Popski's own official words: they "were somewhat disconcerted when they discovered they had surrendered to 12 men in 5 jeeps. They cheered up a little when the rest of P.P.A. drove in later in the day, but they kept on asking when the troops would arrive." **[POPSKI-1945]** **This is the best line in the whole file and it sits in his official report to the King, not just the book.**
  - **What is documented: PPA War Diary, 29 April 1945: "'R' and 'B' Patrols at CHIOGGIA disarming Garrison." [DOC]** Note that the diary describes *disarming*, which follows a surrender rather than constituting it. foPPA add that the operation involved **'Blitz' Patrol with OSS Lieutenant George Hearn and 'R' Patrol**, so **an American OSS officer was present**, a detail that drops out of every popular retelling.
  - **Who actually did the talking. [MEMOIR]** **Lieutenant Henry "Steve" Wallbridge** went into the town **under a flag of truce** with a demand to surrender within 24 hours or be bombed, and spent 24 hours at the German HQ. **Caneri** then received the German commander's representative and, "concealing the fact that his whole force on the spot consisted of nine men in three jeeps," talked for five hours until the man went back recommending unconditional surrender. **The popular retelling compresses the two into one. Wallbridge carried the flag; Caneri did the bluffing.**
  - **Garrison strength is memoir-sourced and varies between his own two accounts:** the 1945 summary says 17 officers and 670 other ranks with three batteries of 88mm guns, coastal batteries, 120 20mm guns and three months' supplies; the 1950 book says 700 men, two batteries of 88mm field guns, one coastal battery and 120 heavy machine guns.
  - **CREDIT DISPUTE, handle carefully.** The Allied campaign chronology records that on **30 April 1945 "Chioggia fell to the Italian Cremona Group."** Chioggia's own civic memory (ANPI, the Comune, local press) dates the town's liberation to **27 April 1945** and credits the local CLN, after the town illuminated itself on the night of 26 April to avert a threatened Allied bombing. **All three can be true at once. The safe script line is that PPA took the surrender of the German garrison, not that PPA liberated Chioggia.**
- **29 April 1945** - **[HUMAN COST]** Trooper Arthur Rogers (56th Reconnaissance Regiment, 'B' Patrol), age 24, of Cheshunt. Padua War Cemetery. [PPA Roll of Honour]
- **1 May 1945** - Private Eric Charles Watkins (RASC, 'Blitz' Patrol), age 45, died five months after leaving PPA. Padua War Cemetery. [PPA Roll of Honour, listed under "Others"]
- **6 May 1945** - A PPA patrol enters **Tarvisio** on the Austrian border. **7 May 1945**, the war with Germany is over. **[POPSKI-1945]**
- **10 May 1945** - **[HUMAN COST] Private Albert "Chalky" White (Oxford & Buckinghamshire Light Infantry, 'S' Patrol), age 31, of Hockley Heath, is killed by Tito's Yugoslav partisans, two days after VE Day, while Sergeant Sizer's half of 'S' Patrol was attempting to "establish good relations" with them. Udine War Cemetery.** [Friends of PPA, "Myths and Goofs," and PPA Roll of Honour, which independently confirms the date, unit and cemetery] **This is the most important somber beat in the episode. The war in Europe was over and he was shot by an ally.**
- **23 May 1945** - **[HUMAN COST]** Corporal James Snape (50th Royal Tank Regiment, 'Blitz' Patrol), age 34, of Chorley. Klagenfurt War Cemetery, Austria. Also after VE Day. [PPA Roll of Honour]
- **1 to 2 May 1945, Venice** - PPA brought five jeeps up the lagoon from Chioggia in three landing craft and put them ashore in Venice. **The trip itself is independently verified. The lap of Piazza San Marco is memoir-only. The "only wheeled vehicles ever" superlative is not verified and Popski never asserted it as a fact.**
  - **Independent evidence the jeeps went to Venice: [DOC-adjacent, two sources, neither of them Popski]**
    1. **IWM, PENIAKOFF VLADIMIR (COLONEL) COLLECTION, catalogue number 2012-03-15**, ninety original black and white prints from Peniakoff's own archive. The catalogue description explicitly lists **"Passing through Venice (1-2nd May 1945)."** **So photographs of PPA in Venice exist in a national collection. They are not currently online, and the description does not say Piazza San Marco.**
    2. **IWM Oral History 14884, Lieutenant Brian Ewart Thomas RE**, the landing-craft officer Popski names in the book, recorded 23 January 1995, fifty years later and independently of the memoir. Reel 4 covers "liberation of Chioggia; arrival of Lieutenant-Colonel Vladimir 'Popski' Peniakoff; **reception from Italians in liberated Venice; threat of magnetic mines in lagoon; end of operation, 2/5/1945.**" **A second participant confirms the landing craft, the mines, the trip and the date.**
  - **Venice was already liberated when PPA arrived.** **2/5 Queen's, 169 Brigade, 56th Division entered Venice and accepted its unconditional surrender at 1800 on 29 April 1945.** Thomas's oral history says "liberated Venice." **Never imply PPA liberated Venice or drove in as the city fell.** The memoir does not claim it either and calls the drive "a purposeless piece of swagger."
- **Post-VE-Day** - PPA disarmed Italian partisans and discouraged Tito's partisans from encroaching on Austrian and Italian territory. The memoir independently describes meeting Soviet **Major Lykov** and his red-flagged tank in a valley east of Klagenfurt, and forcing a way through eight hours of three routed German divisions in the Wolfsberg gorge. **The unit finished in Carinthia, Austria**: the IWM Peniakoff Collection lists a **"disbandment parade at Rosegg, Austria (May 1945)"** and **"POWs captured at the Austrian border (8-9th May 1945)"**, Corporal Snape is buried at Klagenfurt, and the memorial's campaign line reads "1942 - LIBYA - TUNISIA - ITALY - AUSTRIA - 1945."
  - **[NOT FOUND] The Himmler hunt.** The claim that PPA hunted for Himmler is everywhere, including Wikipedia. **The word "Himmler" appears zero times in the 1950 memoir.** The claim originates in foPPA's abridged plaque text and passed from there into Wikipedia. **No record, citation, war diary extract or independent source supports it. Do not put PPA on the Himmler hunt.**
- **14 September 1945** - **PPA disbanded.** **[POPSKI-1945 states the date and IWM object record 30070991 gives it independently. Note however that the war diary series ends at WO 170/7362, July 1945, so the diary does not cover disbandment, and the memoir does not give the date. Treat the exact day as association-sourced; the month is solid.]** Peniakoff was afterwards promoted **Lieutenant-Colonel** and served as **British-Russian liaison officer in Vienna**.
- **7 December 1946** - Peniakoff **naturalised as a British subject**, certificate **AZ21000**. **[DOC: TNA HO 334/167/21000, "Naturalisation Certificate: Vladimir Peniakoff. From Belgium. Resident in London."]** **He fought the entire war as a Belgian citizen in British uniform and only became British fifteen months after it ended.**
- **Summer 1946** - Peniakoff is the guest of **Paolo Caccia Dominioni** in Italy. They work out that they had both been in the main street of **Derna on 6 August 1942**, on opposite sides. "What should I have done had we seen each other?" asked Dominioni. **"There's not much doubt about that,"** Peniakoff replied. [Caccia Dominioni, *El Alamein 1933-1962*, Longanesi 1962; English edition *Alamein 1933-1962: An Italian Story*, George Allen & Unwin, 1966. **[MEMOIR, but his friend's memoir, not Popski's]**]
- **1947** - Made a Belgian **Officier de l'Ordre de la Couronne avec Palme** and awarded the Belgian **Croix de guerre avec Palme**. [Wikipedia]
- **2 April 1948** - Married **Pamela Firth** in Chelsea. [Wikipedia citing *The Times*]
- **1950** - *Private Army* published by Jonathan Cape. Sold very well, reprinted repeatedly, translated into nine languages. Second edition 1951 with a foreword by General Sir John Hackett. [Wikipedia; Friends of PPA]
- **15 May 1951** - **Peniakoff dies of a brain tumour at the National Hospital for Neurology and Neurosurgery, Queen Square, Bloomsbury, London, aged 54.** Buried in the graveyard of **St Leonard's Church, Wixoe, Suffolk**. [Wikipedia; Friends of PPA "Wixoe Memorial" page] **Both the hospital and the burial place are checkable; the cause of death should ideally be confirmed against a death certificate or the Times obituary before being asserted flatly.**

## Key figures

- **Lieutenant-Colonel Vladimir "Popski" Peniakoff, DSO, MC, FRGS** (1897-1951), service number 159661, General List. Founder and CO of No. 1 Demolition Squadron, PPA. Belgian-born, Russian-Jewish parentage, Egyptian sugar-factory engineer, fluent Arabic. **Note carefully: he attained the rank of Lieutenant-Colonel, not Colonel and certainly not General. Caroline Moorehead's biography of Martha Gellhorn calls him "General Popski" and that is wrong.**
- **Lieutenant-Colonel (later General Sir) John Winthrop Hackett** - OC Special Forces, Middle East, in 1942. The officer who conceived and authorised the unit, and, in the memoir version, the man who supplied its name. Later wrote the foreword to the 1951 second edition of *Private Army*.
- **Captain W. B. "Bill" Kennedy Shaw** - Intelligence Officer of the LRDG. Credited with coining the personal nickname "Popski." **[MEMOIR: this attribution rests on Peniakoff, 1950, p. 94]**
- **Major (later Lt-Col) J. R. "Jake" Easonsmith** - LRDG. Commanded the Barce raid and took Peniakoff and two Senussi guides in to reconnoitre the town. **[DOC: NZ Official History]**
- **Captain N. P. "Nick" Wilder** - LRDG T1 patrol. Found **Wilder's Gap**, 12 January 1943. **[DOC: NZ Official History]**
- **Lieutenant Ron Tinker** - LRDG T2 patrol. Did the joint Tebaga Gap reconnaissance with Peniakoff and got the survivors out after Ksar Rhilane. **[DOC: NZ Official History]**
- **Captain Robert Park "Bob" Yunnie, MC** - Popski's second in command, leader of 'B' Patrol. Wrote *Warriors on Wheels* (Hutchinson, 1959), later retitled *Fighting with Popski's Private Army*. His patrol came to Popski's rescue when he lost his hand.
- **Major Jean Max Charles Caneri** (service no. 191277) - Founding officer, took command of PPA when Popski was evacuated. **[DOC: TNA WO 373/9/335, award recommendation]**
- **Lieutenant-Colonel Andrew Marshall Horsbrugh-Porter** - CO of the 27th Lancers. **"Porterforce" was named after him.** He personally wrote and signed Peniakoff's DSO recommendation on 25 November 1944. **[DOC: WO 373/11/10]**
- **Arrigo Boldrini, "Bulow"** - Commander of the Italian partisans of the **28th Garibaldi Brigade**, part of Porterforce at Ravenna.
- **Lieutenant-General Sir Richard McCreery** - GOC Eighth Army, approved the DSO. **[DOC: WO 373/11/10]**
- **General Sir Harold Alexander** - C-in-C Middle East Forces, signed off the MC. **[DOC: WO 373/46/37]**
- **Count Paolo Caccia Dominioni di Sillavengo** (1896-1992) - Italian architect, engineer and officer. Peniakoff's pre-war friend in Egypt. Drew the astrolabe bookplate in 1927 that became the PPA cap badge. **Commanded the Italian 31° Battaglione Guastatori (combat engineers) at El Alamein, on the Axis side.** After the war he spent from 1948 to 1962 recovering the remains of the fallen at El Alamein, Axis and Allied alike, and designed and built the Italian memorial there.
- **Lieutenant-Colonel John Willett** - Eighth Army intelligence officer, possibly the youngest Lt-Col in the army at 27. Tipped Popski off about the basilica. Later wrote *Popski: A Life of Vladimir Peniakoff* (MacGibbon & Kee, 1954), the standard biography, based on interviews with Popski's surviving Jewish relatives.
- **Sergeant David Porter, DCM** - Royal Engineers and SAS, 'R' Patrol. Killed 10 November 1944, age 21. Holder of the unit's only DCM.

- **Establishment in Italy: 80 all ranks.** **[POPSKI-1945; foPPA note: "Actual Establishment was 80 all ranks, but various strays were adopted along the way"]**
- **Actual strength: "the strength rose to over one hundred."** **[POPSKI-1945]** Wikipedia renders this as "about 100 men actually deployed at any one time."

HQ 18 on establishment, 26 with attached. Grand total 66 on establishment, 80 including attached. [DOC]**
- So Popski's "16 men each in 6 jeeps" and Wikipedia's "18" are the same unit counted two ways. **Say "sixteen men on paper, eighteen with the signallers and mechanics attached."**
- **Weapons on establishment: 24 x .50-inch Browning and 24 x .30-inch Browning across 22 jeeps.** That supports the standard "one of each per jeep, plus spares."

## Absurd / comedic beats (all real)

1. **The unit's official name contains a joke about itself.** The full official designation was **No. 1 Demolition Squadron, PPA**. The PPA stood for Popski's Private Army, which had started as staff-officer sarcasm. HQ Middle East formally altered the designation to include it. **[POPSKI-1945]**
2. **The name came from a complaint about pay.** In Popski's 1945 account, he came back from five months behind enemy lines to find his unit disbanded and his pay forgotten, and when he complained he was met with "ribald remarks about disappearing into the 'blue' to run a private war with a private army for his private pleasure and expecting to be paid for his fun." **[POPSKI-1945]** The book gives a different, snappier version. See Myth vs Record.
3. **The cap badge was drawn by the enemy.** The astrolabe was Popski's own bookplate, drawn for him in 1927 by his Italian friend Paolo Caccia Dominioni, who in 1942 was commanding an Italian combat engineer battalion at El Alamein. **[Chiarvetto, from Caccia Dominioni's own 1962 memoir]**
4. **The astrolabe is also, navigationally speaking, useless.** foPPA note dryly that astrolabes were not used for desert navigation in the Second World War. That was done with theodolites, radio time signals and the Bagnold sun compass. The badge is romance, not equipment.
5. **The unit was smaller than the platoon it replaced.** A "squadron" of twenty-three men, six vehicles, commanded by a major. [DOC: the establishment figures]
6. **Six hundred Italians surrendered to three men.** At Sened, 19 March 1943, nearly 600 Italians trapped in a wadi with no way out "surrendered to a Sergeant and two men." **[POPSKI-1945]**
7. **They traded a wreck for a new truck because the Americans wanted a souvenir.** At Sened they recovered their own truck, lost to the Germans near Tripoli two months earlier, still carrying the unit sign, dilapidated but running, and bartered it to an American unit for a brand-new three-quarter-ton Dodge. **[POPSKI-1945]**
8. **The breakfast raid.** Fifteen men watched a German-held watchtower for two days, landed at night, hid in a barn forty yards away, and walked in at 0830 the instant the garrison was called to breakfast, taking the whole post without a sound. Then a second German party wandered up and was "gathered in." Then at dusk a strong patrol led by an officer approached very cautiously, reached the door, and was also captured. The Germans, unable to work out where everyone was going, abandoned every remaining post south of the river overnight. **[POPSKI-1945]**
9. **The oxen.** Jeeps were towed by oxen along flooded roads in the Ravenna sector. **[POPSKI-1945]**
10. **Twenty machine guns and a bluff.** Fossa di Ghiaio was "intimidated into surrender" by ten jeeps coming up under morning mist and opening fire at close range with twenty .50 machine guns. **[POPSKI-1945]**
11. **"They kept on asking when the troops would arrive."** The Chioggia garrison of 687 men with 88mm batteries surrendered to twelve men in five jeeps and were "somewhat disconcerted" to discover it. **[POPSKI-1945]**
12. **The war diary understatement.** After the action in which Popski's left hand was blown off and a bullet went through his right, the report recorded "the only casualty of the patrol was one man slightly wounded." [Parfitt, quoting the report]
13. **The ski school.** With Popski in hospital, Jean Caneri took the unit to a camp in the Apennines, hired Alpine ski instructors, taught them all to ski, and made certain no transport was available to them. **[MEMOIR]**
14. **One flame-thrower jeep, built out of spite.** foPPA record that exactly one Wasp flame-thrower jeep was ever built in PPA, reportedly because Popski, a trained engineer, had been told it could not be done. It turned out more dangerous to its own operator, Trooper Nick Hubbard, than to the enemy, and was last seen in the disbandment parade photographs. **Two published historians, William Seymour and Julian Thompson, wrongly state there were several.**
15. **The badge went to the King.** Popski's 1945 Historical Summary was written at the command of George VI, who read it almost immediately, and **a specimen cap badge was sent with it.** foPPA suggest it may still be in the Royal Archives.

## Human-cost beats (play these completely straight)

**Rule for this section: no jokes in the same breath, no comic music, no ironic framing. Hold the frame.**

1. **The twelve.** Twelve men of Popski's Private Army were killed while serving with the unit. The full Roll of Honour, with ages and burial places, is in the Verified Timeline above and in the SOURCES. They were, in date order: Gaskell and MacGillivray (10 Sept 1943), Hunter (31 Jan 1944), Cameron (21 June 1944), Curtis (9 Sept 1944), Croghan and Porter (10 Nov 1944), MacDowall and McCallum (23 April 1945), Rogers (29 April 1945), White (10 May 1945), Snape (23 May 1945). Three more men associated with PPA are commemorated by the CWGC, having died after leaving the unit or after disbandment.
2. **The first two never fired a shot.** Driver William Gaskell, 26, and Lieutenant Duncan MacGillivray, 32, died on 10 September 1943 when HMS *Abdiel* struck a mine in Taranto harbour and sank in about three minutes, hours after PPA's jeeps had come ashore. The mines had been laid by German torpedo boats as they left the harbour. Gaskell's body was never recovered; he is commemorated on the Brookwood Memorial in England, MacGillivray on the Cassino Memorial in Italy.
2b. **On his dead, he was accurate.** Popski's own 1950 Roll of Honour lists **the same twelve names** that the veterans' association arrived at decades later from personnel records, the war diary and CWGC registers. **In a story about a man whose numbers inflated, this one did not. Worth saying out loud.**

3. **Two graves side by side at Ravenna.** Trooper Thomas Croghan, 23, of Chester, and Sergeant David Porter DCM, 21, of St Helens, were killed on the same day, 10 November 1944, and lie in adjacent graves, Ravenna War Cemetery V.A.9 and V.A.10. Porter held the only Distinguished Conduct Medal the unit ever won.
4. **Two more, side by side at Argenta Gap.** On 23 April 1945, with the war two weeks from ending, a PPA patrol drove into an ambush and lost its commander and his gunner to a Panzerfaust. Lieutenant Ian McCallum was 21, the son of a family in Buenos Aires. Sapper Richard MacDowall was 24, from Edinburgh. They are buried next to each other, Argenta Gap War Cemetery II.E.19 and II.E.20.
5. **Killed by an ally, after the war ended.** Private Albert "Chalky" White, 31, of Hockley Heath, was killed on **10 May 1945, two days after VE Day**, when part of 'S' Patrol tried to open friendly contact with Tito's Yugoslav partisans and was fired on. He is buried in Udine War Cemetery. Corporal James Snape, 34, of Chorley, died on 23 May 1945 and is buried at Klagenfurt in Austria. **The unit's last deaths came after the fighting was supposed to be over.**
6. **The people whose country it was.** Peniakoff's entire desert reputation rested on the Senussi Bedouin of Cyrenaica, who guided him, hid him, ran his road-watch network and carried his intelligence. His own MC citation records that he lived for months "in constant danger of being given away by ARABS." What had happened to those people before he arrived is the darkest material in this story and it is thoroughly documented:
   - Between 1929 and 1934 the Italian colonial authorities **deported the population of the Jebel Akhdar into sixteen concentration camps** on the coast, to cut the guerrillas off from their civilian base. Governor-General **Pietro Badoglio** wrote on 20 June 1930 that he did not hide from himself that the measure meant the ruin of the population, and that the path must be followed to the end even if the whole population of Cyrenaica should perish. [Giorgio Rochat, quoted in Costantino Di Sante, *Italia contemporanea* 252-253, 2008, from the Graziani papers in the Archivio centrale dello Stato]
   - Mussolini instructed Badoglio by confidential circular **not to use the term "concentration camps"** in public but to say "collection camps" or "encampments of the submitted." [Di Sante]
   - Recorded internee numbers from Rodolfo Graziani's own returns: **Marsa Brega 21,119, Soluch 20,123, Sidi Ahmed el-Magrun 13,050, Agedabia 10,000, El Agheila 9,400, El Abiar 3,123**, plus smaller camps. Over 90,000 in total in those returns. [Angelo Del Boca, *Gli italiani in Libia* vol. II, p. 182, quoted in Di Sante]
   - Graziani's own general Cicconetti reported that **at least 100,000 people were deported, of whom only 85,000 reached the camps.** [Gustavo Ottolenghi, quoted in Di Sante] **The perpetrators' own paperwork records roughly fifteen thousand people dying on the way.**
   - **Death toll estimates differ and must not be averaged. Giorgio Rochat and Di Sante: not fewer than 40,000 dead, of whom Italian sources put 6,500 as killed in military operations 1923-1931, the rest dying on the marches and in the camps. Ali Abdullatif Ahmida (*Genocide in Libya*, Routledge, 2020): 60,000 to 70,000 dead, with Cyrenaica's population falling from 225,000 to 142,000.** Ahmida also dates the deportations from 1929, based on survivor testimony, against the archival historians' spring 1930. That is a live scholarly dispute, not an error.
   - **Documented decline inside single camps: Soluch fell from 20,123 to 15,830 internees in one year; Sidi Ahmed el-Magrun from 13,050 to 10,197.** In March 1933 the regional commissioner reported that at Soluch, of 13,000 internees, **3,400 had contracted typhus**. [Del Boca and the Graziani papers, via Di Sante]
   - **The herds died with the people.** Roughly 600,000 animals were driven into the camps. Around 80% of cattle and camels and over 90% of sheep, goats and horses died. Camels went from 39,000 head in 1930 to 11,500 in 1933. **For a pastoral society the herds were the economy, the food and the culture.** [Rochat via Di Sante; E. E. Evans-Pritchard, *The Sanusi of Cyrenaica*, Clarendon Press, 1949]
   - **Omar al-Mukhtar**, the Senussi shaykh and Qur'an teacher who had led the resistance since 1923, was captured wounded on **11 September 1931**, tried by the Special Tribunal of Benghazi on 15 September, and **hanged on 16 September 1931 in the Soluch concentration camp, in front of 20,000 Bedouin brought in from the surrounding camps to watch.** Badoglio had recommended in a "most secret" telegram that the execution be carried out inside one of the large native concentration camps. [Evans-Pritchard; Di Sante, citing Del Boca and Roman Rainero]
   - **The wire.** Between mid-April and September 1931 Italy built a barbed-wire barrier **270 km from Porto Bardia to Giarabub** along the Egyptian frontier, at a cost of 19 million lire, to cut off all supply to the rebellion. **It was built by 2,500 workers, in large part Cyrenaicans interned in the concentration camps.** [*Il reticolato confinario*, Government of Cyrenaica, Benghazi, 1932, via Di Sante] Evans-Pritchard gives the figure that makes the point in one line: **Italian expenditure on barbed wire in Cyrenaica rose from 27,000 lire in 1922-23 to 14,111,000 lire in 1930-31.**
   - **Evans-Pritchard, who served as a British Tribal Affairs Officer in Cyrenaica and wrote the standard academic account, states that Bedouin known to have given the British special assistance were shot by the Italians when the British withdrew.** **NOT FOUND: any number, name, place or date for these reprisals. Do not invent one. Say it happened, attribute it to Evans-Pritchard, and stop.**
   - **The promise.** Anthony Eden told the Commons on **8 January 1942**: "His Majesty's Government are determined that at the end of the war the Senussis in Cyrenaica will in no circumstances again fall under Italian domination." [HC Deb 08 January 1942, vol 377, cc77-8W] **That promise was kept, and it was also much less than independence. Libya did not become independent until 24 December 1951, under Idris I.** Peniakoff had been dead seven months.
7. **The Italian partisans.** PPA fought alongside Arrigo Boldrini's 28th Garibaldi Brigade at Ravenna, and the Popski memorial at the National Memorial Arboretum carries a plaque specifically commemorating the Italian partisans. **NOT FOUND: partisan casualty figures for the Ravenna operations specifically. Do not supply one.**

## Myth vs record (corrections)

1. **"Educated at Cambridge."**
   - *Popular telling:* Peniakoff was educated at St John's College, Cambridge.
   - *Actual:* He was in residence for **seventeen months, Lent term 1915 to June 1916, and took no degree.** He had already completed two years and two diplomas at the Université Libre de Bruxelles. St John's own College magazine published an article by a Fellow expressly to correct the "educated at Cambridge" shorthand. [*The Eagle* vol. 92, article by Dr John Alexander]

2. **The 450,000 gallons of petrol. This is the biggest one.**
   - *Popular telling:* Popski destroyed 450,000 gallons of enemy fuel, and this figure is often attached to his pre-PPA desert raiding or to PPA itself.
   - *Actual, in three documented layers:*
     - **His MC recommendation, written by the CO of the LRDG and signed by General Alexander in 1942, credits him with 50,000 gallons at Giovanni Berta on 17 August 1942.**
     - **His own 1945 Historical Summary says "over 200,000 gallons of petrol in several dumps"** for the May-to-August 1942 period.
     - **The same summary's North Africa results table gives 450,000 gallons** as a PPA total.
   - *And the crucial caveat, from the PPA veterans' own association:* foPPA state plainly that **the Historical Summary "conflates Popski's time with the Libyan Arab Force Commando and with the LRDG on the Barce raid, and PPA's actual operations."** So the 450,000-gallon figure, and the 34 aircraft in the same table, **include work done before PPA existed and work done by other units.** foPPA add that it is "not clear why Popski felt the need to include some 'material results' that were not solely PPA's."
   - **Writer's line: the number grew from 50,000 to 200,000 to 450,000 across three documents, all of them his.**

3. **The prisoners freed from the Derna cage.**
   - *Popular telling / his 1945 summary:* the escape of **45 South African prisoners** from the cage at Derna was arranged.
   - *Actual, per his own MC recommendation in 1942:* **"organised the escape of 5 British P.O.W. from DERNA cage."** [WO 373/46/37] A ninefold difference, and a change of nationality. **Flag the discrepancy; do not pick one silently.**

4. **"A joint LRDG-PPA patrol found the gap that let Montgomery outflank the Mareth Line."**
   - *Popular telling (this is Wikipedia's wording):* PPA co-discovered the gap.
   - *Actual:* The New Zealand Official History distinguishes two things. **Wilder's Gap** was found by **T1 patrol under Captain Nick Wilder alone**, on 12 January 1943, and PPA was not there. The **Tebaga Gap corridor** recce, north from Ksar Rhilane, was genuinely joint: "Tinker and Peniakoff, each with two jeeps, then went north towards Djebel Tebaga, through country that was found to be suitable for the passage of a force of all arms." **PPA has a real share of the Mareth story. It is just not the share usually claimed.** [R. L. Kay, NZ Official History]

5. **"PPA was the first Eighth Army unit to meet First Army in Tunisia."**
   - *Popular telling and his own 1945 claim:* yes, at Gafsa on 7 February 1943.
   - *Actual:* foPPA note, "PPA were pipped at the post by a couple of days by a small group of SAS who had been on a similar mission further North, suffering a similar disaster, including the capture of their c/o, Major David Stirling."

6. **The origin of the unit's name. Two versions, both from Peniakoff, and they do not match.**
   - *Version A, the memoir (1950), the one everybody repeats:* Hackett asked him for a cover name, Popski dithered, and Hackett snapped, "You had better find a name quick or we shall call you Popski's Private Army." "I'll take it."
   - *Version B, his own official Historical Summary (1945):* the name arose in **August 1942**, before the unit existed, when he came back to Cairo to find himself disbanded and unpaid, complained, and was met with ribald remarks about running "a private war with a private army for his private pleasure." "The name Popski's Private Army stuck," and HQ Middle East later decided the new unit would be referred to as P.P.A.
   - *The IWM's own record splits the difference:* the official title "was facetiously dubbed 'Popski's Private Army' by a staff officer. This pleased Peniakof and the name stuck."
   - **Recommendation: tell version B as the documented one, note that the book tells it better and differently, and let the audience enjoy that he improved his own story.**

7. **The personal nickname "Popski."**
   - *Standard account:* given by Captain Bill Kennedy Shaw, LRDG Intelligence Officer, because his signallers could not handle "Peniakoff," and taken from a **Daily Mirror** cartoon character in the strip *Pip, Squeak and Wilfred*.
   - *Status:* **[MEMOIR]** The attribution rests on Peniakoff, 1950, p. 94. The strip is real and ran in the Daily Mirror from 1919 to 1956, and "Popski" is a real character in it, **but sources disagree on what Popski was in the strip**, some describing him as an anarchist villain and others as the dog belonging to a Russian bomb-maker named Otto Petrovitch Wtzkoffski. **Say "a character in a Daily Mirror comic strip" and do not specify further, or note the disagreement.**

8. **"He was rejected by the LRDG."**
   - **NOT VERIFIED.** What is attested (memoir-sourced) is that he was rejected by the **RAF and the Royal Navy** at the outbreak of war and taken by the Army. The record shows the LRDG carried him into the Jebel on all three of his 1942 trips and invited him on the Barce raid, and the LRDG's own commanding officer wrote his MC recommendation. **The "rejected by the LRDG" claim appears to be a garbling of the RAF and Navy rejections. Do not use it.**

9. **"The smallest independent unit in the British Army."**
   - *Status:* This is **Peniakoff's own framing** (memoir, p. 204) and it is repeated everywhere. The underlying fact is solid: the establishment really was 23 all ranks. **The superlative itself is not independently verifiable and should be attributed, not asserted.** Say "on his own proud description, the smallest independent unit in the British Army," or just give the number and let it speak.

10. **The cap badge: "hand-engraved silver."**
    - *Popular telling:* the badge was hand-engraved silver.
    - *Actual:* **The first issue was hand-cut and hand-engraved in brass** by a silversmith in Cairo; **the silver badges came later and were die-struck**, precisely because the hand-made ones were, in Popski's words, "rather too exquisite for the roughness of our manners." Later badges were made by a Roman silversmith. The IWM's example is engraved silver, stamped "A.900" on the reverse, catalogue number **INS 3926**. **Strictly, the device is a spherical astrolabe, or armillary sphere.** [IWM object 30070991; Peniakoff quoted in Chiarvetto]

11. **The Venice jeeps.**
    - *Claim A, from a French paper at the time:* "When Venice was liberated, he was parachuted with a jeep into the Piazza San Marco." **False.** foPPA dismiss it outright; the jeeps came by landing craft.
    - *Claim B, that PPA jeeps went to Venice by landing craft:* **VERIFIED, and by two sources independent of Popski.** The **IWM Peniakoff Collection, catalogue 2012-03-15**, ninety original prints from his own archive, explicitly lists **"Passing through Venice (1-2nd May 1945)"** among its contents. And **IWM Oral History 14884, Lieutenant Brian Ewart Thomas RE**, the landing-craft officer, recorded in 1995, covers the "reception from Italians in liberated Venice; threat of magnetic mines in lagoon; end of operation, 2/5/1945."
    - *Claim C, the seven laps of Piazza San Marco:* **[MEMOIR] only, but in a chapter literally titled "Wheels in Venice."** "We loaded five jeeps in three R.C.L.s and, young Thomas leading recklessly amongst the German mines, we sailed from Chioggia to Venice up the lagoon, entered the Canal San Marco and moored our craft on the quay. I started my jeep and, trembling with excitement for the one and only time during the war, drove into the Piazzetta, passed between the columns, turned left into Piazza San Marco, and, followed by the others, drove seven times round the square. This was my hour of triumph." No war diary entry or record confirms it. **The IWM photographs might settle it, but they are not online and the catalogue does not say San Marco.** foPPA, who are close to the unit, plainly treat the square drive as real and debunk only the parachute version, and in 2005 tried to re-enact it but "the Italian Navy wouldn't lend us a Landing Craft, and the best we could do was wander round making Brrm Brrm noises."
    - *Claim D, "the only wheeled vehicles ever to have been there":* **NOT VERIFIED, and Popski never asserted it as a fact.** The superlative appears in a **different chapter of the book**, as a remembered daydream speech to Corporal John Cameron in a jeep near Fasano in September 1943: "Jock, four hundred miles up this coast lies Venice, an island town of canals and narrow streets, where no wheeled vehicle has ever been seen. One day we shall land our jeeps on the main square..." **That is Popski reporting what he believed in 1943, seven years later, to a man who was killed on 21 June 1944 and could never confirm the conversation.** The flat assertion in Wikipedia is lifted from **foPPA's memorial-plaque wording**, which is exactly the memoir-to-association-to-encyclopedia laundering to avoid. It is also very likely false as an absolute: Piazza San Marco is pedestrian by geography, not by any law, and road vehicles have crossed to Piazzale Roma since the Ponte della Libertà opened in 1933. **Nothing even attempts to prove the negative. Do not assert it.**
    - *Claim E, that PPA liberated Venice:* **False. Venice had already surrendered.** **2/5 Queen's, 169 Brigade, 56th Division entered Venice and accepted its unconditional surrender at 1800 on 29 April 1945.** PPA arrived 1 to 2 May. The memoir does not claim otherwise and calls the drive "a purposeless piece of swagger."
    - **Recommended script wording:** PPA brought five jeeps up the lagoon from Chioggia in three landing craft and, on 1 or 2 May 1945, days after Venice had already surrendered to the 2/5th Queen's, put them ashore in the city. Popski wrote that he drove seven times round Piazza San Marco. Photographs of PPA in Venice survive in the Imperial War Museum's Peniakoff Collection. The often-repeated claim that these were the only wheeled vehicles ever in the square is not something Popski asserted as fact and cannot be substantiated.

12. **Christopher Lee.**
    - *Popular telling:* the actor Christopher Lee served with Popski's Private Army.
    - *Status:* Wikipedia states he was attached as a liaison officer while an RAF intelligence officer, sourced only to a YouTube video. **Historian Gavin Mortimer has publicly questioned Lee's special forces claims.** **This is contested and thinly sourced. Recommend leaving it out of the episode entirely; it is a distraction and the sourcing does not meet the bar.**

13. **"His force kept two German divisions occupied in the desert."**
    - *Popular telling:* Manchester Evening News, quoted in Willett's biography.
    - *Actual:* foPPA's own response is the correction: "that's not less than 20,000 well-trained Germans and 15 PPA who'd only just started."

14. **"Popski's men were behind the risings in Milan and Genoa" / "picked from every unit in the British Empire" / "all his men wore beards."**
    - All wartime and post-war press embroidery, all corrected by foPPA from the unit's own personnel records. Beards were unregulated in North Africa and later "became the privilege of the old hands with at least five operations to their credit."

15. **"Long Range Demolition Squadron."**
    - The unit's official designation **never** included the words "Long Range." foPPA trace this error to **Brigadier John Hackett's foreword to the second edition of Popski's book**, from where it spread.

16. **"General Popski" and "MC and Bar."**
    - He reached **Lieutenant-Colonel**, not General. Caroline Moorehead's *Martha Gellhorn: A Life* (2003, p. 395) calls him General; that is wrong.
    - Caccia Dominioni's 1962 book styles him "Colonel, D.S.O., MC and Bar." **There is no Bar to the MC in the London Gazette and TNA holds only two award recommendations for him, the MC and the DSO. The "MC and Bar" is an error.**

17. **The DSO date.**
    - *Popular telling:* he was awarded the DSO in November 1944, and separately, that he was awarded it after losing his hand.
    - *Actual, cleanly resolved by the documents:* **the recommendation was written and signed by Lt-Col Horsbrugh-Porter in late November 1944** (I read the date as 25 Nov, foPPA read 23 Nov) for actions in the **first fortnight of November**; it was **announced on 30 December 1944** and **gazetted on 26 April 1945**. **He lost his hand on 9 December, about three weeks after the actions the DSO was for and three weeks before it was announced.** foPPA state it flatly, correcting their own earlier belief: "Some sources have incorrectly asserted or implied, including foPPA for a while until we found the citation, that Popski was awarded his DSO because of the action, North of Ravenna on 9th December 1944, in which he lost his left hand. This citation makes clear that his award was about events from several weeks earlier." **Wikipedia's construction ("lost his left hand... leading to him being hospitalised and sent to England where he was awarded a Distinguished Service Order") preserves the false implication. Do not repeat it.**

18. **The date of the Barce raid, in his own official report.**
    - Popski's 1945 Historical Summary says he took part in the Barce raid "on 23rd September 1942." **The raid was on the night of 13 September 1942**, per both the New Zealand Official History and his own MC citation. **His own official report to the King has the date wrong by ten days.**

19. **"Treviso, Austria." An IWM slip with an identifiable cause.**
    - The IWM object record says PPA "finished the war in **Treviso, Austria**." **Treviso is in the Veneto, in Italy, about 300 km from Austria.**
    - **The cause is almost certainly Tarvisio.** The memoir's sequence: "Eight days later we were in a deep Alpine valley below **Tarvisio** waiting morosely for the war to end... At 1800 hours I received a slip from Sergeant Brooks, Germany had surrendered... Two days later again we were driving eastwards from **Klagenfurt** in Austria with a detachment of the Twenty-Seventh Lancers." **Tarvisio is the Italian alpine town at the Austrian and Slovenian border junction, one letter away from Treviso.**
    - **The unit finished in Carinthia, Austria.** The IWM Peniakoff Collection lists a **"disbandment parade at Rosegg, Austria (May 1945)"**; Corporal Snape is buried at Klagenfurt; and the memorial's campaign line reads "1942 - LIBYA - TUNISIA - ITALY - AUSTRIA - 1945."

20. **"PPA hunted for Himmler."**
    - *Popular telling, in Wikipedia and everywhere downstream:* after VE Day PPA hunted for Himmler.
    - *Actual:* **the word "Himmler" appears zero times in the 1950 memoir.** The claim originates in foPPA's abridged plaque story and passed from there into Wikipedia. **No record, citation, war diary extract or independent source supports it. NOT FOUND. Leave it out.**

21. **"PPA fought at Lake Comacchio."**
    - *Popular telling, and the memoir's own shorthand:* PPA fought around Lake Comacchio for seven days from 21 April 1945.
    - *Actual:* the war diary place names for those days (Massa Fiscaglia, Iolanda di Savoia, Mezzo Goro, Ariano Ferrarese, Coccanile) are west and north of the lake, beyond the Argenta Gap. **And the famous Comacchio battle, Operation Roast on 1 to 2 April 1945, was 2 Commando Brigade, not PPA.** The one genuine link is a COPP officer, Derrick Freeman, on a night-navigation raid with PPA near the Po mouth in early April. **Never place PPA in Operation Roast.**

22. **"Popski's Private Navy."**
    - The phrase is **foPPA's, not Popski's**; it appears nowhere in the memoir. The substance is real and better than the nickname: seven Royal Engineers of an Inland Water Transport Company were **all seven Mentioned in Despatches**, possibly uniquely for an entire attached section, for moving PPA's jeeps by night on shallow-draught lighters and untangling mines from their propellers.

23. **The flame-thrower jeeps.**
    - William Seymour, *British Special Forces* (1985, p. 184 fn) and Julian Thompson, *IWM Book of War Behind Enemy Lines* (1998, p. 362) both describe multiple Wasp flame-thrower jeeps in PPA. **foPPA state that exactly one was ever built.**

- **STILL OPEN, if anyone wants to close them:** (a) a full CWGC unit query to confirm all fifteen commemorations, which needs to be run manually because cwgc.org blocks automation; (b) a direct read of Nicholson vol. II pp. 606ff to quote the Canadian official history verbatim on Porterforce and Ravenna; (c) pages 2 to 9 of Popski's 1945 Historical Summary, since only page 1 was ever archived and TNA WO 106/2332 is not digitised; (d) an IWM image-licensing enquiry to establish whether any print in the Peniakoff Collection actually shows jeeps in Piazza San Marco.
- **WEAKLY SOURCED, handle with care:** Italian use of **chemical weapons in Libya**. The strongest attribution is Nicola Labanca, *Journal of Modern Italian Studies* 9:3 (2004), for phosgene and mustard gas used on several occasions in 1923-24, 1927-28 and 1930, but that was available only as a quoted sentence, not read at source. **Do not attach a tonnage, body count or single dramatic incident to Libya, and do not import the far better documented Ethiopian gas material into a Libya segment.**
