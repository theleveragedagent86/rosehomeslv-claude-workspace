# RESEARCH REPORT — Manfred von Richthofen (Red Baron)
Compiled via live web verification, 2026-07-14 (inline, after research subagent was stopped by user).

## VERIFIED TIMELINE (each item confirmed against 2+ reputable sources)
- Born May 2, 1892, Breslau, Prussian nobility; hunting/riding upbringing. [Wikipedia; Britannica; 1914-1918-online]
- 1914: Uhlan cavalry officer; trench warfare made cavalry useless; reassigned to supply duty. [Wikipedia; history.blog.gov.uk]
- Transfer request quote: "I have not gone to war in order to collect cheese and eggs, but for another purpose." Widely reported wording, originates in his own memoir; joined flying service end of May 1915. [Wikipedia; airpowerasia; history.com]
- Chance meeting with Oswald Boelcke Oct 1915 (famously asked how he shoots planes down); inspired pilot training. [Wikipedia; 1914-1918-online]
- Crashed on first solo flight. [history.com; multiple]
- Aug 1916: Boelcke personally selected him for Jasta 2. [Wikipedia; Boelcke bio]
- First confirmed victory Sept 17, 1916 (Morris/Rees, over Cambrai). Ordered engraved silver cup from Berlin jeweler per victory. [Wikipedia; IWM; theaerodrome]
- Boelcke died Oct 28, 1916, mid-air collision with friendly aircraft (Erwin Böhme) during combat; Richthofen witnessed it. German pilots had no parachutes (fighter pilots got them only in 1918). [Wikipedia (Boelcke); RAF Museum]
- Nov 23, 1916: killed Major Lanoe Hawker VC, his 11th victory, after a long circling duel; Richthofen himself called Hawker "the British Boelcke". [Wikipedia (Hawker); IWM]
- Jan 16, 1917: command of Jasta 11. Pour le Mérite Jan 1917 (after 16th kill). Late Jan 1917: painted his Albatros D.III bright red. Jasta 11 pilots later added red/own colors; accounts incl. sharing the target/protecting the boss. [Wikipedia; Jasta 11 wiki; warfarehistorynetwork]
- Bloody April 1917: 22 victories that month incl. 4 in one day, tally to 52. RFC replacement pilots arrived with minimal hours; RFC April 1917 losses catastrophic (life expectancy of new pilots commonly given in weeks). [Wikipedia; IWM; historynet]
- June 24, 1917: JG I formed (Jastas 4,6,10,11) under his command; "The Flying Circus" from bright colors + rail/tent mobility. [Wikipedia (JG I); Osprey]
- July 6, 1917: head wound in combat with F.E.2d's of No. 20 Sqn RFC (Cunnell pilot / Woodbridge observer credited with the shot in most accounts); instant disorientation + temporary partial blindness; recovered sight, forced landing near Wervicq; returned to duty July 25 against doctor's orders; lasting headaches, post-flight nausea, temperament change. [Wikipedia; Cunnell wiki; KUMC medical essay]
- 1917 propaganda memoir "Der rote Kampfflieger"; post-publication writings (1933 edition addenda) record him repudiating it as "too insolent," no longer that person, and: "I am in wretched spirits after every aerial combat." [Wikipedia (The Red Fighter Pilot); PBS NOVA]
- Germany repeatedly urged him to retire/ground himself (morale value); he refused, citing duty alongside the ordinary soldier in the trenches. [Wikipedia; PBS NOVA; RAF Museum]
- 80 confirmed victories by April 1918 — the war's highest officially credited score. [Wikipedia; Britannica]
- April 21, 1918: combat over Morlancourt Ridge / Vaux-sur-Somme vs Sopwith Camels of No. 209 Sqn RAF. Wilfrid "Wop" May: newcomer, Brown's school friend (Victoria HS, Edmonton), ordered to stay clear; guns jammed; fled. MvR chased to ground level. Capt. Arthur Roy Brown dove, fired one short burst, had to climb away. Chase continued westward. Unusual ~40 km/h EAST wind (reverse of prevailing westerly) pushed the fight/ground speed deep over Allied lines. Australian ground fire (Popkin's Vickers, 24th MG Coy; Lewis gunners Buie and "Snowy" Evans also claimed). Single .303 entered right side (near right armpit, shattering rib), tumbled through chest, exited near left nipple — from below/right/behind: ground fire geometry. He landed the Dr.I (serial 425/17) roughly but controlled in a field; died in the cockpit; alleged last word "kaputt" (reported by witnesses incl. Australian medic Edward Smout; contested). Age 25. Plane stripped by souvenir hunters. [Wikipedia; KUMC; AWM; gunsamerica Vickers article; Roy Brown wiki]
- Kill credit: RAF officially credited Brown. Modern forensic/historical consensus: ground fire, most likely Popkin (position/timing/trajectory); Brown's burst ~2 minutes before the fatal wound per reconstructions; medical analyses (e.g., 1998 Popular Mechanics / 2002 Discovery "Unsolved History" / Franks & Bennett "The Red Baron's Last Flight" 1997) agree such a wound allows only seconds-to-a-short-time of function, incompatible with minutes of continued flying after Brown's pass. Brown reportedly never publicly celebrated/insisted. [KUMC; AWM blog "Who killed the Red Baron?"; Franks & Bennett; NBC News]
- Brain-injury hypothesis: retired USAF researchers (Univ. of Missouri-Columbia + Ohio collaborator) argue the 1917 head wound produced post-concussive impairment; his last-flight behavior = uncharacteristic target fixation, breaking his own rule "never obstinately stay with an opponent." [NBC News "Did brain injury doom the Red Baron?"; PBS NOVA "Inside the Red Baron's Mind"]
- Funeral: buried Bertangles Apr 22, 1918 by No. 3 Sqn AFC, full military honors, six officers as pallbearers, firing-party salute, Allied squadrons' wreaths incl. "To Our Gallant and Worthy Foe." [Wikipedia; AWM; CriticalPast footage]

## MYTH VS RECORD
- "Roy Brown killed the Red Baron": official but contradicted by trajectory + timing; consensus favors Popkin. State as strong consensus, not certainty.
- Last word "kaputt": alleged/witness-reported; hedge.
- "Bounty on his head": legend; NOT used in script.
- Silver cups: 60 (not 80); stopped due to silver shortage; refused ersatz metal. Verified.
- The east wind: pushed him WEST (tailwind) — not a headwind.
- "British Boelcke": Richthofen's own description of Hawker.

## NOT FOUND / CONTESTED
- Exact recipient/date of cheese-and-eggs letter: memoir-sourced; wording standard.
- Precise seconds of consciousness: medical opinions range (seconds to ~a minute+); script says "seconds, maybe a minute."
- Whether Jasta 11's all-red livery was primarily protective vs esprit: mixed accounts; script hedges ("partly in style, partly so their boss wasn't the only giant target").
