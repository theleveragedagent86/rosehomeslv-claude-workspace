## YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION

She did not sail into Malta. She was dragged in sideways, lashed between two destroyers, with her back broken and two crashed aircraft still stuck to her.

In August 1942 an entire island was days from running out of food, and the Royal Navy built the largest escort ever assembled for a convoy to get one American tanker through. This is Operation Pedestal and the SS Ohio, told from the despatches and the war diaries rather than the legend. The popular version gets the ending wrong, gets the submarine wrong, and quietly skips the part where nine of the fourteen merchant ships never arrived.

## PACKAGE NOTES

- Chapter timecodes are lifted directly from the act headings in `script.md`. Do not renumber them unless the act timings change in the final cut.
- The description deliberately does NOT mention Malta's George Cross. It was awarded in April 1942 and has nothing to do with this convoy. Captain Mason's own George Cross is fine, it is in the script and in the outro.
- The description never says the Ohio steamed in under her own power. She was towed, then lashed between HMS Penn and HMS Bramham with HMS Ledbury shoving her bow round the turns.
- Every claim in the hook and summary is in the QA-passed script: two destroyers lashed either side, broken keel, two crashed aircraft aboard, nine of fourteen merchant ships lost, the Italian submarine Axum (not a U-boat), the largest escort ever assembled for a convoy, and Malta's food reserves running out on 15 August.
- Teaser line matches the OUTRO exactly: the next episode is the pigeon-medal story, bridged by the ninety carrier pigeons on the convoy manifest.
