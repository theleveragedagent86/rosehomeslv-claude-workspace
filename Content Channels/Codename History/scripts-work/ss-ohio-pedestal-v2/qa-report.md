## 2. PASS 1 BLOCKERS, RE-VERIFIED

### B1. Aircraft sequence reversed and half-wing misattributed. FIXED.

The script now runs the two aircraft in the correct documented order:

> That morning her gunners shot down a Junkers 88.
> It came down on her foredeck and stayed there.
> ...
> At twenty five past nine the Stukas came specifically for the Ohio.
> Ohio and the destroyer Ashanti shot one down between them.
> It went into her starboard side, forward of the bridge.
> Half a wing landed on the upper bridge. The bomb never went off.

This matches the record: the Ju 88 on the foredeck first, then the 0925 Ju 87 into the starboard side forward of the upper bridge, with half a wing on the upper bridge and an unexploded bomb. Mason's own quoted line is now consistent with the sequence the narration has just described, which was the specific incoherence flagged in Pass 1. The cold open no longer contradicts the body.

### B2. "Inside were two words. God Speed." FABRICATION. FIXED.

Now reads:

> Every master carried a sealed envelope he could not open until the tenth.
> Inside, the First Lord of the Admiralty wished them God Speed.
> Syfret's report calls this, and I quote, very highly appreciated.

Checked against Syfret's despatch, para 7. The despatch describes personal messages from the First Lord to be opened on a set date and records that they were very highly appreciated. The script no longer asserts a two-word text that no source supports. The quoted fragment is in context and correctly attributed.

### B3. Clan Ferguson placed on the wrong night. FIXED BY REMOVAL.

Clan Ferguson does not appear anywhere in the body. The replacement line is:

> That night Empire Hope and Glenorchy were bombed and sunk.

Verbatim-supported by Syfret Part II, para 25. The accompanying VISUAL was correctly reduced to two burning merchant ships, so the tag no longer asserts a third loss the narration does not carry.

No hole was opened in the loss ledger. The nine lost ships are never enumerated in the script; the ledger is carried arithmetically as fourteen sailed, five arrived. That still closes.

### B4. HMS Eagle death toll presented as settled. FIXED.

Now four straight beats:

> Sources still do not agree on how many died with her.
> The Royal Navy's own historians say a hundred and thirty one.
> The war graves rolls give over a hundred and sixty.
> Some counts run higher still. Nobody agrees.

Each rung is independently supportable. 131 is the Naval Historical Branch information brief, which I read directly rather than via the research report. Over 160 is supported by my own paragraph-level count of the CWGC-derived casualty roll for 11 August 1942 (164 entries under the Eagle heading) and by uboat.net at 160. "Higher still" is supported by Wikipedia at 231, by complement arithmetic (1,160 less 927 survivors = 233), and by Arthur at 260. The conflict is now disclosed as a conflict instead of being resolved by the script. No comedy sits adjacent to this block, and the TONE: STRAIGHT marker at line 180 covers it.

### B5. Tone defect, "They had jumped into the fire for nothing." FIXED.

The ironic punchline over fourteen real deaths is gone. The Melbourne Star passage now reads straight:

> She pulled dozens of them out, many from the ship behind.
> Their own ship was never hit.
> Fourteen of the men who jumped were never found.

Fourteen is confirmed as 9 crew plus 5 DEMS gunners. Six consecutive straight factual beats separate this line from the next joke. The inline marker undercounts that gap as "the next two beats", but the delivered effect is correct and the joke does not land near the death beat.

## 7. REMAINING ITEMS (RECOMMENDED, NOT BLOCKING)

None of these meets a FAIL rule. There is no false claim, no unsourced claim, no fabricated specific, no unresolved conflict presented as settled, and no joke over a death beat in any of them.

**R1. On-screen numbers name the same quantity two ways.** One VISUAL shows a margin count reading **164**, and a summary card two beats later reads **131. 160. 231.** Both 164 and 160 are sourced (164 is the casualty-roll count, 160 is uboat.net), but on screen they read as a contradiction about the same figure. Suggested one-word fix: make the card **131. 164. 231.**

**R2. "Brisbane Star limped in the next afternoon" is ambiguous.** The last explicit date anchor before it is "She was still there in the morning" (14 August), so "the next afternoon" can be read as 15 August. Brisbane Star in fact arrived at 1430 (Syfret) or 1415 (Malta War Diary) on **14 August**, and the following line is "At six on the morning of the fifteenth", which produces a forward-then-backward jump. No false claim is asserted, but the sequencing reads wrong. Suggested fix: "Brisbane Star limped in on the fourteenth, holed in the bows."

**R3. Glenorchy beat lost its straight marker.** In the previous draft the "blowing up with no survivors" line carried its own TONE: STRAIGHT marker. In the current draft the marker at line 336 begins *after* that line and covers Waimarama onward, so the two Glenorchy death lines sit outside any marker while the Ohio's "I'm fine. This is fine. Where's Malta." joke is two beats earlier. The narration itself is straight and the joke precedes rather than lands on the death beat, so this is not a tone FAIL. But the VO director now has no register cue there. Suggested fix: move the line 336 marker up two lines so it opens before "That night Empire Hope and Glenorchy were bombed and sunk."

Related: the production notes claim five inline TONE: STRAIGHT blocks including "the Glenorchy report line". The body contains four straight-blocks plus one resume marker, and the Glenorchy line is not separately marked. If R3 is applied, the notes become accurate again.

**R4. Minor wording nits.** Cold open says "There was half an airplane on her bridge"; the record is half a wing on the upper bridge, with the aircraft itself in her starboard side forward of the bridge. Defensible as compression and the VISUAL is correct, but "half a German wing" would be exact. Separately, a "5 KNOTS" card sits against narration describing walking pace; these are compatible but not identical.
