# Research Report: SS Ohio and Operation Pedestal (August 1942)

## One-paragraph summary

By summer 1942 the British island of Malta, sitting astride the Axis supply routes to North Africa, was under relentless siege and running out of everything, above all fuel. If nothing got through, Malta would be starved into surrender by September. Britain gathered one of the most heavily escorted convoys of the war, Operation Pedestal: 14 fast merchant ships from Britain, shepherded through the Mediterranean from Gibraltar by a huge Royal Navy force of battleships, carriers, cruisers and destroyers. Over four days in August 1942 the convoy ran a gauntlet of U-boats, Italian submarines, hundreds of German and Italian aircraft, and Axis motor torpedo boats. It was extremely costly: 9 of the 14 merchant ships were sunk, the carrier HMS Eagle went down in minutes, the cruisers Manchester and Cairo and the destroyer Foresight were lost, the carrier Indomitable was badly damaged, and more than 500 sailors and airmen were killed. Only 5 merchant ships reached Malta. The most important was the American-built tanker SS Ohio, carrying the fuel Malta needed. Ohio was torpedoed, bombed, near-missed, had two aircraft crash onto her, and had her back broken, yet was kept afloat and towed the final stretch lashed between Royal Navy destroyers, reaching Grand Harbour, Valletta on 15 August 1942, the Feast of Santa Marija. Her fuel was pumped ashore just before she settled onto the harbour bottom. Her master, Captain Dudley William Mason, received the George Cross.

## Verified timeline

- Night of 2/3 August 1942 - the convoy sails from Britain (the Clyde). [Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"; Warfare History Network]
- Night of 9/10 August 1942 - the convoy passes through the Strait of Gibraltar into the Mediterranean. [Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"; IWM "Operation Pedestal and the Siege of Malta"]
- 11 August 1942 - HMS Eagle is torpedoed by the German submarine U-73 (Helmut Rosenbaum) and sinks within minutes. [Wikipedia "HMS Eagle (1918)"; Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"]
- 12 August 1942 - mass air attacks; HMS Indomitable badly damaged by dive bombers; the Italian submarine Axum torpedoes SS Ohio amidships. [Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"; Wikipedia "SS Ohio"; Warfare History Network]
- 12-13 August 1942 - HMS Cairo sunk, HMS Foresight crippled (later scuttled/sunk), and in the night action HMS Manchester crippled and scuttled; multiple merchant ships sunk. [Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"; Warfare History Network]
- 13 August 1942 - first survivors of the convoy (Port Chalmers, Melbourne Star, Rochester Castle) reach Malta. [Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"]
- 14 August 1942 - Brisbane Star reaches Malta. [Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"; Warfare History Network]
- 15 August 1942 - SS Ohio, towed lashed between destroyers, reaches Grand Harbour, Valletta, on the Feast of Santa Marija; fuel is pumped out and she settles on the harbour bottom. [Wikipedia "SS Ohio"; Warfare History Network; IWM]

## Key figures

- **Captain Dudley William Mason, GC** - British Merchant Navy master of SS Ohio during Operation Pedestal. Born 7 October 1901 (Surbiton), died 26 April 1987. Awarded the George Cross (gazetted/presented September 1942). Also received the Lloyd's War Medal for Bravery at Sea. [Wikipedia "Dudley Mason"]
- **Helmut Rosenbaum** - commander of German submarine U-73, which sank HMS Eagle on 11 August 1942. [Wikipedia "HMS Eagle (1918)"; Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"]

## Numbers (high-risk, source each)

- **14 merchant ships** in the convoy (13 cargo ships plus the tanker Ohio). [Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"; IWM; Warfare History Network]
- **5 merchant ships reached Malta**: Ohio, Melbourne Star, Port Chalmers, Rochester Castle, Brisbane Star. Therefore **9 of 14 lost**. [Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"; Warfare History Network]
- **More than 500 Merchant Navy and Royal Navy sailors and airmen killed.** [IWM "Operation Pedestal and the Siege of Malta"; Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"]
- **HMS Eagle:** sunk 11 August 1942 by four torpedoes from U-73; sank within about four minutes; **131 officers and ratings lost** (mainly from the machinery spaces). NOTE CONFLICT: Warfare History Network states ~260 killed; the dedicated HMS Eagle record gives 131. Use 131 (most specific, dedicated source) and note that some accounts give higher. [Wikipedia "HMS Eagle (1918)"; Warfare History Network]
- **Royal Navy warships lost:** aircraft carrier HMS Eagle; cruisers HMS Manchester and HMS Cairo; destroyer HMS Foresight. Carrier HMS Indomitable badly damaged (plus other cruisers damaged, e.g. Nigeria and Kenya). [Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"; Warfare History Network]
- **SS Ohio tonnage:** ~9,514-9,625 GRT. Built 20 April 1940 by Sun Shipbuilding & Drydock Co., Chester, Pennsylvania, for The Texas Company (later Texaco). [Wikipedia "SS Ohio"]
- **Cargo:** roughly 12,000 long tons of oil/fuel aboard Ohio; the convoy delivered about 32,000 tons of supplies total, enough to sustain Malta for roughly 10 more weeks. [Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"; Warfare History Network]
- **Ohio damage:** torpedoed amidships by the Italian submarine Axum (hole roughly 24 by 27 ft in the port side); repeated bombing and near-misses; a shot-down Ju 87 Stuka crashed into her starboard side forward; a second downed aircraft (reported as a Ju 88) came down on the ship; her back was almost certainly broken. [Wikipedia "SS Ohio"; Warfare History Network]
- **Tow:** three Royal Navy destroyers, HMS Penn, HMS Bramham, and HMS Ledbury, assisted; Ohio was lashed between destroyers for the final approach. [Wikipedia "SS Ohio"; Warfare History Network]

## Absurd / comedic beats (all real)

- Malta's survival, and a big slice of the Mediterranean war, hinged on one American-built oil tanker built for Texaco, crewed by British sailors, limping along with its spine snapped.
- Ohio absorbed a torpedo, multiple bomb hits, dozens of near-misses, AND had two separate aircraft crash onto it, and still would not sink. It essentially had to be told to sink after it had done its job.
- The final leg was a tanker that could no longer steer being physically strapped between two destroyers like an injured player carried off the field by teammates, plus a third helping, at walking pace, while the whole thing could have blown up.
- The engineering answer to "our cargo ship's back is broken and the decks are underwater" was, roughly, "tie it to other ships and keep dragging."
- The Axis threw U-boats, Italian submarines, hundreds of aircraft, and motor torpedo boats at the convoy. The one ship they most needed to sink was the one that refused to die.

## Human-cost beats (play straight)

- More than 500 sailors and airmen were killed across Operation Pedestal. Play completely straight.
- HMS Eagle was hit by four torpedoes and sank in minutes; 131 of her crew died, many trapped in the machinery spaces below. Straight.
- Nine of the fourteen merchant ships were sunk, along with the cruisers Manchester and Cairo, the destroyer Foresight, and the carrier Eagle. Men died on tankers and freighters loaded with fuel and ammunition. Straight.
- The convoy succeeded, but at a cost the Royal Navy judged too high to repeat; no comparable convoy was risked again until after El Alamein. State plainly.

## Myth vs record (corrections)

- Popular telling: "Ohio limped into Malta under its own power." / Actual: Ohio's engines were dead and her back was broken; she could not steer or move herself and had to be towed the final stretch, lashed between destroyers. [Wikipedia "SS Ohio"; Warfare History Network]
- Popular framing: Pedestal was a clean triumph. / Actual: it was a costly, near-run operation, 9 of 14 merchantmen and four major warships lost, over 500 dead, and it is remembered as a strategic success bought at a very heavy price. [IWM; Wikipedia "Operation Pedestal"]
- Detail often blurred: the torpedo that broke Ohio's back came from the Italian submarine Axum, not a German U-boat. [Wikipedia "SS Ohio"; Warfare History Network]
