# Research Report: The Wooden Horse Escape, Stalag Luft III, 1943

## One-paragraph summary

At the German prisoner-of-war camp Stalag Luft III, near Sagan (now Zagan, Poland), the Luftwaffe deliberately built an "escape-proof" camp: prisoner huts sat far from the perimeter wire, the barracks were raised off the ground, and bright yellow sandy subsoil under a dark grey topsoil made spoil from any tunnel obvious. Buried seismograph microphones listened for digging. To beat this, Captain Michael Codner (Royal Artillery), Flight Lieutenant Eric Williams (RAF), and Flight Lieutenant Oliver Philpot (RAF) built a hollow wooden gymnastics vaulting horse, a nod to the ancient Trojan Horse. Each day other prisoners carried the horse out to the same spot near the wire and vaulted on top of it for exercise while a man hidden inside dug a tunnel, hid the entrance, and carried the excavated sand back inside the horse. Over roughly three months they dug about 100 feet (30 m) of tunnel. On the evening of Friday 29 October 1943 all three escaped through it and made "home runs": Williams and Codner traveled together via Stettin and a Danish/Swedish ship, Philpot separately via Danzig on a Swedish ship, all three eventually reaching neutral Sweden and returning to Britain. This was a different, earlier, smaller, and far less deadly escape than the March 1944 "Great Escape" from the same camp, after which the Gestapo murdered 50 recaptured escapers.

## Verified timeline
- March 1942 - Stalag Luft III established near Sagan, Lower Silesia (now Zagan, Poland), about 160 km southeast of Berlin. [Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III"; b17museum.ch PDF]
- June 1943 - Codner and Williams approach Philpot, hut escape co-ordinator, to register the vaulting-horse scheme with the escape committee. [Wikipedia "Oliver Philpot"; RAFA]
- Roughly July to October 1943 (about three months) - the men dig the tunnel in shifts, a man hidden inside the horse each day. [Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III"; "Michael Codner"]
- Evening of Friday 29 October 1943 - all three escape through the completed tunnel. [Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III"; "Oliver Philpot"; "Michael Codner"; RAFA; IWM]
- 4 November 1943 - Philpot reaches the British Legation in Stockholm. [Wikipedia "Oliver Philpot"]
- 7-12 November 1943 - Williams and Codner's ship docks Copenhagen (7 Nov), they are put ashore at Stromstad, Sweden (11 Nov), reach Stockholm (12 Nov). [Wikipedia "Michael Codner"]
- March 1944 - the separate, larger "Great Escape" from the same camp (context/contrast only). [Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III"]

## Key figures
- **Captain Michael Codner, Royal Artillery** - conceived the idea of using a wooden vaulting horse to escape; one of the three diggers/escapers; traveled with Williams. [Wikipedia "Michael Codner"]
- **Flight Lieutenant Eric Williams, RAF** - co-originator, digger/escaper; later wrote the memoir "The Wooden Horse" (1949; originally "Goon in the Block"); traveled with Codner. [Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III"; RAFA; IWM]
- **Flight Lieutenant Oliver Philpot, RAF (Canadian-born, British and Canadian citizenship)** - the hut's escape co-ordinator who helped dispose of sand, then joined as the third escaper; posed as Norwegian margarine salesman "Jon Jorgensen"; wrote the memoir "Stolen Journey" (1950); awarded the Military Cross (16 May 1944) and had earlier received the DFC (1941). [Wikipedia "Oliver Philpot"]

## Numbers (high-risk, source each)
- **Tunnel length: about 100 feet / over 30 m.** Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III" and "Michael Codner"; Coconut Times/OCMD reprint states "over 30 m (100 ft)." One source ("Michael Codner") specifies "95 to 100 feet long." Consensus: roughly 100 feet. [Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III"; "Michael Codner"]
- **Exit distance beyond the wire: about 15 feet.** Stated on Wikipedia "Michael Codner" ("about 15 feet outside the wire"). Single-source; treat as approximate. [Wikipedia "Michael Codner"]
- **Digging duration: roughly three months.** Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III" and "Michael Codner" both say "For three months." NOTE CONFLICT: the RAFA (Royal Air Forces Association) article says "four-and-a-half months." Present as "roughly three months" and note the disagreement. [Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III"; conflicting: RAFA]
- **Barracks raised 60 cm off the ground** (an anti-tunnel measure). [Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III"]
- **Escape date: Friday 29 October 1943.** Multiple sources; Philpot's day-of-week ("Friday") is confirmed and 29 Oct 1943 was indeed a Friday. [Wikipedia "Oliver Philpot"; "Michael Codner"; "Stalag Luft III"]
- Philpot reached Stockholm's British Legation less than five days after the breakout (4 Nov 1943). [Wikipedia "Oliver Philpot"]

### The Great Escape (context/contrast numbers, March 1944)
- 76 men crawled through the "Harry" tunnel before discovery. [Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III"]
- 73 of the 76 recaptured; only 3 fully evaded (Bergsland, Muller, van der Stok). [Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III"]
- 50 recaptured escapers murdered by the Gestapo on Hitler's orders. [Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III"]

## Absurd / comedic beats (all real)
- The Germans built a camp specifically to make tunneling hopeless (huts far from wire, raised barracks, tell-tale yellow sand, buried listening microphones), and the prisoners beat it with a gym prop.
- The disguise WAS exercise. Prisoners literally did gymnastics on top of a box every day, in plain sight of the guards, while a man dug underneath. The vibration and thumping of the vaulting even helped mask the digging noise from the microphones.
- The horse was carried in and out with a man plus tools plus bags of sand hidden inside, by prisoners who then had to look casual carrying a suspiciously heavy "gym box."
- The scheme is a deliberate, self-aware nod to the Trojan Horse: hide men inside a wooden horse to get past the enemy's defenses. Roughly three thousand years later, it still worked.
- Sand was hidden in trousers to trickle out into the camp gardens (a POW method) and carried back inside the horse.
- Philpot escaped by pretending to be a Norwegian margarine salesman, a cover based on his actual prewar career in the margarine/food business.

## Human-cost beats (play straight) - TONE: STRAIGHT
- These were downed airmen held prisoner for years; escape carried real risk of being shot.
- The contrast beat: the March 1944 "Great Escape" from the same camp ended in atrocity. Of 76 who got out, 73 were recaptured, and 50 of those were murdered singly or in pairs by the Gestapo on Hitler's orders. This must be delivered completely straight, no jokes, as the sober counterweight to the Wooden Horse's happy ending.

## Myth vs record (corrections)
- **Wooden Horse vs Great Escape.** Popular telling often blurs the two, or assumes any Stalag Luft III tunnel escape ended in the 50 murders. Actual: the Wooden Horse (Oct 1943) was earlier, smaller (about 100 ft, three men), and all three got home. The Great Escape (March 1944) was a separate, larger tunnel; that is the one where 50 were murdered. [Wikipedia "Stalag Luft III"; "The Wooden Horse"]
- **Dramatization caveat.** Williams' 1949 memoir and the 1950 film use fictionalized character names (e.g. Peter Howard for Williams, John Clinton for Codner) and dramatize the journey. Anchor route/tunnel facts to the encyclopedic/biographical record, not the film's character names. [Wikipedia "The Wooden Horse"]
- **Date typo watch.** One reprint source (Coconut Times/throughouthistory) gives "October 29, 1942." That is an error; the correct year is 1943, confirmed by multiple independent sources including the day-of-week "Friday." [Wikipedia x3]
