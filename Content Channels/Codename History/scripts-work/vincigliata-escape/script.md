# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE GENERALS WHO DUG OUT OF A CASTLE WITH A CROQUET SET

**Target runtime:** 8:51 at 158 wpm (Standard tier). 1,400 spoken narration words, 112 images, 103 unique generations after REUSE. At the fast end (173 wpm) 8:06, still above the eight-minute mid-roll floor. At the slow end (151 wpm) 9:16, which is 4.97 s per image, inside the 5-second ceiling. Act timecodes are computed from the spoken word count over a 531 second runtime.
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake country dialogue. Static stills only, hard cuts, no image held past 5 seconds.
**Story type:** Obscure but real WW2 escape. Campo P.G. 12, Castello di Vincigliata, September 1942 to March 1943. Ensemble piece.
**Title (standard tier):** `The Generals Who Dug Out of a Castle With a Croquet Set | Codename History`

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: This is an ensemble episode and that is deliberate. Adrian Carton de Wiart is a listicle staple and the "unkillable soldier" lane is saturated. This script does not run that lane. The famous man ran the lookouts and was recaptured. Neame designed the tunnel, Boyd made the tools, and the two men who got clear of Italy were two New Zealanders. Two Tier 1 sources disagree by one day on the break-out and the Swiss crossing, so no hard date is spoken for the crossing. The unverified "roughly 1,500 escape attempts" denominator is NOT in this script, and neither is any pre-escape guard ratio. Do not re-simplify the Carton de Wiart armistice correction back into the popular version, and do not sharpen it into a gotcha either.
>
> VOICE NOTE: The comedy lives in the NARRATION. Deadpan. Treat six months of tunnelling like an unreasonable amount of admin. Roast only real, documented decisions. Human cost is played completely straight, and no joke sits in the beat immediately before or immediately after a straight beat. No bracket, tag or stage direction appears inside a NARRATOR line anywhere in this script, because NARRATOR lines are read verbatim by the voice model. The straight beats are named in prose under Build notes in PRODUCTION NOTES.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Voice choices**

- **The ensemble is the commission and the script obeys it.** Carton de Wiart appears in three places only: as one of six escapers, as the recaptured walker in Act 6, and as the subject of the Act 9 twist. He is never called unkillable, indestructible, or the leader. Neame gets the design, Boyd gets the tools, Combe gets the two days at San Vittore, and Hargest and Miles get Switzerland and the ending. Act 6 contains a deliberate on-screen refusal of the listicle lane ("Not indestructible. Just extremely recognisable.") with a visual of greyed-out competitor thumbnails.
- **The injuries are used only as the report uses them.** They are the reason he was conspicuous on the run, and they are the reason the Italians picked him as credentials. Both uses are documented and both cut against the hero framing.
- **Fake dialogue:** three short blocks, ITALY in Act 2, NEAME in Act 8, ROME in Act 9. Neame's is drawn from the fact of the WO 32/10706 complaint, not from a quoted document, so it is written as character voice and clearly marked as such.
- **Second person:** "Hold your hands up. A letterbox." (Act 3) and "You have never heard of either." (Act 7).
- **Comedy density:** roughly a joke, roast or sarcastic aside every 12 to 18 seconds across Acts 1 to 10. Almost all of it is documented material rather than invented framing: the whittling air vice marshal, the croquet hoops, the aspirin tube, the fake deck tennis court, the ping-pong table, ringing for room service, the trattoria, the two-and-a-half-year revenge of the Italian army, and the complaint.
- **Tone-adjacency, the rule that failed the previous episode.** Three straight beats close Act 6 (Combe), and the whole of Act 11 and the first beat of the outro are straight. That is the full list and it is written out under Build notes below. Checked in both directions: the beat before Combe's straight run is the neutral "But in Milan, Combe had done something the popular telling leaves out", not a punchline; the beat before Act 11 is the neutral "They landed at Termoli. But not everyone got a road home."; and no joke follows any straight beat anywhere in the script. The next-episode teaser is three beats after the last straight beat and is factual rather than a punchline.
- **The one 6-word beat**, "Brigadier Reginald Miles never got home", is deliberate. It sits on a REUSE of the Miles portrait card, so the short hold costs no generation.

**Build notes:** Tag as **TONE: STRAIGHT** the whole of **ACT 11: THE COST**, all seventeen beats, from "Every one of those escapes ran on Italian civilians who hid them" through "The twelfth of August, nineteen forty-four. He was killed by shell fire." Outside Act 11 there are four more. Three of them close **ACT 6: FOUR OF THEM**, the San Vittore run: "Two days. He refused to admit he was a prisoner of war.", "Saying so would have got him treated as one. Instead, civilian prison." and "He bought two days for men he could not know were free." The fourth is the **first beat of the OUTRO**, "Six went out. Two got clear. Within eighteen months, both were dead." Twenty-one beats in total. **No music sting plays in any of them**, and no sting plays in the beat immediately before or immediately after any of them. The three hardest lines in the episode are "What happened to them, I cannot tell you. It is not recorded.", "That, in depression and exhaustion, he took his own life." and "The twelfth of August, nineteen forty-four. He was killed by shell fire." Give those the longest holds in the build, read flat against completely still images, with no lift at the end of the line. Nothing outside those twenty-one beats is straight, so a joke may never be scored under any of them, and the comedy does not resume until the subscribe call to action, three beats after the last straight beat.

**Accuracy corrections carried into the script**

1. **Carton de Wiart at Lisbon.** Phrased as "what he was actually there for", not "everyone has this wrong", because The National Archives itself uses the loose "to negotiate peace terms" phrasing in one clause. The correction rests on the US Army official history. There is no gotcha beat.
2. **"They tunnelled out with kitchen knives"** expanded to the real toolkit, because the full list is better and is primary.
3. **Carton de Wiart did not lead or design the escape.** He ran the watchers. The script never says otherwise and never implies it.

**Deliberately excluded, and why**

- The "roughly 1,500 escape attempts" denominator. Unverified, explicitly forbidden.
- Any pre-escape guard ratio or "many times their number". Only the post-escape figure is sourced.
- "Near Como" for Boyd's recapture. TNA says only "trying to cross the border".
- Any hard date for the Swiss crossing, and any spoken "29th or 30th" hedge.
- Any completion date for the tunnel (20 March and late February conflict), and any total tunnel length (36 vs 40 feet conflict).
- The 120 Germans and the 29 October 1943 date. TNA does not carry them.
- "Over 20,000 lire in the parcel post". Uncited even in the Tier 2 source.
- Boyd burning his own aircraft, and the Wellington serial and squadron. Tier 2/3 only.
- Neame's Olympic gold medal. A genuinely great line, but the research flags it as unanchored to an IOC record and says verify before it becomes a punchline. **If production verifies it, it belongs in Act 1 beat 3 as an extra beat.**
- Carton de Wiart's "hardly a centimetre out" line. It is his own memoir praising his own camp, and the script had no room to hedge it properly.
- Gambier-Parry hiding in the Vatican, Todhunter the librarian by name, Combe the hen-keeper by name, and the escape-proof and "Mussolini's Colditz" publisher phrasing. All cut for length or for hedging cost.
- Any framing that the story is untold. Mark Felton's *Castle of the Eagles* is a full-length history of exactly this escape and is credited in SOURCES.
