# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE ZEPPELIN THEY SENT TO AFRICA

**Target runtime:** 8:05 to 8:52 (1,400 narration words at 158 to 173 wpm)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake country dialogue.
**Story type:** Single obscure operation, real and documented. Zeppelin L 59, "Das Afrika-Schiff", November 1917.
**Title (standard tier):** `The Zeppelin Germany Sent To Africa And Never Expected Back | Codename History`

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: Every fact here comes from the Wave 1 research report. The Meinertzhagen "faked signal" myth is corrected on camera, and the correction is deliberately limited: the script says the British almost certainly did not trick her, and says out loud that they almost certainly did know she was coming. Do not tighten that hedge in any later pass. Do not re-simplify a corrected beat back into the myth for a cleaner line.
>
> VOICE NOTE: Comedy lives in the narration. Deadpan. Roast the documented incompetence, never invented incompetence. Human cost is played completely straight. See Build notes in PRODUCTION NOTES for exactly which beats carry TONE: STRAIGHT.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Build notes: TONE: STRAIGHT tagging. Read this before the build.**
Do NOT put any tone marker inside a `NARRATOR:` line. Narration is fed verbatim to TTS. Tag tone at the build layer only.

Tag **every beat in ACT 8: WHAT IT COST** as `TONE: STRAIGHT`. That is all twenty one beats, from "They rebuilt her as a bomber. She raided Naples in March nineteen eighteen." through "And that counts only the British side. It does not include the German one." No music sting, no comic timing, no reaction cutaways anywhere in that act. Delivery slows and flattens at "That evening a German submarine watched her pass under seven hundred feet." and stays flat to the end of the act.

Two sub-blocks inside Act 8 that must not be broken up by a graphic flourish:
- The loss of L 59, beats "That evening a German submarine watched her pass under seven hundred feet." through "That was all anyone found. The cause has never been settled."
- The carrier corps, beats "There is a larger cost underneath this story, and it is rarely counted." through "And that counts only the British side. It does not include the German one." Every visual here is deliberately plain and uncaricatured. Do not draw African carriers in the cartoon style used for the German and British figures. No comic proportions, no reaction faces.

**Adjacency check, done and confirmed.** The beat immediately BEFORE Act 8 is the last beat of Act 7, "Afterwards he doubted she would ever have found him. A month too late." That is a sober irony line with no punchline, and it is intended as the tonal ramp down. The beat immediately AFTER Act 8 is the first outro beat, "Four thousand two hundred miles, ninety five hours, nothing delivered." That is a flat summary, not a joke. There is no joke in the beat before or after any straight beat, and no joke anywhere inside Act 8.

**Comedy density.** Roughly thirty two comic, sarcastic or roasting beats across the six and a half minutes of non-straight material, about one every thirteen seconds. All of them sit on documented absurdity: a doctor's newspaper idea, the China Area filing, thirty machine guns and four rifles, the sewing kits, the mail, the schooner arrest, Strasser's reaction, the ship going deaf inside a storm, and Berlin cancelling its own mission three and a half hours after launch. None of them invent a flaw in anybody.

**Fake dialogue, three lines, all built on documented content.** The Colonial Office line dramatises the real decision to go over a closed sea. The Zupitza line dramatises his real proposal. The Strasser line dramatises Robinson's Tier 1 record that the crews loved the schooner capture, that Strasser read it as a foolhardy stunt, and that he made sure it was not repeated. None of the three is presented as a quotation.

**Deliberate omissions, for the record.**
- The tidy "envelope to tents, girders to radio masts, covering to bandages" list is **cut entirely**. The one way mission and the break up on the ground are Tier 1 and are in the script. The itemised list is Tier 2 only, the versions disagree, and the research found no Tier 1 support. The script says "Fabric, girders, engines, given away" and stops there.
- The Dakhla Oasis "cargo cult" anecdote is cut, per the research report's recommendation.
- The near-mutiny after the recall, Strasser's verdict quote on Bockholt, and the L 57 gas cell shooting story are all cut. Robinson's pages 308 to 309 and 311 to 313 are destroyed by bad OCR in the available scan, so those beats are Tier 2 only.
- Hugo Eckener's alleged delivery flight to Bulgaria is cut. Famous name, uncorroborated.
- No precise clock time for the take off is spoken, because German and Bulgarian local time sources disagree. No precise date for the Naples raid is spoken, because Robinson and German Wikipedia give 10 March while popular accounts give 11-12.
- No crew number is spoken for the 7 April 1918 loss. The script says "Her entire crew was lost."

- `[NOT FOUND: Garfield's own pages, and the exact place in Meinertzhagen's published diary where the L 59 claim appears.]` The script therefore attributes the finding to "his biographer" rather than naming a page or a book, and rests the actual debunk on the German chronology instead.

Standard tier format used deliberately. The operation's real code name was the German *China-Sache*, which is rendered in English both as "China Show" (MilitaryHistoryNow and others) and as "China Matter" (Robinson's own index). "China Show" is a real and common rendering, not an invention, but it is a translation rather than the original code name. The `Codename:` title form is reserved anyway, and this story is better sold on the one way flight than on an obscure code word.

**Thumbnail direction.** The airship low over orange desert with her shadow on the sand, and a burned in two line clickbait phrase. Suggested: line one "4,200 MILES", line two "NEVER MEANT TO RETURN".

**Shorts pulls, best candidates.** The schooner arrest (Act 3). Thirty machine guns and four rifles (Act 4). Saint Elmo's fire and going deaf (Act 5). The transmitter that could not ask if the order was real (Act 6). Ngomano, where he took it himself (Act 7).

**Corrections made to the popular telling, on camera:**
1. The British did not fake the recall signal. The recall was a genuine German Admiralty Staff order issued in Berlin three and a half hours after take off. The debunk is built on that chronology, not on Meinertzhagen's character, and his character is offered only as supporting context.
2. The recall did not report a surrender. It reported the last base lost and nowhere left to land.
3. She was not turned back "on the doorstep". She was in Sudan, roughly two thirds of the way.
4. Lettow-Vorbeck was not waiting for her and by his own later account doubted she would have found him.
5. The submarine that witnessed her destruction was UB 53, not U 53.

**Corrections made against overcorrection, also on camera:** the script does not claim the British had nothing to do with it. It says out loud that they almost certainly knew she was coming, that German accounts admit later rumours of espionage and planted false reports, that no contemporary German document accuses them of forgery, that this is not the same as proof it never happened, and that Robinson declines to settle it.
