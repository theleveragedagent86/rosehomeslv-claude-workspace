# EPISODE SCRIPT: SHE DROVE THE BREAKOUT, THEN JOINED THE FOREIGN LEGION

**Target runtime:** 9 minutes (1,394 narration words at ~158 wpm, 8:49)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake dialogue. Comedy is weighted hard into the first half. The register darkens through Act 4 and drops fully straight at Act 5, and it is never lifted again.
**Story type:** Single documented siege, told through the garrison nobody names correctly, plus the two national myths that have been built on top of it. Bir Hakeim, Libya, 26 May to 11 June 1942, and the driver Susan Travers.
**Title (standard tier):** `She Drove the Breakout, Then Joined the Foreign Legion | Codename History`

> **ACCURACY NOTE:** The pitch for this episode was wrong in four ways and every correction is on screen. **Do not re-simplify any of them back into the myth because the myth is punchier.** (1) **Susan Travers was NOT in the Foreign Legion at Bir Hakeim.** She was a Free French volunteer driver attached to the 13e DBLE. Her formal enrolment as adjudant chef, with service number, was **June 1945, three years later**. The cold open says "She was not in it that night. That part comes later" before the title card, and the Legion enrolment is quarantined in the outro with its date spoken. She is never given a rank at Bir Hakeim. (2) **She did not refuse the evacuation order.** Koenig ordered the women out at the end of May, she left, she attached herself to a resupply convoy and **asked permission to come back**, which he granted. The script says "She did not refuse. She left" out loud, and names this as the point where the English version goes wrong. (3) **The 3,300 figure is the AXIS loss figure, not a rival French count.** The script states the French figure as "more than a thousand killed, wounded, captured or missing," names the residual ~900 conflict and says plainly that nobody has reconciled it, and gives 3,300 as Axis. **Nothing is averaged anywhere.** (4) **The strategic claim is refused in both directions.** The French telling overclaims the battle and the English telling overclaims Travers, and Act 7 declines both. That refusal is the episode's spine. **Hedged in the spoken narration and not to be stripped out:** the eleven bullet holes ("One account says eleven holes"), the Rommel handwritten ultimatum (cut for length, not asserted), the Koenig relationship (attributed to the French defence ministry's own magazine, which states it in its own voice, not to the memoir), the head-of-column detail (softened to "near the column's front"), the "get down in the back" line ("By her own account"), the one-handed gunner ("That comes from a French general who was there"), the prisoner deaths (French Wikipedia's Bilan section, single-sourced, so the script states them plainly but attributes nothing to a Tier 1 body), and the ministry's own modest claim, which is quoted as the ministry's claim rather than as the record. **NOT FOUND and therefore never spoken:** any general Hitler order that captured Free French be shot. Only the documented narrow version appears, that Hitler ordered captured German political refugees killed and Rommel ignored it, plus the Radio Berlin threat and the same-day retraction. **Also never spoken:** the half a million mines figure, the Jewish sappers and Star of David flag, the equal water ration story, and Churchill in any form. **The Fighting French renaming was cut at QA:** it was decided by the Comite national francais on 13 July 1942, not by Churchill, and nothing at Tier 1 ties it causally to Bir Hakeim.
>
> **VOICE NOTE:** Deadpan sarcasm carries the cold open and Acts 1 to 3, and it thins deliberately through Act 4 as the water and the wounded arrive. **It stops completely at Act 5 and does not resume.** Rommel gets two short fake lines and both are compressions of documented positions, never invented failings. The Italians are the opening act of this battle and the comedy in Act 2 is on the plan, not on the men carrying it out. **Act 5 and Act 6 are flat, slow and quiet.** No music sting from "Two enemy automatic weapons opened on the leading company" to the end of Act 6. Act 7 is dry and slightly tired, not triumphant. **French terms:** Koenig is spoken KUR-nig. Amilakvari is a-mil-a-KVA-ree. Oubangui Chari is oo-BAN-gee SHA-ree. Bir Hakeim is beer ha-KEEM. Adjudant chef is ad-ju-DAHN SHEF. Bersaglieri is ber-sal-YAIR-ee. Ariete is ar-YEH-teh. **"La Miss" is deliberate**, a French article on an English noun, and should not be read as a stumble.

## ACT 4: SIXTEEN DAYS (4:00 - 5:14)

**[VISUAL: Two Italian officers under a white flag being led blindfolded between French trenches.]**

**[VISUAL: A cartoon general in a kepi frowning at a map spread across an ammunition crate.]** (REUSE: scene 16)

**NARRATOR:** Koenig said no. They asked twice more over the next three days.

**[VISUAL: Heavy Axis artillery pieces firing in a long line across open desert at dusk.]**

**NARRATOR:** Forty thousand heavy shells came in, by one estimate. Forty two thousand back.

**[VISUAL: Waves of Stuka dive bombers in formation above a small dust cloud on flat desert.]**

**NARRATOR:** By Rommel's own count the Luftwaffe flew thirteen hundred attacks on the position.

**[VISUAL: A German air commander shouting into a field telephone, absolutely furious.]**

**[VISUAL: Rommel standing beside a map holding a telephone handset with a completely flat expression.]**

**ROMMEL (character voice):** "You cannot drive tanks through a minefield full of anti tank guns."

**[VISUAL: Six hundred soldiers in worn uniforms walking into a defended perimeter on foot.]**

**NARRATOR:** Six hundred and twenty abandoned Indian soldiers walked in. The garrison took them in.

**[VISUAL: Small water containers dropped by parachute landing on open desert in daylight.]**

**NARRATOR:** The RAF air dropped a hundred and seventy litres. Mostly for the wounded men.

**[VISUAL: Dive bombers turning above a marked medical position with a red cross.]**

**NARRATOR:** Eighth of June. The heaviest raids yet. Bombs hit the brigade aid post.

**[VISUAL: A wrecked medical dugout with stretchers and folded blankets, flat even light, still air.]**

**[VISUAL: An ammunition dump reduced to a few crates with men counting shells by lamplight.]**

**NARRATOR:** Tenth of June. Seven working guns left, and almost no ammunition anywhere.

**[VISUAL: A German staff officer marking a start line on a map for the following morning.]**

**NARRATOR:** The Germans decided to finish the job the next morning. They did not know.

**Voice choices**

- **Comedy is weighted into the front half, on purpose, and the register darkens as the siege does.** Deadpan sarcasm carries the cold open and Acts 1 to 3. It thins through Act 4 as the water and the wounded arrive. It stops completely at Act 5 and does not come back. Act 7 is dry and tired rather than funny.
- **Comedy density check.** Counting jokes, roasts and sarcastic asides: **27 across the episode** (one was removed with the beat cut at QA). Distribution: cold open 1, Act 1 six, Act 2 twelve, Act 3 three, Act 4 two, Act 5 none, Act 6 one (the closing "Depends who you ask", which is the transition hook), Act 7 four. **The comedy-carrying front of the episode, cold open through Act 3, is 634 words or about 4:00, and carries 22 of them, one every 10.9 seconds.** That is inside the every-15-to-20-second bar with margin. **Acts 5 and 6, which are 350 words or about 2:13 of breakout and casualties, contain exactly one joke and it sits at the very end of Act 6, after the last casualty beat, not adjacent to one.** This is the shape the brief asked for: do not force jokes onto sixteen days of shelling.
- **Rommel speaks twice and both lines are compressions of documented positions.** Scene 29, "Take the little fort in the corner. Should not take long," compresses his 27 May order to De Stefanis to take Bir Hakeim from the southeast, an order that is on the record and that went catastrophically. Scene 59, "You cannot drive tanks through a minefield full of anti tank guns," is a near-paraphrase of the reason Rommel gives in his own account for refusing Kesselring's demand for a full armoured assault. **Neither line invents a flaw.**
- **The Italians are the opening act, not a footnote.** Act 2 is the Italian attack in full and the jokes are on the plan, the sequencing and the arithmetic, never on the men carrying it out. Rommel gives the order and Rommel wears the joke.
- Second person and direct address: "Now, who were they actually?" "This went exactly as well as you are imagining." "Depends who you ask."
- **Forward reference seeded and paid off.** Five cold-open images return in Act 5 and the outro: the machine gun pits, the hands on the wheel, the car crossing the gap, the enlistment form and the close view of the form. The audience is shown the last ninety seconds of the story first, then has to earn it.
- **One tonal drop, at Act 5, and it is never lifted.** From "Two enemy automatic weapons opened on the leading company inside the minefield" to the end of Act 6 there is no joke, no lift at the end of a line and no music sting.

**Accuracy corrections honored**

1. **Travers is never a legionnaire at Bir Hakeim, and the script says so before the title card.** Scene 9, the last beat of the cold open, is "She was not in it that night. That part comes later." She is never given a rank in the desert. The word Legion never attaches to her until the outro, where the beat opens "June nineteen forty five. Three years after the breakout." **The FSALE page's "sergent-chef Susan Travers" is an anachronism and does not appear.** The "only woman ever" claim survives verification and is used in exactly the report's safe formulation, "the only woman ever formally enrolled in the Foreign Legion," with "still" attached.
2. **She did not refuse the order and the script says the words out loud.** Scene 48: "Here is where the English version goes wrong. She did not refuse. She left." Then the convoy, then "she asked Koenig for permission to come back. He said yes." The act closes on "She talked her way back into a siege. Which is bolder than refusing," which makes the correction the punchline rather than a caption.
3. **The casualty figures are separated by what they measure and nothing is averaged.** Act 6 gives the French figure as the ministry's, "more than a thousand were killed, wounded, captured or missing," then names the residual conflict in its own beat, "Other official French figures say around nine hundred. Nobody has reconciled them." **The first draft explained the gap as a difference in counting the missing. QA showed the arithmetic refuses that explanation and no source offers it, so the beat now states the disagreement and offers no cause.** The 3,300 is given four beats later and labelled **Axis** in the same sentence. The pitch's category error is not reproduced anywhere.
4. **The strategic claim is refused in both directions, and that refusal is Act 7.** The French version is stated, then rebutted by the named specialist, then broken on its own timeline, then answered with the French defence ministry's own deliberately modest three-part claim, which is the single most useful calibrating fact in the research. Then the act turns and does the same thing to the English version, which overclaims the driver. **The episode declines the flattering version in the country the viewer is most likely to be from.**
5. **The eleven bullet holes are hedged in the spoken narration.** Scene 7: "Her car came out riddled with bullets. One account says eleven holes." Riddled is the official French account's own word and is asserted; the count is one origin only and is now marked as one account rather than as her testimony, because a competing count of roughly twenty is also in circulation.
6. **The Hitler order appears only in the documented narrow form.** Scene 93: "Hitler had separately ordered captured German political refugees killed. Rommel ignored it." **No general order to shoot captured Free French is asserted anywhere**, because none is documented. The Radio Berlin threat, de Gaulle's BBC reciprocity and Berlin's same-day retraction carry the beat instead, in that order, in two beats.
7. **Rommel is quoted once, in the cleared form, from his own account.** "Unfortunately for us, the French did not wait" closes Act 5. The "seldom in Africa" line was cut for length rather than paraphrased loosely. **No inspirational unsourced Rommel quote about Bir Hakeim appears.**
8. **The garrison gets nine consecutive beats and it is the longest single stretch in the episode.** Scenes 17 to 25: the strength, the roast of the flattening ("Every retelling calls them the French"), the two thirds figure, the Spanish Republicans, the Pacific battalion, Oubangui Chari, the North African company, the German refugees, the British battery, and Amilakvari. Three of those pictures come back in Act 6 when the losses are read, which is the whole reason the roster earns its room.
9. **The Italians open the battle and get their own act.** Act 2 is the Ariete attack from Rommel's order to the ring closing. The word Afrikakorps appears nowhere in the narration, because by the record the DAK's panzers were largely not fighting here.
10. **Everything the report and the QA gate told me to hedge is hedged in the spoken line, not in a note.** "One account says eleven holes." "The French defence ministry's own magazine says." "By her own account she told the general." "That comes from a French general who was there." "Near the column's front" rather than at the head of it. "By the French count that morning." "By one count, forty thousand heavy shells." "The order promised trucks fifteen kilometres away." "Other official French figures say around nine hundred."
11. **The Ariete arithmetic was rebuilt twice.** The 45-minute figure is French Wikipedia's and the ministry booklet says "en une heure," so the narration says "Under an hour," which is true under both and rests on the Tier 1 source. QA then found the tank arithmetic broken: seventy minus the next beat's thirty two is thirty eight, not thirty three, and the 33 at source counts wrecks left behind rather than survivors. The beat now says "The Italians lost about half their tanks that morning," which holds under the French estimate of about seventy and the Italian official history's sixty to sixty two.

**Deliberately not in the episode**

- The half a million mines figure. No authoritative source gives a mine count.
- The Jewish sappers from Mandatory Palestine and the Star of David flag on Koenig's jeep. Unverified.
- Rommel's alleged order giving French prisoners equal water rations. Flagged "reference necessaire" at source.
- **Churchill, entirely.** The quote about Bir Hakeim saving Egypt is flagged "reference necessaire" at source. The Fighting French renaming, which the first draft used in his name, was **cut at QA as a false attribution**: the change from la France libre to la France combattante was decided by the Comite national francais on 13 July 1942, and no source connects Churchill or the British government to it.
- The 33 surviving Ariete tanks, the named Sergeant Walter Grand, the 200 shells and 700 mortar bombs, the 42 Stukas, the 17 dead wounded, the 118 lost on the Nino Bixio, and "semi professional" tennis. **All removed at QA**, each for its own reason, recorded in the fact-check log below.
- The handwritten Rommel ultimatum delivered by a released English prisoner. Real and well cited, but cut for length; it is the first thing to restore if a beat frees up.
- Travers's father's rank. "Admiral" could not be independently verified, so her father is not mentioned at all.
- The French materiel loss figures. The 250 versus 50 vehicle gap is unresolved, so no vehicle or gun total is spoken.
- Earlier relationships attributed to Travers. Single-sourced, and not the episode's spine.
- The exact number who got out, 2,619. The narration says "about two thousand six hundred," which sits honestly inside the 2,500 to 2,700 spread rather than borrowing French Wikipedia's precision.

**Title options**

1. `She Drove the Breakout, Then Joined the Foreign Legion | Codename History` (**recommended**). It keeps the Legion hook, which is the entire click, and the word "then" does the whole accuracy job in four letters. It cannot be read as claiming she was a legionnaire in the desert, because it puts the two facts in the only order that is true. It is also literally the shape of the episode.
2. `Three Thousand Men Held Rommel for Sixteen Days | Codename History`. Truer to the battle than to Travers, and it is the version to use if the thumbnail is the position rather than the car.
3. `The Woman Who Asked to Go Back Into a Siege | Codename History`. The strongest pure-curiosity option, and it leads with correction two instead of correction one. Weakest of the three on search, best on retention.

**Rejected:** the pitch's "The Only Woman in the Foreign Legion Drove the Breakout," which fails constraint one as written, because it puts the Legion status in the present tense of the battle.

**Channel placement:** A standard-tier episode and the channel's first siege. It continues the myth-versus-record spine of Popski, Operation Tracer and the Mark 14, but with a new move: this one refuses **two** national myths that point in opposite directions, and refuses the flattering one in each country's own telling. It sets up an obvious sibling episode in Amilakvari, teased in the outro, and a second in the Afrikakorps-versus-the-legend format that Rondeau's work opens up.

**Build notes:** Tag as **TONE: STRAIGHT** the whole of Act 5, the whole of Act 6 up to and including scene 93, and Act 4 scenes 58 to 64. No music sting anywhere in that stretch. "One fell on the shelter of the worst wounded. All were killed" is the hardest line in the episode and should be the longest hold, read flat against a completely still image with no lift at the end. Rommel's two lines should be read unhurried and entirely reasonable in tone; he is not a cartoon villain here, he is a man being told to do something stupid. Act 2 should be read briskly and Act 7 slowly and slightly tired.

## YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION (paste-ready)

Night, the tenth of June 1942. A Ford staff car waits at a narrow gap cut through a minefield in the Libyan desert. Behind it, what is left of a brigade surrounded for two weeks. Ahead of it, three lines of German and Italian positions. The driver is a thirty two year old Englishwoman named Susan Travers.

Three years later she became the first woman ever enrolled in the French Foreign Legion. She was not in it that night, and she never refused an order to leave.

This is Bir Hakeim, a dry well and an old Italian camel corps post seventy kilometres inland from the Mediterranean, held from 26 May to 11 June 1942, and the two national myths built on top of it. The French telling overclaims the battle. The English telling overclaims the driver. We go through the record instead: a garrison of about 3,700 men that every retelling calls French, though two thirds came from France's colonies and overseas territories, plus a British anti aircraft battery sitting inside the position. The first attack was Italian, not German, and the Italian armoured division that made it lost about half its tanks in under an hour. Koenig ordered every woman out at the end of May and Travers went, then attached herself to a resupply convoy and asked Koenig for permission to come back, which he granted. That is a stranger and better story than the refusal everybody repeats. On the night of 10 June the brigade broke out through a lane the sappers never finished clearing, and her car came out riddled with bullets. One account says eleven holes. She was formally enrolled in the Legion as an adjudant chef in June 1945.

And on what the sixteen days actually bought, we take the French defence ministry's own answer rather than the national one. The ministry claims three things: the battle slowed the German advance, delayed the fall of Tobruk, and let the British pull back. It does not claim the battle made El Alamein possible. A historian of the Afrika Korps calls that part ridiculous, and the calendar agrees with him, because Bir Hakeim fell on the eleventh of June and Tobruk did not fall until the twenty first.

## PACKAGE NOTES

- The hook is two lines and both sit above the "...more" fold. Line one is the cold open verbatim in substance, the car at the gap, which is the curiosity gap. Line two is the payoff and carries **both** corrections at once: she was not in the Legion that night, and she never refused an order to leave. Putting the corrections in the fold is deliberate, because the description is what gets scraped and quoted, and this episode exists to refuse two myths rather than to retell them.
- **Susan Travers is never given Legion membership or a rank at Bir Hakeim anywhere in this file.** The word "then" in the recommended title, the "three years later" in the hook, and the "June 1945" in the body all do the same job, and at least one of them has to survive whatever the build's title pass does.
- **She did not refuse the evacuation order, and the description says so in the fold and again in the body.** The body gives the full sequence, Koenig ordered the women out, she went, she attached herself to a resupply convoy, she asked permission to return and got it. The word "refused" appears only in the negative.
- **The strategic claim is limited to the French defence ministry's own three items and nothing else:** slowed the German advance, delayed Tobruk, let the British pull back. The description states outright that the ministry does not claim the battle made El Alamein possible, attributes "ridiculous" to a historian of the Afrika Korps as the script does, and backs it with the dates, 11 June and 21 June, which need nobody's opinion. **"Saved the Eighth Army" and "made El Alamein possible" appear nowhere in this file in the channel's own voice.**
- **The 3,300 figure is not in this file at all.** It is the Axis loss figure, it is the number the original pitch mistook for a French count, and a description is exactly the place where a bare number floats free of the sentence that qualifies it. It stays on screen inside the narration's "Axis losses at Bir Hakeim" sentence and does not travel.
- **No French casualty number appears here either**, for the same reason. The script states more than a thousand, notes the rival figure of around nine hundred, and says nobody has reconciled them. That is a three-sentence problem and it does not compress, so the description does not attempt it.
- **The eleven bullet holes carry the script's exact hedge**, "One account says eleven holes." It traces to one origin and there is a competing figure of roughly twenty in circulation. The unhedged version must not appear in the description, the title, the thumbnail or the pinned comment.
- **No Hitler order about shooting captured Free French.** That sweeping version is NOT FOUND and is absent from the script.
