# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE PRISONER ON THE BICYCLE

**Target runtime:** 12 to 13 minutes (1,992 narration words; 13:17 at 150 wpm, 12:27 at 160 wpm)
**Style:** Flat vector cartoon. Bold black outlines, flat solid color fills, no gradients, no textures, no photorealism. Simplified rounded characters with big expressive heads and small bodies. Faces carry the joke. Backgrounds are simple flat color blocks and shapes: alpine green, snow white, castle grey, SS black, US olive drab, Wehrmacht field grey. Bold sans-serif ALL CAPS for any in-frame text. Every `[VISUAL:]` tag is ONE frozen still image held 3 to 7 seconds. No animation, no camera movement, no video.
**Story type:** Single-event deep cut (real, documented). A relay of five people with no authority who each did one impossible thing.
**Title (standard tier):** `One Prisoner. One Bicycle. WW2's Strangest Battle | Codename History`

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: This is a ground-up rewrite, revised after a QA pass that re-verified every checkable claim against the full text of Harding's *The Last Battle* instead of against the research reports. Twelve blocking corrections and ten refinements from that pass are applied. The research reports were wrong on three points (Borotra's age, Gangl's efficiency report wording, and the route past Wörgl) and Harding's own text wins. Several beats are deliberately corrected against the popular version of this story (Lee's rank, Gangl's medal, how many Frenchmen fired, what killed the tank, who commanded the SS, whether this was the first time Americans and Germans fought together). Those corrections are the good part. Do not let anyone re-simplify them back into the myth. Where the record stops, the script says the record stops.
>
> VOICE NOTE: Comedy lives in the NARRATION. Deadpan. Explain a catastrophe like it is the world's dumbest group chat argument. Second person "you" pulls the viewer in. Every act ends on a hook. Human cost is sacred: the Dachau subcamp, the number prisoners, Gangl's death, and Jack Lee's decline are played completely straight, no jokes, narration quiet and factual. Those blocks are marked `TONE: STRAIGHT`.

## ACT 1: A CASTLE WITH TWO KINDS OF PRISONER (0:40 - 2:50)

**[VISUAL: The castle on its rock, flat grey, alpine green below, flat snow-white peaks behind. Small ALL CAPS label: "AUSTRIAN TYROL. 2,300 FEET."]**

**NARRATOR:** Schloss Itter sits on a rock above a village in the Austrian Tyrol.

**[VISUAL: The castle with a jaunty striped hotel awning and a flat 1930s car outside. A rounded cartoon man in a hotel armchair reading a pamphlet stamped "WÖRGL CURRENCY". Bold label: "1932".]**

**NARRATOR:** In the thirties it was an alpine hotel. Édouard Daladier checked in during thirty two, studying the local currency experiment.

**[VISUAL: An official in a suit frozen mid-air, arms flailing, sailing out of the castle doorway past a large flat black SS silhouette. A clipboard tumbling. Behind him a sign reading in bold ALL CAPS: "GERMAN ALLIANCE FOR COMBATING THE DANGERS OF TOBACCO".]**

**NARRATOR:** After the Anschluss it briefly housed the German Alliance for Combating the Dangers of Tobacco. Then Himmler wanted the building.

**[VISUAL: Two flat grey boxes joined by a thick black line. Left box "DACHAU". Right box "SCHLOSS ITTER". Bold caption underneath: "SUBCAMP. FEBRUARY 1943."]**

**NARRATOR:** The SS took it in February nineteen forty three. Not as a hotel. As a subcamp of Dachau.

**[VISUAL: A line of small prisoner figures in striped uniforms frozen mid-step under the castle gate, carrying planks, buckets, coils of wire. Huge ALL CAPS numeral: "27".]**

**NARRATOR:** Twenty seven concentration camp prisoners built it. Twelve from Dachau, fifteen from Flossenbürg.

**[VISUAL: Flat cutaway of the keep. Three stacked floors drawn as a grid of small barred cells. Bold labels: "19 CELLS", "DOUBLE LOCKS", "FLOODLIGHTS", "3 MACHINE GUN POSTS".]**

**NARRATOR:** Nineteen cells on three floors. Barred windows, double locks, machine gun posts over the courtyards.

**[VISUAL: Čučković frozen on a ladder in a stone corridor, screwdriver in hand, wiring a light fitting. Striped jacket under a work coat.]**

**NARRATOR:** One of them was an electrician, kept on after the others went back.

**[VISUAL: Portrait frame. Čučković, thin face, calm eyes. Bold ALL CAPS caption: "ZVONIMIR ČUČKOVIĆ. CROATIAN. PRISONER."]**

**NARRATOR:** That is the man on the bicycle. Not a servant. A prisoner.

**[VISUAL: One frame split down the middle by a thick black wall line. Left, a dining table with a white cloth, wine bottles, a chandelier. Right, a bare room with bunks and a scrubbing brush.]**

**NARRATOR:** Because Itter held two completely different kinds of prisoner.

**[VISUAL: A group of prisoners in striped uniforms standing flat and still, one forearm turned outward showing a small tattooed number. Muted colours, no exaggeration. Caption: "NUMBER PRISONERS".]**

**NARRATOR:** The first kind built it. The castle called them the number prisoners.

**[VISUAL: Seven women in plain grey dresses frozen carrying laundry baskets and trays across a courtyard. A female SS overseer standing rigid at the edge of the frame.]**

**NARRATOR:** From August forty three that included seven or eight women transferred in from Ravensbrück.

**[VISUAL: A flat still of a scullery. A stack of dirty plates, a mop, a coal bucket, an apron on a hook. No people.]**

**NARRATOR:** They did the cooking, the laundry, the cleaning, the labour.

**[VISUAL: A flat still of an ornate room. A barber's chair, a bookcase, a wine rack. Bold ALL CAPS label: "TWO LITRES OF WINE A WEEK".]**

**NARRATOR:** The people they served had a library, a hairdresser, and a wine ration.

**[VISUAL: Plain flat black frame. White ALL CAPS text only: "SAME BUILDING. SAME YEARS."]**

**NARRATOR:** Same building. Same years. Most retellings leave that part out.

`END TONE: STRAIGHT`

**[VISUAL: A flat trophy cabinet graphic. Shelf one, two figures with sashes labelled "PRIME MINISTER". Shelf two, two figures in kepis labelled "COMMANDER IN CHIEF". Shelf three, a tennis racket, a trade union banner, and a woman in a coat labelled "DE GAULLE'S SISTER".]**

**NARRATOR:** The second kind was two former French prime ministers, two former army chiefs, a union boss, a Wimbledon champion, and Charles de Gaulle's older sister.

**[VISUAL: A price sticker slapped over the whole castle, bold ALL CAPS: "HOSTAGES".]**

**NARRATOR:** The Nazis called them honour prisoners. They were hostages with better bedding.

**[VISUAL: Dining room from directly above, flat. Three tables drawn as three coloured blocks with small figures, separated by thick black lines like walls. Bold labels: "TABLE 1: THE RIGHT", "TABLE 2: AGAINST TABLE 1", "TABLE 3: NEUTRAL".]**

**NARRATOR:** Which brings us to the seating chart. Three tables, arranged by who could not stand whom.

**NARRATOR:** Reynaud and Daladier had both been prime minister. Reynaud pushed him out. For two years they ate at separate tables.

**[VISUAL: Reynaud frozen mid-shout, finger jabbing at General Maxime Weygand, who recoils. Speech bubble from Reynaud: "Traitor, collaborator!"]**

**REYNAUD (character voice):** "Traitor, collaborator!"

**[VISUAL: Weygand, elderly, enormous white moustache, frozen mid-chase with one leg up and a fist shaking. Speech bubble from Weygand: "Hooligan!"]**

**[VISUAL: A flat escape-plan graphic. A dotted line over the mountains from the castle to a box labelled "SWITZERLAND". Prisoners in striped uniforms pointing at it.]**

**NARRATOR:** In forty four, the historian Volker Koop records, the number prisoners planned an escape to Switzerland. It would have taken the VIPs too. They refused.

**[VISUAL: The castle gate standing wide open. No guard. A dropped rifle on the cobbles. An empty dog lead.]**

**NARRATOR:** Which mattered, because a year later the guards left anyway.

## ACT 2: THE RELAY (2:50 - 4:55)

**[VISUAL: A calendar page torn to "30 APRIL 1945". Behind it, flat black cars pulling up at the castle gate stacked with suitcases, women and children.]**

**NARRATOR:** On the thirtieth of April, the last commandant of Dachau arrived at the gate.

**[VISUAL: SS-Obersturmbannführer Eduard Weiter, heavy set, uniform rumpled, frozen mid-stagger with a bottle in one hand.]**

**NARRATOR:** Eduard Weiter. He brought staff, wives, children, and a great deal to drink.

`TONE: STRAIGHT`

**[VISUAL: Weiter slumped at a table, bottle beside him, French prisoners in the background not looking at him. Muted flat colours, no comic exaggeration.]**

**NARRATOR:** At the castle he bragged to Itter's commandant that he had ordered some two thousand executions before he left Dachau.

**[VISUAL: A closed heavy wooden door seen from an empty corridor. Flat grey. Nothing else in frame. Small ALL CAPS in the corner: "2 MAY 1945".]**

**NARRATOR:** On the second of May he shot himself. Unmarked grave, just outside the walls.

`END TONE: STRAIGHT`

**[VISUAL: SS-Hauptsturmführer Sebastian Wimmer, thickset, small mouth, frozen holding out a sheet of paper to a French prisoner. Bold ALL CAPS on the paper: "PLEASE SIGN".]**

**NARRATOR:** Two days later the commandant, Sebastian Wimmer, left too. But first he asked his prisoners to sign a statement saying he had treated them correctly.

**[VISUAL: Wimmer's record laid out as a flat stamped file card: "MUNICH POLICE 1923", "SS 1935", "DACHAU", "POLAND 1939", "MAJDANEK". A blank silhouette photo in the corner.]**

**NARRATOR:** Wimmer had served at Dachau, in Poland in thirty nine, and at Majdanek.

**[VISUAL: The signed paper being snatched back, Wimmer frozen mid-grab with wide eyes. His wife already sitting in a loaded car behind him.]**

**NARRATOR:** Some of them signed it. Then he ran.

**[VISUAL: Empty guard posts. A machine gun on its mount with nobody behind it. Six open dog kennels, all doors swinging. Wide and still.]**

**NARRATOR:** The guards went with him. Fourteen SS men, one female auxiliary, six dogs. Gone.

**[VISUAL: A heavy door labelled in bold ALL CAPS "WAFFENKAMMER", hanging open, a broken padlock on the floor, racks of rifles and submachine guns inside.]**

**NARRATOR:** Which left the armoury unlocked.

**[VISUAL: Elderly French VIPs frozen in a scrum around the weapons rack. One holding a rifle upside down. One clutching three pistols. One with a submachine gun and a delighted expression.]**

**NARRATOR:** The prisoners broke in and armed themselves. Several of them were over seventy.

**[VISUAL: A bold white arrow curving backwards over a small calendar card reading "3 MAY". The bicycle figure drawn small underneath it.]**

**NARRATOR:** But go back one day, because the relay had already started.

**[VISUAL: Čučković frozen mid-pedal past a flat grey checkpoint barrier, a bored sentry waving him through. Small label: "1:30 PM".]**

**NARRATOR:** Half past one, third of May. Čučković rides out on a work order.

**[VISUAL: Close on the folded letter inside his coat, English handwriting visible, one line readable in bold: "TO ANY ALLIED OFFICER".]**

**NARRATOR:** In his jacket is a letter in English for the first American he finds.

**[VISUAL: A flat grey town street, black Waffen SS figures going door to door on both sides. Čučković frozen mid-pedal straight down the middle of it, head down. Bold ALL CAPS: "WÖRGL".]**

**NARRATOR:** The first town is Wörgl. The SS are searching it door to door. He rides straight through and keeps going.

**[VISUAL: A long flat valley road. Čučković tiny on his bicycle. Along the roadside, flat silhouettes of German trucks, marching columns, and a Tiger tank, all enormous next to him.]**

**NARRATOR:** Thirty five miles up the Inn valley. Past checkpoints, past columns, past Tiger tanks.

**[VISUAL: Deep blue flat dusk sky. Čučković frozen with one foot down at the roadside, facing an olive drab American armoured car with a white star.]**

**NARRATOR:** That evening, outside Innsbruck, he found the American Hundred and Third Infantry Division. It should have worked.

**[VISUAL: A flat map graphic. A dotted rescue route from "INNSBRUCK" toward "ITTER", stopped dead by a bold red burst labelled "RATTENBERG". Huge ALL CAPS: "TURNED BACK".]**

**NARRATOR:** Next day a rescue party set out with him, found a hundred SS holding Rattenberg, and turned back. Thirty five miles. Nothing.

**[VISUAL: A second man on a second bicycle at the castle gate. An apron under his coat, a wooden spoon in his pocket. Bold caption: "ANDREAS KROBOT. CZECH. THE COOK."]**

**NARRATOR:** So a second prisoner got on a second bicycle.

**[VISUAL: Krobot frozen mid-pedal on the downhill road, flat green trees either side, the village in the valley below. Small label: "4 MAY, 11 AM".]**

**NARRATOR:** Andreas Krobot was the castle cook. He carried a second letter from Reynaud's secretary.

**[VISUAL: A grey street. Black SS silhouettes firing up at an upper window with a white sheet hanging from it. Krobot frozen turning his bicycle into a narrow side street.]**

**NARRATOR:** In Wörgl the SS were shooting at any window flying a white flag. Krobot turned down a side street and gambled on a stranger.

**[VISUAL: A man in civilian clothes in a doorway, frozen pulling the corner of a small red and white Austrian flag out of his coat pocket. Krobot's feet still on the pedals.]**

**NARRATOR:** The stranger pulled the corner of an Austrian flag out of his pocket. Minutes later Krobot was talking to a major in the German army.

## ACT 3: THE MAJOR AND THE LIEUTENANT (4:55 - 7:10)

**[VISUAL: Portrait frame. Major Josef Gangl in field grey, tired eyes, plain unheroic expression. Bold ALL CAPS caption: "MAJOR JOSEF 'SEPP' GANGL. WEHRMACHT."]**

**NARRATOR:** Major Josef Gangl. Everybody called him Sepp.

**[VISUAL: Gangl at a forward observation post with binoculars, frozen, flat grey ruins behind him. A flat timeline strip along the bottom of the frame: "1928 ENLISTED", "1938 SERGEANT MAJOR", "1940 COMMISSIONED", "1941 UKRAINE", "1944 NORMANDY".]**

**NARRATOR:** He enlisted at eighteen and came up from the bottom. Ukraine in forty one. Normandy in forty four.

**[VISUAL: The German Cross in Gold drawn large and flat, a gold wreathed starburst, with two smaller Iron Crosses beside it. Bold caption: "GERMAN CROSS IN GOLD. 8 MARCH 1945."]**

**NARRATOR:** In March forty five they gave him the German Cross in Gold. Not the Knight's Cross.

**[VISUAL: An open personnel file, flat, one typed line highlighted in bold: "A DEDICATED NATIONAL SOCIALIST. CORRECT POLITICAL VIEWS."]**

**NARRATOR:** His own efficiency reports called him a dedicated National Socialist with correct political views.

**[VISUAL: Flat text card, bold white ALL CAPS on black: "NOT A LIFELONG RESISTER." A small field grey figure standing alone beneath the words.]**

**NARRATOR:** So he was not a lifelong resister. He was, in his biographer's phrase, politically adaptable. Then he changed his mind.

**[VISUAL: One small figure in field grey standing alone in the middle of an empty flat frame, a thin scatter of tiny soldier silhouettes behind him.]**

**NARRATOR:** By April his battalion could barely fill a single gun battery. He was sent to hold the Alps.

**[VISUAL: Gangl frozen shaking hands with a civilian in a suit in a back room, an open crate of rifles on the floor between them.]**

**NARRATOR:** Instead he made contact with the local resistance and started handing them weapons.

**[VISUAL: Flat text card, bold white ALL CAPS on deep red: "PUNISHABLE BY DEATH". A small figure of Gangl standing beneath the words.]**

**NARRATOR:** That is treason. The penalty is death. Everybody involved knew it.

**[VISUAL: A row of alpine houses, one with a white sheet hanging from a window, a flat black SS silhouette standing in front of it with a rifle.]**

**NARRATOR:** Himmler's order was simple. Any house flying a white flag would have its men shot.

**[VISUAL: Gangl in a greatcoat standing alone in an empty street, the tail of a German column drawn small and distant on the road out of town.]**

**NARRATOR:** When the rest of his battle group pulled out, Gangl stayed. Ten of his men stayed too.

**[VISUAL: Gangl holding Mabire's letter, reading it, expression flat and unreadable. Krobot standing beside the bicycle behind him.]**

**NARRATOR:** That is who Krobot handed the letter to. Gangl had nowhere near enough men for a rescue.

**[VISUAL: The Kübelwagen frozen on a road between two flat wooded slopes, a white bedsheet tied to a broom handle standing stiff beside the driver, small black SS figures among the trees on both sides.]**

**NARRATOR:** So he drove through SS held ground under a white flag to surrender to the Americans.

**[VISUAL: Four flat olive drab Sherman tanks parked in a row across a street, guns level, the tiny Kübelwagen stopped in front of them. Bold label: "KUFSTEIN".]**

**NARRATOR:** Forty five minutes later he was looking at four Sherman tanks.

**[VISUAL: Portrait frame. Jack Lee, broad shouldered, tanker jacket, unlit cigar clamped in his teeth, one eyebrow raised. Bold ALL CAPS caption: "1ST LT. JOHN 'JACK' LEE JR. AGE 27."]**

**NARRATOR:** The man in the turret was First Lieutenant Jack Lee. Not a captain. That came later.

**[VISUAL: Lee drawn full body, stocky, fists on hips, with flat labelled callout arrows pointing at him: "AGE 27", "5 FOOT 10", "190 POUNDS", "FOOTBALL PLAYER", "UNLIT CIGAR".]**

**NARRATOR:** Twenty seven years old. A college football player from upstate New York.

**[VISUAL: Lee mid-laugh, head back, holding a glass, three other tankers around him. Bold banner across the top of the frame: "HELL FOR LEATHER".]**

**NARRATOR:** One of the best tank officers in the division, his commander said. Platoon nickname: Hell for Leather.

**[VISUAL: Édouard Daladier, small and prim in a suit, frozen mid-sniff with his nose in the air. Speech bubble: "Crude in both looks and manners."]**

**DALADIER (character voice):** "Crude in both looks and manners."

**[VISUAL: Paul Reynaud in a separate frame, one finger raised, conceding. Speech bubble: "The fine figure of a football player."]**

**REYNAUD (character voice):** "The fine figure of a football player."

**[VISUAL: Lee up in the open turret hatch, elbows on the rim, cigar in his teeth, reading the letter. Gangl standing at attention below by the tank track. Bold ALL CAPS across the sky: "HE SAID YES".]**

**NARRATOR:** An enemy major hands an American lieutenant a letter about French politicians. Lee read it and said yes.

**[VISUAL: A column of seven Shermans and support vehicles frozen on a road at dusk, deep blue flat sky. Bold hull lettering on the lead tank: "BESOTTEN JENNY".]**

**NARRATOR:** They left Kufstein just before seven. Lee's tank was named Besotten Jenny.

**[VISUAL: A stone bridge with a bold red weight warning sign. Five tanks parked short of it behind a thick black stop line.]**

**NARRATOR:** At Wörgl a bridge would not take the weight, and the column got halved.

**[VISUAL: A flat inventory graphic, icons in a row: two Sherman tanks, fourteen American soldier figures, ten German soldier figures, one Kübelwagen, one small truck.]**

**NARRATOR:** What continued was two tanks, fourteen Americans, ten Germans, and a borrowed truck.

**[VISUAL: The second Sherman parked alone on a bridge, crew frozen crouched at the parapet cutting demolition wires. Bold hull lettering: "BOCHE BUSTER".]**

**NARRATOR:** Then the second tank, Boche Buster, was left behind at another bridge. Remember it.

**[VISUAL: Besotten Jenny parked square across the castle's arched main gate at night. Deep blue flat sky, castle grey, small yellow window squares. Bold ALL CAPS in the sky: "4 MAY 1945".]**

**NARRATOR:** They reached the castle after dark and parked the tank across the gate. Nobody slept much.

## ACT 4: THE FIFTH OF MAY (7:10 - 10:00)

**[VISUAL: Grappling hooks hooked over a low wall, flat black, one hand appearing over the parapet. Pre-dawn deep blue. Small label: "4:00 AM".]**

**NARRATOR:** Four in the morning. Grappling hooks on the wall.

**[VISUAL: American and German soldiers side by side at the parapet, rifles frozen mid-recoil with bold flat muzzle flash shapes, both firing down the same slope.]**

**NARRATOR:** A small SS party tried the slope and got driven off.

**[VISUAL: A flat chart of icons in a row: a US white star, a Wehrmacht cross, SS runes, an Austrian flag, a French tricolour. Bold caption: "COUNT THE SIDES".]**

**NARRATOR:** Count the sides in this fight. Take your time.

**[VISUAL: The same chart, each icon now labelled: "AMERICANS", "WEHRMACHT", "ONE WAFFEN SS CAPTAIN", "ONE AUSTRIAN TEENAGER", "FIVE FRENCH PRISONERS". A thick bold arrow points all of them at a flat black block labelled "WAFFEN SS".]**

**NARRATOR:** Americans, German soldiers, one Waffen SS captain, an Austrian teenager, five French prisoners. All on the same side.

**[VISUAL: Portrait frame. SS-Hauptsturmführer Kurt-Siegfried Schrader in civilian clothes, a folded SS uniform on a chair behind him. Bold caption: "SS-HAUPTSTURMFÜHRER SCHRADER".]**

**NARRATOR:** The Waffen SS captain was Schrader. He had broken with Hitler after the July bomb plot.

**[VISUAL: Schrader in a stone stairwell, one arm pointing up the stairs, directing two German riflemen to window positions. His wife and two children frozen sitting on the steps behind him.]**

**NARRATOR:** He ran the defence of the keep alongside Lee. His wife and children were inside the whole time.

**[VISUAL: A hillside eight hundred yards off. Two flat grey guns being set up, a small one labelled "20MM" and a long one labelled "88MM".]**

**NARRATOR:** Half past eight. Lee watched them siting a twenty millimetre cannon and an eighty eight.

**[VISUAL: Trucks unloading a packed crowd of camouflaged Waffen SS figures, one vehicle marked with a mailed fist insignia. Huge ALL CAPS: "100 TO 150".]**

**NARRATOR:** The trucks unloaded between one hundred and one hundred and fifty Waffen SS.

**[VISUAL: Flat text card, bold white ALL CAPS on black: "NOBODY KNOWS WHO COMMANDED THEM." A faceless SS silhouette below the text.]**

**NARRATOR:** Elements of the Seventeenth SS Panzergrenadier Division. No reliable source names the officer commanding them.

**[VISUAL: A Kübelwagen skidding into the courtyard with flat dust puffs, two German soldiers and one teenage boy in civilian clothes standing up in it holding a rifle.]**

**NARRATOR:** Gangl telephoned for reinforcements. What arrived was two soldiers and one Austrian teenager.

**[VISUAL: The 88 frozen mid-recoil with a bold flat blast shape at the muzzle. Across the frame, the third floor of the keep with a hole punched through it and flat chunks of stone hanging in the air.]**

**NARRATOR:** A few minutes after ten, an eighty eight round went into the keep.

**[VISUAL: Besotten Jenny frozen at the instant of the hit, a bold orange burst on the left side of the hull, flat black smoke shapes, hatches flung open.]**

**NARRATOR:** Seconds later, an antitank round hit Besotten Jenny in the left side and set her on fire.

**[VISUAL: Tank crewmen frozen mid-leap off the burning hull, one caught in mid-air over the lip of a ravine, arms out.]**

**NARRATOR:** The crew got out. Nobody inside the tank was killed.

**[VISUAL: A flat comparison graphic. Left, an 88mm gun with a big red X. Right, a smaller antitank gun labelled "75MM PAK 40" with a bold tick. Caption across the top: "WHO KILLED THE TANK".]**

**NARRATOR:** Everyone says the eighty eight killed her. The record says an antitank gun.

**[VISUAL: The front courtyard, flat cobbles, a low parapet wall, five elderly French figures spread along it holding weapons.]**

**NARRATOR:** And here is where the French prisoners pick up guns. Five of them. Not all of them.

**[VISUAL: Reynaud crouched behind the parapet with a Thompson submachine gun, small round glasses, an open steamer trunk behind him.]**

**NARRATOR:** Paul Reynaud, former prime minister, got a Tommy gun out of his own luggage.

**[VISUAL: Michel Clemenceau, huge moustache, standing dead calm at a loophole in the wall, rifle level, the burning tank visible through the gap.]**

**NARRATOR:** Michel Clemenceau posted himself at a loophole and stayed there.

**[VISUAL: The whole parapet wall in one wide flat frame, five figures spread along it firing, each labelled in bold ALL CAPS: "REYNAUD", "CLEMENCEAU", "GAMELIN", "DE LA ROCQUE", and a fifth label reading only "?".]**

**NARRATOR:** General Gamelin joined them. All five fired, in the historian's phrase, enthusiastically, if somewhat randomly.

**[VISUAL: Colonel de La Rocque, gaunt and grey, propped against the wall with a submachine gun, one spare magazine held up in his free hand.]**

**NARRATOR:** Colonel de La Rocque fired until he was down to his last magazine.

**[VISUAL: A fifth figure at the wall, lean and upright in shirtsleeves, drawn with an athlete's posture among the older men. Face deliberately turned away. No caption.]**

**NARRATOR:** And a fifth man at that wall, who we are coming back to.

**[VISUAL: General Weygand, very old, white moustache, shepherding two women through a doorway into the keep. His hands are empty.]**

**NARRATOR:** General Weygand did not fire a shot. He moved people into the keep instead.

**[VISUAL: Daladier sitting on a stone step inside the keep, two German soldiers standing over him protectively. All three flat and still.]**

**NARRATOR:** Daladier stayed inside. Two Polish born German soldiers looked after him and said Hitler was finished.

**[VISUAL: Reynaud standing in flat grey smoke afterwards, hands spread, apologetic. Speech bubble: "I regret that I cannot confirm that I killed one enemy."]**

**REYNAUD (character voice):** "I regret that I cannot confirm that I killed one enemy."

**[VISUAL: Lee crouched behind a wall with a radio handset, cigar gone, shouting into it. Speech bubble: "They're shelling the bajabers out of us."]**

**LEE (character voice):** "They're shelling the bajabers out of us."

**[VISUAL: Lee in the same crouch, other hand cupped over the handset, face tight. Speech bubble: "Listen, better get some doughs up here right away."]**

**LEE (character voice):** "Listen, better get some doughs up here right away."

`TONE: STRAIGHT`

At 3 to 7 seconds per still, 144 frames covers 7 to 17 minutes of screen time, so the frames are the flexible layer: hold the straight beats at 6 to 7 seconds and cut the joke beats to 3 to 4 seconds to hit the runtime exactly.

**Visual style spec (apply to every frame, no exceptions):**
- Flat vector cartoon. Bold black outlines of consistent weight. Flat solid color fills only.
- No gradients, no textures, no photorealism, no film grain, no lens effects, no lighting rigs. Nothing that implies live action.
- Simplified rounded characters, big expressive heads, small bodies. Faces carry the joke.
- Backgrounds are simple flat color blocks and shapes. Palette: alpine green, snow white, castle grey, SS black, US olive drab, Wehrmacht field grey, plus a single bold red used only for warnings, X marks, and the Austrian flag.
- Every frame is one frozen instant. Motion is implied by pose only: mid-stride with a foot off the ground, mid-vault, mid-leap, caught leaning back. Supported by motion lines, dust puffs, splash shapes, and objects in the air.
- One clear subject per frame. Nothing is stacked.
- In-frame text is bold sans-serif ALL CAPS. Used for labels, signs, map callouts, stamped dates, and full-frame text cards.
- **Every `(character voice)` line has its own dedicated frame with its own speech bubble, and the bubble text matches the spoken line exactly.** Nine speech-bubble frames total: Reynaud x3, Weygand x1, Daladier x1, Lee x4 (one at the gate, two on the radio, one in the epilogue). Verified programmatically: every bubble string matches its spoken line character for character. The Kufstein pair is now Daladier calling Lee crude and Reynaud conceding the football-player line, which is what Harding actually reports and plays as two Frenchmen disagreeing about the American.

**Voice choices:**
- Gangl is given **no spoken line anywhere in the script.** No verbatim quote from him survives in any source. Rather than dramatize one and flag it, the script keeps him silent and lets the narration carry him. Do not let a later pass add Gangl dialogue.
- The four speaking characters (Reynaud, Daladier, Weygand, Lee) use only quotes verified verbatim against Harding. Nothing invented, nothing reassigned.
- Four `TONE: STRAIGHT` blocks are marked inline for the downstream build: (1) Act 1, the number prisoners and the two classes of prisoner; (2) Act 2, Weiter's suicide; (3) Act 4, Gangl's death and his family; (4) Act 6, the number prisoners' disappearance from the record and Jack Lee's decline. In those blocks the narration goes quiet and factual, the art drops to muted flat colours with no comic exaggeration, and no joke lands.
- The French VIPs are texture and comedy, compressed into one act, carried by the seating chart and the feuds. No roll call with a portrait each. Borotra is deliberately withheld until Act 5 and is not foreshadowed by name.

26. **Daladier's 1932 stay was a working visit** to study Wörgl's emergency-currency experiment, not a holiday. The joke now rests on the true version.
27. **Weiter bragged about the Dachau executions to the commandant**, per Harding, not "by Reynaud's account." Reynaud is the source for the suicide.

29. **"Battle group," not "division,"** for what pulled out of Wörgl without Gangl. **No burial date** for Gangl, which Harding does not give. **The American platoon cleared the two SS positions**, with Borotra leading them.

## YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION

On 3 May 1945 a Dachau prisoner named Zvonimir Čučković was let out of a castle to run an errand, and instead rode thirty five miles up the Inn valley to find the American army.
Fourteen French hostages were locked in that castle. What saved them was a relay of five people, and not one of them had the authority to save anybody.

This is the Battle for Castle Itter, 5 May 1945, checked line by line against Stephen Harding's "The Last Battle" instead of against the internet's version. The legend and the record do not match, so we go with the record: Jack Lee was a First Lieutenant, not a Captain. Sepp Gangl held the German Cross in Gold, not the Knight's Cross. Exactly five French prisoners actually fired, and Weygand was not one of them. An antitank gun killed Besotten Jenny, not the 88. No reliable source names the Waffen SS officer who commanded the assault, and no reliable count of SS losses exists, so we do not invent either. And this was not the only time Americans and Germans fought side by side. Operation Cowboy came first, a week earlier, which makes Itter the second and the last.

Schloss Itter was a subcamp of Dachau. The forced labourers who built it, the people the castle called the number prisoners, are in this video, and they are treated straight.
