# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE COMMANDO HEIST BRITAIN SWORE NEVER HAPPENED

**Target runtime:** 15-18 minutes (~2,700 narration words at ~150 wpm)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake country dialogue.
**Story type:** Single genuinely codenamed WW2 operation, deep-dive. Operation Postmaster, the deniable ship theft at Fernando Po.
**Title (deep-dive, codenamed op):** `Codename: Postmaster - The Commando Heist Britain Swore Never Happened | Codename History`

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: This was a near-bloodless deniable theft, NOT a gun-blazing commando battle. The 2024 film "The Ministry of Ungentlemanly Warfare" dramatizes it with firefights and body counts that did not happen. Keep the record: no casualties to the raiders, essentially no shooting, ships lured and towed. Do not re-simplify the theft back into a Hollywood shootout for a better beat. Corrections are marked in the fact-check log.
>
> VOICE NOTE: The comedy lives in the NARRATION, not just the visuals. Deadpan delivery. This is a heist movie, so play it like one: a plan, a crew, a diversion, a getaway. Countries get fake dialogue. The absurdity is that Britain stole three ships by throwing a dinner party. The human cost, the later deaths of three of these men, gets played completely straight, no jokes, quiet and factual.

## ACT 1: THE ANNOYING SHIP IN THE NEUTRAL HARBOUR (0:45 - 3:15)

**[VISUAL: A map of West Africa, 1941. A tiny island in the Gulf of Guinea gets a spotlight: Fernando Po.]**

**NARRATOR:** Okay. Setup. This is a small island off the coast of West Africa called Fernando Po. Today it is called Bioko, and it is part of Equatorial Guinea. Back in 1941, it belonged to Spain.

**[VISUAL: A cartoon Spain sitting in a chair labeled "NEUTRAL," sweating, refusing to make eye contact with anyone.]**

**NARRATOR:** And here is the important part. Spain was neutral. It was run by a dictator named Franco, who had not picked a side in the war yet, and both sides desperately wanted him to pick theirs.

**[VISUAL: Cartoon Germany and cartoon Britain each holding flowers behind their backs, leaning toward Spain.]**

**NARRATOR:** So Britain had to be extremely careful. Do one rude thing to Spain, and Spain might get grumpy and join the other team. Nobody wanted that.

**[VISUAL: A giant scale. On one side, a single enemy ship. On the other, the entire country of Spain joining the Axis.]**

**NARRATOR:** Think about the math there for a second. On one side, some annoying enemy ships. On the other side, the very real risk of adding another whole country to the enemy. That is the kind of pressure that makes governments do careful, sensible, boring things.

**[VISUAL: The careful sensible option gets crossed out. A shady option gets circled instead.]**

**NARRATOR:** Britain, of course, was about to do the exact opposite of a careful, sensible, boring thing.

**[VISUAL: The harbour of Santa Isabel. Three ships highlighted: a huge liner, a chunky tug, a small barge.]**

**NARRATOR:** Which brings us to the problem. Sitting in the harbour of Santa Isabel, on this neutral Spanish island, were three enemy ships. One big Italian liner called the Duchessa d'Aosta. A German tug called the Likomba. And a little barge called the Bibundi.

**[VISUAL: The Duchessa d'Aosta with a radio antenna glowing, sending a signal. A cartoon German U-boat lurking under the waves, receiving it.]**

**NARRATOR:** The British were nervous the big Italian ship might be secretly radioing the positions of Allied ships to German submarines. Maybe it was, maybe it was not. But it was sitting there being suspicious in a neutral port, and Britain could not just sail in and sink it.

**[VISUAL: Britain staring at the ship, thought bubble filling with a giant question mark.]**

**NARRATOR:** Because sinking a ship in a neutral harbour is an act of war against the neutral country. That would hand Spain to the Axis on a plate. So Britain had a ship it really wanted gone, sitting in the one place it was not allowed to touch it.

**[VISUAL: A lightbulb appears over Britain's head. A very small, very shady lightbulb.]**

**NARRATOR:** Unless. What if the ship did not get sunk. What if the ship just, quietly, went missing. And nobody could prove who did it.

**[VISUAL: Cut to a cramped office in London. A door with a small plaque: "SPECIAL OPERATIONS EXECUTIVE."]**

**NARRATOR:** For a job like that, Britain had exactly the right department. And the right department had exactly the wrong kind of men.

## ACT 2: THE WRONG KIND OF MEN (3:15 - 6:00)

**[VISUAL: The SOE door swings open. A gallery of characters walks out in slow motion, heist-crew style.]**

**NARRATOR:** The job went to a unit with a wonderfully boring cover name: the Small Scale Raiding Force. On paper, No. 62 Commando. In reality, a tiny handful of men who specialized in doing insane things very quietly. They answered to the Special Operations Executive, Britain's official department of dirty tricks.

**[VISUAL: The SOE building has a hand-painted sign underneath: "MINISTRY OF UNGENTLEMANLY WARFARE." Winston Churchill nodding approvingly.]**

**NARRATOR:** People sometimes called the SOE the ministry of ungentlemanly warfare. Which tells you the exact energy we are working with here. This was a department created specifically to do the things a proper army was not supposed to do. Sabotage. Deception. Quiet chaos behind enemy lines. If it was underhanded, illegal, and useful, it was their whole reason to exist.

**[VISUAL: A recruitment poster that reads "MUST BE COMFORTABLE DOING CRIMES." A queue of eager applicants forms.]**

**NARRATOR:** And the men they recruited for it were not typical soldiers. They were the sort of person who hears the phrase "this is technically an act of piracy" and gets a little excited.

**[VISUAL: Cartoon Brigadier Colin Gubbins at a desk, moving little ship models around a map like a board game.]**

**NARRATOR:** The whole thing sat under a senior SOE man named Brigadier Colin Gubbins. But the man actually leading it on the water was this guy.

**[VISUAL: Maj. Gus March-Phillipps, tall, intense, standing on the deck of a small wooden fishing boat like he owns the ocean.]**

**NARRATOR:** Major Gustavus March-Phillipps. Gus to his friends. He had already sailed a tiny wooden fishing trawler called the Maid Honor all the way from Britain to West Africa, which is a genuinely terrifying trip, mostly because he wanted to.

**[VISUAL: The Maid Honor, a small Brixham trawler, bobbing on a huge open ocean, dwarfed by the waves.]**

**NARRATOR:** To be clear, this is a fishing boat. A small one. He took a fishing boat down the length of the Atlantic, in wartime, past enemy submarines, to go looking for trouble in West Africa. And he found it.

**[VISUAL: Capt. Geoffrey Appleyard beside him, calm and competent-looking.]**

**NARRATOR:** With him was Captain Geoffrey Appleyard. Steady. Brilliant. The planner of the group.

**[VISUAL: Capt. Graham Hayes, quiet, dependable, arms folded.]**

**NARRATOR:** And Captain Graham Hayes, one of the founding members of the whole unit. Remember him. We will come back to him, and it matters.

**[VISUAL: Anders Lassen, young, blond, holding a knife and grinning like this is the best day of his life.]**

**NARRATOR:** And then there was the Dane. Anders Lassen. A young Danish volunteer who had signed up to fight for Britain, and who was, by every account, alarmingly good at this. Hold that thought too.

**[VISUAL: The crew assembled on a dock, looking like a heist team. A big "THE PLAN" title slides in.]**

**NARRATOR:** So this is the crew. Now they need a plan. And the plan they came up with is one of the funniest things any military has ever seriously written down.

**[VISUAL: A scroll unrolls. Step one written in fancy letters: "THROW A PARTY."]**

**NARRATOR:** Step one of the plan to steal three enemy ships was, and I want to be clear this is real: throw a really good dinner party.

## ACT 3: THE MOST IMPORTANT DINNER PARTY IN THE WAR (6:00 - 8:45)

**[VISUAL: A different man onshore: SOE agent Richard Lippett, in a nice suit, shaking hands and smiling.]**

**NARRATOR:** For the party to work, they needed a man already on the island. That was an SOE agent named Richard Lippett. His job was the diversion. Get the officers off the ships and keep them off.

**[VISUAL: Lippett handing out fancy printed invitations to Italian and German officers on the docks.]**

**NARRATOR:** So Lippett arranged a dinner. A nice one. And he invited the officers of the enemy ships to come ashore and enjoy themselves. And, because sailors on a boring blockade duty will absolutely say yes to a free dinner, they came.

**[VISUAL: A calendar. Life on Fernando Po shown as extremely dull: officers staring at a wall, watching paint dry, counting seagulls.]**

**NARRATOR:** And you have to understand how boring their lives were. These were officers stuck on ships that mostly just sat in one harbour, on a small island, going nowhere. A fancy dinner ashore was the most exciting thing that had happened to them in months. It was an easy sell.

**[VISUAL: The dinner party in full swing. Count them: twelve Italian officers, two German officers, all seated at a long table.]**

**NARRATOR:** Twelve officers from the Italian ship. Two German officers from the tug. Sitting down to dinner. Getting comfortable. Ordering the good wine.

**[VISUAL: A close-up of the seating: every officer's chair faces inward, backs to a big window that looks out over the dark harbour.]**

**NARRATOR:** And here is the detail that makes this perfect. The officers were seated with their backs to the windows. The windows that looked out over the harbour. So the entire enemy officer corps of these ships was sitting there, laughing, eating, facing exactly the wrong direction to notice anything.

**[VISUAL: Out the window behind them, in the dark, two tugboats slide silently into the harbour.]**

**NARRATOR:** And out that window, in the dark, over their shoulders, the getaway had already begun.

**[VISUAL: The two British tugs, Vulcan and Nuneaton, creeping toward the anchored ships, engines low.]**

**NARRATOR:** Two tugs, the Vulcan and the Nuneaton, borrowed with a little help from the Governor of Nigeria, slipped into the harbour with their lights out. On board, the raiding party. Around three dozen men. A mix of the commandos, a few SOE agents, and local volunteers.

**[VISUAL: Small boats push off from the tugs toward the hulls of the Duchessa and the Likomba.]**

**NARRATOR:** They came alongside the ships as quietly as they could. Some crew were still aboard. But the officers, the men who could have organized any real defense, were three courses into dinner across town.

**[VISUAL: A raider climbing the anchor chain of the Duchessa, placing a package against it.]**

**NARRATOR:** Now, a ship this big does not just drive away. It is held in place by enormous anchor chains. You cannot untie those. So the plan was simpler than that. Blow them.

**[VISUAL: Charges being fixed to both anchor cables. A raider gives a thumbs up.]**

**NARRATOR:** They fixed explosive charges to the anchor cables. And then they waited for the party, several streets away, to reach exactly the right moment.

**[VISUAL: Split screen. Left: raiders crouched silently on a dark deck. Right: the dinner party in full noisy swing.]**

**NARRATOR:** So picture the two halves of this happening at once. In one building, fourteen enemy officers are getting louder and happier and more relaxed. On their ships, in the dark, a group of British commandos is quietly wiring up their anchor chains to blow. And the whole plan depended on the party staying loud enough, and the officers staying comfortable enough, for exactly long enough.

## ACT 4: THE THIRTY-MINUTE HEIST (8:45 - 11:30)

**[VISUAL: The dinner party. Glasses raised in a toast. Someone stands to make a speech.]**

**NARRATOR:** The idea was that the noise of the charges would be covered, or at least excused, by the party. A big loud dinner, a lot of confusion. And it mostly worked.

**[VISUAL: BOOM. The anchor cables snap in a bright flash. Water sprays. The ships lurch free.]**

**NARRATOR:** The charges went off. The anchor cables snapped. Both ships were suddenly loose in the water, no longer tied to anything.

**[VISUAL: The dinner party. Every officer freezes mid-bite, heads slowly turning toward the window.]**

**NARRATOR:** Now, obviously, an explosion in a quiet harbour is noticeable. The officers heard it. The town heard it. Some shots were reportedly fired in the confusion ashore.

**[VISUAL: Officers scrambling up from the table, knocking over chairs, running to the window to see nothing but dark water where their ships used to be.]**

**NARRATOR:** But by the time anyone got to a window, or down to the docks, it was already too late. Because the tugs had hooked on.

**[VISUAL: The Vulcan and Nuneaton straining, ropes taut, dragging the enormous liner and the German tug toward the harbour mouth.]**

**NARRATOR:** The Vulcan and the Nuneaton took the ships under tow and started hauling them straight out of the harbour and into the open sea. A little Italian barge, the Bibundi, went along for the ride too.

**[VISUAL: A cartoon clock on screen. The hands sweep around, showing about thirty minutes elapsed.]**

**NARRATOR:** From entering the harbour to leaving with the ships, the whole thing took about thirty minutes. Thirty minutes to steal an eight thousand ton ocean liner out from under an entire enemy garrison.

**[VISUAL: The raiding party on deck, checking on rounded-up prisoners sitting calmly.]**

**NARRATOR:** They took twenty-nine prisoners in the process, the crew members who had still been aboard. Later, at sea, twenty-seven of them, the Italian men, would be handed over to be interned safely in Nigeria.

**[VISUAL: A "MYTH vs REAL" split card. Left: an explosive Hollywood firefight with gunfire everywhere. Right: two tugs quietly towing ships away.]**

**NARRATOR:** And here is the part that matters most, and that most tellings get completely wrong. Not one member of the raiding party was killed. There was essentially no battle. It was a theft.

**[VISUAL: A movie poster for a dramatic action film, with a big red stamp over it reading "EMBELLISHED."]**

**NARRATOR:** If you have seen the recent film about this raid, full of gunfights and explosions and heroic last stands, know that a lot of that is invented for the screen. The real operation was quieter, sneakier, and honestly cleverer. The whole point was to get in and out without a fight, and they did.

**[VISUAL: The town of Santa Isabel behind them, lit up, people running around in total confusion, pointing at the empty water.]**

**NARRATOR:** Behind them, the island erupted into chaos. Because from the shore, it genuinely looked like three ships had simply vanished into the night on their own. And that, it turns out, was the whole idea.

**[VISUAL: The stolen ships disappearing over the horizon. A cartoon Britain, back in London, straightening its tie.]**

**NARRATOR:** Now came the hard part. Not stealing the ships. Lying about it.

## ACT 5: THE COVER STORY (11:30 - 14:00)

**[VISUAL: A furious cartoon Spain storming around, holding a newspaper, steam coming out of its ears.]**

**NARRATOR:** Because remember, Spain was neutral, and this all happened in a Spanish harbour. Spain was, understandably, absolutely livid. Its foreign minister called it an act of piracy and an attack on Spanish sovereignty. And he was, to be fair, completely correct.

**[VISUAL: Spain pointing an accusing finger at a lineup of suspects: Britain, America, and a shady figure labeled "REBELS?"]**

**NARRATOR:** So the finger got pointed. Was it the British? The Americans? Some anti-government Spanish rebels? Nobody could quite prove it.

**[VISUAL: Flashback to the raid: a raider tossing a couple of French military caps onto the empty dock as they leave.]**

**NARRATOR:** Which was not an accident. The raiders had reportedly left some Free French caps behind, just to muddy the water and give everyone a different suspect to argue about.

**[VISUAL: Cartoon Britain at a podium, hand on heart, the picture of wounded innocence.]**

**BRITAIN (character voice):** "Us? In a neutral harbour? We would never. Frankly, we are appalled that anyone would suggest it."

**NARRATOR:** Britain publicly denied any involvement whatsoever. But a denial is not enough on its own. If the ships just showed up in a British port, the story falls apart instantly. So the Royal Navy staged a little play.

**[VISUAL: The stolen ships at sea. A Royal Navy corvette, HMS Violet, sailing dramatically toward them.]**

**NARRATOR:** They sent a corvette called HMS Violet out to sea. And out there, in international waters, HMS Violet "intercepted" the ships. Dramatically. As if catching them by surprise.

**[VISUAL: HMS Violet firing a signal, the stolen tug Vulcan "surrendering" with exaggerated theatrical shock.]**

**NARRATOR:** It was completely staged. The Navy was "capturing" ships that its own people had already stolen. But now there was a clean official story. These enemy ships had left port and tried to make a run for Europe, and the Royal Navy had heroically caught them at sea. Nothing to do with any harbour. Nothing to do with neutral Spain.

**[VISUAL: HMS Violet escorting the Duchessa into Lagos harbour, a huge cheering crowd on the docks.]**

**NARRATOR:** Escorted by HMS Violet, the giant Italian ship sailed triumphantly into the British port of Lagos, to a cheering crowd, as a prize of war. Cleanly. Legally. On paper.

**[VISUAL: Back on Fernando Po. Richard Lippett, the dinner-party host, being led away by Spanish police for questioning.]**

**NARRATOR:** It was not perfectly clean for everyone. Richard Lippett, the man who had thrown the dinner party, was still on the island. A few days later the Spanish authorities took him in for questioning. They knew someone had made those officers vanish for the evening, and he was the obvious suspect. The cover story had to hold up close to home, too.

**[VISUAL: A cartoon spy in a tuxedo, martini in hand, admiring the cover story. A small label reads "cover story credited to Ian Fleming."]**

**NARRATOR:** The cover story was reportedly shaped by a naval intelligence officer you may have heard of. A man named Ian Fleming. Yes. That one. The one who later wrote James Bond. He helped sell the lie that let Britain keep the ships and keep Spain calm.

**[VISUAL: The Duchessa d'Aosta getting a fresh coat of paint and a new name on the hull: "EMPIRE YUKON."]**

**NARRATOR:** The stolen Italian liner was quietly renamed the Empire Yukon and put to work for the British war effort. The perfect crime. Almost nobody was hurt. The enemy lost three ships. And Spain never got the proof it needed.

**[VISUAL: The whole heist crew on a dock, silhouetted, walking away like the end of a heist film.]**

**NARRATOR:** For the men who pulled it off, it should have been the story they told for the rest of their lives. But the war was not done with them. And this is where the jokes stop.

## ACT 6: WHAT HAPPENED TO THE CREW (14:00 - 16:30)

**[VISUAL: Tone shifts. Muted colors. The crew photo from earlier, now faded, three faces gently highlighted. No jokes now.]**

**NARRATOR:** Operation Postmaster made these men legends inside the secret world. Anders Lassen, the young Danish volunteer, was commissioned as an officer on the spot and given an immediate Military Cross for his part in the raid. For a group of men who had just pulled off one of the cleanest operations of the war, the future must have looked survivable. But most of the men who stood on that deck did not live to see the end of the war.

**[VISUAL: A quiet map of the Normandy coast. A small wooden boat approaching a dark beach at night.]**

**NARRATOR:** In September 1942, Gus March-Phillipps led another raid, this time on the coast of occupied France. It was called Operation Aquatint. It went wrong on the beach. March-Phillipps was killed there, on the Normandy sand, along with others of his men.

**[VISUAL: Graham Hayes, the quiet dependable one, shown escaping across a border on foot.]**

**NARRATOR:** Graham Hayes survived that same failed raid and escaped inland, all the way across the border into neutral Spain. He must have thought he had made it.

**[VISUAL: Spanish border guards handing Hayes over to German soldiers. A prison door closing.]**

**NARRATOR:** He had not. He was handed over to the Germans. They took him to Fresnes prison near Paris and held him in solitary confinement for nine months. On the thirteenth of July, 1943, Graham Hayes was executed by firing squad.

**[VISUAL: Anders Lassen, older now, a major, leading men across dark water in Italy.]**

**NARRATOR:** Anders Lassen kept fighting, and kept rising. By 1945 he was a major. On the night of the eighth of April, 1945, on the shore of Lake Comacchio in Italy, in the final weeks of the war, Lassen led an attack on German positions. He assaulted the enemy strongpoints himself, and he was mortally wounded.

**[VISUAL: A Victoria Cross medal, rendered plainly and respectfully, on a dark background.]**

**NARRATOR:** For that action, Anders Lassen was awarded the Victoria Cross, Britain's highest award for valor. He received it after his death. He was the only man from outside the Commonwealth to earn the Victoria Cross in the entire Second World War. His parents accepted it from the King at the end of that year.

**[VISUAL: The three faces, still and quiet, side by side.]**

**NARRATOR:** Three men from one impossible night. One killed on a beach in France. One executed in a prison near Paris. One killed leading from the front in Italy. The heist made them famous. The war took them anyway.
