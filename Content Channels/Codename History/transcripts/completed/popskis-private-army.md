# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE MAN WHO TALKED HIS WAY INTO HIS OWN ARMY

**Target runtime:** 9 minutes (~1,400 narration words at ~158 wpm)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake dialogue. Hard tonal drops to straight at every human-cost beat, and they are held.
**Story type:** Single obscure unit, plus a sourcing problem. No. 1 Demolition Squadron, PPA, 1942 to 1945, and the gap between the legend and the paperwork.
**Title (standard tier):** `The Man Who Talked His Way Into His Own Army | Codename History`

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: This episode has an unusual sourcing problem and the whole thing is built on it. Vladimir Peniakoff is his own primary source, and his three surviving accounts contradict each other: the 1942 and 1944 award citations written by other people, his own 1945 Historical Summary written for King George VI, and his 1950 bestseller. The numbers grow with each retelling. **Every memoir-only beat in this script is attributed inside the narration itself**, with "by his own account", "he wrote that", "in his own report", or "nobody else recorded it". Do not strip those attributions out to tighten a line. Corrections honored: the DSO was NOT awarded for the action in which he lost his hand, it was recommended three weeks earlier and is not linked to it on screen; PPA did NOT find Wilder's Gap and the script says so; "Treviso, Austria" is a garble of Tarvisio and neither name is used; the Himmler hunt has zero sourcing and does not appear anywhere; PPA was NOT at Operation Roast and Lake Comacchio is never named; the "only wheeled vehicles ever in San Marco" line was a 1943 daydream and is not asserted; the weapon that took his hand is NOT FOUND and is not named; founding strength (23) and Italian establishment (80) are given as two different numbers at two different dates, because they are. The joke is never "he lied". He was genuinely brave and genuinely decorated. The joke is that the man could not round downwards. **One number is not part of that joke and must never be played as part of it.** His 1945 casualty table gives ten killed. His 1950 book lists twelve. The dead are not the exception to the growth, so the script no longer says they are, makes no remark of any kind about the discrepancy, and ends the episode on the roll of honour instead of on a line about his arithmetic.
>
> VOICE NOTE: Deadpan sarcasm is the default register from the cold open through Act 5. The comedy is in the narration, not just the visuals. The material does the work: a middle-aged sugar engineer with a jeep and a grudge, a unit whose official name sounds like a demolition contractor, a cap badge with a navigational instrument on it that nobody navigated with, and a founder whose petrol tally is nine times larger in the last document than in the first. It stops cold, every time, at the Senussi block in Act 1, at the Abdiel in Act 4, and at the graves in Act 6. Those passages are flat, slow and quiet. Do not put a music sting under any of them. Act 6 ends straight and stays straight into the outro.

## ACT 1: THE SUGAR FACTORY ENGINEER (0:33 - 1:57)

**[VISUAL: A large stone house in Huy, Belgium, wrought iron gate, autumn light, 1897.]**

**NARRATOR:** Vladimir Peniakoff was born in Belgium in eighteen ninety seven, to Russian Jewish parents.

**[VISUAL: A fifteen year old boy in a crowded Brussels university lecture hall, 1912.]**

**NARRATOR:** He started university in Brussels at fifteen and had two diplomas by seventeen.

**[VISUAL: The gates of St John's College, Cambridge, in heavy frost, January nineteen fifteen.]**

**NARRATOR:** Then he went to St John's College, Cambridge. You will read that everywhere.

**[VISUAL: An open college register page, a blank space where a degree should be recorded.]**

**NARRATOR:** He was there seventeen months and took no degree. Cambridge published a correction.

**[VISUAL: A steamer docking at Alexandria harbour in nineteen twenty four, cranes and palms.]**

**NARRATOR:** In nineteen twenty four he moved to Egypt and ran sugar factory machinery.

**[VISUAL: A cartoon middle aged civilian holding a hopeful sign at an RAF recruiting desk.]**

**NARRATOR:** War came. By his own account the RAF and Navy both refused him.

**[VISUAL: A quiet British Army recruiting office with an open door and one bored sergeant.]**

**NARRATOR:** The Army took him. October nineteen forty, second lieutenant, on the General List.

**[VISUAL: An officer in desert shorts talking with Senussi Bedouin at a camp, camels behind.]**

**NARRATOR:** They sent him to the Libyan Arab Force, because he spoke fluent Arabic.

**[VISUAL: A wide, plain photographic view of the Jebel Akhdar highlands, empty and green.]**

**NARRATOR:** This is the part that is not funny, and it happened before he arrived.

**[VISUAL: A plain map of Cyrenaica with sixteen coastal camp locations marked, no styling.]**

**NARRATOR:** Italy deported the population of the Jebel into sixteen concentration camps.

**[VISUAL: Rows of tents behind wire at a coastal internment camp, flat grey light.]**

**NARRATOR:** Over ninety thousand people, counted in the colonial authorities' own returns.

**[VISUAL: An empty stretch of desert track, no figures, low sun, nothing else in frame.]**

**NARRATOR:** Estimates of the dead differ. Not fewer than forty thousand. Some scholars say seventy.

**[VISUAL: A quiet Bedouin encampment at dusk, tents and low firelight, no people visible.]**

**NARRATOR:** The people who later hid Popski and carried his messages were those survivors.

**[VISUAL: An empty stretch of desert track, no figures, low sun, nothing else in frame.]** (REUSE: scene 19)

**NARRATOR:** The anthropologist Evans-Pritchard recorded that Bedouin who helped the British were later shot.

**[VISUAL: A blank archive index card in a wooden drawer, no names written on it.]**

**NARRATOR:** No names, no numbers, no places survive for those reprisals. That is all.

**[VISUAL: A fuel dump of stacked drums burning at night at Giovanni Berta, Libya.]**

**NARRATOR:** On the seventeenth of August, nineteen forty two, he burned a petrol dump.

**[VISUAL: Close-up of a typed Military Cross recommendation, one figure clearly legible.]**

**NARRATOR:** Fifty thousand gallons. Write that number down. We are going to need it.

## ACT 3: THE NUMBERS START GROWING (3:15 - 4:34)

**[VISUAL: PPA jeeps driving west across Libya past burned out enemy vehicles, early 1943.]**

**NARRATOR:** The unit drove into Libya to raid the German rear. The Germans left first.

**[VISUAL: A wrecked camp at Ksar Rhilane in Tunisia, every vehicle burning, smoke columns.]**

**NARRATOR:** In Tunisia the base camp was strafed. The wounded drove out. Everybody else walked.

**[VISUAL: A single New Zealand officer standing alone in a Tunisian mountain pass, jeep behind.]**

**NARRATOR:** You will read that PPA found the gap that outflanked the Mareth Line.

**[VISUAL: Two jeeps side by side heading north toward the bare Djebel Tebaga hills.]**

**NARRATOR:** They did not. Nick Wilder found that alone. PPA scouted a different corridor.

**[VISUAL: A Tunisian wadi packed with hundreds of Italian soldiers, hands raised, no exit.]**

**NARRATOR:** At Sened, by his account, six hundred Italians were trapped in a wadi.

**[VISUAL: One sergeant and two soldiers standing at the wadi mouth, tiny by comparison.]**

**NARRATOR:** Six hundred surrendered to a sergeant and two men. Nobody else recorded it.

**[VISUAL: A single sheet of typed nineteen forty two citation paper, one figure circled in pencil.]**

**NARRATOR:** Now. The petrol. This is my favourite thing in the entire story.

**[VISUAL: Close-up of a typed Military Cross recommendation, one figure clearly legible.]** (REUSE: scene 24)

**NARRATOR:** Nineteen forty two. A citation written by somebody else says fifty thousand gallons.

**[VISUAL: A typed nineteen forty five historical summary page, a larger figure in the text.]**

**NARRATOR:** Nineteen forty five, his own official summary. Over two hundred thousand gallons.

**[VISUAL: A results table on that same report, a much larger figure in one column.]**

**NARRATOR:** Three pages on, in that same report, a table says four hundred fifty thousand.

**[VISUAL: Three stacked documents on a desk, each showing a larger handwritten number than the last.]** (REUSE: scene 7)

**NARRATOR:** Fifty thousand. Two hundred thousand. Four hundred fifty thousand. The last two are his.

**[VISUAL: Cartoon Popski at a typewriter rolling in fresh paper, looking extremely pleased.]**

**NARRATOR:** The number grew nine times larger between the first document and the last.

**[VISUAL: A cartoon fuel drum swelling up like a balloon as pages turn beside it.]**

**NARRATOR:** His own veterans' association point out that table counts other units' work too.

**[VISUAL: A prisoner of war cage at Derna, barbed wire and Italian sentry towers.]**

**NARRATOR:** Same with prisoners. His citation says five British prisoners escaped the Derna cage.

**[VISUAL: Forty five South African prisoners strolling out through an open wire gate.]**

**NARRATOR:** His own summary says forty five South Africans. Five became forty five.

**[VISUAL: A calendar page turning over to a night time Italian coastline, ships offshore.]**

**NARRATOR:** He was brave, decorated, and unable to round downwards. Then came Italy.

## ACT 5: THE BREAKFAST RAID (5:26 - 6:35)

**[VISUAL: A composite battlegroup of armoured cars and infantry on the Adriatic flank, autumn.]**

**NARRATOR:** October nineteen forty four. PPA joins Porterforce, holding the Adriatic flank toward Ravenna.

**[VISUAL: A flooded canal landscape near Ravenna, mist over water, bare trees, winter.]** (REUSE: scene 68)

**NARRATOR:** In his report, never more than forty five PPA men were engaged there.

**[VISUAL: Jeeps racing along pine forest tracks firing mortars and machine guns, Pineta di Classe.]**

**NARRATOR:** So they drove in circles firing everything, to look like a much bigger army.

**[VISUAL: A team of oxen towing a stranded jeep along a flooded Italian road.]**

**NARRATOR:** When roads flooded, the jeeps were towed by oxen. Elite special forces.

**[VISUAL: A mediaeval stone watchtower at a river mouth, German sentries on the parapet.]**

**NARRATOR:** Then there was the tower. A German post guarding the approaches to Ravenna.

**[VISUAL: Two men lying under bushes with binoculars, watching a tower across flat marsh.]**

**NARRATOR:** A small party watched it for two days until they knew the garrison's routine.

**[VISUAL: Fifteen men hiding inside a barn at night, forty yards from the tower.]**

**NARRATOR:** Fifteen men landed at night and hid in a barn forty yards away.

**[VISUAL: German soldiers filing indoors for breakfast as British soldiers walk in behind them.]**

**NARRATOR:** At half past eight the Germans were called to breakfast. So were the British.

**[VISUAL: A tower interior with the whole garrison seated, hands raised, nobody shouting.]**

**NARRATOR:** They walked in behind them and took the entire post without a sound.

**[VISUAL: A second German patrol strolling up to the tower door in bright daylight.]**

**NARRATOR:** At midday a second party wandered over. At dusk an officer brought another.

**[VISUAL: Empty German positions south of a river at dawn, abandoned kit, no soldiers.]**

**NARRATOR:** The Germans could not work out where everybody went. They abandoned every post.

**[VISUAL: Jeeps ferried across a river in amphibious trucks, moving into Ravenna at midday.]**

**NARRATOR:** Ravenna was liberated on the fourth of December. PPA had helped considerably.

**[VISUAL: A typed DSO recommendation signed by a lieutenant colonel, late November nineteen forty four.]**

**NARRATOR:** In late November his commanding officer recommended him for a Distinguished Service Order.

**[VISUAL: A jeep on a canal bridge in December fog, bare trees, still water.]**

**NARRATOR:** Five days after Ravenna fell, he went forward on a reconnaissance.

## ACT 6: THE HAND, A NAVY, AND A LAP OF THE SQUARE (6:35 - 8:21)

**[VISUAL: A jeep on a canal bridge in December fog, bare trees, still water.]** (REUSE: scene 82)

**NARRATOR:** Ninth of December, half past eleven in the morning, north of Ravenna.

**[VISUAL: A jeep halted at a bridge approach, figures taking cover, plain and unstylised.]**

**NARRATOR:** Germans were thirty yards away. Left hand, right hand and ear, all hit.

**NARRATOR:** The summary report recorded that the only casualty was one man slightly wounded.

**[VISUAL: Cartoon Popski in a hospital bed, bandaged, reading that report, one eyebrow raised.]**

**NARRATOR:** That man was the commanding officer, and he had just lost a hand.

**[VISUAL: A DSO citation and a hospital chart lying side by side on a plain desk.]**

**NARRATOR:** The Distinguished Service Order was not for this. It was for three weeks earlier.

**[VISUAL: Two adjacent headstones in Ravenna War Cemetery, pale stone, level ground.]**

**NARRATOR:** A month before, on the tenth of November, the unit lost two men.

**[VISUAL: A single wreath at the base of two graves, quiet winter light, no people.]**

**NARRATOR:** Trooper Thomas Croghan was twenty three. Sergeant David Porter was twenty one.

**[VISUAL: Ramped landing craft carrying jeeps up the Po delta at dawn, minesweeper ahead.]**

**NARRATOR:** In April nineteen forty five PPA went afloat, onto the canals and lagoons.

**[VISUAL: Royal Engineers on a shallow draught lighter untangling a mine from a propeller.]**

**NARRATOR:** The Royal Engineers who ran the jeeps by night were Mentioned in Despatches.

**[VISUAL: The town of Chioggia across water, German coastal batteries visible on the shoreline.]**

**NARRATOR:** Then Chioggia, and a German garrison Popski counted at nearly seven hundred men.

**[VISUAL: A British lieutenant walking into a town under a white flag of truce.]**

**NARRATOR:** He wrote that Lieutenant Wallbridge walked in under a flag of truce.

**[VISUAL: A French born major talking calmly across a table from a German officer.]**

**NARRATOR:** He wrote that Caneri talked at the envoy for five hours, concealing one detail.

**[VISUAL: Two plain drawings side by side, twelve men with five jeeps, nine men with three.]**

**NARRATOR:** Popski's report says twelve men in five jeeps. His book says nine.

**[VISUAL: Hundreds of German soldiers laying down weapons in front of a handful of jeeps.]**

**NARRATOR:** The garrison surrendered. In that report, they kept asking when the troops would arrive.

**[VISUAL: Five jeeps being put ashore on a Venetian quay from landing craft, May 1945.]**

**NARRATOR:** On the first of May they put five jeeps ashore in Venice.

**[VISUAL: A jeep standing in the Piazza San Marco, pigeons scattering, stone arcades behind.]**

**NARRATOR:** Venice had already surrendered days earlier. He wrote that he drove seven laps.

**[VISUAL: An archive box of photographic prints labelled with a museum catalogue number.]**

**NARRATOR:** Photographs survive. The laps do not. He called it purposeless swagger himself.

**[VISUAL: A plain war cemetery headstone at Udine, level grass, overcast sky, no ornament.]**

**NARRATOR:** On the tenth of May, two days after the war in Europe ended.

**[VISUAL: An empty hillside track on the Yugoslav frontier, no figures, flat grey light.]**

**NARRATOR:** Private Albert White, aged thirty one, was shot by Tito's partisans. Allies.

**[VISUAL: A quiet row of twelve headstones seen from a distance, no ornament, still air.]**

**NARRATOR:** The unit's roll of honour lists twelve men killed serving with PPA.

**[VISUAL: A plain memorial panel with twelve carved names, pale stone, flat even light.]**

**NARRATOR:** Gaskell. MacGillivray. Hunter. Cameron. Curtis. Croghan. Porter. MacDowall. McCallum. Rogers. White. Snape.

## YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION (paste-ready)

In 1942 the British Army approved a new fighting unit and wrote a staff officer's insult onto the paperwork. Its official name was Number One Demolition Squadron, P.P.A., and P.P.A. stood for Popski's Private Army.

The man it was named after also wrote most of the surviving record, and his numbers keep growing. The petrol he destroyed is fifty thousand gallons in a 1942 citation written by somebody else, over two hundred thousand in his own 1945 report, and four hundred fifty thousand three pages later in that same report.

This is the story of Vladimir Peniakoff, a forty five year old Belgian sugar factory engineer in Egypt who talked the British Army into chartering him five officers, eighteen men and six vehicles of his own. It is also a story about the gap between the legend and the paperwork, because Peniakoff is his own main source and the archive quietly disagrees with him: five escaped prisoners at Derna in the official citation and forty five in his own summary, a Cambridge education the college itself published a correction about, and a founding scene that exists only in his memoir. Every memoir claim here is flagged as one, the corrections are on screen, and the parts that are not funny are told straight.
