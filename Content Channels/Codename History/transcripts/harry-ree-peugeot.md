# EPISODE SCRIPT: THEY WRECKED THEIR OWN FACTORY TO STOP THE NEXT AIR RAID

**Target runtime:** 9 minutes (1,406 narration words at ~158 wpm, 8:53)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake dialogue. Comedy is heavy through the cold open, Act 1 and Act 2, drops completely for Act 3, comes back dry for Acts 4 to 7, and stops for good at Act 8.
**Story type:** Single documented sabotage campaign, told as a correction of the story that is normally told about it. The Peugeot works at Sochaux, eastern France, July 1943 to spring 1944, and the schoolteacher who ran the circuit, Harry Ree.
**Title (standard tier):** `They Wrecked Their Own Factory to Stop the Next Air Raid | Codename History`

> **ACCURACY NOTE:** The pitch for this episode was wrong in three ways and the research found a fourth trap. Every correction is on screen and **none of them may be re-simplified back into the myth because the myth is punchier.** (1) **There was no second RAF raid on the Peugeot works.** A second Bomber Command attack was planned for March 1944 and **cancelled** after SOE sent London proof of the sabotage. Foot's line about the RAF returning "to do the job properly" was Ree's **threat** to Peugeot, not an event, and Act 7 says the raid was cancelled in plain words. (2) **"Tank turrets" is SOE's word and it is not used anywhere.** The narration lists what the French record lists: tank track shoes, light trucks, later aero engine parts for the German air force, plus military car parts after Porsche and Volkswagen took the plant over in early 1943. **Connecting rods, cylinder heads and aircraft landing gear were cut at the QA gate**, the first two because no source ties them to German tank work and the landing gear because it was pre-occupation French Army contract work that ended in 1940. (3) **The causality runs the other way from the popular telling and Act 4 says so out loud.** Ree's first contact with Peugeot management was **early July 1943, before the raid**, and it was about a loan repayable by the British Treasury. The raid supplied the argument, not the introduction. (4) **The occupation-economy figures are not in this script at all.** The 80 per cent productivity collapse and the fall from 6,000 vehicles a month to 500 are raw-material shortage and labour conscription, they mostly predate the sabotage, and attributing them to Ree would be the worst error available. The only effect figures spoken are **Foot's Appendix G**, the postwar verified list: three months, three weeks, five weeks, several months, output cut to forty per cent. **Foot's own page 287 claim that the plant was out of production for much of the rest of the war does not survive his own appendix and is not spoken.** (5) **Casualties are 123 killed, named on screen as the local French count**, with Besancon's 51 dead stated as separate and never merged. **The injured are given only as "hundreds more" and no number is spoken**, because 336 is Middlebrook's British figure while the French sources give about 250. **Foot's "some hundreds of townspeople were killed" is named on screen as wrong**, because it is the most-copied error in English. (6) **The contact is Rodolphe Peugeot**, per **Ascherson in the LRB and the IWM catalogue summary of Ree's own oral history**, and the Howarth "Robert Peugeot" error is corrected on screen. **Hedged in the spoken narration and not to be stripped out:** the truck total ("Around fourteen thousand"), the Sire first name (both given), and the sabotage effect figures (always "the verified list"). **NOT FOUND and therefore never spoken:** any second raid; any independent non-SOE test of the claim that sabotage beat bombing. **Deliberately cut:** the football match against the German guards and the returned bakelite bomb, which is the single most quotable beat in the file and is single-sourced to Howarth alone. **Cut at the QA gate against Ree's own edited papers, A Schoolmaster's War:** the Bradford Grammar posting, which is postwar; the conscientious-objector tribunal and the fire-service beats, because Ree says he had given up being a conchie before the fall of France and enlisted as a gunner in November 1940; "fifteen thousand trucks", which exceeded that model's total production; the line calling the river swim an embellishment, because Ree really did swim the flooded River Allan and only the four-mile crawl is invented; the cars lent by Peugeot for the Swiss crossing; the twenty-four arrests in one sweep; and the "seriously ill" detail on Robert Peugeot.
>
> **VOICE NOTE:** Deadpan sarcasm carries the cold open, Act 1 and Act 2. **It stops completely at Act 3 and does not return until Act 4.** Act 3 is the raid on the town and it is flat, slow and quiet throughout. It resumes dry through Acts 4 to 7, where the comedy is on the German procurement system and on the historians, never on the dead. **It stops again at Act 8 and is never lifted.** **Pronunciation:** Sochaux is spoken so-SHOH. Peugeot is puh-ZHOH. Rodolphe is ro-DOLF. Besancon is buh-zon-SON. Cesar is SAY-zar. Tarbes is TARB. Halifaxes is HAL-ih-fax-ez. Sire is SEER, never the English word "sire". Cauchi is KOH-shee. Petainism is PAY-tan-izm. Bonal is bo-NAL. Henri is on-REE. Jean Pierre is zhon-PYAIR. Michelin is the English MISH-uh-lin. "Stockbroker" is the English word and is read plainly. Montbeliard, Franche-Comte, Feldgendarme and Kubelwagen were all deliberately kept out of the narration so the English TTS never has to attempt them.

**Accuracy corrections honored**

1. **There is no second RAF raid anywhere in the script.** Act 7 states the March 1944 attack was **scheduled and cancelled** after London was sent proof of the damage. **The word "photographs" was cut at the QA gate**, because no source establishes what form the evidence took. Ree's threat about the RAF coming back is spoken by Ree, in quotation marks, in Act 4, five acts before the cancellation, so it can only be read as the threat it was.
2. **"Tank turrets" appears nowhere.** Act 2 lists tank track shoes, later aero engine parts for the German air force, light trucks, and after the Volkswagen takeover, military car parts. **Connecting rods, cylinder heads and aircraft landing gear were all cut at the QA gate**, the first two because nothing ties them to German tank work and the landing gear because it was French Army contract work that ended in 1940. **The truck figure was cut from fifteen thousand to around fourteen thousand**, because 15,463 exceeds the model's total production of about 15,306. Act 5 says "the foreman of the machine shop", dropping the words Foot puts in front of it.
3. **The causality is corrected out loud and it is the whole spine of Act 4.** "Ree's first contact with Peugeot management happened in early July. Before the raid." Then Sire, then the loan repayable by the British Treasury, then "That predates the raid," then "What the raid created was the argument."
4. **No occupation-economy figure appears anywhere.** The 80 per cent productivity collapse and the fall from 6,000 vehicles a month to 500 are not spoken, not implied and not visualised. **The only effect figures in the episode are Foot's Appendix G**: three months, three weeks, five weeks, several months, output cut to forty per cent, and each time the narration says "the verified list" rather than naming a stoppage as Ree's personal achievement.
5. **Casualties are stated as 123 killed and labelled "the local French count," and the injured are given only as "hundreds more."** The 336 figure was cut at the QA gate: it is Middlebrook's British count, the French sources give about 250, and the script had attached the British number to the French label. Besancon's 51 dead get their own two beats and the narration says the deaths "are separate, and are often wrongly combined." **Foot's "some hundreds of townspeople were killed" is quoted and then contradicted on screen**, with the corrected figure given as "about a hundred and twenty five", which sits honestly inside the 120 to 135 spread rather than borrowing one source's precision.
6. **The contact is Rodolphe Peugeot and the Howarth error is corrected on screen.** The script calls him "the family member behind the deal" rather than putting him in a specific room, because whether the decisive meeting was with Rodolphe in person or brokered through Sire is unresolved. Sire is named with **both** first names, Paul from SOE's report and Pierre from the French records, in one beat. **Robert Peugeot is named as the error and identified as the elderly father**, given as "seventy years old" rather than "seriously ill"; the wheelchair detail and the illness were both cut because they rest on Chatelot alone.
7. **The raid is dated to the night of the fifteenth of July.** Foot's 14 July is not used anywhere.
8. **Foot's page 287 claim that the plant was out of production for much of the rest of the war is not spoken.** Appendix G's numbers are used instead, and the log below says which is which every time.
9. **The myth inversion gets an entire act.** Act 4 is 183 words, the third longest in the episode, and it ends on "It is brave. It is also, quite openly, a sound business decision." That is the episode's argument and it is carried by SOE conceding against its own interest.
10. **The outro refuses the triumphalist ending.** It asks the question, answers it with the official historian's own hedged verdict, and then gives the one version of the claim the record can carry, which is about cost rather than effectiveness. **No independent, non-SOE test of "sabotage beat bombing" exists, so the episode does not claim one.**

**Title options**

1. `They Wrecked Their Own Factory to Stop the Next Air Raid | Codename History` (**recommended**). It is the whole story in one line, the click is the inversion rather than a name nobody knows, and **it is accurate in a way the obvious alternatives are not**: the next raid is the March 1944 one that was cancelled, so the title cannot be misread as claiming a second bombing happened. "Wrecked" rather than "blew up" also covers the machine tools, which is where most of the verified damage actually is.
2. `The Detonators Were In Backwards | Codename History`. The strongest pure-curiosity option and the best thumbnail pairing, since the picture is one small metal part. Weakest on search, because nothing in it says war, France or factory.
3. `The Factory That Paid to Be Sabotaged | Codename History`. Shortest and cleanest, and it leads with the money, which is the correction the research is proudest of. Slightly overstates the payment relationship, which is why it is third.

**Rejected:** `Codename: Stockbroker`. STOCKBROKER is a real SOE circuit name and would be legitimate, but the codename carries none of the episode and the brief's default is against the form.

**Channel placement:** A standard-tier episode and the channel's first industrial-sabotage story. It continues the myth-versus-record spine of Popski, Operation Tracer, the Mark 14 and Bir Hakeim, but with a different move: on Bir Hakeim the episode refused two national myths, and here **the myth and the correction come from the same book.** Foot supplies the wrong date, the wrong casualty figure and the overstated outcome in his narrative, and then supplies the corrective in his own appendix and his own summary judgment. That is a shape worth reusing. **There is no meaningful existing video coverage of this story**, and the circulating English errors have never been publicly corrected, so if the dates and numbers here hold, this becomes the reference version by default. It sets up an obvious sibling in the Michelin refusal at Clermont-Ferrand, which is teased in the outro.

**Build notes:** Tag as **TONE: STRAIGHT** the whole of Act 3 and the whole of Act 8. No music sting anywhere in either. "A hundred and twenty three people were killed that night" and "German troops shot him beside a road in April, nineteen forty five" are the two hardest lines in the episode and should be the longest holds, read flat against completely still images with no lift at the end. The cold open should be read briskly and the outro slowly and slightly tired. The three character-voice lines should all be read reasonably and unhurriedly; nobody in this episode is a cartoon villain, including the German works manager, who is simply a man whose replacement parts keep exploding.

## YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION (paste-ready)

Night, the third of November 1943. Six factory workers slip into a guarded German war plant in eastern France, press plastic explosive onto the transformers and the forge compressors, and walk back out. Nobody stops them. In the morning the factory is running exactly as it was, because every single detonator had been fitted the wrong way round.

So they went home and came back two nights later, on Guy Fawkes Night. And the family that owned the factory wanted the whole thing wrecked. They were helping pay for it, and by London's own account they had been considering it already.

This is the Peugeot works at Sochaux, July 1943 to spring 1944, and Harry Ree, the London schoolmaster and former pacifist who ran the SOE circuit called Stockbroker. The story that usually gets told has a British agent talking a reluctant company into sabotage after an RAF raid, and has the RAF coming back later to finish the job. The record is stranger and more uncomfortable than that, and we go through it in order.

Ree's first contact with Peugeot management was in early July 1943, before the raid, and it was about a loan to his circuit repayable by the British Treasury after the war. The raid supplied the argument, not the introduction. On the night of the fifteenth of July, Bomber Command sent 165 Halifaxes, the Pathfinder markers went down 700 yards beyond the factory over streets of houses, about 600 bombs landed on the town and about 30 landed in the plant. A hundred and twenty three people were killed, which is the local French count, and hundreds more were injured. Fifty one people died the same night at Besancon, and those deaths are separate and are often wrongly combined into the Sochaux figure. The RAF's own record assessed the factory at five per cent damaged and noted that production was normal afterwards. The official British history says some hundreds of townspeople were killed, and it is wrong.

Then the part the legend gets backwards. SOE's own 1946 internal report says Peugeot were already toying with the idea of sabotaging their own factory to stop production and prevent another disastrous British bombardment. The family member behind the deal was Rodolphe Peugeot, not Robert, the elderly father who had given up the presidency in 1941. Rodolphe tested the circuit by composing a personal message and asking for it on the BBC, then handed over the plans and the inside men. The postwar verified list records a three month stop after the fifth of November, then three weeks, five weeks, several months, and output cut to forty per cent as replacement machine tools kept being destroyed in the yard before they could be unloaded. In March 1944 Bomber Command scheduled a second raid on Sochaux, the circuit sent London proof of the damage, and London cancelled it. There was no second raid.

And it cost. The Gestapo broke up much of the local network, Ree's sub agent Eric Cauchi was shot and killed in early 1944, several directors were taken straight from a weekly board meeting, and one of them, Auguste Bonal, was deported and shot beside a road in Germany in April 1945, two weeks before the surrender. In the autumn of 1944 the Germans stripped the plant anyway. As for whether sabotage beat bombing, the official historian's own verdict on industrial sabotage in France is that the results were only moderately good.

## TITLE OPTIONS (working titles)

**The `Codename:` form is deliberately not used here.** STOCKBROKER is the name of an SOE circuit, a standing network of people, not a codenamed operation, and the channel reserves the `Codename: X` form for operations. Leading with `Codename: STOCKBROKER` would also promise a spy-network episode and this episode is about a factory, a bombing error and a business decision. All three options use the Standard form.

**What I did with the script's recommendation:** kept it, unchanged, as option 1 and as the recommendation. The word "next" is load-bearing and is not touched. ## PACKAGE NOTES

- The hook is the first two paragraphs and both sit above the "...more" fold. Paragraph one is the cold open's curiosity gap, the raid that failed on a technicality. Paragraph two is the payoff, the owners wanting it wrecked and having thought of it first.
- **There is no second RAF raid anywhere in this file.** The description states the March 1944 raid was scheduled and cancelled, and then says "There was no second raid" in plain words so that a scraped or quoted excerpt cannot invert it. Ree's threat that the RAF would come back is not quoted in the description at all, because out of its Act 4 quotation marks it reads as an event.
- **The occupation-economy figures are absent, as they are from the script.** No 80 per cent collapse, no fall from 6,000 vehicles a month to 500. Those measure raw material shortage and labour conscription, they mostly predate the sabotage, and attributing them here would be the worst error available. The only effect figures in the description are Foot's Appendix G list: three months, three weeks, five weeks, several months, output cut to forty per cent, and it is named as "the postwar verified list" for that reason. Foot's own p. 287 line about the plant being out of production for much of the rest of the war is not used.

The injured are "hundreds more" with no number, because 336 is Middlebrook's British figure and the French sources give about 250.
