# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE JAPANESE NAVY ATTACKED THE WRONG FLEET

**Target runtime:** 8-10 minutes (~1,400 narration words at ~150 wpm)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat 2D colors, simple shapes, exaggerated reactions, fake ship and admiral dialogue. Image-only build: every visual is one static still, held 4 to 5 seconds, hard cut.
**Story type:** Single-engagement disaster comedy with a straight human-cost core. Battle off Samar, 25 October 1944.
**Title (standard tier):** `The Japanese Navy Attacked the Wrong Fleet | Codename History`

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: Every claim traces to the two research reports in this folder. Several popular-telling errors are corrected on screen on purpose. Do NOT re-simplify them back into the myth. Specifically: the "world wonders" phrase was cryptographic padding, Nimitz never insulted anyone, and it has no connection to Kurita. Taffy 3 did not do it alone, Taffy 2's aircraft under Stump did much of the killing. Two of the three Japanese heavy cruisers lost were effectively killed by their own torpedoes. Paul Henry Carr received the Silver Star, no quoted dying words exist, and no upgrade campaign exists. Heermann's torpedoes missed Haruna. The Yamato torpedo chase is stated with no duration and no distance, because the popular "ten minutes" figure is undocumented. Samuel B. Roberts did not sink Chikuma. St. Lo was the first major warship SUNK by a kamikaze, not the first attack. Kurita explicitly denied breaking off for fuel. Evans' commissioning quote is remembered, not documented.
>
> VOICE NOTE: The comedy lives in the NARRATION, not just the visuals. Deadpan delivery, like explaining the world's dumbest group chat. Ships and admirals get fake dialogue. Second-person "you" pulls the viewer in. Punchy fragments for timing. Human cost is sacred: every beat marked TONE: STRAIGHT is played completely flat and factual, no jokes anywhere near it. Ship names must be pronounced exactly as written. Heermann has two Es and two Ns.

### Accuracy corrections made on screen

1. **The "world wonders" message.** Written as an internal US Navy cryptographic padding accident. The **complete dispatch text is shown on screen with no words omitted**, the GG and RR markers are explained, the real question is isolated by greying out the padding rather than by re-typing a shortened version, and an explicit on-screen statement says it never reached Kurita. This is the single most common error in retellings, so it is a structural beat rather than a footnote.
2. **Taffy 3 did not do it alone.** Stump and Taffy 2 are named, and the Chikuma kill is explicitly given to aircraft torpedoes rather than to Samuel B. Roberts' guns.
3. **Two of the three lost heavy cruisers were killed by their own torpedoes.** Used as the cold-open hook and paid off in Act 5.
4. **Heermann's torpedoes missed Haruna.** Stated as a miss. The real payoff, that they ran on and chased Yamato north, is the better story and is the one told.
5. **No duration and no distance is stated for Yamato's run north.** The popular "ten minutes" figure is undocumented, and the ten-mile figure is a track distance rather than a measured chase, so the beat ends at "until the torpedoes ran out of fuel." Do not add a number in the edit.
6. **Paul Henry Carr.** Silver Star, no quoted speech, no Navy Cross, no Medal of Honor upgrade campaign. The documented beat used is that he tried twice, and it is **attributed on screen to what survivors remembered** rather than narrated as observed fact. No timing is attached to it.
7. **Evans' commissioning quote** is framed as "he is remembered as saying," immediately followed by an on-screen acknowledgment that the wording is not in the archival record.
8. **St. Lo** is described as the first major warship sunk by a kamikaze, not the first attack.
9. **Kurita and fuel.** Explicitly denied on screen, in his own recorded framing.
10. **Sprague's "check his identification" line is not used.** The documented equivalent beat, that Brooks was told to go back and confirm the sighting, is used instead.
11. **Yamato's gun range is never stated numerically**, which sidesteps the bare "22 miles" trap entirely.
12. **St. Lo's dead are deliberately not stated.** The sources conflict at 141, "more than 140," and 143, and stating a St. Lo figure next to the one thousand seven hundred eighty five would reintroduce the scope problem that correction 14 exists to prevent. Do not add a St. Lo casualty number in the edit.
13. **Halsey's cap, not hat.** Stomping is Cox's rendering and is not stated. Weeping is not stated at all. The line is "an insult that Nimitz never wrote," with no claim about what Nimitz later knew.
14. **The casualty figure is scoped.** One thousand seven hundred eighty five is given as the complement of the **four ships sunk in the gun battle**, with St. Lo explicitly excluded on screen, because that is the scope of the source figure.
15. **Kumano.** Her bow was hit and set alight and her speed fell to twelve knots. The script does not say the bow was blown off.
16. **Hoel's forty-plus hits** are attributed to her captain's estimate in his own report, not stated as a count.
17. **Suzuya.** The script names the mechanism the record supports, her own loaded torpedo mount detonating. It does not assert what triggered it.
