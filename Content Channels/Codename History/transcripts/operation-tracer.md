# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE SIX MEN BRITAIN PLANNED TO BRICK INSIDE A MOUNTAIN

**Target runtime:** 9 minutes (~1,400 narration words at ~158 wpm)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake dialogue. Hard tonal drop to straight in Act 4 and again from Act 8 to the end, and both are held.
**Story type:** Single obscure operation, plus a myth problem. Operation Tracer, Gibraltar, 1941 to 1943, and the gap between what everybody repeats and what the one surviving document actually says.
**Title (standard tier):** `The Six Men Britain Planned to Brick Inside a Mountain | Codename History`

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: This episode exists because the popular retelling of Operation Tracer is wrong in several specific ways, and every one of those corrections is on screen. **Do not re-simplify any of them back into the myth because the myth is punchier.** (1) **"Seven years of supplies" is not in the archive.** Say it that way and no other way. The seven-year figure **is sourced**: it is printed in the Government of Gibraltar's own heritage listing HLMP2/036, and it is repeated by Wikipedia. What is true, and all that is true, is that it **does not appear in the record**. The surviving Admiralty summary specifies stores and water for **a year**, the researcher who transcribed the whole file states he found nothing about seven years in it, and the Independent of 4 February 2007 has Cooper's own account as up to a year and possibly longer. **Never say the seven-year figure cannot be sourced. Say it cannot be found in the archive.** The script plants the myth in the cold open, names the listing as its actual home in Act 7, and closes on the archive distinction. (2) **Only two of the six men are named with confidence: Bruce Cooper and Arthur Milner.** One source spells Milner "Milne" and is wrong. A third man is known only as "Windy" Gale of Kent, and it is not confirmed he deployed. **Three telegraphists are entirely unidentified** and the Government of Gibraltar says so directly. Lieutenant White RNVR was proposed and **rejected** and was never a team member, so he does not appear. **No complete roster is ever implied.** (3) **Ian Fleming was 17F, not 17M.** 17M was Ewen Montagu. Fleming's only documented Tracer link is attending the meeting of 25 January 1942. He did not conceive it, build it, or invent gadgets for it. Godfrey conceived it. The "self-heating soup" and the "tonsils and appendix removed" claims are unverified, trace to one uncited local article, were never attributed to Fleming in that source, and **do not appear anywhere in this script.** (4) **ADM 223/464 is real and its catalogue note names Operation Tracer**, covering dates 1942 to 1950. The "only document that mentions it" framing is an overstatement: the record itself references at least five other Tracer papers that are simply unlocated. The script says "the surviving account" and then says outright that it names five more. (5) **ADM 223/457 is a different Operation TRACER**, a June 1941 Malta convoy operation with Hurricanes off Ark Royal. It is not mentioned anywhere here and must not be added. (6) **The discovery year is genuinely disputed** between December 1996 and December 1997, and the evidence favours 1997 (the cavers' own words, the Gibraltar Heritage Journal 2002, the Government of Gibraltar, the Independent 2007) against Wikipedia and BFBS for 1996. The script refuses to resolve it in either direction and says so out loud: "One Christmas, ninety six or ninety seven, nobody agrees which." **The chamber length is disputed**, 45 ft in the 1942 archive against about 18 m measured today, so the script uses only the archive figures, which are the ones the arithmetic joke depends on, and never presents a modern measurement. (7) **The lime bath is tour-guide lore and is absent.** The record says embalming and cementing up, and that is what the script says. **Major Braithwaite's death is unverified** and the name Braithwaite does not appear at all. (8) **The pinhole camera is out.** The claim that the western slit projected the bay onto the cavers' notepaper is the discoverers' own speculation and the only technical assessment of it rebuts it on physics. It is not in this script and must not be added back for the picture. **Levick's sweet-shop hallucination is out** for the same class of reason: one uncited Tier 3 source for a claim about a real man's mental state. (9) **Bruce Cooper is the single human source for most of the interior detail and every beat that rests on him is attributed inside the narration**, with "by Cooper's own account", "by his own telling", "comes from one man". Do not strip those out to tighten a line.
>
> VOICE NOTE: Deadpan sarcasm is the default register through the cold open and Acts 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7. The comedy is in the bureaucracy and the engineering, never in the premise. The material does the work: an operation whose success condition is losing, a chamber whose exit strategy is that there isn't one, a survival consultant hired because he had already survived exactly this, a power plan that is a man on a bicycle, and a secret so well kept that the people who owned the Rock could not find it. **It stops cold at Act 4 and does not resume until Act 5**, and it stops again at Act 8 and stays stopped through the outro. Those passages are flat, slow and quiet. No music sting under Act 4, under the three unnamed men, or under any beat after "He confirmed it was the place." Nobody died in Operation Tracer, so this is not a casualty story. The straight material is what six men agreed to, and it is played exactly as straight as a casualty beat would be.

**[VISUAL: A German staff map of Gibraltar covered in fresh pencil marks, date stamped 1941.]**

**[VISUAL: A queue of Gibraltarian families with suitcases boarding a ship at the harbour, 1940.]**

**[VISUAL: Royal Engineers drilling a new tunnel gallery deep inside the Rock, dust and lamps.]**

**[VISUAL: A cartoon Naval Intelligence officer at a desk staring hard at a Gibraltar map.]**

## ACT 4: WHAT THEY SAID YES TO (4:03 - 5:03)

**[VISUAL: A plain rock tunnel entrance with a stack of loose bricks against the wall.]**

**NARRATOR:** This is the part that is not funny. The volunteers were told the terms.

**[VISUAL: A plain calendar with no end date marked, months running off the edge of the page.]**

**[VISUAL: A meeting room table with four chairs and a single typed minute sheet, January 1942.]**

**[VISUAL: A close view of one typed line in a set of meeting minutes, plain black type.]**

**[VISUAL: A patch of loose bare soil on a floor outside a heavy closed chamber door.]**

**[VISUAL: A ship approaching Gibraltar harbour beneath the Rock, August nineteen forty two.]**

**Narration word count: 1,399.** Recounted after the Wave 3 revision, on NARRATOR and (character voice) lines only, across 110 spoken beats, excluding VISUAL tags, headers, blockquotes and notes. Inside the 1,390 to 1,410 window. At the measured 158 wpm that is 8:51 of narration, comfortably above the 8:00 mid-roll floor. At the fast end of the observed VO range (173 wpm) it still runs 8:05. At the slow end (151 wpm) it runs 9:15, which is 5.05 seconds per image, marginally over the ceiling, so the build should let the short beats run short rather than padding holds.

**Revision accounting.** The QA pass removed four beats: Levick's sweet-shop hallucination (single Tier 3 source), and the three-beat pinhole-camera sequence at the western slit (discoverers' speculation, rebutted on physics by the only technical survey). That is 49 narration words gone. Three documented beats were written to replace them: the March 1941 revision of Felix as Felix-Heinrich, submitted to Halder on 10 March 1941 and to be launched once the Soviet Union was beaten (Act 1, the strategic why, and the reason the British could not treat Felix as dead), the carved recess at the western observation post, set back from the slit and hedged by the source as probably for binoculars (Act 6, the as-built sightline the popular telling misses), and the beat that names the Government of Gibraltar heritage listing as the actual home of the seven-year figure (Act 7, which is what makes the closing correction exact rather than rhetorical). - One (character voice) line, Franco's "Spain would love to. Spain is also completely broke.", which is the documented substance of his December 1940 refusal delivered as a shrug. ## YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION (paste-ready)

In 1942 the Royal Navy asked six men to volunteer to be sealed inside the Rock of Gibraltar. They would hold no key, the entrance would be bricked up behind them, and the plan only activated if Britain lost.

Every retelling of this story says they had seven years of supplies. The one surviving Admiralty account says a year.

This is Operation Tracer, the stay behind observation post built high inside the Rock between 1941 and 1943, and the gap between the version everybody repeats and the version in the file at Kew. The seven year figure is real and it is printed in the Government of Gibraltar's own heritage listing, but it cannot be found in the archive, where the record provides stores and water for a year. We go through the rest of it the same way: Ian Fleming attended one meeting and that is the entire documented link, the chamber's power plan was a man on a bicycle, and after the operation was closed down in August 1943 nobody could find the room again for over fifty years.

## TITLE OPTIONS (working titles)

Operation Tracer is a genuinely codenamed operation, so the `Codename:` deep-dive form is legitimately available here, unlike the last two episodes. Option 3 uses it.

1. **`The Six Men Britain Planned to Brick Inside a Mountain | Codename History`** (RECOMMENDED. Standard form. It is the script's own working title, it lands the whole premise in one line without needing a name nobody recognises, and "planned to" keeps it accurate, because nobody was ever sealed in.)
2. `Britain Built a Room With No Way Out, On Purpose | Codename History`
3. `Codename: Tracer - The Room Britain Built With No Way Out`

**Convention recommended: Standard.** The `Codename:` form works when the operation name itself carries recognition or intrigue, like Gunnerside. "Tracer" carries neither. It reads as a bullet type, it collides with the unrelated June 1941 Malta convoy operation of the same name, and it spends the first fourteen characters of the title on a word the viewer has no reason to click. The premise is the hook here, so the Standard form should lead. Option 3 is kept as the alternate because the convention is available and the build's title pass may want it.

Note: codename-history runs its own MrBeast title pass at build time, so treat these as the working / file titles.
