# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE SHIP THEY HAD TO TIE TWO DESTROYERS TO

**Target runtime:** 10:34 (1,584 narration words at 150 wpm, 150 still images, none held over 5 seconds; every act verified at or under 5.0 seconds per image)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake dialogue. STILL IMAGES ONLY, nothing held longer than 5 seconds, hard cuts, no camera moves.
**Story type:** Single operation, obscure-but-real. Operation Pedestal and SS Ohio, August 1942.
**Title (standard tier):** `The Ship They Had To Tie Two Destroyers To | Codename History`

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: The popular version of this story is wrong in four specific ways and this script deliberately corrects all four. Ohio did NOT steam into Malta under her own power, she was towed and lashed between destroyers. The torpedo that started her destruction came from the ITALIAN submarine Axum, not a German U-boat, and her keel was broken the next evening by a German bomb. Pedestal was NOT a clean win, nine of fourteen merchant ships were sunk. Malta's George Cross was awarded in April 1942, four months BEFORE Pedestal, and had nothing to do with this convoy. Do not re-simplify any of these back into the myth for a better joke.
>
> VOICE NOTE: Comedy dial is turned up on this one. The narration is doing the work, deadpan and sarcastic. The Ohio, HMS Penn, HMS Ledbury, Kesselring and the Italian admiral all get fake dialogue. Running gag: the Ohio simply will not sink, no matter how politely she is asked. Every joke is aimed at a real, documented decision. HMS Eagle, Waimarama, the Melbourne Star men who jumped, the Glenorchy report line and the whole of Act 8 are TONE: STRAIGHT and are marked inline. Act 1's rations and food-reserve stretch is marked straight in the narration, and its frames carry no visual gags either. No jokes anywhere near those blocks.
