# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE ESCAPE THAT HID INSIDE A GYM CLASS

**Target runtime:** 11-13 minutes (~2,000 narration words at ~150 wpm)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake dialogue.
**Story type:** Single obscure real operation. The Wooden Horse escape from Stalag Luft III, October 1943.
**Title (standard tier):** `The Escape That Hid Inside a Gym Class | Codename History`

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: This is the WOODEN HORSE escape of October 1943. It is NOT the "Great Escape." That was a different, larger tunnel from the same camp in March 1944, and it ended in a mass murder. This episode covers the earlier one where all three men got home. Keep those two events clearly separate. Character names in the 1949 memoir and the 1950 film are fictionalized. Use the real names. Do not re-simplify any corrected beat back into a myth.
>
> VOICE NOTE: The comedy lives in the NARRATION, not just the visuals. Deadpan delivery. The narrator explains a genius escape like it is the world's most elaborate gym-class prank. Second-person "you" pulls the viewer in. The ONE human-cost beat, the fate of the later Great Escape prisoners, is played completely straight. No jokes there.

## COLD OPEN (0:00 - 0:40)

**[VISUAL: A German officer proudly gesturing at a huge, sunny prison camp behind barbed wire, like a realtor showing a house.]**

**NARRATOR:** The Germans built this prison camp to be impossible to escape from. They thought of everything. Every angle, every trick, every possibility. And they were about to lose to a piece of gym equipment.

**[VISUAL: A plain wooden gymnastics vaulting horse sitting alone in the middle of the camp yard. Ominous music sting on the dumbest possible object.]**

**NARRATOR:** Not a plane. Not a bribe. Not a giant machine. A wooden vaulting horse. The box you jumped over in school and hated. Yes, that thing. That is our hero today.

**[VISUAL: Three British airmen peeking out from behind the horse, grinning.]**

**NARRATOR:** Three men were going to hide a tunnel inside a gym class. And I need you to understand that every part of this actually happened. Nobody made this up. History just did this.

**[TITLE CARD: "THE ESCAPE THAT HID INSIDE A GYM CLASS"]**

## ACT 1: THE ESCAPE-PROOF CAMP (0:40 - 3:00)

**[VISUAL: A map zooming into eastern Germany, landing on a camp labeled "STALAG LUFT III, near Sagan."]**

**NARRATOR:** This is Stalag Luft III, a German prison camp near a town called Sagan, in what is now Poland. It is run by the German air force, and it is full of captured Allied airmen. Men who got shot down, survived, and now have absolutely nothing to do all day except think about one thing. Leaving.

**[VISUAL: A German engineer with a clipboard, checking off a list titled "WAYS TO STOP TUNNELS."]**

**NARRATOR:** So the Germans designed this camp specifically to make tunneling hopeless. And honestly? Credit where it is due. They did a really good job.

**[VISUAL: The prisoner huts drawn far, far away from the perimeter fence, with a comically long empty gap in between.]**

**NARRATOR:** First, they put the huts a long way from the fence. Any tunnel would have to be really, really long, which means more digging, more time, and more chances to get caught. The idea being that anyone ambitious enough to try would just give up and take a nap instead.

**[VISUAL: A hut lifted up on little stilts, 60 centimeters off the ground, with a guard peeking underneath.]**

**NARRATOR:** Second, they raised the huts off the ground on stilts, so guards could just crouch down, look underneath, and spot a suspicious hole. No tunnel starts itself in a hut when the whole floor is basically a window.

**[VISUAL: A cross-section of the ground: a thin dark grey layer on top, and glowing bright yellow sand underneath.]**

**NARRATOR:** And third, the ground itself was a trap. The topsoil was dark grey. But dig down a little, and underneath was this bright yellow sand. So the second you started tunneling, you had piles of glowing yellow evidence that did not match the ground. It is hard to overstate how unhelpful it is when the earth itself is snitching on you.

**[VISUAL: A prisoner nervously trying to hide a bucket of neon-yellow sand behind his back while a guard stares.]**

**NARRATOR:** So the moment fresh yellow sand showed up anywhere, the guards knew exactly what it meant.

**PRISONER (character voice):** "This? No, this is just my personal sand. Everyone has some."

**GUARD (character voice):** "That is tunnel sand."

**PRISONER (character voice):** "It is decorative."

**[VISUAL: Underground microphones buried around the fence line, drawn with little sound waves, listening.]**

**NARRATOR:** And in case all of that was somehow not enough, they buried microphones around the perimeter. Sensitive ones, listening for the sound of digging. So even if you somehow hid the glowing sand, the camp could quite literally hear you scratching your way out.

**[VISUAL: The German engineer dusting off his hands, satisfied. A big stamp comes down: "ESCAPE-PROOF."]**

**NARRATOR:** Far from the fence. Raised huts. Glowing sand. Buried microphones. On paper, this camp was perfect. The Germans looked at all of it and saw a fortress. The prisoners looked at the exact same thing and saw a puzzle.

**[VISUAL: Three airmen huddled together, a lightbulb appearing over them.]**

**NARRATOR:** What if the tunnel did not start way back at the huts? What if it started right out in the open, close to the fence, in a place the guards would never think to check? All they needed was a reason to be standing near the wire every single day. A completely innocent, boring, nobody-look-at-this reason. Hold that thought.

## ACT 2: THE TROJAN GYM HORSE (3:00 - 5:30)

**[VISUAL: Captain Michael Codner, an army officer, sketching a wooden box on a scrap of paper.]**

**NARRATOR:** The idea came from this man, Captain Michael Codner of the Royal Artillery. And the idea was insane. It was also, and this is important, about three thousand years old.

**[VISUAL: A quick cartoon flashback: the ancient Trojan Horse being wheeled into a city, Greek soldiers hidden inside.]**

**NARRATOR:** You know the story of the Trojan Horse. The ancient Greeks could not get into the city of Troy. So they built a giant wooden horse, hid soldiers inside, and let the enemy carry it in for them. It is the oldest trick in the book. Genuinely, it might be the book. Codner looked at that ancient trick and thought, yeah, let's do that. But make it a gym prop.

**[VISUAL: A plywood vaulting horse being built from flattened Red Cross parcel boxes, hollow inside.]**

**NARRATOR:** He teamed up with Flight Lieutenant Eric Williams of the RAF. Together they built a hollow wooden vaulting horse out of plywood from Red Cross parcels. From the outside, a totally normal gymnastics box. Inside, enough room to hide a man, his tools, and bags of sand. Think about how confident you have to be to look at a wall of barbed wire, buried microphones, and armed guards, and decide the answer is arts and crafts.

**[VISUAL: Prisoners lifting the horse, one man plus tools crammed inside, carrying it toward the fence and trying to look casual.]**

**NARRATOR:** Here was the plan. Every day, a group of prisoners would carry the horse out to the exact same spot near the fence. What they would not mention to anyone, ever, under any circumstances, is that a man was already hidden inside it.

**[VISUAL: Prisoners energetically vaulting over the horse, doing gymnastics on top. Guards in a tower watching, bored.]**

**NARRATOR:** Then, on top of the horse, the other prisoners would do gymnastics. Real gymnastics. Vaulting, jumping, exercising, for hours. Not fake, keep-up-appearances gymnastics. Actual sweaty, exhausting gymnastics. To the guards, it just looked like a bunch of bored prisoners staying fit. Every single day. In the same spot. Which is exactly what you want them to get used to.

**[VISUAL: Cutaway to underneath: the hidden man quietly opening a trapdoor in the ground and starting to dig straight down.]**

**NARRATOR:** But underneath, hidden by the horse, the man inside was digging. Straight down first, then along. And every thump of a prisoner landing on top of the horse helped cover the sound of the digging from those buried microphones. The exercise was not a distraction from the tunnel. The exercise was protecting the tunnel.

**[VISUAL: The digger scraping earth into bowls, filling small bags with glowing yellow sand.]**

**NARRATOR:** He dug with bowls for shovels and metal rods to poke breathing holes up to the surface. No power tools, no diagram, just a man and some kitchenware, defeating a fortress. And all that glowing yellow sand, the stuff that would give everything away? He bagged it up and stashed it inside the horse.

**[VISUAL: End of the day. A wooden board slots over the tunnel mouth, grey surface sand sprinkled on top to hide it.]**

**NARRATOR:** At the end of each session, he covered the tunnel entrance with a board and sprinkled grey surface sand over it, so it blended in. Then the prisoners carried the horse back inside, now noticeably heavier, loaded with a man and bags of sand, all while trying very hard to look like it weighed exactly the same as it did this morning.

**[VISUAL: Prisoners casually scattering yellow sand into the camp vegetable garden and letting it trickle out of their trouser legs.]**

**NARRATOR:** Later, they got rid of the sand the classic prisoner way. Trickling it out of their trouser legs into the gardens, scattering it where no one would notice. The guards were watching a gym class. They were funding an escape.

**[VISUAL: A calendar flipping, day after day, the same horse, the same vaulting, over and over.]**

**NARRATOR:** And they did this over and over. For weeks. Then months. The same horse. The same spot. The same performance. If the guards had gotten bored, that was the whole point.

---

## ACT 3: THREE MONTHS UNDER THE HORSE (5:30 - 7:45)

**[VISUAL: A cross-section of the tunnel slowly extending underground, inch by inch, toward the fence.]**

**NARRATOR:** Now, digging a tunnel one gym session at a time is agonizingly slow. You can only dig while the horse is out, which means your entire escape plan runs on gym-class hours. The tunnel was cramped, dark, and could collapse on top of you at any moment.

**[VISUAL: The digger alone in a tiny tunnel, sand trickling from the ceiling, a single air hole of light above him.]**

**NARRATOR:** Picture being that man. You are alone, underground, in a tunnel barely wider than your body, breathing through a hole poked in the dirt, while directly above you your own friends are cheerfully jumping up and down on the entrance. And you cannot rush, because rushing gets you buried. This is not a job. This is a nightmare you volunteered for.

**[VISUAL: A third airman, Oliver Philpot, joining the group and helping haul sand.]**

**NARRATOR:** Around this point a third man joins the story. Flight Lieutenant Oliver Philpot, RAF. He was actually the escape organizer for their hut, which is a real job, the guy you registered your escape plan with, like a booking agent for prison breaks. He started by helping get rid of the sand, and then talked his way onto the actual escape team. Three men now. One tunnel.

**[VISUAL: The calendar flipping faster. The tunnel line creeping past the fence line on the map.]**

**NARRATOR:** For roughly three months, they kept it up. Same horse. Same spot. Same gymnastics. Three months of the most committed gym routine in human history. And bit by bit, the tunnel crept out under the fence.

**[VISUAL: A measuring tape stretching along the finished tunnel, reading about 100 feet.]**

**NARRATOR:** By the end, the tunnel ran about a hundred feet. Long enough to reach past the wire and come up just outside the camp. Every foot of it dug by a man hidden inside a piece of gym equipment.

**[VISUAL: The three men looking at each other, nodding. A calendar page reading "29 OCTOBER 1943."]**

**NARRATOR:** On the evening of Friday, the twenty-ninth of October, nineteen forty-three, it was time. The tunnel was finished. And three men were about to crawl out of it into the middle of Nazi Germany, which, if you think about it, is a strange place to escape to.

**[VISUAL: The tunnel entrance under the horse, one man lowering himself in, the fence looming ahead.]**

**NARRATOR:** But getting out of the camp was the easy part.

---

## ACT 4: THE HOME RUN (7:45 - 10:30)

**[VISUAL: A wide shot of occupied Europe at night. A tiny hole opening in the ground outside the camp fence, three figures emerging.]**

**NARRATOR:** Here is the thing nobody tells you about escaping a prison camp. Escaping is step one. And step one, it turns out, is the easy step. Now you are three foreign airmen, in wartime Germany, hundreds of miles from any friendly border, surrounded by an entire country that would very much like to catch you. You do not speak the language like a local. You have fake papers at best. And every train, every checkpoint, every suspicious stare could end the whole thing. Getting all the way home is so rare it has a name. Escapers called it a "home run." Most attempts never came close.

**[VISUAL: The three men splitting into two groups at a fork in the road. Williams and Codner one way, Philpot the other.]**

**NARRATOR:** So they split up. Williams and Codner traveled together. Philpot went alone. Different routes, same goal. Reach neutral Sweden, where the Germans could not touch them.

**[VISUAL: Philpot in a suit and hat, carrying a briefcase, walking confidently into a train station.]**

**NARRATOR:** Philpot's plan was pure nerve. He was not going to sneak across Germany. That is what everyone expects an escaped prisoner to do. He was going to buy a train ticket. Because Philpot had a disguise. He was now Jon Jorgensen, a Norwegian businessman.

**[VISUAL: Philpot holding up a sample of margarine, straight-faced, next to a sign reading "MARGARINE SALESMAN."]**

**NARRATOR:** And not just any businessman. A margarine salesman. Which sounds like a joke, except before the war, selling food products was Philpot's actual day job. So when a German asked what he did, he could talk about margarine convincingly, in detail, for as long as anyone was unlucky enough to want. And here is the genius of it. Nobody interrogating a man that boring about margarine expects him to be an escaped RAF pilot. The disguise was not the suit. The disguise was being deeply, professionally uninteresting.

**[VISUAL: A map showing Philpot's train route: Sagan to Frankfurt to Kustrin to the Baltic port of Danzig.]**

**NARRATOR:** He rode trains right across Germany. Through Frankfurt, then Kustrin, on to Danzig, a port on the Baltic Sea. Sitting in train carriages, surrounded by German soldiers and officials, calmly being the most boring man alive. In a matter of days he got closer to freedom than most prisoners ever dreamed.

**[VISUAL: Philpot at the docks, slipping aboard a ship flying the Swedish flag, hiding below deck.]**

**NARRATOR:** At the docks, he found a Swedish ship and smuggled himself aboard. On the fourth of November, less than five days after crawling out of that tunnel, Oliver Philpot walked into the British Legation in Stockholm, Sweden. From a hole under a gym horse to a neutral country in under a week. He had made it.

**[VISUAL: Cut back to Williams and Codner, moving north on foot and by train toward the port of Stettin.]**

**NARRATOR:** Meanwhile, Williams and Codner were working their way north to the port of Stettin, hunting for the same thing. A neutral ship.

**[VISUAL: A friendly Danish sailor whispering to the two men on a dock, then hiding them below deck.]**

**NARRATOR:** They got lucky. A Danish sailor agreed to help, and hid them aboard a ship. It carried them through Copenhagen and, days later, put them ashore in Sweden, at a town called Stromstad. By the middle of November, they had reached Stockholm too. Two more men who had crawled out of a tunnel under a gym class, now standing in a neutral country, free, and presumably never wanting to see a vaulting horse again as long as they lived.

**[VISUAL: All three men, separately, arriving safe in neutral Sweden, a big "SWEDEN" sign glowing.]**

**NARRATOR:** All three of them. Out of an escape-proof camp, across occupied Europe, to safety. And then home to Britain. Three home runs, from one wooden horse.

**[VISUAL: The empty vaulting horse still sitting back in the camp yard, guards walking past it without a second glance.]**

**NARRATOR:** For pulling this off, the men were later decorated for their escape. And the horse? It just sat there in the yard, having quietly done more damage to that camp than any weapon. And that is where this story turns.

---

## ACT 5: THE OTHER TUNNEL (10:30 - 12:30)

**[VISUAL: The camp again. The tone stays lighter for one more beat. A caption: "FIVE MONTHS LATER."]**

**NARRATOR:** Because the Wooden Horse was not the only escape planned at Stalag Luft III. And this is the part where it is really important not to mix two stories up, because almost everyone does. Just a few months after these three got home, the prisoners still inside that camp tried something much, much bigger.

**[VISUAL: A huge, deep, professional-looking tunnel, far longer and more elaborate than the Wooden Horse one, packed with men waiting.]**

**NARRATOR:** In March nineteen forty-four, dozens of men broke out through a single enormous tunnel. This is the escape most people have actually heard of. It became a famous book, and a famous film. It is called the Great Escape.

**[VISUAL: The tone shifts. Colors mute. No jokes now. A quiet nighttime shot of men crawling out of the tunnel into a snowy forest.]**

**NARRATOR (TONE: STRAIGHT):** Seventy-six men got out through that tunnel before the guards discovered it. But this time, there was no happy ending. Almost all of them were recaptured. Seventy-three of the seventy-six were caught.

**[VISUAL: A plain, somber title-style card. No animation gags. Just a stark statement on screen.]**

**NARRATOR (TONE: STRAIGHT):** And then, on Adolf Hitler's direct orders, fifty of those recaptured men were murdered by the Gestapo. Shot, in ones and twos, and never returned. They were unarmed prisoners of war, and they were killed for escaping.

**[VISUAL: Fifty small candles lit in a quiet row. Held for a beat. No music sting, no joke.]**

**NARRATOR (TONE: STRAIGHT):** Only three of the seventy-six ever reached safety. It is important to hold those two escapes next to each other. Same camp. Same idea. Same barbed wire. One ended with three men laughing their way to Sweden. The other ended in a war crime.

**[VISUAL: A gentle transition back to the Wooden Horse, sitting in the sun, but the mood stays respectful.]**

**NARRATOR:** Which is part of why the Wooden Horse is remembered the way it is. Not just because it was clever. But because, against enormous odds, everybody who climbed into that tunnel came home.

## OUTRO (12:30 - 12:55)

**[VISUAL: Eric Williams at a typewriter after the war, a book cover reading "THE WOODEN HORSE" appearing. Then Philpot's book "STOLEN JOURNEY" beside it.]**

**NARRATOR:** Eric Williams and Oliver Philpot both wrote books about it. Williams' became a bestseller and a hit film, which is why, of all the tunnels dug in that entire war, the one people still talk about is the one hidden inside a gym class. History rewards the man with the best idea. It rewards the man who writes it down slightly more.

**[VISUAL: End card. Subscribe button. Two thumbnails for next episodes.]**

**NARRATOR:** For the true stories from this war that almost nobody tells, subscribe. Next time: the escape attempt that involved building a full glider in an attic. Yes, an attic. Yes, a glider. And yes, also completely real.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Voice choices:**
- Comedy sits in the narration and rides entirely on the real absurdity: an escape-proof camp beaten by gym equipment, a Trojan Horse revival, a margarine cover story that was really the guy's day job. No invented flaws or invented gags.
- Second-person "you" beats kept ("Picture being that man.").
- Punchy fragments for timing ("He had made it." / "Three men now. One tunnel.").
- The whole Act 5 Great Escape section is played completely straight and marked TONE: STRAIGHT. No jokes over the 50 murdered prisoners. This is the sacred human-cost beat.

**Accuracy corrections / clarifications (see fact-check log):**
- This is the WOODEN HORSE (Oct 1943), explicitly separated from the GREAT ESCAPE (March 1944). Never merged.
- Real names used, not the fictionalized memoir/film names.
- Digging duration given as "roughly three months" per encyclopedic consensus; one source says four-and-a-half months, so the script hedges with "roughly."
- Tunnel length given as "about a hundred feet," matching the "over 30 m / 100 ft" sourcing.
- Philpot's medals and exact awards are not narrated as specific decorations to avoid overclaiming; the script says only that the men were "decorated for their escape," which is accurate (Philpot received the Military Cross for it).

**Title options:**
1. `The Escape That Hid Inside a Gym Class | Codename History` (recommended, the gym-prop hook is the most surprising and 100% true)
2. `The POWs Who Tunneled Out Using a Trojan Horse | Codename History`
3. `How 3 Prisoners Escaped the Nazis' Escape-Proof Camp | Codename History`

**Placement in the channel:** Perfect single-operation episode. Real, obscure to general audiences (people know the Great Escape, not the Wooden Horse), clever, and it pairs naturally as a companion piece to a future Great Escape episode.
