# EPISODE SCRIPT: THE ESCAPE THAT HID INSIDE A GYM CLASS

**Target runtime:** 9:30 to 10:30 (approximately 1,465 narration words at 150 wpm)
**Style:** OverSimplified format. Comedic animated history. Flat colors, simple characters, exaggerated reactions, fake dialogue. Sarcastic deadpan narrator.
**Story type:** Single obscure real operation. The Wooden Horse escape from Stalag Luft III, East Compound, 8 July to 29 October 1943.
**Title (recommended):** `The Escape That Hid Inside a Gym Class | Codename History`

**BUILD SPEC:** STILLS ONLY. No video clips anywhere in this episode. Every scene is a single dead-static generated image or locally rendered card. Every image is held for a maximum of 5 seconds, most for 3 to 4. Hard cuts only, no dissolves. The camera is locked at all times. No zoom, no pan, no push, no Ken Burns, no drift. Energy comes from the cutting rhythm and the narration, not from motion. `[VISUAL: REUSE ...]` beats re-cut an image already generated earlier. `[CARD: ...]` beats are rendered locally as text and never touch the image generator.

> ACCURACY NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: This is the WOODEN HORSE escape of October 1943. It is NOT the Great Escape. That was a different, larger tunnel from a different compound of the same camp in March 1944, and it ended in mass murder. Keeping those two events separate is the central correction of this episode. Michael Codner was a LIEUTENANT, not a Captain. Do not say the vaulting masked the seismograph microphones, that claim has no participant source. Do not state a weight or dimensions for the horse. Do not claim the horse survives or was destroyed. Character names in the 1949 memoir and the 1950 film are fictionalized. Use the real names. Do not re-simplify any corrected beat back into a myth.
>
> VOICE NOTE: The comedy lives in the NARRATION. Deadpan, dry, faintly amazed that any of this worked. The narrator is a person watching the Third Reich lose to a piece of school gym equipment and not quite getting over it. Guards, escape committee and prisoners all get short stupid lines. Second-person "you" pulls the viewer in. The ONE human-cost beat, the Great Escape murders, is played completely straight. No jokes, no wink, no bit. Marked TONE: STRAIGHT.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Voice choices**
- The narrator is drier and more sarcastic than the v1 pass. The running attitude is quiet disbelief that this worked, sustained across four months of screen time.
- Three running gags carry the episode: the horse as the world's most suspicious piece of exercise equipment, the gym class that nobody ever questioned, and the sheer number of days.
- Fake dialogue is used as its own beats because short stupid lines fit the five second ceiling perfectly. The guards get the "it is a box" exchange, Philpot gets the two line escape committee bit, and the sand exchange lands the yellow soil rule.
- Second-person pull-ins appear in the cold open ("You are about to watch three men beat the Third Reich") and in Act 4.
- Every act ends on a hook: the cold open on "I promise you", Act 1 on the one answer, Act 2 on Philpot talking his way onto the escape he approved, Act 3 on the spoil dispersal, Act 4 on the stick and the wrong direction, Act 5 on the run to the trees, Act 6 on Stockholm in six days, Act 7 on the myth correction.
- The cost act is a hard stop. Desaturate, slow the cut rhythm to the full five seconds, drop the score to a single sustained tone, and remove every comic device.

**Accuracy corrections made against v1**
1. Codner is a **Lieutenant**, Royal Artillery, not a Captain. v1 said Captain.
2. v1 said the vaulting masked the seismograph microphones. **Removed.** That claim traces to a single Wikipedia-lineage sentence with no participant source. This script states the microphones as a camp defence and gives the sourced reason for the horse, that the tunnel had to start out in the open near the wire.

The Luftwaffe built Stalag Luft III to make tunnelling impossible. Three prisoners beat it with a gymnastics vaulting horse, in broad daylight, in front of the guards.

For 114 days, from 8 July to 29 October 1943, British prisoners carried a hollow wooden vaulting horse out to the same patch of sand near the wire and held a gym class over a man digging underneath it. This is the real record of that escape: who actually planned it, why the tunnel had to start out in the open, and how all three men got home. It is not the Great Escape. That was a different, larger tunnel from a different compound of the same camp on the night of 24 to 25 March 1944, and this episode keeps the two apart.
