In-N-Out Opens Second-Largest U.S. Location at BLVD on the Las Vegas Strip

In-N-Out Burger opened a giant new restaurant right on the Las Vegas Strip, and it is one of the biggest the chain has ever built. The new spot sits inside the brand-new BLVD Las Vegas retail center at 3747 South Las Vegas Boulevard. It opened to the public on June 9, 2026. This location measures about 10,500 square feet and spreads across two floors. That makes it the second-largest In-N-Out in the entire country by indoor seating. Only the famous Barstow, California, location is bigger.

The numbers tell the story. The restaurant seats 170 people indoors and another 50 people on an outdoor patio. That is room for 220 hungry customers at one time. It does not have a drive-thru, which is a big surprise for a chain known for long drive-thru lines. The location hired about 70 associates, and starting pay is $18.25 per hour. For a brand that California fans treat almost like family, planting a flagship-size store in the middle of the world's most famous tourist street is a huge deal.

People in Las Vegas have strong feelings about In-N-Out. Locals already know and love the chain. Tourists from all over the world come to the Strip every day, and many of them have never tried it. Now they can walk right in. This blog will break down exactly what opened, why it matters for people who live here, the history behind it, what comes next, and what you can do if you want to check it out yourself.

What Happened

On June 9, 2026, In-N-Out Burger opened its newest restaurant inside BLVD Las Vegas. BLVD is a fresh retail and dining center built right on the Las Vegas Strip at 3747 South Las Vegas Boulevard. News outlets including FOX5, News3LV, and ABC7 Los Angeles covered the grand opening. The buzz was loud because this is not just any In-N-Out. It is one of the largest the company has ever opened.

The building is about 10,500 square feet. For comparison, a normal In-N-Out is usually around 3,000 to 4,000 square feet. So this one is more than double the size of a regular store. It has two full floors. The first floor holds the kitchen, ordering counter, and a big seating area. The second floor adds even more seating and the outdoor patio space. In total, the restaurant seats 170 guests inside and 50 guests on the patio. That comes to 220 seats overall.

The seating count is what earns the title. By indoor seating, this is the second-largest In-N-Out in the United States. The only one bigger is the well-known Barstow, California, store, which is a popular stop for road trippers driving between Las Vegas and Los Angeles. Being number two in the whole country is a serious bragging right for Las Vegas.

One of the most talked-about details is what this store does not have. There is no drive-thru. In-N-Out built its name partly on its drive-thru service, where lines of cars sometimes wrap around the block. But the Strip is not a place for car lines. The sidewalks are packed with people walking, and there is little room for cars to stack up. So the company built this as a walk-in restaurant only. Guests order at the counter and find a seat, much like a sit-down spot.

The store also created jobs. In-N-Out hired about 70 associates to run the new location. Starting pay is $18.25 per hour, which is above the Nevada minimum wage. In-N-Out is known across the industry for paying workers well and offering benefits that many fast-food jobs do not. That made the hiring news welcome for people looking for steady work near the Strip.

Background and History

In-N-Out Burger started in 1948 in Baldwin Park, California. Harry and Esther Snyder opened the first stand with one of the first drive-thru speaker systems in the state. The chain grew slowly and carefully over the decades. Even today it stays in just a handful of western states. It has never sold franchises, which means the company owns and runs every single store. That tight control is part of why the food and service feel the same everywhere.

In-N-Out came to Nevada years ago and built a loyal following in Las Vegas, Henderson, and the suburbs. Locals know the simple menu by heart. There is the Double-Double, the cheeseburger, the hamburger, fries, and shakes. There is also the famous "secret menu" with items like Animal Style fries. Despite the small menu, the lines stay long because fans trust the quality and the prices.

For a long time, though, there was no In-N-Out right on the Las Vegas Strip itself. The Strip is some of the most expensive real estate in the country, and the land is built up wall to wall with hotels and casinos. Most In-N-Out stores need land for a drive-thru and a parking lot, which the Strip simply does not have. So fans visiting the Strip usually had to drive off the boulevard to get their fix.

That changed with BLVD Las Vegas. BLVD is a new retail center built to give the Strip more shopping and dining choices in one walkable spot. By designing a walk-in store with no drive-thru, In-N-Out found a way to finally plant itself in the heart of the action. The decision to make it one of the largest stores in the chain shows the company expected huge crowds from day one.

What Happens Next

The first weeks after a big opening like this are usually wild. Expect long lines, especially during lunch, dinner, and late at night when the Strip is busiest. Because there is no drive-thru, all of those people will be walking in and finding seats. The 220 seats will help, but on busy nights the wait could still be long. If you go in the first month, plan for patience or pick an off-peak time like mid-afternoon.

Over time, the crowds should settle into a steady rhythm. The store will likely become a regular stop for tourists who want a taste of California while they are in town. It may also pull in locals who work or play on the Strip. Social media will keep the location popular, since visitors love to post photos of their first In-N-Out meal.

This opening could also push other brands to rethink the Strip. If a walk-in In-N-Out works well without a drive-thru, other beloved chains may decide they can make the Strip work too. That could bring even more familiar, affordable names to the boulevard in the next few years. Watch BLVD Las Vegas and similar centers for new announcements.

For the housing market, the next thing to watch is whether the energy at BLVD spreads. New centers can spark more building and more interest in nearby properties. If BLVD draws steady crowds, expect more talk about new projects and rentals in the surrounding blocks.
