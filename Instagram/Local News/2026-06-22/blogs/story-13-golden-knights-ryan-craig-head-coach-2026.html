Vegas Golden Knights Promote Ryan Craig from Henderson Silver Knights to Head Coach

The Vegas Golden Knights have a new head coach, and he did not come from far away. On June 17, 2026, the team named Ryan Craig as the fifth head coach in franchise history. He takes over for John Tortorella. What makes this hire special is where Craig has been working. For the past three seasons, he coached the Henderson Silver Knights. That is the team's minor league club, and it plays right here in our own backyard.

Craig is not a stranger to the organization. He has been part of the system for years, and he built a strong record in Henderson. In the 2025-26 season, his Silver Knights set team records with a 39-21-12 mark and 90 points. They also reached the second round of the Calder Cup Playoffs. That kind of success in the American Hockey League, or AHL, made him a name people watched closely.

Reporting indicates Craig signed a three-year deal with the Vegas Golden Knights, often called the VGK. This is a big moment for local hockey fans. It ties the future of the NHL team directly to the development pipeline in Henderson. In simple terms, the team grew its own coach instead of hiring a stranger from another city. For families in Las Vegas and Henderson, this story is about more than hockey. It touches local pride, jobs, and the neighborhoods around the rinks. Below, I will walk you through what happened, why it matters to you, and what comes next.

What Happened

On June 17, 2026, the Vegas Golden Knights made it official. They named Ryan Craig their head coach. He becomes the fifth person to hold that job since the team started play. The move closes the door on the John Tortorella era. Tortorella will not return as the head coach, and the team had to pick someone to lead going forward.

The VGK did not look outside the family for the answer. They looked down the road to Henderson. Craig spent the prior three seasons as the head coach of the Henderson Silver Knights. The Silver Knights are the team's AHL affiliate. That means they are the main minor league club where young players grow and get ready for the NHL. Craig was the man shaping those players, and he did it well.

His final season in Henderson was the best in club history. In 2025-26, the Silver Knights went 39-21-12. That added up to 90 points, which set a franchise record. The team also made a deep run in the Calder Cup Playoffs, which is the AHL's championship tournament. They reached the second round. Setting records and winning in the playoffs is exactly the kind of resume that gets a coach promoted.

Reporting indicates Craig signed a three-year deal. That length of contract shows the team wants stability. It is not a one-year tryout. The front office is betting on him to lead the group for several seasons. A three-year window gives a coach time to build a system, earn trust from players, and chase another championship.

This kind of move is called a homegrown promotion. The team did not bring in a famous outside name. Instead, they rewarded someone who already knew the players, the staff, and the way the organization likes to do things. Many of the players Craig coached in Henderson have already moved up to the NHL roster or could soon. That means he will be coaching familiar faces. He knows their strengths and their habits. That can make the change at the top feel smoother for the whole team.

For long-time fans, the timeline is easy to follow. Craig built a winner in Henderson over three years. He set records in his last season there. Then the NHL job opened up, and he was the choice. It is a clean, local story. The new face of the VGK was already working a short drive from the home arena.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might wonder why a coaching hire matters to people who do not even follow hockey. The answer is that the Golden Knights are a huge part of the local economy and the local mood. When the team plays well, restaurants, bars, and hotels near the arena get busy. Game nights bring crowds, tips for workers, and money that spreads through the valley. A steady, smart coach helps keep the team competitive, and a winning team keeps those crowds coming.

This hire also shines a light on Henderson. The Silver Knights play at Lee's Family Forum in Henderson. Having a strong minor league team in the area means more events, more jobs, and more reasons for families to spend time close to home. When the parent club promotes a Henderson coach, it sends a message that the local pipeline works. That can help draw fans to Silver Knights games and keep that part of the valley active and lively.

For homeowners, sports teams play a quiet role in how an area feels and how it grows. A region with pro and minor league teams often draws more visitors and new residents. People like living near entertainment they can reach in a short drive. Arenas and the businesses around them can lift the appeal of nearby neighborhoods. A team that stays strong and stays in town is a long-term plus for the area's image.

There is also a pride factor that is hard to measure but easy to feel. Las Vegas waited a long time to get a major pro team. The Golden Knights gave the city something to rally around from the very first season. When the team grows its own coach instead of importing one, fans feel a deeper bond. It says the organization trusts the people and the players it has built here. That story of local growth resonates with families who are putting down roots in the valley.

Finally, stability matters for everyone who plans around the team. Season ticket holders, local sponsors, and small business owners near the arena all do better when they know who is in charge and what to expect. A three-year deal for a coach who already knows the system points to a calm, steady plan. For a city that depends on tourism and entertainment, that steadiness is good news.

Background and History

To understand this hire, it helps to know the recent history of the Vegas Golden Knights. The team has had several head coaches in a short time. Craig is the fifth. The franchise has built a reputation for high expectations. It has reached the Stanley Cup Final more than once and won a championship in 2023. When a team holds that high a bar, every coaching choice gets a lot of attention.

The most recent chapter involved John Tortorella, a well-known coach with a long NHL history. When the team decided he would not return, the front office had to choose a direction. They could chase a big outside name, or they could promote from within. They chose to promote, and they chose Ryan Craig.

The Henderson Silver Knights have served as the team's AHL affiliate, giving Vegas a close place to grow young talent. Most NHL clubs have a minor league team, but many are far away in other states. Having the Silver Knights nearby lets the parent club watch prospects closely and move players up and down with ease. Craig was at the center of that work, turning raw prospects into players ready for the bright lights of the NHL.

His record speaks for itself. The 2025-26 season set franchise marks in wins and points, and the team made a real playoff push. Coaches who win in the AHL often earn a look for NHL jobs. Craig did more than earn a look. He earned the top job in the organization he already served. That path, from minor league bench to NHL head coach in the same system, is a classic story of hard work paying off close to home.

What Happens Next

With the hire official, the next steps for Ryan Craig and the Vegas Golden Knights come quickly. The first big task is building out his coaching staff. A head coach usually brings in assistants who fit his style. Fans should watch for those announcements in the weeks ahead. The names he picks will hint at how he plans to run the team on offense, defense, and special teams.

The summer also brings roster moves. The NHL Draft and the start of free agency fall around this time of year. Craig will work with the front office to shape the team he wants. Because he coached many current players in Henderson, he likely has strong opinions about who fits and who needs more time. His input could shape which young players get a real shot at making the NHL roster.

Then comes training camp in the fall, followed by the new season. That is when fans will get their first real look at a Craig-led team. People will watch how the team plays, how the young players develop, and whether the locker room responds to the new voice. Early results will set the tone, but a three-year deal gives him room to build over time rather than rush.

For the Henderson Silver Knights, the move opens a question of its own. The club will need a new head coach now that Craig has moved up. That hire matters too, because the Silver Knights remain the place where future VGK players are made. Local fans should keep an eye on who takes over in Henderson and how the team performs next season.
