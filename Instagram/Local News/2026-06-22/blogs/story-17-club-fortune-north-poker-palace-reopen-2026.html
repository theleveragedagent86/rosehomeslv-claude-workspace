Club Fortune North to Reopen Former Poker Palace in North Las Vegas

The old Poker Palace in North Las Vegas is coming back to life. The longtime locals' casino is expected to reopen as Club Fortune North by the end of June 2026. The new name comes after a near-complete remodel by Truckee Gaming, a company based in Reno. Truckee Gaming bought the property in October for $20 million and has spent the months since then giving the building a fresh look inside and out.

When it reopens, Club Fortune North will employ around 100 people. The casino will offer new slot machines, an expanded restaurant and kitchen, a redesigned bar, and a Boomer's Sportsbook for people who want to place bets on games. That is a lot of new energy for a building that has been a part of North Las Vegas life for decades.

There is one big change that has people talking. The new casino will not have a poker room or table games. That means the spot that was once called the Poker Palace will reopen without any poker at all. For many longtime locals, that is a bittersweet detail. The poker tables were the heart of the place for years, and losing them marks the end of an era even as the building gets a new start.

This story matters to anyone who lives in North Las Vegas or owns a home nearby. A reopened casino brings jobs, foot traffic, and new spending to the area. Let's look at what happened, why it matters, and what comes next.

What Happened

The Poker Palace closed its doors after many years as a small, no-frills casino popular with North Las Vegas locals. In October, Truckee Gaming, a gaming company headquartered in Reno, stepped in and bought the property for $20 million. Truckee Gaming runs several smaller casinos across Nevada, and the company saw a chance to bring this North Las Vegas landmark back under a new brand.

After the purchase, Truckee Gaming began a near-complete remodel of the building. This was not a small touch-up. The company set out to refresh almost everything about the property so it would feel modern and welcoming to both old fans and new customers. The plan included a full set of new slot machines, which are the main draw at most locals' casinos in the valley.

The remodel also focused heavily on food and drink. Club Fortune North will feature an expanded restaurant with a larger kitchen, giving the casino more room to serve meals to guests. The bar area has been redesigned as well, aiming for a fresh and comfortable place to sit, eat, and watch sports. On top of that, the casino is adding a Boomer's Sportsbook, which lets visitors bet on sporting events right inside the building.

One of the most notable parts of the plan is what will not be there. Despite the building's history as the Poker Palace, Club Fortune North will open with no poker room and no table games. The focus will be on slots, food, drink, and sports betting instead. For a place that built its name on poker, this is a major shift in identity.

The reopening was originally aimed at mid-April 2026. That target slipped, and the new expected opening is now the end of June 2026. Delays like this are common with big remodels, since construction, permits, and final inspections can all take longer than planned. The good news for the community is that the project is described as near-complete, so the wait should be nearly over.

When the doors do open, the property is expected to bring back roughly 100 jobs to the area. That includes workers in the casino, the kitchen, the bar, and the sportsbook. For a part of the valley that has seen plenty of change, the return of a working casino with a full staff is a real boost.

Background and History

The Poker Palace was one of those small North Las Vegas casinos that built a loyal following over many years. It was never one of the big resorts on the Strip. Instead, it was a true locals' spot, the kind of place where regulars knew the staff and each other. Its name made its purpose clear, and the poker room was the centerpiece of the whole operation.

Casinos like the Poker Palace have a special place in the valley's story. While tourists fill the Strip, locals' casinos serve the people who actually live and work here. They tend to offer lower limits, friendly service, and a neighborhood feel. Over time, many of these smaller properties have changed hands, closed, or been remodeled as the area around them grew and shifted.

North Las Vegas itself has gone through major growth in recent years. The city has added housing, warehouses, and new businesses as the valley has spread north. With that growth comes new demand for places to eat, shop, and have fun. A property like the former Poker Palace sits in the middle of that change, which makes it an attractive target for a company looking to invest.

Truckee Gaming, the new owner, is based in Reno and runs a group of casinos across Nevada. Companies like this often look for older properties they can buy, fix up, and rebrand. By spending $20 million to purchase and remodel the building, Truckee Gaming is betting that a modern locals' casino can thrive in this growing part of the valley. The Club Fortune name ties it to the company's brand and signals a clean break from the past.
