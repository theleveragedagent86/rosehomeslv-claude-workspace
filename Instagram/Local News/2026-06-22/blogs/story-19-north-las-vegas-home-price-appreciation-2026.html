North Las Vegas Leads Valley in Year-Over-Year Price Appreciation

North Las Vegas now leads the entire Las Vegas Valley in home price growth. New market data for June 2026 shows that homes in North Las Vegas gained value faster than any other major part of Clark County over the past year. The growth rate is about 6.6 percent year over year. That means a home that was worth a certain amount last June is now worth roughly 6.6 percent more today. No other big submarket in the valley grew that fast.

This is a big deal because North Las Vegas has long been seen as the more affordable corner of the valley. For years, buyers who could not afford homes in Summerlin or Henderson looked north for a better deal. Now those same buyers are helping push prices up. New homes are going up across the area, and people who got priced out of the central valley are moving in. Both of these forces are pushing North Las Vegas values higher.

The median price in North Las Vegas falls somewhere between $370,000 and roughly $435,000, depending on which report you read. Different sources count things in different ways, so we will treat that as a range rather than one exact number. The area has about 3.5 months of supply, and homes are selling for close to 99.2 percent of their asking price. In plain terms, sellers are getting almost everything they ask for, and there are not a lot of extra homes sitting on the market. If you own a home in North Las Vegas, this story is good news. If you want to buy there, it means you may need to move a little faster than before. Here is what is happening and what it means for you.

What Happened

A new market report from the Nevada Real Estate Group, with data drawn through Houzeo, took a close look at how each part of the Las Vegas Valley performed over the past year. The valley is not one single market. It is made up of several submarkets, like the central valley, the southwest, Henderson, Summerlin, and North Las Vegas. Each one moves at its own speed. When you split the numbers apart this way, North Las Vegas stood out.

The report found that North Las Vegas posted the strongest year-over-year home price appreciation among the major Clark County submarkets. The growth rate came in at about 6.6 percent. To put that in real money, a home worth $400,000 a year ago would be worth around $426,400 today if it tracked that rate. That is more than $26,000 in added value in just twelve months for a typical home. Owners who bought before the run-up are sitting on real gains.

The data points to two main drivers behind this growth. The first is new construction. Builders have been busy in North Las Vegas, putting up fresh single-family homes in growing communities. New homes often sell for more than older ones, and when a lot of new homes close at higher prices, the median for the whole area climbs. The second driver is buyers who got priced out of the core valley. When prices in places like the central valley and Summerlin kept rising, many buyers simply could not keep up. They turned to North Las Vegas, where the dollar still stretched further. That extra demand pushed prices up there too.

The supply picture helps explain the strength as well. North Las Vegas has about 3.5 months of supply. Months of supply tells you how long it would take to sell every home on the market if no new listings came in. A balanced market usually sits around five to six months. Anything well below that favors sellers. At 3.5 months, North Las Vegas leans toward a seller's market, which keeps prices firm. On top of that, homes are selling for about 99.2 percent of the asking price. That tells you buyers are not getting deep discounts. They are paying close to full price, and bidding has stayed competitive.

The median price sits in a range between $370,000 and roughly $435,000. We are showing a range on purpose because the published reports do not all agree. Some count only single-family homes, while others may fold in condos, townhomes, or new construction in different ways. Different time windows can also shift the number. Rather than pick one figure and call it final, the honest read is that the typical North Las Vegas home now trades somewhere in that band. Even at the low end, that is real value in a market many people still think of as the bargain corner of the valley.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

This news matters in different ways depending on where you stand. Let us walk through the main groups of people it touches.

If you already own a home in North Las Vegas, you are the clear winner here. Your home is worth more than it was a year ago, and it grew faster than homes in most other parts of the valley. That extra value is more than a number on paper. It can become real money. You can borrow against it with a home equity line if you need cash for repairs or other needs. You build a bigger cushion if you ever decide to sell. And you gain more freedom to move up to a larger home, because the equity you take out can become a down payment somewhere else. Owners who bought a few years back, when prices were lower, are in an especially strong spot.

If you are a first-time or value-seeking buyer, the picture is more mixed. North Las Vegas has long been the affordability and growth corridor of the valley. It is where many people make their first jump into homeownership. The good news is that even with the gains, prices there still tend to come in below the valley-wide median, which recently hit record highs in the central market. The harder news is that the bargain window is getting tighter. With supply low and homes selling near full price, you have less room to negotiate than buyers did a year or two ago. If North Las Vegas is your target, it pays to get pre-approved and be ready to act when the right home shows up.

If you are an investor or a move-up buyer, the strong appreciation rate makes North Las Vegas worth a serious look. An area that leads the valley in price growth is showing demand that is likely to continue, especially with new jobs and new construction in the corridor. Rental demand tends to stay healthy in growing areas, and price growth helps build long-term wealth.

Finally, this matters for the whole valley, because it shows where the energy in our market is moving. For years the spotlight sat on Summerlin and Henderson. Now North Las Vegas is proving it can lead. That shift affects schools, roads, shopping, and jobs across the northern part of the valley. When an area grows in value, it usually grows in other ways too.

Background and History

North Las Vegas has come a long way. For much of its history it was seen as the more industrial, working-class side of the valley. It had warehouses, rail lines, and plenty of open desert land. While Summerlin and Henderson grabbed headlines as polished master-planned communities, North Las Vegas often got overlooked. That reputation is now badly out of date.

The turning point came as the rest of the valley ran short on cheap, buildable land. Summerlin and Henderson filled in over the years, and land there grew expensive. North Las Vegas still had wide open spaces, much of it near Interstate 15 and the growing northern edge of the valley. Builders saw an opening. Communities began to rise in areas like Aliante, Tule Springs, and the Deer Springs corridor. Master-planned neighborhoods, parks, and shopping centers followed. Each new project made the area more attractive to families.

During the housing crash of the late 2000s, North Las Vegas was hit hard, with many foreclosures and falling prices. But the recovery that followed has been strong. Affordable land, new jobs, and a growing population helped the area bounce back and then push higher. Big employers and warehouse and logistics centers near the I-15 corridor brought steady work. The Las Vegas Motor Speedway and Nellis Air Force Base have long anchored the area. New retail and dining at places like Aliante and Deer Springs Town Center gave residents reasons to stay close to home.

Over the past several years, as valley-wide prices climbed to record levels, the gap between North Las Vegas and the rest of the valley narrowed. Buyers who once skipped the area now chase it, drawn by newer homes and lower entry prices. The 6.6 percent appreciation rate in this latest report is the clearest sign yet that North Las Vegas has moved from the overlooked corner to one of the most closely watched growth corridors in all of Clark County.

What Happens Next

So where does North Las Vegas go from here? A few things are worth watching over the coming months.

First, keep an eye on new construction. Builders are a big reason prices rose, and they will keep shaping the market. If they bring a large number of new homes to market at once, that extra supply could ease price growth a bit, because buyers would have more to choose from. If they slow down, low supply could keep prices firm or push them higher. Either way, the pace of building will help set the tone.

Second, watch mortgage rates. Rates affect every part of the valley, but they hit affordability-focused areas like North Las Vegas especially hard. Many buyers there are stretching to afford their first home. If rates fall, more of them can qualify, and demand could heat up further. If rates rise, some buyers may step back, which would cool things down. [NOT VERIFIED] The exact direction of rates in the second half of 2026 is not something anyone can promise.

Third, watch the supply number. Right now North Las Vegas sits at about 3.5 months of supply, which favors sellers. If that number climbs toward five or six months, the market would feel more balanced, and buyers would gain more room to negotiate. If it drops further, expect continued competition and firm prices. The 99.2 percent sale-to-list ratio is another gauge. As long as it stays near full price, sellers hold the upper hand.

Finally, watch how the rest of the valley moves. North Las Vegas does not exist alone. If central valley prices keep setting records, more priced-out buyers will look north, which supports North Las Vegas values. If the broader valley cools, the northern corridor may cool too, though its affordability could help it hold up better than pricier areas.

What You Can Do

Here are simple, concrete steps you can take based on this news.

If you own a North Las Vegas home: Ask for a current market value so you know your real equity. Compare it to what you paid and what you owe. If you have plenty of equity, think about whether a move-up, a refinance, or a home equity line fits your goals. Do not make a big move on a guess. Use real numbers.

If you want to buy there: Get pre-approved with a lender before you shop. Know your monthly budget, not just the price. Look into down payment assistance programs, since several have expanded for Nevada buyers and can help with the upfront cost. Tour a few neighborhoods so you know which streets and communities fit you best.

If you are an investor: Run the numbers on both rental income and likely appreciation. North Las Vegas leading the valley in price growth is a signal worth studying, but always check the specific property and street.

Everyone: Keep an eye on monthly market reports so you can spot trends early. The valley moves submarket by submarket, and knowing where the energy is heading helps you make better choices.
