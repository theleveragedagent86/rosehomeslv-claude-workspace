Marufuku Ramen Coming to Downtown Summerlin

Big news for ramen fans on the west side of the valley. Marufuku Ramen, a popular ramen brand that got its start in California, is coming to Downtown Summerlin. The restaurant will open its second Las Vegas location in the heart of the valley's most well known outdoor shopping district. It is moving into the space on Festival Plaza Drive that used to be home to SkinnyFats.

If you have ever waited in a long line for a steaming bowl of rich, creamy ramen, you may already know the Marufuku name. The brand is known for Hakata-style tonkotsu ramen. That is a kind of ramen built on a pork bone broth that is cooked for a long time until it turns thick and milky. It is paired with ultra-thin noodles that soak up the flavor fast. The Summerlin menu will also feature kushiyaki, which are grilled skewers of meat and vegetables cooked over a flame.

This is more than just one more restaurant opening. It is a well loved California food brand choosing one of the most visible spots in all of Summerlin. The old SkinnyFats space sits in a prime corner of the shopping center, where thousands of people walk by every week. Filling that empty storefront with a busy, buzzy ramen shop says a lot about how strong the Summerlin dining scene has become. In this post, we will break down what we know, why it matters for people who live nearby, and what to watch for as the opening gets closer.

What Happened

According to reporting from News3LV and Summerlin.com, Marufuku Ramen is opening a new location at Downtown Summerlin. This will be the brand's second spot in the Las Vegas area. The restaurant is taking over the former SkinnyFats space, which sits along Festival Plaza Drive. That street runs through the center of the shopping district, near many of the stores, restaurants, and gathering spaces that draw crowds all year long.

The main draw at Marufuku is Hakata-style tonkotsu ramen. Hakata is a district in the city of Fukuoka, Japan, and it is famous for this exact style of ramen. The broth is made from pork bones that are boiled for many hours. Over time the broth turns thick, rich, and a creamy white color. The noodles served with this style are very thin and firm. Thin noodles cook fast and are made to be eaten quickly so they do not get soft in the hot broth. Many Hakata ramen shops let you choose how firm you want your noodles, and some even let you order a second helping of noodles to add to your leftover broth.

Beyond the ramen, the Summerlin location will serve kushiyaki. These are skewers of food grilled over heat, similar in idea to other grilled skewer dishes you may have tried. Think bite sized pieces of meat, chicken, or vegetables threaded onto a stick and cooked until they are hot and a little charred. Kushiyaki gives the menu more variety, so a table can share small plates while waiting for their ramen, or order a few skewers as a side.

Background and History

Marufuku Ramen began in California, where it built a strong following for its Hakata-style tonkotsu bowls. The brand grew by focusing on doing one thing very well, which is the long simmered pork bone broth that defines this ramen style. Over time it expanded to more locations, and now it is bringing that approach to the Las Vegas market with a second valley spot at Downtown Summerlin.

To understand why this location is such a big deal, it helps to know about Downtown Summerlin itself. It is an open air shopping and dining center in the master planned community of Summerlin, on the western edge of the Las Vegas valley. It features stores, restaurants, entertainment, and gathering spaces, all built around walkable streets and plazas. Festival Plaza Drive is one of the main streets running through the district, which is why a storefront there gets so much foot traffic.

The space Marufuku is taking over belonged to SkinnyFats, a brand that built its own local following. When a tenant like that leaves a marquee shopping center, the empty space becomes a closely watched opportunity. Landlords in top tier centers can be choosy about who they bring in, because they want tenants that draw crowds and fit the feel of the district. A ramen brand with a built in fan base checks those boxes.

Ramen has also gone through a long rise in popularity across the United States. What was once seen mostly as a quick, cheap meal has grown into a craft food category, with shops competing on broth recipes, noodle texture, and toppings. Hakata-style tonkotsu, with its rich broth and thin noodles, is one of the most popular styles in that movement. The arrival of a dedicated Hakata ramen shop in Summerlin fits right into that national trend, brought down to the neighborhood level.
