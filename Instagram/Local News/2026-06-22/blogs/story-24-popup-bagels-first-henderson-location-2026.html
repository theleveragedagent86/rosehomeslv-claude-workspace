PopUp Bagels Plans First Henderson-Area Location

PopUp Bagels, the viral bagel brand that built a national following by serving bagels whole, uncut, and untoasted, is planning its first store in the Las Vegas Valley. June 2026 reporting points to a location in the Henderson area, near East Cactus Avenue, in the east part of the valley. If the plans move forward, this would be the brand's very first shop in our market.

This is a first-to-market food story, and it has people talking. PopUp Bagels is not just another bagel shop. It is a social media phenomenon. The company calls itself "Not Famous but Known," and its bagels have racked up millions of views on TikTok and Instagram thanks to a simple idea: rip the warm bagel open with your hands and dip it into a tub of whipped spread. No knife. No toaster. Just you, a hot bagel, and a schmear.

For Henderson residents and the wider east valley, this kind of buzzy national brand picking our area for its debut is a notable sign. It tells us that brands with big followings are now treating Henderson as a place worth opening first, not last. That matters for the neighborhood, for nearby home values, and for anyone who likes seeing fresh options arrive close to home.

What Happened

According to June 2026 reporting from What Now Las Vegas and Review-Journal Neon, PopUp Bagels is working on its first location in the Las Vegas Valley. The reporting places that location in the Henderson area, near East Cactus Avenue, on the east side of the valley. This would be the brand's debut in our market, meaning the first time local fans can buy these bagels without leaving town.

Here is what makes PopUp Bagels stand out. The brand started as a pandemic hobby in Westport, Connecticut. Founder Adam Goldberg began baking bagels at home during lockdown and giving them to friends and family. Word spread fast. People loved the warm, fresh bagels and the simple "rip and dip" style. The hobby turned into a business, and the business turned into a national chain with shops in cities like New York, Los Angeles, and Atlanta.

The menu is on purpose very short. PopUp Bagels typically offers only a handful of bagel types, such as plain, everything, salt, poppy seed, and sesame. You buy them in bags, often in counts of three, six, or a dozen. Instead of slicing and toasting, the shop hands you a hot, whole bagel and a tub of whipped spread, called a schmear. You tear the bagel apart and dunk it. The spreads include classics like cream cheese and salted butter, plus rotating weekly flavors like cinnamon brown sugar butter or pimento cream cheese.

That dipping move is the whole reason the brand went viral. The "grip, rip, and dip" videos are everywhere on TikTok and Instagram. Food influencers helped push the shop into the spotlight by filming the warm, pull-apart bagels and the creative spreads. The visual is simple and fun, and it travels well online. That is how a small Connecticut bagel maker became a name people recognize across the country.

Background and History

PopUp Bagels is part of a wave of food brands that grew up on social media rather than through traditional advertising. The story starts in Westport, Connecticut, during the COVID-19 pandemic. With time on his hands during lockdown, founder Adam Goldberg started baking bagels. He gave them away at first. People asked for more. That demand turned a hobby into a weekend pop-up, and the pop-up gave the brand its name.

From there, the growth was fast. The brand leaned into its short menu and its hands-on, dip-style serving. Instead of fighting for attention with dozens of sandwich combos, it kept things simple and let the warm, fresh product do the talking. Customers filmed themselves tearing bagels apart and dunking them into thick spreads, and those clips spread across TikTok and Instagram. The simple, repeatable visual is a big reason the brand caught on so quickly.

By 2026, PopUp Bagels had grown well beyond Connecticut, with shops opening in major markets across the country. Reporting in recent months has noted new locations and deals in cities far from its East Coast roots, which shows the brand is in a strong expansion phase. A first store in the Las Vegas Valley fits right into that pattern of pushing into new metro areas with strong food scenes.

The Henderson area near East Cactus Avenue sits in the east valley, an area that has steadily added homes, shopping, and dining over the years. Henderson grew from a wartime industrial town into a sprawling suburban city known for master-planned communities. As rooftops went up, retail and restaurants followed. A national brand choosing this corridor for a debut is the latest chapter in that long pattern of growth feeding new business.
