World Series of Poker Opens First Permanent Strip Retail Store

The World Series of Poker, better known as the WSOP, now has its first permanent store on the Las Vegas Strip. The shop held a ribbon-cutting on June 12, 2026, and opened its doors to the public on June 15, 2026. It sits in the walkway that connects Horseshoe Las Vegas and Paris Las Vegas, right in the heart of the Strip. This is a big deal for a brand that has called Las Vegas home since the 1970s but never had a store of its own here until now.

The man who cut the ribbon was Jack Binion. He is the son of Benny Binion, the man who started the World Series of Poker. Having a Binion open the store ties the new shop back to the very beginning of the event. The store is 2,320 square feet, which makes it the largest retail space the WSOP has ever had. Inside, you will find more than 100 different merchandise designs. The whole layout is built like a timeline, so as you walk through, you move through the history of poker step by step.

The timing is no accident. The store opened during WSOP season, when poker players from all over the world come to Las Vegas to compete. That means thousands of extra visitors are in town, and many of them want a shirt, a hat, or a keepsake to remember the trip. In this post, I will break down what the store carries, exactly where it is, why a permanent shop matters to the city, and what it could mean for the people who live and own homes nearby.

What Happened

On Thursday, June 12, 2026, the World Series of Poker opened its first permanent retail store on the Las Vegas Strip with a ribbon-cutting event. The public got their turn three days later, on June 15. The store is open to anyone walking the Strip, not just people playing in the tournament. You do not need a poker badge or a casino account to step inside and shop.

The store measures 2,320 square feet. That may not sound huge, but for the WSOP it is the biggest store space the brand has ever had. Before this, WSOP merchandise was mostly sold from temporary pop-up booths set up during the summer tournament and then taken down when the event ended. Fans could also buy items online. Now there is a real, year-round storefront with a fixed address.

Inside, the design is meant to feel like a walk through poker history. The layout follows a timeline, so shoppers move from the early days of the game toward the modern era as they travel through the space. Several reports describe themed touches throughout, including design elements that nod to the desert roots of Nevada gaming and to famous moments from past tournaments. [NOT VERIFIED] Some coverage mentions a chandelier made to look like cascading poker chips and a checkout counter inspired by the old Golden Horseshoe look, though specific design details can vary by source.

The merchandise count is the headline number for shoppers. More than 100 designs are available, spread across clothing, hats, accessories, and collectible items. Coverage of the opening pointed to a partnership with the hat maker New Era that brings dozens of cap styles to the shelves. Reports also mention other brand tie-ins and special collections rolling out over the summer. The idea is to give visitors a reason to come back more than once, since new items keep arriving.

Location matters a lot here. The store is in the corridor between Horseshoe Las Vegas and Paris Las Vegas. This is the same resort area where the WSOP tournament is actually played each summer, so players walking from their rooms to the poker tables pass right by the shop. It also puts the store in one of the busiest foot-traffic spots on the entire Strip, with millions of visitors passing through that stretch every year. For a brand trying to sell shirts and hats, that location is about as good as it gets.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might wonder why a poker merchandise shop matters if you do not play cards. The answer is that the Strip is the engine of the local economy, and what happens there ripples out into neighborhoods all over the valley. When a famous brand decides to plant a permanent store on the Strip, it is a vote of confidence in Las Vegas. Brands do not sign long-term leases in expensive spots unless they expect strong, steady business. That confidence supports the jobs, tax money, and tourism that pay for a lot of life in Clark County.

A new permanent store also means jobs. Someone has to run the register, stock the shelves, manage the floor, and handle the back office. A year-round store, unlike a summer pop-up, means year-round paychecks. Those are the kinds of steady retail jobs that help local families pay rent and mortgages. For people who work in or near the tourism industry, more permanent businesses on the Strip is good news for job security.

There is also the visitor angle. The WSOP draws tens of thousands of poker players and fans to town every summer. A flagship store gives them one more reason to spend money here and one more memory to take home. Happy visitors come back, and they tell friends. The more reasons people have to visit and return, the healthier the local economy stays. That matters to homeowners because a strong tourism economy helps keep home values steady and supports demand for housing across the valley.

For residents who live close to the Strip, in neighborhoods like the area east of the resorts or the communities near Paradise and Winchester, business growth on the corridor can be a double-edged sword. On one hand, more activity can mean more traffic and more crowds. On the other hand, a busier, more successful Strip can lift property values nearby and bring more services to the area. If you own a home or are thinking about buying near the resort corridor, it is worth watching how the Strip keeps evolving from a place you only visit into a place with year-round shopping and dining.

Finally, this opening is part of a bigger story. The Strip is changing from a gambling-only destination into a full entertainment, shopping, and sports hub. Permanent stores, new restaurants, and major projects like a ballpark all point in the same direction. That long-term direction shapes what kind of city Las Vegas becomes, and that affects everyone who lives here.

Background and History

The World Series of Poker started in 1970, created by Benny Binion at his downtown Las Vegas casino, then known as Binion's Horseshoe. In the early years, it was a small gathering of top players invited to compete and decide who the best was. Over the decades it grew into the most famous poker event in the world, with thousands of players from many countries chasing gold bracelets and big cash prizes each summer.

The event eventually outgrew its downtown home. The main series moved to the Rio resort off the Strip for many years, and more recently the tournament action shifted to the Horseshoe Las Vegas and Paris Las Vegas resorts on the Strip itself. The Horseshoe name, by the way, is a direct callback to Benny Binion's original casino, which keeps the history alive even as the event moves to new buildings.

For most of its history, WSOP gear was sold from temporary booths during the summer event. If you wanted a WSOP shirt, you usually had to buy it during the tournament or order it online later. There was no permanent place to walk in any time of year and shop. That is exactly what makes this new store such a notable first. It is the brand's first permanent retail flagship, and it is tied directly to the city most connected to poker in the public mind.

Having Jack Binion cut the ribbon connects the new store to the family that started it all. It is a reminder that the WSOP is not a brand invented yesterday by a marketing team. It grew out of real Las Vegas history, from a downtown casino owner who wanted to find the best poker player alive. The store leans into that history with its timeline layout, inviting shoppers to see where the game came from before they buy something that ties them to it.

What Happens Next

The most immediate thing to watch is the rest of the WSOP summer season. The store opened right as the tournament was in full swing, which means it will get its biggest test of foot traffic in its very first weeks. If sales are strong during this peak period, expect the brand to lean even harder into permanent retail. Reports already mention new merchandise collections set to roll out through the summer, so the shelves should keep changing for return shoppers.

Longer term, success here could lead to more WSOP stores in other cities or other parts of Las Vegas. Brands often test a flagship store first, learn what sells, and then expand if the numbers look good. A 2,320-square-foot flagship in the busiest tourist corridor in the country is a strong test case. If it works, the WSOP has a clear template to copy.

For the Strip itself, this fits a clear pattern of more permanent, non-gaming attractions. Over the past few years, the resort corridor has added big restaurants, branded stores, and major sports projects. The trend points toward a Strip that gives visitors plenty to do and buy even when they are not gambling. That broadens who comes to town and helps smooth out the ups and downs that come with a gambling-only economy.

I would also keep an eye on how the store handles the slower seasons. Summer is easy because the tournament fills the area with poker fans. The real question is whether a WSOP store can stay busy in, say, the fall or early spring, when poker crowds are smaller. The answer will tell us a lot about how strong the brand really is as a year-round draw.
