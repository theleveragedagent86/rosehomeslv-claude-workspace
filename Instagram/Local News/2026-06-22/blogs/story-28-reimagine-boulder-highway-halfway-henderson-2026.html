Reimagine Boulder Highway Project Passes Halfway Point in Henderson

The big road project on Boulder Highway in Henderson is now more than halfway done. The Regional Transportation Commission of Southern Nevada, also called the RTC, says its $184 million Reimagine Boulder Highway project crossed the halfway mark as 2026 began. Work started in the summer of 2024, and the whole job is expected to be finished in 2027. If you drive, walk, or ride the bus along this road, this is one of the biggest changes coming to your part of the valley.

So what is actually changing? The project takes Boulder Highway from six lanes down to four lanes. It adds wider sidewalks so people can walk more safely. It builds raised bike lanes on both sides of the road. And it creates a brand new bus rapid transit system that runs right down the center of the road. Henderson also recently turned on 400 new streetlights along the corridor, which should make the road brighter and safer at night. The project covers a 7.5-mile stretch of Boulder Highway inside Henderson.

The short version is this: a road that was built mostly for fast cars is being rebuilt to work for everyone, including walkers, bikers, and bus riders. Below, I break down what has happened so far, why it matters for your home and your neighborhood, the history behind the project, what comes next, and what you can do right now to plan ahead.

What Happened

The RTC announced that the Reimagine Boulder Highway project passed its halfway point as the new year started in 2026. That means crews have finished about half of the planned construction along the 7.5-mile stretch inside Henderson. The full project is expected to wrap up in 2027.

The work is large and detailed. Crews are not just repaving the road. They are rebuilding how the whole corridor works. The number of through lanes is dropping from six to four. That may sound like less room for cars, but the goal is to slow traffic down and make the road safer. Boulder Highway has long been known as one of the most dangerous roads in the area, especially for people on foot. Fewer, narrower lanes tend to calm traffic and cut down on the worst crashes.

On both sides of the road, crews are building wider sidewalks and raised bike lanes. A raised bike lane sits a little higher than the car lanes and is separated from traffic, which gives bike riders more protection than a simple painted line. The center of the road is being turned into a path for bus rapid transit, often shortened to BRT. Bus rapid transit works almost like a train on wheels. Buses get their own lanes and stations, so they do not get stuck in regular car traffic and can move people quickly along the route.

Building the BRT means crews are also constructing stations in the median, the middle strip of the road, for future bus stops. This setup is sometimes called a center-run system because the buses run down the center instead of pulling over to the curb. Along with all of this, the project includes new storm drains to handle rain, upgraded traffic signals, and fresh asphalt.

One of the most visible recent changes is lighting. Henderson activated 400 new streetlights along the corridor. Poor lighting was a known problem on Boulder Highway and was tied to nighttime crashes and pedestrian deaths. More light makes it easier for drivers to see people crossing and easier for people walking to feel safe after dark.

It is worth being clear about one thing. The 7.5-mile segment that runs north of Henderson, into other parts of the valley, does not have a confirmed start date yet [NOT VERIFIED for an exact date]. So the work happening now is focused on the Henderson portion of the corridor. The rest of Boulder Highway may be improved later, but that timing is not set.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

If you live in Henderson or anywhere near Boulder Highway, this project touches your daily life in real ways. The most obvious one is your drive. During construction, lane closures and detours are normal. Trips that used to be quick can take longer, and the patterns change as crews move from one section to another. Knowing the project is halfway done helps you understand that the disruption is real but temporary, with a finish line in 2027.

For homeowners, road projects like this one can affect property values over time. Roads that feel unsafe or run down can hold a neighborhood back. A road that is brighter, calmer, and easier to walk along can do the opposite. When a major corridor gets nicer sidewalks, protected bike lanes, and reliable transit, the homes and businesses near it often become more attractive to buyers and renters. That is not a guarantee, but it is a pattern seen in many cities that have done similar upgrades.

Safety is a huge part of why this matters. Boulder Highway has been one of the deadliest roads in Nevada, with poor lighting and high speeds adding to the danger. People walking and biking have been hurt and killed along this corridor for years. The new design aims to fix that by slowing traffic, adding light, and giving walkers and bikers their own protected space. If you have kids, older parents, or neighbors who walk or take the bus, these changes are meant to protect them.

Transit access matters too. A center-run bus rapid transit system can give people a faster, more reliable way to get to work, school, and stores without needing a car for every trip. In a region where many families stretch their budgets, having a strong transit option along a major road can lower the cost of getting around. It can also open up parts of the valley to people who do not drive.

Local businesses along Boulder Highway feel both sides of this. During construction, some customers avoid the area because of delays, and that can hurt sales in the short term. But once the work is done, more foot traffic, better parking access, and a more pleasant street can bring customers back. The hope is that a rebuilt corridor supports stronger small businesses for the long haul.

Background and History

Boulder Highway is one of the oldest and most important roads in the Las Vegas valley. For decades it has served as a major route connecting Henderson to other parts of the region. It was built in an era when the main goal was moving cars quickly. Over time, that design led to high speeds, wide crossings, and a road that was hard and dangerous to cross on foot.

As the area grew, the problems grew with it. Apartments, motels, stores, and bus stops lined the road, which meant a lot of people were walking and waiting for buses right next to fast-moving traffic. The combination of speed, width, and weak lighting made Boulder Highway one of the deadliest roads in Nevada. Year after year, the corridor showed up in reports about traffic deaths and serious crashes, especially for people on foot.

The Reimagine Boulder Highway project was created to answer those problems. The RTC, which plans and funds transportation across Southern Nevada, partnered with the City of Henderson to rethink the whole corridor. Instead of just patching the road, the plan was to change its purpose. The idea was to shift Boulder Highway from a high-speed, car-only route into a calmer, safer street that works for drivers, walkers, bikers, and transit riders alike.

Construction began in the summer of 2024 with a budget of $184 million. The project area runs about 7.5 miles inside Henderson. The work includes the lane reduction, the wider sidewalks, the raised bike lanes, the center-run bus rapid transit, the new streetlights, upgraded signals, and new storm drains. Each of these pieces fits into the larger goal of safety and access. Reaching the halfway point in early 2026 was a key milestone that showed the plan was moving forward on a real timeline.

What Happens Next

With the project past the halfway point, the focus now turns to finishing the second half of the work by the expected 2027 completion date. That means more sections of new pavement, more sidewalk and bike lane construction, and the buildout of the bus rapid transit stations in the median. As crews move along the corridor, drivers should expect lane shifts and closures to continue in different spots until the work is done.

One thing to watch is when the bus rapid transit service actually starts running. Building the stations and the center lanes is one step. Launching the bus service that uses them is another. The exact start date for full BRT service has not been confirmed in the information available [NOT VERIFIED]. Keep an eye on RTC announcements for that timeline.

Another open question is the 7.5-mile segment north of Henderson. That stretch does not have a confirmed start date yet. It is possible that once the Henderson portion is finished and proven, attention could shift to extending similar improvements farther along Boulder Highway. But there is no set schedule for that part right now, so it would be a mistake to assume it is coming soon.

In the near term, residents can expect the corridor to keep looking like an active construction zone, with progress visible as new sidewalks, lighting, and lanes come online section by section. The 400 streetlights already turned on are a sign of how the finished road will feel: brighter and easier to navigate at night. As more pieces are completed, the daily experience of using Boulder Highway should steadily improve, even before the official finish in 2027.

Ryan's Take

It is busy, it is wide, and it has a real safety record problem. So a $184 million effort to slow it down, light it up, and make room for people who are not in cars is a big deal for Henderson.

From a real estate point of view, corridors like this often turn a corner once the construction dust settles. Homes and small businesses near a rebuilt, safer, transit-friendly street can become more appealing over time. I would not promise anyone a quick jump in value, but the long-term direction looks healthy for the area. A road that families feel safe walking and biking along is a road that helps a neighborhood.

My honest advice is to be patient through the rest of construction. The short-term headache of detours and slower drives is real, but it is buying a much better corridor. If you are thinking about buying or selling near Boulder Highway, factor this project into your timeline. The version of this road that exists in 2027 will be very different from the one we have today, and in my view, different for the better.

What You Can Do

Here are some simple, practical steps you can take while the Reimagine Boulder Highway project finishes up.

  • Plan extra time for trips along Boulder Highway, since lane closures and detours will continue until the work wraps up in 2027.
  • Check the RTC website for the latest construction updates and any news about when bus rapid transit service will start.
  • If you walk or bike, use the newly lit sections and finished sidewalks where they are open, and stay alert in active work zones.
  • Support local businesses along the corridor during construction, since they often feel the pinch most while the road is torn up.
  • If you own a home near the corridor, keep notes on how the improvements progress, since a safer, brighter street can matter when it is time to sell.
  • Share accurate information with neighbors so everyone knows the project is halfway done and on track, not stalled.
