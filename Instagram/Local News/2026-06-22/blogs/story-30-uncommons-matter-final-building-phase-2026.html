Matter Real Estate Completes Final Mixed-Use Building Phase at UnCommons

One of the biggest projects in southwest Las Vegas is reaching the finish line. Matter Real Estate Group has announced the final office building at UnCommons, its $850 million, 40-acre campus near the I-215 and Durango Drive. This last building marks the end of a years-long plan to turn raw desert land into one of the valley's flagship live-work-play neighborhoods. If you live in the southwest, drive past Durango, or just wonder what keeps rising near the Durango hotel-casino, this is a big moment for your part of town.

The new building is small in footprint but full of activity. It sits on a 1.4-acre site and stands three stories tall. It will hold 39,262 square feet of office space and 19,804 square feet of retail space. Part of that retail will be a fitness concept of more than 9,900 square feet. The building is expected to be finished and ready for tenants in the first quarter of 2028. Once it opens, UnCommons will have completed the last piece of its master plan.

UnCommons already has more than 350,000 square feet of office space and 385 luxury homes for rent. It has restaurants, coffee shops, and gathering spaces that draw people from across the valley. This final building does not just add a few more offices. It signals that one of the most watched developments in Las Vegas is now built out. Below, we break down exactly what is happening, why it matters to local residents, and how it could shape home values and daily life in the southwest for years to come.

What Happened

Between May 31 and June 2, 2026, Matter Real Estate Group announced the final office building planned for UnCommons. The company shared the news through local outlets, and it quickly spread because UnCommons has become a name people in Las Vegas know well. This is the building that completes the campus.

Here are the key facts. The building will rise three stories on a 1.4-acre site. It will offer 39,262 square feet of office space and 19,804 square feet of retail space. The ground floor is the most public part. It will feature a fitness concept of more than 9,900 square feet, along with retail spaces aimed at athleisure and lifestyle tenants. That means stores tied to clothing, wellness, and active living. Levels two and three will hold office suites. The target completion date is the first quarter of 2028, so construction and leasing will play out over the next two years.

To understand the size of the whole project, look at the totals. UnCommons is an $850 million development spread across 40 acres. It already has more than 350,000 square feet of office space and 385 luxury residential units. It also has a growing list of dining and lifestyle spots. The campus sits along Interstate 215 at Durango Drive, directly across from Durango Casino and Resort. It is positioned about midway between Summerlin and Henderson, which puts it within reach of two of the valley's most active suburbs.

UnCommons has already attracted major employers. Tenants include the commercial real estate brokerage CBRE Group, the online sports-betting firm DraftKings, and the accounting and consulting giants Deloitte and EY. These are national names that chose to plant offices in southwest Las Vegas. Their presence is part of why the campus is described as a Class A destination, which is a term for top-tier, high-quality commercial space.

The final building was designed to keep that energy going. By mixing a gym, shops, and offices in one structure, the developer is following the same recipe that made the rest of UnCommons work. People can park once and then work, exercise, shop, and eat without getting back in the car. That is the heart of what planners call a mixed-use, walkable design. With this last piece, Matter Real Estate is saying the vision is essentially complete.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might wonder why one office building matters to your household. The answer is that big employment centers shape the neighborhoods around them. When national companies bring high-paying jobs to an area, demand for nearby housing usually goes up. More demand often means stronger home values and steady rent. For homeowners in the southwest, a finished UnCommons can be a quiet boost to the value of where you live.

The southwest is already one of the fastest-growing parts of the valley. New rooftops, schools, and shopping centers keep appearing. UnCommons sits at the center of that growth. Having a built-out campus with offices, homes, gyms, and restaurants gives the area an anchor. Anchors matter because they keep money and people circulating close to home. Instead of driving to the Strip or to Summerlin for work and play, more residents can stay near their own neighborhood.

There is also a daily-life benefit. The new building adds a fitness concept of more than 9,900 square feet and several retail spaces. That gives people who live in or near UnCommons more places to go without a long drive. A walkable mix of services can cut down on traffic for short trips and make a neighborhood feel more complete. For families weighing where to buy, that kind of convenience is a real selling point.

Jobs are the other piece. Offices for firms like CBRE, DraftKings, Deloitte, and EY mean professional roles that pay well. Workers who earn good wages can afford to buy or rent nearby. They also spend money at local shops and restaurants, which helps small businesses. When a campus reaches its final phase, it tends to lock in that base of workers and shoppers for the long term, which gives the surrounding housing market a steadier footing.

It is fair to keep expectations realistic. One building will not change home prices overnight, and broader market forces like interest rates still matter most. But completed, high-quality projects send a signal that an area is maturing. For the southwest, UnCommons reaching its final phase is a sign that this corner of the valley is now a true destination, not just a stretch of land waiting to be developed.

Background and History

UnCommons did not appear overnight. Matter Real Estate Group built it in phases over several years, starting with raw land near the I-215 and Durango. The early phases focused on offices and the first residential and dining pieces. Later phases added more office buildings, a conference center, and parking. Each step was designed to fit the live-work-play idea, where homes, jobs, and fun share the same campus.

The location was a key part of the plan. By building along Interstate 215 at Durango Drive, the developer placed UnCommons at a midpoint between Summerlin and Henderson. That spot is easy to reach for workers and residents from many parts of the valley. When Durango Casino and Resort opened across the street, it added even more energy and foot traffic to the immediate area. The casino and the campus now feed off each other.

UnCommons earned attention for its design. It aimed to redefine what Class A commercial real estate looks and feels like in Las Vegas. Instead of plain office towers surrounded by parking lots, it leaned into open spaces, shops, and gathering spots. That approach helped it land major tenants and win praise as one of the valley's standout mixed-use projects. The southwest itself has been one of the valley's growth stories. Over the past decade, the area filled in with new homes, retail, and roads as families looked for newer housing away from the older core of the city. UnCommons rode that wave and helped accelerate it. The final building announced in mid-2026 is the last chapter of a plan that began years earlier, and it shows how a single project can grow alongside the neighborhood around it.

What Happens Next

The timeline is the thing to watch. The final building is expected to be ready for tenants in the first quarter of 2028. That means roughly two years of construction, leasing, and buildout. Over that stretch, you can expect to see crews on the 1.4-acre site, then a slow reveal as the three-story structure takes shape near the rest of the campus.

Leasing is the next big question. The ground floor is set for a fitness concept of more than 9,900 square feet and retail spaces for athleisure and lifestyle tenants. Levels two and three will hold office suites. As the opening date nears, watch for announcements about which gym brand and which shops sign on. Those names will tell you a lot about the feel of the finished campus and the kind of crowd it hopes to draw.
