A's $2 Billion Las Vegas Strip Ballpark Hits Key Construction Milestones

The Athletics' new $2 billion ballpark on the Las Vegas Strip has passed some big building steps, and new drone video from July 10, 2026, shows just how far the work has come. Crews have put up steel roof arches and started work on a giant 400-foot-wide glass window that will face Las Vegas Boulevard. This matters because a major league baseball park on the old Tropicana site will change traffic, jobs, and home demand across the South Strip long before the first pitch.

For years, people wondered if this stadium was really going to happen. Now the answer is easy to see from the air. The project leaders say the roughly 33,000-seat enclosed stadium is still on time and on budget for a February 2028 opening. About $400 million has been spent so far. That is a real, physical sign that Las Vegas is about to add its first Major League Baseball team's home field.

If you live near the Strip, own a rental close by, or are thinking about buying in the coming years, this is a story worth watching closely. A ballpark is not just a place to watch games. It is a huge anchor that pulls in visitors, changes street traffic, and shapes what nearby homes are worth. Below, we break down what the drone footage showed, why it matters to everyday Las Vegas residents, how we got here, what happens next, and what you can do now to get ready.

What Happened

On July 10, 2026, FOX5 Vegas released new drone camera footage that flew over the Athletics' ballpark site on the Las Vegas Strip. The video showed major progress at the construction area, and it gave the public its clearest look yet at how the building is coming together. The site sits on the former Tropicana location, right along Las Vegas Boulevard.

The most eye-catching part of the footage was the steel roof arches. Workers have installed these large curved steel pieces that will hold up the roof of the enclosed stadium. Because the ballpark is designed to be fully covered, the roof is a very big deal. It has to keep out the desert heat and let games happen no matter the weather. Seeing those arches in place tells everyone the building is now taking its real shape and is not just a hole in the ground.

The drone also showed work on a 400-foot-wide outfield glass window. This window will face Las Vegas Boulevard, which is the heart of the Strip. The idea is that fans inside will be able to look out at the famous Strip skyline while they watch the game. At 400 feet across, this is a huge wall of glass. It is one of the features that could make this park stand out from other stadiums around the country.

Project leaders shared some key numbers along with the video. They said the roughly 33,000-seat stadium remains on time and on budget. The target opening is set for February 2028. So far, about $400 million has been spent on the project, which carries a total price tag of around $2 billion. Staying on schedule and on budget is not always easy for big builds, so this update was meant to show that the plan is holding steady.

It is worth noting how much a roof and a big glass wall change a build like this. A closed roof means heavy steel work high off the ground, and that takes cranes, careful planning, and skilled crews. A 400-foot glass window means the wall facing the Strip has to be strong enough to hold all that glass and still stay cool inside. Seeing both moving forward at the same time tells you the different building teams are working in step, which is a good sign for the schedule.

Put together, the July 10 footage was a progress report the whole city could see. The steel arches, the giant glass window, and the on-time message all point in the same direction. The ballpark is moving from paper plans to a real building. For a project this size and this public, that kind of visible progress carries a lot of weight with fans, workers, and neighbors alike.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

A $2 billion ballpark on the Strip is not only a sports story. It touches many parts of daily life for people who live and work in the valley. The most direct effect is jobs. Building a stadium this large takes thousands of construction workers, and running it after 2028 will need many more people for game days, food service, cleaning, security, and parking. That is a steady stream of work close to home.

Traffic is the next big thing residents will feel. The Strip is already one of the busiest areas in Southern Nevada. Adding a 33,000-seat stadium means big crowds coming and going on game days and event nights. Roads near the old Tropicana site, plus nearby freeway ramps, will likely see more cars and rideshare pickups. People who live in that area will want to plan for busier streets during the season once the park opens.

Then there is the effect on homes. Big anchors like stadiums often raise interest in nearby neighborhoods. Some people want to live within walking or short driving distance of a place where games, concerts, and events happen. That can push up demand for both homes to buy and homes to rent. Short-term rental interest can also climb, since visitors sometimes prefer a house or condo over a hotel room when they come for a series of games or a special event.

There is also the pride and identity piece. Las Vegas has grown into a real sports town over the last several years, with pro teams now calling the valley home. A Major League Baseball park adds another reason for locals to feel like their city is on the national stage. That kind of energy can bring more visitors year round, not just for baseball, which helps local shops, restaurants, and service businesses near the Strip.

Of course, growth brings trade-offs. More people and more traffic can mean more noise and busier roads for those living closest to the site. Home prices going up is good news for owners but can be harder for renters and first-time buyers. That is why it helps to understand the project early, so you can make smart choices about where to live, buy, or invest before the crowds arrive in 2028.
