Waikiki's Stix Asia Food Hall Begins Construction at UnCommons

Big news for anyone who loves Asian food in the southwest Las Vegas valley. A well-known food hall from Honolulu is on its way here. It is called Stix Asia, and crews have started building it at UnCommons. That is the popular work-and-play spot near the 215 Beltway and Durango Drive. The new food hall is planned to open in the fall of 2026.

Stix Asia is not a small project. It will take up about 18,000 square feet. Inside, you will find roughly a dozen different food stalls, and each one will be run on its own. Think Japanese ramen, Chinese dumplings, Korean rice bowls, Vietnamese noodles, and more, all under one roof. The space is built to seat around 400 people, both inside and outside. There is even a plan for a DJ booth to bring some energy at night.

For a part of town that is growing fast, this is a fun and useful addition. Families get more places to eat close to home. Workers at UnCommons get more lunch choices. And people who like to try new food have a reason to visit. In this article, we will walk through what is coming, why it matters for local homeowners, and what you can do to make the most of it.

What Happened

According to Las Vegas Weekly, Stix Asia has begun construction at UnCommons in the southwest valley. The report ran on July 9, 2026. The food hall comes from Honolulu, where the brand is known for gathering many small Asian eateries in one place. Now the same idea is coming to Las Vegas.

Here are the main facts to know. The food hall will cover about 18,000 square feet. It will hold roughly a dozen food concepts, and each stall will be run by its own operator. That means the ramen shop, the dumpling shop, and the rice bowl shop can each focus on doing one thing really well. The menu across the hall is set to include Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Vietnamese, and other Asian styles.

The seating is built for a crowd. Plans call for about 400 seats, spread across indoor and outdoor areas. That is a lot of room. It means groups, families, and coworkers can all find a spot, even during a busy lunch or a weekend dinner rush. Outdoor seating is a smart choice for the cooler months in Las Vegas, when people love to eat outside.

One of the most interesting parts is a 100-year-old Japanese restaurant that traces back to the Ginza district in Tokyo. Ginza is one of the most famous shopping and dining areas in all of Japan. Bringing a restaurant with that kind of long history to Las Vegas is a big deal. It gives the food hall a story and a level of trust that new spots do not always have.

Then there is the DJ booth. Many food halls are quiet and close early. Stix Asia seems to want more than that. The DJ booth points to nighttime energy, where the space turns into a lively hangout after the dinner hour. That mix of good food and a fun mood can help a food hall stand out in a city full of dining options.

It also helps to picture how a food hall like this actually works when you walk in. You do not sit and wait for one waiter to take a single order. Instead, you and your group can split up, each visit a different stall, and then meet back at a shared table. One person grabs ramen, another picks up dumplings, and a third orders a rice bowl. You pay at each stall and bring the food back together. This setup is fast, and it lets everyone eat what they want without a long menu debate.

That style is a big reason food halls have taken off in cities across the country. It is friendly for small food businesses, since a stall costs far less to run than a full restaurant. It is also friendly for diners, who get to try many kinds of food in one stop. For a place like Las Vegas, where people love variety, the fit makes sense.

The target opening is fall 2026. Since crews just started building, that timeline could shift, as building timelines often do. Still, fall is the goal for now. If you want to plan ahead, keep an eye on official updates as the season gets closer.

Background and History

To understand why this matters, it helps to know a little about both Stix Asia and UnCommons.

Stix Asia started in Honolulu, Hawaii. There, it built a name as a place where many small Asian eateries share one large hall. Instead of picking just one type of food, guests can wander and sample from several. That format has grown popular across the country because it lowers the risk for small food businesses and gives diners lots of variety. Bringing that idea to Las Vegas fits a city that already loves food from around the world.

UnCommons is a newer mixed-use spot in the southwest valley, near the 215 Beltway and Durango Drive. It blends offices, homes, shops, and restaurants in one walkable area. The goal is a place where people can work, live, and relax without hopping in the car for every little thing. Since it opened, UnCommons has drawn a steady stream of restaurants, coffee shops, and gathering spots. It has become one of the go-to hangouts for the southwest side.

The southwest valley itself has been one of the fastest-growing parts of the Las Vegas area for years. New homes, apartments, and businesses keep filling in the land near the 215. As more people move in, they need more places to shop, eat, and spend free time. A big Asian food hall is a natural next step for a growing community like this one.

Food halls also match how many people like to eat today. Diners want choice, they want a fun setting, and they want to share a table with friends who each order something different. Stix Asia is built around exactly that. By landing at UnCommons, it joins a spot that already knows how to draw a crowd. Right now, the project is in the building stage. Crews have started work on the roughly 18,000-square-foot space at UnCommons. The plan is to open in the fall of 2026. Because construction just began, that date is a target and not a promise. Building projects can run into delays from permits, materials, or last-minute changes. So it is smart to treat fall as the hoped-for window and stay flexible.

As the opening gets closer, expect more details to come out. The team will likely announce the full list of food stalls, their menus, and their hours. Watch for news about the 100-year-old Ginza restaurant, since that is one of the standout parts of the plan. There should also be updates on how the DJ booth and nighttime hours will work.

Before a big food hall opens, there is often a soft launch or a preview period. That is when a few stalls open early or the hall runs shorter hours to work out any issues. If Stix Asia does this, it could be a great time to visit with smaller crowds. Keep an eye on local food news and the UnCommons social pages for those first announcements.

For nearby residents, the months ahead may bring some construction noise and traffic near the site. That is normal for a project this size. The upside is that the finished hall should add a lively new spot to the neighborhood. If you drive through UnCommons often, you may start to see the build take shape over the coming weeks and months.

It is also fair to expect some buzz once the food hall gets closer to opening. Food writers and local news teams tend to cover new dining spots, and a brand from Honolulu with a 100-year-old Ginza restaurant is the kind of story people share. That attention can bring first-week crowds. If you like a calmer visit, you may want to wait a few weeks after opening, once the early rush settles down.

The bottom line is that the wheels are in motion. A large, multi-stall Asian food hall is on the way to the southwest valley, with a fall 2026 goal. The next big step will be the reveal of the stall lineup, so that is the thing to watch for.
