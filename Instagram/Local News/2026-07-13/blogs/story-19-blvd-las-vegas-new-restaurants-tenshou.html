BLVD Las Vegas Adds TENSHOU Collection, Pepper Lunch and More

BLVD Las Vegas, the big new shopping and dining spot on the Las Vegas Strip, just announced four new food tenants: the TENSHOU Collection, Pepper Lunch, Mothership Coffee Roasters, and GoGreek. This matters because it shows one of the most-watched retail projects on the Strip is still filling up with names that draw both tourists and locals, and that means more jobs and more foot traffic in the heart of the city.

The announcement came out on July 7, 2026, when FOX5 Vegas reported that the mixed-use property had signed the new group of restaurants. Each one brings something different. TENSHOU is a multi-concept Japanese experience. Pepper Lunch is a teppanyaki chain where the food cooks right at your table. Mothership Coffee Roasters is a woman-owned local coffee company. And GoGreek is a Greek yogurt bar. Together they add a mix of quick bites and sit-down meals to a property that is still growing after its opening.

For anyone who lives in the Las Vegas valley, news like this is worth watching. The Strip is not just a place tourists go. It is one of the biggest job centers in Southern Nevada, and the money it pulls in helps shape everything from local wages to home prices in nearby neighborhoods. When a large project keeps adding tenants, it is usually a sign of confidence in the local economy. Below, I break down what was announced, why it matters to you, and what may come next.

What Happened

On July 7, 2026, FOX5 Vegas reported that BLVD Las Vegas signed four new dining tenants. The property is a mixed-use destination on the Las Vegas Strip, which means it blends retail, dining, and other uses in one large space. The four new names each bring a different flavor to the lineup.

The headliner is the TENSHOU Collection. This is described as a multi-concept Japanese experience. In plain words, that means it is not just one restaurant. It gathers several Japanese food styles under one roof. According to the announcement, TENSHOU will offer sushi, tempura, yakitori, and wood-fired kaiseki. Sushi is the well-known dish of rice and fish. Tempura is food that is battered and fried until light and crispy. Yakitori is grilled skewers, often chicken, cooked over a flame. And kaiseki is a traditional Japanese multi-course meal that is known for being careful, seasonal, and beautiful to look at. Having all of these in one spot is a big draw for people who love Japanese food.

The second new tenant is Pepper Lunch. This is a Japanese teppanyaki chain. Teppanyaki means the food is cooked on a hot iron plate. At Pepper Lunch, the plate often comes to your table still sizzling, and you finish cooking the meal yourself. It is a fun, fast, and hands-on way to eat. The chain has locations around the world, so its arrival adds a familiar name to the Strip.

The third tenant is Mothership Coffee Roasters. This one is special because it is a local, woman-owned coffee company. Mothership roasts its own beans, and it already has a following in the Las Vegas area. Adding a homegrown brand to a big Strip project is a nice touch. It gives visitors a taste of something that is truly from Southern Nevada, not just a national chain.

The fourth tenant is GoGreek, a Greek yogurt bar. Greek yogurt is thick and creamy, and a yogurt bar usually lets you build your own bowl with toppings like fruit, honey, and granola. It is a lighter, healthier option that rounds out the group.

FOX5 Vegas noted that these additions continue the property's growth following its opening. In other words, BLVD Las Vegas is not done. It keeps adding tenants, and this batch is the latest step. The mix of a high-end Japanese collection, a fun teppanyaki chain, a local coffee roaster, and a healthy yogurt bar shows the property wants to appeal to a wide range of people and budgets.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might wonder why a few new restaurants on the Strip should matter to someone who lives out in Summerlin, Henderson, or the northwest valley. The answer comes down to how the local economy works. The Las Vegas Strip is the engine that drives much of Southern Nevada. When it grows, the effects spread far past the resort corridor.

First, there are the jobs. Every new restaurant needs cooks, servers, hosts, dishwashers, managers, and cleaning crews. A multi-concept spot like the TENSHOU Collection can employ a large staff because it runs several kitchens at once. Coffee roasters need baristas and roasters. Yogurt bars need counter workers. Add it all up, and four new tenants can mean dozens of new jobs. Those are paychecks that go to people who live in the valley and spend their money at local stores, gas stations, and grocery shops.

Second, there is the visitor draw. The Strip lives and dies by its ability to give people reasons to visit and to stay a little longer. New dining options do exactly that. A tourist who might have come just for a show now has another reason to walk the Strip and spend money. More visitor spending means more tax revenue for the region, which helps pay for roads, parks, and schools.

Third, there is the local angle. Not every tenant is aimed only at tourists. Mothership Coffee Roasters is a local, woman-owned brand that valley residents already know. Its spot at BLVD gives locals a familiar reason to visit the property too. When a project mixes national names with homegrown ones, it feels more connected to the community instead of being just a tourist trap.

Fourth, and this is where my day job comes in, there is housing. A strong visitor economy supports jobs, and jobs support housing demand. When people feel secure in their work, they are more willing to rent or buy homes near where they work. Workers on the Strip often look for housing in nearby parts of the valley. Steady growth in Strip dining and retail can help keep that demand healthy. It is not a direct one-to-one link, but the pattern is real. A busy Strip usually means a busy housing market in the neighborhoods that feed it. [NOT VERIFIED: the exact number of jobs these four tenants will create was not stated in the source.]

Background and History

BLVD Las Vegas is a mixed-use destination on the Strip. The term mixed-use means it blends more than one purpose in the same place. Instead of being only a mall or only a set of restaurants, it brings shopping, dining, and other uses together. The goal of a project like this is to keep people on-site longer. If you can shop, eat, grab a coffee, and relax all in one spot, you are more likely to spend the whole day there.

The property opened not long ago, and it has been signing tenants in waves ever since. The FOX5 Vegas report describes these four new restaurants as part of the property's continued growth following its opening. That is a common pattern for large retail projects. They rarely open completely full. Instead, they line up anchor tenants first, open their doors, and then keep adding new names over the following months and years. Each new announcement builds buzz and gives people a fresh reason to come back.

The Strip has a long history of blending dining with entertainment. For decades, the resorts have used food as a way to draw crowds. Big-name chefs, buffets, and themed restaurants have all been part of the pull. In recent years, the trend has leaned toward variety and toward Asian food concepts in particular. Sushi bars, ramen shops, teppanyaki spots, and Asian food halls have opened across the valley and along the Strip. The TENSHOU Collection and Pepper Lunch fit right into that wave.

Local coffee has also grown as a scene. Companies like Mothership Coffee Roasters built their names by roasting beans in the valley and serving neighbors first. Putting a local roaster inside a major Strip project is a sign of how far the homegrown coffee scene has come. It shows that Strip developers see value in brands that locals trust, not just national chains. [NOT VERIFIED: the specific opening dates for each of the four tenants were not listed in the source.]

What Happens Next

The next thing to watch is when each tenant actually opens its doors. The July 7 announcement confirmed that the leases are signed, but signing a lease and opening a restaurant are two different steps. Building out a kitchen, hiring staff, and passing health and safety checks all take time. It would not be a surprise to see these spots open in stages over the coming months rather than all at once. [NOT VERIFIED: no firm opening dates were given in the source.]

The TENSHOU Collection is likely to take the longest because it is the biggest and most complex. Running sushi, tempura, yakitori, and wood-fired kaiseki all in one place means building several kitchens and hiring skilled chefs for each style. A project that large usually needs more time to get right. Pepper Lunch, as an established chain, may be able to move faster since it has a set playbook for opening new stores.

Mothership Coffee Roasters and GoGreek are smaller footprints, so they could open sooner. Coffee bars and yogurt bars are simpler to set up than full-service restaurants. If BLVD wants quick wins to keep buzz going, these two are the kind of spots that can open early.

Beyond these four, keep an eye on whether BLVD announces even more tenants. The pattern so far suggests the property will keep filling out its lineup. Each new name adds to the reasons people have to visit. For locals, it is worth checking the property's website and local news outlets for opening dates and any grand-opening deals. Restaurants often offer discounts or free samples in their first weeks to build a crowd.

Finally, watch how these openings affect nearby traffic and parking. New draws bring new crowds, and that can change how busy the roads and garages near the property feel. If you plan to visit, going on a weekday or during off-peak hours can make the trip easier.
