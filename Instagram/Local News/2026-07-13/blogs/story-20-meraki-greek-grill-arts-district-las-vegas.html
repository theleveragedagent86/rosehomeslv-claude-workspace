Meraki Greek Grill Opens Sixth Location in the Arts District

Meraki Greek Grill just opened a new spot in the Las Vegas Arts District, and it is a big deal for anyone who loves Greek food. The new restaurant sits at 1551 S. Commerce St., Suite 110. This is the brand's sixth location in the Las Vegas valley. The space is about 3,300 square feet and can seat 85 people. That is a good size for a sit-down meal or a quick lunch with friends.

The company was started by Jerry Goumroian. Under his lead, Meraki Greek Grill has grown fast across Southern Nevada. It even earned a spot on Yelp's Top 100 Restaurants in America. That kind of honor does not come easy. It means real people wrote real reviews and ranked the food near the top of the whole country. For a homegrown Las Vegas brand, that is something to be proud of.

And the growth is not stopping here. A seventh location is planned for St. Rose Parkway in Henderson. That one is slated to open by the middle of July 2026, which is right around the corner. So while the Arts District welcomes its new Greek grill this month, Henderson is about to get one too. In this article, I will walk through what happened, why it matters to people who live here, and what it says about our neighborhoods.

What Happened

Here is the short version. Meraki Greek Grill opened a brand new restaurant in the heart of the Las Vegas Arts District. The address is 1551 S. Commerce St., Suite 110. The building gives the restaurant about 3,300 square feet of space, and there is seating for 85 guests. This marks the sixth Meraki Greek Grill location in the Las Vegas area.

The news was shared by the Las Vegas Review-Journal through its Neon dining section, along with Vegas24Seven. The report came out on July 8, 2026. According to that report, the same company is also getting ready to open a seventh location. That new spot will be on St. Rose Parkway in Henderson, and it is expected to open by mid-July 2026.

So in a very short window, this one Greek brand is adding two new places to eat. One is in the Arts District downtown. The other is in Henderson, which is a growing suburb on the south end of the valley. That is a strong sign of how popular the food has become. The Arts District is a smart pick for a new restaurant. It is one of the most walkable and lively parts of downtown Las Vegas. It gives visitors one more reason to spend an evening walking around the district.

Background and History

Meraki Greek Grill is a Las Vegas story at its core. The brand was founded by Jerry Goumroian, and it has built a strong name for Greek food across the valley. The word "meraki" itself is a Greek term. It describes doing something with love and putting a piece of yourself into your work. That idea fits a restaurant that has grown by word of mouth and good reviews.

One of the biggest markers of that success came when the brand was named to Yelp's Top 100 Restaurants in America. Yelp builds that list from real customer reviews and ratings. Landing on it means a business is not just liked locally but ranks near the top on a national scale. For a Las Vegas grill that started small, that is a rare and hard-earned honor.

The Arts District, where the newest location sits, has its own long history. For years it was a quiet stretch of downtown filled with warehouses and older buildings. Over the last decade or so, artists, small business owners, and restaurant folks turned it into one of the most creative parts of the city. Today it is often called the 18b Arts District, and it draws people with murals, galleries, and a growing food and drink scene.

Henderson, the site of the coming seventh location, has grown in a different way. It went from a small industrial town into one of the biggest and most family-friendly cities in Nevada. The St. Rose Parkway area in particular has seen a lot of new homes, shops, and restaurants. It sits near major roads and fast-growing neighborhoods, which makes it a natural spot for a busy grill.

It also helps to remember why Greek food travels so well. The menu leans on grilled meats, fresh vegetables, olive oil, and simple, bold flavors. Dishes like gyros, souvlaki, and Greek salads are easy to love and easy to share. They work for a fast lunch or a slow dinner. That wide appeal is part of why a Greek brand can open six or seven spots without wearing out its welcome. There is room for it in almost any part of town.

Put those pieces together and the pattern is clear. A local brand with a strong track record is planting new roots in two of the valley's most active areas. One area is known for art and downtown energy. The other is known for family living and steady growth. That is a smart way to expand, and it is the kind of steady, thoughtful growth that tends to last.
