Maryland Parkway Bus Rapid Transit Red Line to Launch in August

After nearly two years of construction, a faster new bus line is almost ready to roll on one of the busiest streets in Las Vegas. The Regional Transportation Commission of Southern Nevada, better known as the RTC, plans to launch its new Red Line bus rapid transit service in August 2026. The route runs 12.5 miles along the Maryland Parkway corridor, and it will replace the current Route 109 bus that riders use today. This is not just a small change to a bus schedule. It is a big upgrade to how people move up and down one of the most important streets on the east side of the valley.

Bus rapid transit, or BRT for short, is a style of bus service built to feel more like a train. Buses come more often, stops are spaced out, and the ride is meant to be smoother and quicker. According to the Las Vegas Sun, most of the underground work for the project is already finished. Crews have now shifted to landscaping and streetscape work, which means planting, sidewalks, and the finishing touches that make the corridor look and feel better. If you drive or live near Maryland Parkway, you have probably watched this work happen for months. Now the finish line is close.

What Happened

The Las Vegas Sun reported on July 4, 2026, that the RTC's Red Line bus rapid transit service is set to begin in August 2026. The project has been under construction for close to two years. That is a long time for any road project, and neighbors along Maryland Parkway have felt the effect of cones, detours, and closed lanes for much of it. The good news in the report is that the hardest part appears to be behind the crews. Most of the underground infrastructure is done, and the remaining work is landscaping and streetscape improvements.

Underground infrastructure is the part of a project you cannot see once it is finished. It includes things like drainage, utility lines, and the base layers that hold up new pavement and stations. Getting that work done first is normal. It is also the messiest and slowest stage, so finishing it is a real milestone. Once that is complete, the surface work tends to move faster and cause fewer big disruptions.

The new Red Line will run 12.5 miles along the Maryland Parkway corridor. When it opens, it will replace Route 109. Route 109 has been one of the most used bus routes in the whole RTC system for years, so this is a swap that many daily riders will notice right away. The RTC has branded the upgraded service as the Red Line, which points to a higher-frequency level of service than a standard local bus.

Bus rapid transit lines in other cities often include features like raised boarding platforms, off-board ticket machines, and traffic signals that hold a green light a little longer for the bus. These features help buses stop for less time at each station and keep moving. On a higher-frequency line, the wait is usually shorter, which means you can often walk to a stop without checking a schedule and still catch a bus soon. Background and History

The Regional Transportation Commission of Southern Nevada is the public agency that runs bus service across the Las Vegas valley. It also helps plan roads and manages regional transportation projects for the area. For years, the RTC has looked for ways to speed up service on its busiest corridors instead of building expensive rail lines. Bus rapid transit is the tool many growing cities use to get train-like service at a lower cost, because it uses buses and existing roads rather than new tracks.

Maryland Parkway has long been near the top of the list for this kind of upgrade. Route 109 already carried heavy ridership, which is exactly the kind of demand that justifies a faster line. Planning and studies for a Maryland Parkway transit project stretched over several years before crews ever broke ground. Turning a normal street into a corridor built for bus rapid transit takes time, because it can mean rebuilding stations, adjusting lanes, and updating utilities under the road.

That is why the construction has run for nearly two years. Big corridor projects almost always follow the same path. First comes the underground and utility work, which is slow and disruptive. Then comes the surface work, stations, and streetscape. The Las Vegas Sun report makes clear that the project has now crossed from the first stage into the second, with landscaping and streetscape improvements as the main remaining tasks. Reaching that point after two years of digging is a sign the project is in its home stretch. The naming of the service as the Red Line fits the RTC's approach of giving upgraded routes a clear brand so riders know they are getting faster, more frequent service than a basic local bus.
