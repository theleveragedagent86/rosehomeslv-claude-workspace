Golden Knights Announce Eleven Free Agent Signings to Open Free Agency

The Vegas Golden Knights started the 2026 offseason fast. On July 1, 2026, the team announced that it had agreed to deals with eleven free agents on the very first day of free agency. Seven of the new players are forwards and four are defensemen. That is a big group to add all at once, and it changed how deep the team is at almost every position.

The forwards are Jakub Demek, Joe Fleming, Marc Gatcomb, Tanner Laczynski, Raphael Lavoie, Victor Olofsson, and Jonas Rondbjerg. The defensemen are Dylan Coghlan, Adam Ginning, Ville Heinola, and Antti Tuomisto. These moves were made to give the club more depth for both the NHL roster and its American Hockey League team down the road in Henderson.

The signings came right after the Golden Knights locked up defenseman Jeremy Lauzon with a six-year deal that carries an average yearly value of $4 million. So in a short window, the team both kept a player it already had and added nearly a dozen new names to the organization. If you live in Las Vegas or anywhere in Clark County, this is the kind of news that shapes the coming hockey season. Below, I break down what happened, why it matters to people who live here, and what to watch for next.

What Happened

On July 1, 2026, the first day teams across the league are allowed to sign players who are not under contract, the Golden Knights moved quickly. They announced eleven agreements in one batch. That is a lot of paperwork and a lot of new faces for one day.

Here is the full list again so it is easy to read. The seven forwards are Jakub Demek, Joe Fleming, Marc Gatcomb, Tanner Laczynski, Raphael Lavoie, Victor Olofsson, and Jonas Rondbjerg. The four defensemen are Dylan Coghlan, Adam Ginning, Ville Heinola, and Antti Tuomisto. Every one of these players is now part of the Golden Knights organization.

It helps to know what "free agency" means. In pro hockey, players sign contracts that last for a set number of years. When a contract ends, the player can become a free agent. That means he is free to sign with any team that wants him. Free agency opens on the same day for the whole league, and teams race to sign the players they want before someone else does. The Golden Knights used that opening day to add depth in one big push.

"Depth" is a word you will hear a lot in hockey. It means having enough good players so the team can keep going even when someone gets hurt or plays poorly. A hockey season is long and rough. Players get injured. Some go into slumps. A team with strong depth can plug in another skater and barely miss a beat. The eleven signings were about building that kind of depth.

Some of these players are expected to compete for spots on the main NHL roster. Others are likely to start with the team's minor league affiliate, the Henderson Silver Knights, who play in the American Hockey League. The AHL is one level below the NHL. It is where teams keep players ready to be called up when the big club needs them. These signings also came on the heels of a contract for defenseman Jeremy Lauzon. His new deal runs six years and pays an average of $4 million each season. Keeping Lauzon while adding eleven new players shows the team was working on both ends of the roster at the same time. It kept a known piece and stacked up new options behind him.

It is worth pausing on the size of this move. Most teams sign one or two free agents on opening day and then wait to see how the market shakes out. The Golden Knights went the other way and moved on eleven players at once. That kind of push takes planning. The team had to have its list ready and its offers set before the market even opened. Adding both forwards and defensemen in the same batch means the club wanted to strengthen every part of the roster, not just one weak spot. Doing all of that in a single day tells you the front office came into free agency with a clear plan and acted on it fast.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might wonder why a list of hockey signings matters to someone who lives in Summerlin, Henderson, or the northwest valley. The answer is that the Golden Knights are more than a sports team. They are a big part of daily life and the local economy in Southern Nevada. When the team is set up to win, the whole valley feels it.

Start with the simple stuff. Games at the arena on the Strip draw big crowds. Those crowds spend money at restaurants, hotels, and shops nearby. Parking lots fill up. Rideshare drivers stay busy. Bars and grills across the valley fill their tables with fans wearing gold and black. A team that is expected to compete keeps those crowds coming back all winter long. Deep rosters help a team stay in the playoff race, and playoff races put more people in seats.

There are jobs tied to all of this too. The arena needs workers on game nights. So do the team stores, the concession stands, and the many businesses that live off game day traffic. When the club is strong and fans are excited, that work stays steady. A rebuilding team with empty seats can mean fewer hours for a lot of people.

There is also the Henderson side of the story. The Golden Knights own a minor league team, the Henderson Silver Knights, who play in the American Hockey League. Several of these eleven signings could end up in Henderson. That means more live pro hockey right in the valley, not just on the Strip. Silver Knights games give families a lower cost way to watch skilled players who might be the next stars for the NHL club. More depth signings can mean a stronger, more fun product in Henderson.

Finally, there is what a winning team does for a city's name. Las Vegas spent decades known mostly for casinos and shows. The Golden Knights helped change that. Now the valley is known as a real sports town. That reputation helps draw new residents and new businesses. As someone who helps people buy and sell homes here, I see how a strong local identity makes the valley more appealing. A team built to keep winning is part of that bigger picture.

Background and History

The Golden Knights are still a young team by league standards, but they packed a lot into a short run. They played their first season in 2017 as an expansion club. An expansion team is a brand new team added to the league, built mostly from players other teams left unprotected. Most new teams struggle for years. The Golden Knights did the opposite. They reached the Stanley Cup Final in their very first season, which shocked the hockey world.

From there, the team stayed near the top of the standings almost every year. They built a habit of making the playoffs and playing deep into the spring. That habit paid off in 2023, when the Golden Knights won the Stanley Cup for the first time. Winning a title just a few years after starting from scratch is rare, and it made the team a point of pride across the valley.

A big reason for that steady success has been the way the front office builds the roster. The team has been active in trades and in free agency, always looking to add useful players. It has also leaned on its minor league system to grow talent. The Henderson Silver Knights joined the American Hockey League and gave the organization a place close to home to develop young players and keep extra bodies ready.

The eleven signings from July 1, 2026, fit that pattern. Rather than making one giant splash, the team spread its work across many players. Some are meant to push for NHL time. Others are meant to make the Henderson roster stronger and to give the club options if injuries hit. This is the same recipe that helped the Golden Knights stay in contention since day one. The Lauzon extension is part of the story too, showing the team wanted to hold onto a player it trusts while filling in depth around him.
