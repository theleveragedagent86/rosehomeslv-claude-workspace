Home Is Possible Offers Up to 4% Down Payment Help for Nevada Buyers

If saving for a down payment is the only thing standing between you and a home in Las Vegas, there is real help available. The Nevada Housing Division runs a program called Home Is Possible that gives buyers up to 4% of their loan amount to cover the down payment and closing costs. That money does not have to be paid back if you stay in the home for three years.

This matters right now because home prices in Clark County are near record highs. Many renters can handle a monthly mortgage payment but cannot come up with the cash to get in the door. Home Is Possible was built to solve that exact problem, and you do not have to be a first-time buyer to use it.

What the Home Is Possible Program Actually Offers

Home Is Possible is run by the Nevada Housing Division, a state agency. The program pairs a 30-year fixed-rate first mortgage with a chunk of money to help with your upfront costs. That help can be worth up to 4% of your total loan amount. You can put it toward your down payment, your closing costs, or both.

Here is the part that surprises most people. The assistance is set up as a second loan, but it is forgivable. If you keep the home as your main residence for three years, the money is wiped away and you owe nothing back. It works like a gift that rewards you for staying put. Sell or refinance too early and a portion may need to be repaid, so the timing matters.

The rules are friendlier than many buyers expect. You do not need to be a first-time buyer. You need a minimum credit score of 640. There are income limits and a home price cap, and you must complete a short homebuyer education course before closing. The home has to be your primary residence, not a rental or a flip.

There is also a special version for educators. The Home Is Possible for Teachers program offers even more help, up to 6% of the loan amount, for licensed Nevada teachers who qualify. That extra 2% can make a big difference in a market like this one. Both programs run through approved lenders across the state, and Clark County buyers use them every day.

One thing to keep in mind is that the amount you receive is tied to your loan size. Since the help is a percentage of the loan, a bigger loan means a bigger dollar figure of assistance, up to the program cap. The money is applied at closing, so you see it work directly against the cash you would otherwise have to wire in. Your lender handles the paperwork with the Nevada Housing Division, which keeps the process simple on your end.

It also helps to understand how the assistance and your regular loan work together. The first mortgage is a normal 30-year fixed loan, so your rate and monthly payment are set the usual way. The Home Is Possible help sits on top as the forgivable piece. You still may have mortgage insurance depending on your loan type and how much you put down, so ask your lender to lay out the full monthly picture, not just the down payment savings, before you commit.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

The math on buying a home in Clark County has gotten harder. The Las Vegas median single-family home price has been sitting near a record $490,000. On a home at that price, a standard down payment can run tens of thousands of dollars. That is a giant wall for a working family that pays rent every month and cannot save much on top of it.

Home Is Possible knocks a big hole in that wall. Say you buy a home and take out a loan around $470,000. Four percent of that is close to $18,800. That is real money that can cover most or all of what you need to bring to the table. For a lot of renters in the valley, that is the difference between waiting five more years and moving in this year.

This also helps people who already own but got stuck. Maybe you sold a small condo and the profit was not enough for a bigger place. Maybe a life change reset your savings. Because you do not have to be a first-time buyer, Home Is Possible is open to you too. That flexibility fits the real lives of people in Henderson, North Las Vegas, Spring Valley, and the rest of the county.

Think about who this reaches in everyday terms. A nurse at a valley hospital, a young couple renting in Spring Valley, a warehouse worker in North Las Vegas, a teacher at a local elementary school. These are people with steady income and good habits who simply have not been able to save a five-figure down payment while paying rent. For them, the gap has never been about being responsible. It has been about the cash. Closing that gap changes what is possible for a huge slice of working Clark County.

There is a quieter benefit as well. When more renters can buy, they lock in a fixed monthly payment instead of facing rent hikes every year. In a valley where rents keep climbing, owning gives families a stable base. A program that helps you cross that line is not just a nice perk. For many households, it is the whole ballgame.

It is worth being clear-eyed about the local market too. Nevada has been posting some of the highest foreclosure rates in the country, and the Las Vegas-Henderson metro shows up near the top of those lists. Down payment help does not erase risk, and buying a home you cannot comfortably afford is never smart. But used the right way, this program helps steady buyers get in with less strain on their savings, which can leave more of a cushion for the surprises that come with owning a home.

Background and History

Down payment help is not new in Nevada, but the need for it has grown fast. For years, home prices in the Las Vegas Valley climbed while wages did not keep pace. The Nevada Housing Division created and expanded programs like Home Is Possible to keep ownership within reach as the market heated up.

The idea behind the program is simple. Most buyers with steady jobs can afford a monthly payment. The trouble is the pile of cash needed at closing. By handing buyers that upfront money and forgiving it over time, the state helps people who are ready in every way except savings. It is a targeted fix for a specific roadblock.

Nevada is not alone in offering this kind of help, but the state version stands out for how it is built. The forgivable structure, the 640 credit score floor, and the fact that repeat buyers can use it make it broader than many programs in other states. The teacher version came out of a push to keep educators living in the communities where they work, since housing costs were driving some of them out.

It helps to know the program next to the regular loan options too. An FHA loan, which is very common in Clark County, lets buyers put down as little as 3.5%. The 2026 FHA loan limit for a single-family home in Clark County is $524,225. Pairing an FHA loan with Home Is Possible assistance is a popular combo, because the low down payment and the forgivable help work together to shrink your out-of-pocket cost even further.

The homebuyer education requirement is part of this history for a reason. State programs learned that buyers who understand budgeting, credit, and the true costs of ownership tend to do better and stay in their homes longer. So the class is not busywork. It is a short course that helps you avoid common mistakes, and finishing it is what unlocks the assistance. Most buyers can complete it online in an afternoon.

What Happens Next

The program is active and taking buyers right now. It is not a one-time event or a lottery. As long as funding is available and you meet the rules, you can apply through an approved lender and move toward closing on your own timeline. That said, program details like income limits and price caps get updated over time, so the smart move is to check the current numbers before you plan around them.

The next step for most people is a two-part check. First, get pre-approved with a lender that offers Home Is Possible so you know your price range and confirm you clear the 640 credit score line. Second, sign up for the required homebuyer education course early, since you cannot close without it. Handling both at the start keeps your purchase from stalling later.

Watch the housing market too. Prices in the valley remain near record highs, and interest rates move week to week. Down payment help does not change the rate you get, but it lowers the cash you need up front, which can make a home you thought was out of reach suddenly work. Running the numbers now, even if you are a few months from buying, tells you exactly where you stand.

Keep an eye on funding as well. State assistance programs draw from set pools of money, and demand rises when prices climb and buyers go looking for help. That does not mean you should panic or rush a bad decision. It just means that if the program fits your plan, it is better to get pre-approved and ready to move than to sit on the idea for a year. A lender can tell you whether funds are flowing before you get your heart set on a specific house.

What You Can Do

Start by pulling your credit and getting a real number. If you are at or above 640, you clear the main hurdle for Home Is Possible. If you are close, a few months of on-time payments and paying down a card or two can get you there. Do not guess on this. A quick pre-approval tells you where you actually stand.

Next, connect with a lender approved for the program and ask them to run your options side by side. Look at Home Is Possible with a 30-year fixed loan, and look at pairing it with an FHA loan and its 3.5% down. Ask about the income limits and the home price cap for Clark County so you know your range. If you are a licensed Nevada teacher, ask specifically about the Home Is Possible for Teachers program and its 6% help. You can also review current program details at the Nevada Housing Division's official Home Is Possible site.

Then sign up for the homebuyer education course early so it never becomes the thing that delays your closing. Once those pieces are in place, you are ready to shop with confidence and a clear budget.
