Emeril Lagasse Opens Meril at M Resort, His First Off-Strip Vegas Restaurant

Emeril Lagasse just opened Meril at M Resort Spa Casino in Henderson, and it is the first restaurant the famous chef has ever placed off the Las Vegas Strip. The New Orleans rooted spot opened on July 16, 2026, and it gives the south valley a big name kitchen without the drive to the resort corridor.

This is a real moment for Henderson diners. For years, if you wanted a plate from a celebrity chef, you drove to the Strip, paid to park, and fought the crowds. Now one of the most recognized names in American cooking is serving contemporary American food a few minutes from home, off St. Rose Parkway.

What Happened

Celebrity chef Emeril Lagasse opened Meril at M Resort Spa Casino on July 16, 2026. Lagasse is a three time James Beard Award winner, and he is one of the most famous chefs in the country thanks to decades of cookbooks, television shows, and restaurants. Meril is his first restaurant located off the Las Vegas Strip, which is a first for both the chef and the resort.

The restaurant carries the name of Lagasse's daughter, Meril, and it is modeled on his casual New Orleans concept of the same name. The menu is contemporary American, with dishes drawn from Lagasse's global travels and his deep New Orleans roots. Reports describe a large main dining room with floor to ceiling windows and a wrap around terrace with outdoor seating that looks over the M Pool and Las Vegas Boulevard.

The food leans into comfort and flavor. Breakfast plates include beignets, bananas foster French toast, and andouille hash. Dinner runs from boudin balls and barbecued shrimp to a ribeye with chimichurri. It is the kind of menu built for both a quick weekday meal and a special night out, which fits a neighborhood resort more than a formal Strip showroom.

The space is built to match the food. Reports point to a large main dining room, big windows that pull in natural light, and an outdoor terrace where guests can eat with a view of the pool and the boulevard beyond. That indoor and outdoor mix is a smart fit for the desert, where mild mornings and warm evenings make patio seating a real draw for much of the year.

For M Resort, this is the property's first celebrity chef partnership. The resort sits at Las Vegas Boulevard and St. Rose Parkway on the south edge of Henderson, and it has long drawn a local crowd from Green Valley, Anthem, Seven Hills, and the southern highlands. Adding a name like Emeril gives the resort a fresh reason for locals to book a table and for out of town guests to make the trip south.

The concept is built to be flexible. Meril is designed as an all day spot, which means you can stop in for breakfast, grab lunch, or settle in for a full dinner. That is different from the special occasion only feel of many Strip restaurants. The idea is a place you can visit on a random Tuesday, not just a night you are celebrating something.

The New Orleans influence runs through the whole menu. Beignets, andouille sausage, boudin, and barbecued shrimp are all Louisiana staples, and they pair with dishes pulled from Lagasse's travels around the world. That mix of comfort food and global flavor is the heart of what Meril has been since it first opened in New Orleans.

Background and History

Emeril Lagasse built his name in New Orleans. He opened Emeril's there in 1990, and his food, his television shows, and his catchphrases turned him into a household name through the 1990s and 2000s. He has won the James Beard Award multiple times and has run restaurants across several cities over his career.

Lagasse is not new to Las Vegas. He has run restaurants on the Strip for years, including well known spots inside major resorts. What makes Meril different is the location. Every Lagasse restaurant in the market before this one sat on or near the Strip. Meril at M Resort is the first time he has planted a flag in the suburbs, and that is why the opening drew so much attention.

The original Meril opened in New Orleans in 2016 as Lagasse's more relaxed, all day concept. It was named for his daughter and built around shareable, globally inspired plates rather than white tablecloth formality. Bringing that same idea to Henderson fits the M Resort crowd, which skews local and casual rather than tourist and formal.

M Resort itself has been a south valley anchor since it opened in 2009. It has grown its dining, gaming, and entertainment over the years and has leaned into serving nearby residents. A celebrity chef partnership is a natural next step for a property that has spent more than fifteen years building a loyal local base.

Henderson has changed a lot in that time. Once seen mostly as a quiet bedroom community, it is now the second largest city in Nevada and one of the fastest growing parts of the metro. Master planned communities like Green Valley Ranch, Anthem, Seven Hills, Inspirada, and Cadence have brought in tens of thousands of residents who want good schools, safe streets, and now, good food close to home.

That growth is exactly why a chef would look south. Restaurants follow rooftops. As more high earning households settle in Henderson, the demand for higher end dining grows with them. A decade ago the biggest names stayed on the Strip. Today they are willing to open where the residents actually live, and Meril is a clear example of that shift.

What Happens Next

Now that Meril is open, expect the first several weeks to be busy. New celebrity chef restaurants in this valley almost always draw a rush of curious diners at the start. If you want a quieter first visit, an early weekday reservation is usually a safer bet than a Friday or Saturday night.

Watch for the restaurant to settle into a rhythm across breakfast, lunch, and dinner. All day concepts like Meril live or die on being a place people return to, not a one time bucket list stop. The real test will be whether Henderson locals make it a regular table over the coming months.

It is also worth watching what this opening does for the area around M Resort. When a big name lands, other operators pay attention. More dining and retail interest near St. Rose Parkway would be a healthy sign for the south end of Henderson, and it is the kind of trend I keep an eye on for buyers and sellers in that part of the valley.

Keep an eye on the menu too. All day concepts often adjust once they learn what a neighborhood wants. Breakfast and brunch could become a big draw for locals, while the dinner menu may shift with the seasons. If Meril leans into the New Orleans classics that made the brand famous, it could carve out a real niche in a valley that does not have many strong Creole and Cajun options.

Longer term, the opening adds to the case that Henderson's south end is a destination and not just a place to sleep. That reputation builds slowly, one new spot at a time. Meril is a headline addition, but it joins a steady stream of restaurants, shops, and services moving into the area, and together they make the whole south valley more appealing.
