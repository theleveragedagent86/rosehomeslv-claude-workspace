Irv's Burgers Opens Third Valley Location at Green Valley Ranch in Henderson

Irv's Burgers, the Los Angeles burger institution with roots going back to 1946, opened inside the Green Valley Ranch food court in Henderson on July 9, 2026. It is the brand's third location in the Las Vegas Valley, and it landed with a splash: free Roadside Burgers for the first 100 people in line, plus a full year of free burgers for 25 lucky guests.

For Henderson foodies, this means a cult California burger name is now a short drive away instead of a five hour trip to Route 66. For everyone else, it is one more sign that Green Valley Ranch and the surrounding neighborhoods keep pulling in bigger and better dining. Here is the full story on what opened, why locals care, and what it says about the east valley.

What Happened

Irv's Burgers held its grand opening on Thursday, July 9, 2026, inside the food court at Green Valley Ranch Resort Casino and Spa, located at 2300 Paseo Verde Parkway in Henderson. The opening marked the third Irv's location in the Las Vegas area, adding to the brand's growing Nevada footprint.

The debut leaned hard into freebies. The first 100 guests in line each received a free Roadside Burger, the chain's signature item. On top of that, Irv's ran a giveaway tied to its loyalty program. Twenty-five people who signed up were randomly chosen to win free burgers for a year, which works out to one free Roadside Burger every single week for 52 weeks.

The prizes did not stop at food. Guests also had a chance to win two Green Valley Ranch staycation packages and a block of 200 tickets to see Grace Potter perform at the resort on July 10. The whole event was built to draw a crowd, and it did.

The Roadside Burger at the center of all those giveaways is the item the brand hangs its name on. It is the classic, no-nonsense burger that traces straight back to the original 1946 stand, the kind of simple, well-made sandwich that turned a small California shop into a landmark. Handing that exact item out for free, and promising it every week for a year to the winners, was a clear message: come taste the thing that made us famous, then come back for it.

The menu sticks to what made Irv's famous. That means made-to-order burgers, crispy fries, and hand-spun milkshakes. The Green Valley Ranch spot keeps long hours too, open from 9 a.m. to midnight Sunday through Thursday and from 9 a.m. to 2 a.m. on Friday and Saturday. That late-night window matters in a resort setting, where hungry guests roll out of shows and off the casino floor well after most kitchens close.

Placing the shop inside the resort's food court was a deliberate move. A food court gives a growing brand a lower-cost way to enter a new market than a full standalone restaurant, and it puts the counter right in the path of resort guests, moviegoers, and locals grabbing a quick bite. Foot traffic is built in, and Irv's does not have to build a crowd from scratch. For a brand still expanding across Nevada, that is a smart, low-risk way to plant a flag in Henderson.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Food is one of the quiet ways a neighborhood tells you how it is doing. When a brand with real fans and real history picks your area, it is placing a bet that people nearby have money to spend and a reason to gather. Irv's choosing Green Valley Ranch is a small but real vote of confidence in Henderson's east and central neighborhoods.

For families in Green Valley, Seven Hills, and Anthem, this is simply a fun, affordable new option close to home. A burger, fries, and a shake is an easy weeknight dinner or a stop after a kid's ballgame. Not every new opening in the valley is a fine dining splurge. Places like this, priced for regular people, are the ones neighbors actually use week after week.

There is also a convenience angle that locals feel right away. Green Valley Ranch sits near the 215 Beltway, so the food court is easy to reach from a wide slice of Henderson and the southeast valley. A quick, casual bite inside a resort gives residents a reason to visit without booking a table or planning a night on the Strip.

It is worth remembering how much this part of the valley has grown. Green Valley Ranch anchors a stretch of Henderson that has filled in fast over the past two decades, with master-planned neighborhoods, parks, and shopping wrapping around the resort. Each new draw, whether a burger counter or a concert, gives the people who live nearby one more reason to stay close to home for their fun instead of driving across town. Over time, that is what turns a cluster of subdivisions into a real community with its own gravity.

And for anyone who follows the local dining scene, the timing is worth noting. Irv's opened the same stretch of July that saw Emeril Lagasse debut Meril at nearby M Resort. Henderson is stacking new names fast, from celebrity chefs to cult burger stands, and that kind of momentum tends to feed on itself. More options bring more foot traffic, and more foot traffic brings even more options.

There is a value angle here too, and it matters in 2026. Groceries and dining out both cost more than they did a couple of years ago, so families are choosier about where they spend. A brand built on a simple burger, fries, and a shake fits a budget in a way that a tasting menu never will. That is part of why casual openings like this one land so well with local crowds; the price of entry is a burger, not a reservation and a big check.

Background and History

Irv's Burgers is not a new brand chasing a trend. Its story starts in 1946 on Route 66 in West Hollywood, where the stand first opened under the name Queen's Burgers. An early sign captured its no-frills attitude perfectly: "No Lobster, Just Burgers." Owner Irv Gendis later bought the business and gave it the name it still carries today.

Over the decades, the little stand at Santa Monica Boulevard became a genuine Los Angeles landmark. In 2005, the City of West Hollywood designated Irv's Burgers as a cultural resource, a rare honor for a roadside burger joint. The spot picked up a devoted following, helped along by longtime owner Sonia Hong, who was known for drawing cheerful doodles and the words "Just For You" on customers' paper plates.

The brand's recent history reads like a comeback tale. Irv's closed for a stretch, and fans rallied hard to bring it back. In 2022, it reopened, still on historic Route 66, just one door down from its earlier home. That mix of history, loyalty, and a simple, well-made burger is exactly what the brand is now carrying into Southern Nevada as it grows.

Nevada has become a key part of that growth. The Green Valley Ranch opening is the third valley location, part of a steady push into the Las Vegas market that shows the brand sees real demand here. For a name that spent most of its life as a single beloved stand in California, that expansion is a big step.

It also fits a pattern Las Vegas knows well. Southern California brands with loyal followings have long treated Southern Nevada as a natural second home, since so many valley residents moved here from Los Angeles and already know the names. When a California favorite opens in Henderson, it is not starting cold; it arrives with built-in nostalgia for a big share of the transplant population. Irv's is tapping into that same pull, trading on decades of goodwill it earned one paper plate at a time.

What Happens Next

The grand opening crowds fade, but the real test for any new restaurant is the ordinary Tuesday. Now that the free-burger buzz has passed, Irv's has to turn first-time visitors into regulars. The long daily hours and the resort's steady flow of guests give it a strong shot, especially with that late-night window few kitchens in the area match.

Watch for the loyalty program to keep doing work here. The year-of-free-burgers giveaway was a smart hook, because those 25 winners now have a reason to come back every week and bring friends who pay full price. Expect Irv's to keep leaning on that loyalty sign-up and on limited-time deals to build a habit among east valley diners.

The bigger thing to watch is whether Irv's keeps expanding in Nevada. Three valley locations in a fairly short span suggests the brand likes what it sees. If these stores perform, more Southern Nevada openings would not be a surprise, and neighborhoods on the growing edges of Henderson and the northwest valley would be natural targets.

Keep an eye on how the resort itself uses the addition. Green Valley Ranch pairs its food court with a movie theater, a bowling alley, live music, and a busy casino floor, so Irv's benefits from every reason people already have to be there. If the resort leans into that mix with events and promotions, the burger counter could become a steady anchor rather than just a passing novelty. That is the difference between an opening that fades and one that becomes part of a neighborhood's routine.

Reviews will tell part of the story too. A brand can open with free burgers and a big crowd, but word of mouth is what carries it through month three and beyond. Watch the local food pages and neighborhood groups over the next few weeks. If Henderson diners start calling Irv's their new go-to for a quick, honest burger, that is the signal the brand hoped for when it chose Green Valley Ranch.

If you are the type who eats out often in Henderson, a quick sign-up can pay for itself fast.
