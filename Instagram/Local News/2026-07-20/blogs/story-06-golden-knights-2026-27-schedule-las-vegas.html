Vegas Golden Knights Release 2026-27 Season X Schedule, Open at Home vs. Chicago

The Vegas Golden Knights will open the 2026-27 season at home against the Chicago Blackhawks on Tuesday, September 29, with puck drop set for 7:30 p.m. Pacific Time at T-Mobile Arena. The National Hockey League released the full VGK schedule on July 16, 2026, and Las Vegas fans can now start circling the dates that matter most.

This is a big one. The 2026-27 season marks the franchise's 10th year, a milestone the team is branding as "Season X." It is also the first NHL season with an 84-game schedule, which means two extra nights of hockey to plan around. For a city that builds its fall and winter social calendar around home games, the schedule drop is one of the most anticipated days of the summer.

What Happened

On Thursday, July 16, 2026, the NHL released the Vegas Golden Knights regular season schedule for 2026-27, according to the Las Vegas Sun. The reveal kicked off the countdown to the franchise's 10th season since it first hit the ice in 2017.

The headline date is opening night. VGK will host the Chicago Blackhawks on Tuesday, September 29 at T-Mobile Arena, with the puck dropping at 7:30 p.m. Pacific Time. Opening a season at home in front of a sold-out crowd has become a tradition in Las Vegas, and the pregame show at T-Mobile Arena is a spectacle all its own.

The other date fans will not want to miss is December 21, when the Carolina Hurricanes come to town. That game is a rematch of the 2026 Stanley Cup Final, so the emotions and the storylines will run high. A Final rematch in the middle of the regular season gives the whole valley a marquee night to look forward to before the new year.

Those two dates anchor the calendar, but the full slate is what fans really wanted. The release lays out every home and road game, the back-to-back sets, the long homestands, and the road trips that will keep the team away from Las Vegas for stretches at a time. Season ticket holders use that full picture to decide which games to keep and which to sell or share. Casual fans use it to pick one or two nights that fit their budget and their schedule. Either way, the July 16 reveal gave everyone the information they needed to start planning.

The 2026-27 campaign also brings a structural change to the league. This is the first NHL season played under an 84-game schedule, up from the long-running 82-game format. That means each team plays two more games than in past years, and it gives Las Vegas two additional nights of hockey to enjoy. The Vegas Golden Knights, often shortened to VGK after the first mention, will spread those games across the fall, winter, and early spring as usual.

The schedule release is also the moment the season starts to feel real. For months, fans track free agent signings and roster moves in the abstract. The moment the dates land, all of that turns into concrete plans. People start texting friends about which games to attend, checking the calendar against birthdays and holidays, and deciding whether to renew a season ticket or split a partial plan with neighbors. The July 16 drop set all of that in motion across the valley.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Here is the thing about the Golden Knights schedule in Las Vegas. It is not just a sports calendar. It shapes traffic, dinner reservations, hotel demand, and weekend plans across Clark County. When the schedule drops, families, season ticket holders, and local businesses all start planning around it.

On a home game night, the area around T-Mobile Arena and the south end of the Strip gets busy fast. If you live in or commute through Paradise, the Harmon corridor, or the Tropicana and Las Vegas Boulevard area, you already know to leave a little early. The 84-game season adds two more of those nights to the mix, so it pays to know the dates in advance.

The games also drive real money into the local economy. Bars and restaurants in nearby neighborhoods fill up on game nights. Rideshare drivers, arena workers, and hospitality staff across the valley pick up shifts. From Summerlin to Henderson to the southwest, a strong VGK season means more people heading downtown and to the Strip to watch, spend, and celebrate.

For families, the schedule is a planning tool. Parents can match up home dates with school breaks and weekends, then grab tickets before the marquee games sell out. A weeknight opener like the September 29 game against the Chicago Blackhawks may be tougher for school-age kids, but plenty of weekend and holiday-week home dates make for an easy family outing. Hockey has become a shared local ritual, and the schedule is how everyone gets on the same page.

There is a hospitality angle too, and it reaches well beyond the Strip. Las Vegas draws visitors for big games, and a Final rematch like the December 21 date with the Carolina Hurricanes can bring in out-of-town fans who book hotel rooms and fill restaurants. That kind of demand touches jobs and small businesses in neighborhoods far from the arena. When the team plays well and the building is full, the ripple reaches servers, drivers, retail workers, and vendors across Clark County. A healthy VGK season is good news for a lot of local paychecks, not just for the standings.

Background and History

The Vegas Golden Knights are one of the great success stories in modern pro sports. The team debuted in 2017 as an NHL expansion franchise, and it did something no expansion team had done before. It reached the Stanley Cup Final in its very first season. That run turned a brand-new team into an instant civic obsession and put Las Vegas on the map as a real sports town.

The love only grew from there. In 2023, the Golden Knights won the Stanley Cup, delivering the city its first major professional sports championship. That title cemented the team's place in the fabric of Las Vegas life. Gold jerseys became everyday wear, and T-Mobile Arena became a gathering place for the whole valley, not just die-hard hockey fans.

T-Mobile Arena opened in April 2016 in Paradise, just west of Las Vegas Boulevard behind the New York-New York and Park MGM resorts. The 20,000-seat building was ready and waiting when the franchise arrived, and it has hosted playoff runs, concerts, and championship celebrations ever since. It sits at the heart of the entertainment district that has grown up around it, including the Toshiba Plaza gathering space out front.

Part of what makes the team special is the game-night experience. The Golden Knights built a reputation early for one of the best pregame shows in all of sports, complete with a medieval theme, a live drum line, and a golden knight who battles opponents on the ice before the puck drops. Chance the Gila Monster, the team mascot, has become a local celebrity in his own right. Those touches turned a new team into a beloved one, and they are a big reason home games feel like an event rather than just a match.

Now the franchise is stepping into its 10th year. The "Season X" branding leans into that anniversary, and the team has already teased new marks and merchandise around the milestone. Reaching a decade in a market that once had no major league team of its own is a genuine achievement, and the 2026-27 schedule is the roadmap for that celebration.

What Happens Next

The next step for fans is simple. Lock in the dates that matter and plan around them. Training camp and preseason games come first in September, then the regular season tips off with the September 29 home opener against the Chicago Blackhawks. From there the 84-game slate runs through the fall and winter and into the spring.

Ticket demand tends to spike right after a schedule release. The home opener and the December 21 rematch with the Carolina Hurricanes will be among the hottest tickets of the year, so fans who want those seats should not wait too long. Season ticket packages, single-game sales, and resale markets all move quickly once the marquee dates are public.

It helps to think ahead about the calendar as a whole. Home games cluster during certain weeks, and holiday-season dates tend to draw the biggest crowds and the highest prices. If you are shopping for value, midweek games against less marquee opponents are usually easier on the wallet than weekend nights or a Final rematch. Mapping out two or three dates that fit your budget now, before demand builds, is the smart way to lock in the games you actually want to see. And because this is the first 84-game season, there are simply more nights to choose from than in any year before.

Beyond the games, expect the team to roll out Season X promotions, giveaways, and anniversary events throughout the year. The valley already got a preview when mystery billboards teased the 10th-anniversary logo before the schedule drop. Watch for themed nights, special jerseys, and community events tied to the milestone as the season unfolds.

It is also worth watching how the two extra games in the 84-game format shake out over a long season. More games can mean more travel and a heavier grind on the roster, but for fans it simply means more chances to see the team live. Local sports bars, arena vendors, and nearby restaurants will feel those two added home nights as well. As the calendar fills in with promotions and giveaways, the schedule that dropped on July 16 becomes the backbone of the entire 10th-season celebration.

What You Can Do

If you want to be part of Season X, start with the schedule. Head to the Vegas Golden Knights official site to view every home and road date, then mark the games you care about. The September 29 opener against the Chicago Blackhawks and the December 21 rematch with the Carolina Hurricanes are the two big ones to grab early.

For tickets, check the team's official channels first for single-game sales and season packages, and give yourself options on price and date. If you have kids who are curious about the sport, the west side is full of ways to get involved. City National Arena in Summerlin, the Golden Knights practice home, offers public skating and youth programs, and the Henderson Silver Knights, the team's American Hockey League affiliate, play a more budget-friendly brand of pro hockey at Lifeguard Arena in Henderson.

If you cannot make it to the arena, you can still be part of the fun. Road games and many home games are easy to catch on TV or a stream, and local bars around the valley host watch parties where fans gather in gold. For the biggest nights, like the opener and the December 21 rematch, those watch parties can be almost as loud as the building itself. It is a low-cost way to enjoy the season with other fans, especially for families watching the budget.

Finally, plan your game nights like a local. Leave early, know your route to T-Mobile Arena, and consider a rideshare or a spot to eat nearby so you are not fighting for parking. A little planning turns a busy Strip night into an easy one, and it lets you focus on the reason you came, which is the hockey. Whether you go to one game or twenty, Season X is shaping up to be a big year for Las Vegas, and now you have the schedule to make the most of it.
