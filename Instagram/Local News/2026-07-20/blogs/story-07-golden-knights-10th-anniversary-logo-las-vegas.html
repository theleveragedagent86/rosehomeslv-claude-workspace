Golden Knights Unveil Season X 10th-Anniversary Logo After Mystery Billboards Across the Las Vegas Valley

The Vegas Golden Knights ended days of guessing on Thursday, July 16, 2026, when they revealed that the gold symbol popping up on billboards all over the valley was the team's new 10th-anniversary logo. The mark is called "Season X," and it celebrates the franchise entering its 10th season on the ice.

For weeks, drivers across Clark County kept spotting the same nondescript gold symbol on digital boards and resort marquees, with no team name and no words. It turned out to be a slow-burn teaser for the milestone logo and the release of the 2026-27 schedule. Now that the mystery is solved, the new mark will show up on jerseys, merch, and marketing all season long.

If you saw one of those gold billboards on your commute and wondered what it meant, you were not alone. The reveal was a payoff for the whole valley, from the Strip to the suburbs, and it kicked off the buildup to the team's biggest anniversary yet. Here is a full breakdown of what the Season X logo is, how the reveal played out, and why it matters for anyone who calls Las Vegas home.

What Happened

On the morning of Thursday, July 16, 2026, the Vegas Golden Knights confirmed that the mystery gold symbol was their Season X logo. The reveal came from the team's website and social media channels, and it landed on the same day the club dropped its 2026-27 regular season schedule. Fans who had been trading theories online finally got their answer.

The logo itself is packed with meaning. It centers the Golden Knights crest, the team's helmet mark, inside a large Roman numeral "X." A sword slices through the helmet and the X, a nod to the sword that already appears on the team's secondary logo. The X stands for 10, marking the 10th season.

Two numbers flank the design. The "17" points back to 2017, when the Golden Knights played their very first game as an expansion team. The "27" points ahead to 2027, the year the 10th season wraps up. Together they frame the full decade the team is celebrating. The reveal carried the tagline "History continues," pointing fans to the 2026-27 schedule and ticket memberships at the team's website.

The teaser campaign leaned on some of the biggest screens in the city. Digital billboards and marquees at spots like Park MGM and Fontainebleau Las Vegas showed the plain gold symbol along with the date "07.16.2026." That date was the payoff, the day everything was explained. By tying the countdown to real locations people drive past every day, the team turned a simple logo drop into a citywide guessing game.

The guessing was half the fun. Local news stations, sports blogs, and everyday fans traded theories about what the gold symbol could mean. Many people landed on the right answer before the official reveal, guessing it was tied to the Golden Knights and their upcoming season. That shared curiosity is exactly what the team was after, and it built real momentum heading into the schedule release.

It is worth noting what did not change. The Season X mark is an anniversary logo, not a full rebrand. The team's primary logo, the gold knight's helmet, stays in place, and the colors stay the same. The Season X design is a special mark for this one milestone year, layered on top of the identity fans already know. That is a common move in pro sports, where teams add a commemorative patch or logo for a big anniversary without overhauling their whole look.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

The Vegas Golden Knights are more than a hockey team here. Since 2017, they have become a piece of the city's identity, and a 10th-anniversary logo is a reminder of how far both the team and the town have come together. For a lot of families, VGK was the first major pro team they could call their own, and this milestone hits that emotional note.

There is a practical side too. A fresh anniversary mark means new gear. Expect the Season X logo on shirts, hats, patches, and other merch throughout the year. Fans who like to collect team items now have a reason to shop, and the anniversary branding tends to sell fast because it only lasts one season.

The buzz also spills into daily life around T-Mobile Arena on the Strip. Golden Knights nights bring huge crowds, packed restaurants, and busy rideshare lines in that part of town. When the team leans into a big season like this one, the energy around game days tends to grow, and neighborhoods near the arena feel it in traffic and foot traffic.

For people who live in Summerlin, this milestone matters in another way. City National Arena, the team's official practice facility, sits in Downtown Summerlin. It draws fans year round for open practices, youth programs, and community events. A milestone season keeps that spotlight on the west side and on the local hockey scene that has grown up around the team.

The team's reach goes beyond the two Knights rinks. The Golden Knights helped grow hockey across the whole region, from youth leagues to public skate sessions. Kids who were toddlers when the team started in 2017 are now old enough to play in local tournaments. A 10th-anniversary season is a natural moment to celebrate that growth, and it gives parents a reason to get their families more involved in the sport close to home.

There is also a simple sense of pride at stake. Las Vegas spent decades without a major pro team, and some doubted the city could support one. The Golden Knights proved those doubts wrong fast. A milestone logo is a chance for longtime fans to look back on that journey, and for newer residents to feel like they are part of a story that is still being written. That kind of shared identity is one of the things that makes a growing city feel like a real community.

Local businesses feel it too. Bars, restaurants, and shops near the Strip lean on Golden Knights nights for a steady stream of customers all winter. A milestone season with extra marketing and extra home games can mean more busy nights for those small businesses. When the team wins and the buzz is high, that energy ripples out to the people who work and own shops around the arena district.

Background and History

The Vegas Golden Knights joined the National Hockey League as an expansion team in 2017. Most people expected a new team to struggle for years. Instead, the Golden Knights shocked the sport by reaching the Stanley Cup Final in their very first season. That run turned casual locals into die-hard fans almost overnight.

The team kept winning after that. VGK made the playoffs again and again, and in 2023 they captured the Stanley Cup for the first time, cementing their place as one of the league's strongest young franchises. For a club that only started in 2017, reaching that peak so quickly is rare, and it is a big part of why the 10th season feels like such a milestone.

Branding has always been part of the team's appeal. The gold and steel gray colors, the knight's helmet, and the sword all give the Golden Knights a look that stands out across the league. The Season X logo builds on that identity rather than replacing it. The helmet crest stays front and center, and the sword ties the new mark to the team's existing secondary logo.

Teaser campaigns like this one are not new for VGK, but this rollout stood out. Using blank gold billboards with only a date forced people to talk, guess, and share. By the time the reveal came, plenty of fans had already figured out it was tied to the 10th season, which made the payoff feel like a shared community moment.

The timing was smart too. Summer is usually the quiet stretch for hockey, with the season over and training camp still weeks away. By dropping the mystery campaign in mid-July, the Golden Knights gave fans something to buzz about during the slow months and set the stage for the schedule release on the very same day. It kept the team in the local conversation at a time when many sports fans are focused elsewhere.

What Happens Next

The Season X logo arrives right as the Golden Knights gear up for the 2026-27 season, the franchise's 10th. The team released its full schedule on the same July 16 date as the reveal. VGK opens at home against the Chicago Blackhawks on Tuesday, September 29 at T-Mobile Arena, with puck drop set for 7:30 p.m. Pacific time.

This season also brings a league-wide change. The 2026-27 campaign is the first NHL season with an 84-game schedule, up from the long-standing 82 games. That means two extra dates for fans to circle, and one marquee night already stands out. A rematch of the 2026 Stanley Cup Final is set for December 21, when the Carolina Hurricanes visit Las Vegas.

From here, look for the anniversary logo to spread everywhere. It will likely appear on team apparel, arena signage, in-game graphics, and social media all year. Special anniversary nights, throwback moments, and fan giveaways often come with milestone seasons, so fans should watch the team's channels for those announcements as the opener gets closer.

The schedule gives fans plenty of nights to plan around. Beyond the September 29 home opener and the December 21 rematch with the Carolina Hurricanes, an 84-game season means more home dates at T-Mobile Arena to work into fall and winter plans. For a lot of local families, VGK home games are a standing tradition, and the anniversary branding will be front and center at every one of them. Season ticket members and single-game buyers alike can expect the Season X look woven throughout the fan experience.
