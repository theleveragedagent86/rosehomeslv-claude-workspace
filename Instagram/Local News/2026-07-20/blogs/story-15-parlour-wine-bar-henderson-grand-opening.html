Parlour Wine Bar Plans a Grand Opening Weekend in Henderson

Henderson is getting the cozy, chef-driven wine bar that locals have been asking for. Parlour Wine Bar and Eatery, a new spot from chef Antonio Nunez, is planning a grand opening weekend from July 23 through July 26, 2026, complete with a red carpet entrance, welcome champagne, guest performances, and giveaways.

The bar has been in a soft opening through July, giving the kitchen and staff time to settle in before the big weekend. Parlour pairs curated wines with small plates built around the chef's own cooking, and it is open daily from 5 to 10 p.m. If you have wanted a relaxed date-night place in Henderson that is not a chain and not the Strip, this is aimed right at you.

What Happened

Parlour Wine Bar and Eatery is a new independent wine bar in Henderson led by chef Antonio Nunez. The concept is simple and intentional. It pairs a curated wine list with chef-driven small plates in an intimate setting. This is the kind of place built for lingering over a glass and a few shared dishes, not rushing through a big meal.

The news was reported by the Las Vegas dining outlet Food and Loathing on July 7, 2026. According to that report, Parlour was in a soft opening during July. A soft opening is a quiet trial run. The doors are open and guests can come in, but the business has not made a big public push yet. It gives the team time to test the menu, train staff, and work out the small problems before a formal launch.

The formal launch is the part to circle on your calendar. Parlour has planned a grand opening weekend from July 23 through July 26, 2026. The reported plans include a red carpet entrance, welcome champagne for guests, guest performances, and giveaways across the weekend. That is a full four days of celebration, not just a single ribbon-cutting night.

Day to day, Parlour is open from 5 to 10 p.m. Those evening-only hours tell you a lot about the concept. This is a place for after-work drinks, a slow dinner of small plates, or a nightcap. The pairing of curated wines with chef-driven food is the heart of it. You are meant to order a few plates, try a few pours, and let the night unfold. Exact street address and menu pricing were not detailed in the initial report, so check Parlour's own channels before you head over.

The word "eatery" in the name is a small but telling detail. This is not only a place to drink. The kitchen is meant to carry equal weight with the wine list. A chef-driven small plates menu usually means shareable dishes that change with the seasons and let the kitchen play with fresh ideas. For guests, that format is forgiving. You can build a light snack with one plate and a glass, or turn it into a full dinner by ordering several plates for the table. It fits a first date, a catch-up with friends, or a quiet solo evening at the bar.

Background and History

Parlour is not opening into a quiet dining scene. It is arriving during a real wave of new food and drink concepts across Henderson. The city has been one of the busiest corners of the valley for restaurant openings, and 2026 has kept that pace going.

Just this summer, celebrity chef Emeril Lagasse opened Meril at the M Resort on St. Rose Parkway, his first restaurant located off the Las Vegas Strip. The Los Angeles burger institution Irv's Burgers opened at Green Valley Ranch, its third valley location. And on Water Street in downtown Henderson, The Coffee Class is building a three-story flagship with a coffee shop, a bakery, a cocktail bar, and a rooftop. Parlour joins that group as the intimate, wine-focused option.

Wine bars fill a specific gap in a growing city. They are smaller and more personal than a big restaurant. They lean on curated lists and knowledgeable staff rather than a huge menu. The chef-driven small plates format lets a kitchen show off without committing guests to a long, heavy dinner. That combination has become popular in cities across the country, and Henderson's growth makes it a natural fit here.

For a long time, the valley's most talked-about wine bars and small-plates spots lived on or near the Strip, or in the Arts District downtown. Henderson residents watched those openings from a distance and made the drive when they wanted something special. The recent wave of local openings flips that script. Instead of driving 20 or 30 minutes for a nice evening out, more Henderson households can now find it a few minutes from home. Parlour is part of that shift, and it reflects how much the suburb has matured as a dining destination in its own right.

The soft opening approach that Parlour used is also worth understanding. New restaurants often open quietly first, then hold a grand opening once the team is confident. It protects the guest experience during those first shaky weeks. By the time the red carpet rolls out on July 23, the staff will have already had a few weeks of real service under their belts.

Henderson itself gives that plan a strong base to build on. The city is Nevada's second-largest, home to hundreds of thousands of residents across master-planned communities like Green Valley, Anthem, Inspirada, and Cadence. Many of those households have the income and the appetite for a night out close to home. A wine bar that opens here is not gambling on foot traffic from tourists. It is serving neighbors, and neighbors who like a place come back. That repeat business is what keeps a small, independent spot open past its first year, which is the hardest stretch for any new restaurant.
