The Coffee Class Building Three-Story Flagship on Henderson's Water Street

Downtown Henderson just got a coffee shop unlike anything else on Water Street. The Coffee Class has opened a three-story flagship at 37 South Water Street, with a coffee house and bakery on the ground floor, a cocktail and whiskey bar on the second floor, and a rooftop built for weekend brunch and events. It is a big bet on downtown Henderson, and it signals that the Water Street district is turning into a real destination.

The new location is the sixth in the Las Vegas Valley for entrepreneur Kyle Cunningham, who runs both the Coffee Class and Almond & Oat brands. At more than 6,000 square feet spread over three levels, this is the largest and most ambitious project in the company's history. Here is what it means for Henderson residents, the Water Street corridor, and anyone watching downtown grow.

What Happened

The Coffee Class opened its flagship store at 37 South Water Street in downtown Henderson. The building rises three stories and covers more than 6,000 square feet, which makes it far bigger than a typical neighborhood coffee shop. The company calls it the next stage of growth, and it is easy to see why. This is not a small cafe. It is a full food and drink destination under one roof.

The ground floor is the heart of the operation. It works as a coffee house, bakery, and cafe all at once. The menu covers espresso drinks, scratch-made baked goods, and savory lunch and brunch dishes. So you can grab a latte and a pastry in the morning, then come back at noon for a real meal. The baking is done in house, which is a step above what most coffee chains offer.

The second floor is where things get interesting. It holds a cocktail and whiskey bar with a more intimate, speakeasy-style feel. This part opened after the ground floor coffee house, giving the building a nighttime draw to go with its daytime coffee crowd. A coffee shop that turns into a whiskey bar after dark is a smart way to keep the doors busy from morning to close.

The third level is a rooftop. The company designed it for weekend brunch, seasonal gatherings, and private events, with a full bar available on weekends. A rooftop in downtown Henderson is a rare thing, and it gives the flagship a view and a vibe that most valley coffee shops simply cannot match. The design across all three floors leans on wood tones and industrial-chic touches, so the look stays consistent from the sidewalk to the roof.

Putting all of that under one roof is the part that stands out. Most coffee shops pick a lane and stay in it. This flagship tries to be a morning coffee stop, a lunch spot, a nighttime bar, and an event venue all at once. That is a demanding model to run, but it also means the building can earn its keep at almost any hour. For a downtown that has struggled to stay busy after 5 p.m., that around-the-clock energy is a big deal.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

For Henderson residents, this is a quality-of-life win. Downtown Henderson has spent years trying to shake its sleepy reputation, and a three-story coffee and cocktail spot gives locals a real reason to walk Water Street. You now have one place where you can meet a friend for coffee, hold a work session in the afternoon, and grab a whiskey at night. That kind of all-day, all-night draw is exactly what a downtown needs to feel alive.

It also matters for the small-business feel of the district. The Coffee Class is a local brand built here in the valley, not a national chain dropping in a cookie-cutter box. Cunningham has grown the company to six locations across the Coffee Class and Almond & Oat names, and putting the flagship in Henderson says a lot about where he sees the city heading. Local money staying local is good news for the neighborhood.

For families and remote workers, the space fills a gap. Henderson has plenty of drive-through coffee, but sit-down spots with room to linger, work, or gather are harder to find. A 6,000-square-foot ground floor gives people space to spread out. The rooftop and event areas also give residents a nearby option for showers, birthdays, and small parties without driving to the Strip or Green Valley.

There is a housing angle too. Walkable amenities like this one raise the appeal of nearby homes and condos. When buyers ask me what makes a neighborhood desirable, walk-to coffee and nightlife come up again and again. A flagship coffee house on Water Street is the kind of amenity that helps downtown Henderson compete with newer master-planned areas for young buyers and downsizers alike.

Henderson has long been ranked among the safest and most livable cities in the country, and its dining and nightlife scene has grown fast over the past few years. A three-story coffee and cocktail flagship adds to that story. It gives residents one more reason to stay in town for a night out instead of driving to the Strip. Every dollar that stays in Henderson helps local jobs, local sales tax, and the small businesses that make a downtown feel like a community rather than a strip mall.

Background and History

The Coffee Class started as a local specialty coffee and bakery bistro concept and grew steadily across the Las Vegas Valley. Under Kyle Cunningham, the company expanded to multiple locations, adding the Almond & Oat brand along the way. The Water Street flagship represents the sixth valley location and the most complex one the company has ever built. Going from a single coffee counter to a three-story flagship is a major leap for any local operator.

Water Street itself has a long history as the original heart of Henderson. Decades ago, it was the main commercial strip for a young industrial town. Over the years, as growth pushed south and west toward Green Valley, Anthem, and Inspirada, the old downtown lost some of its shine. City leaders have worked for years to bring it back with streetscape upgrades, events, and incentives for new businesses.

That revival has picked up speed lately. Restaurants, bars, and shops have slowly filled in storefronts along Water Street, and the city has leaned into festivals and public gatherings downtown. A project as visible as a three-story coffee flagship fits right into that push. It is the kind of anchor that draws foot traffic and gives other operators the confidence to open nearby.

The flagship rolled out in phases. The ground-floor coffee house opened first in early 2026, the second-floor whiskey bar followed in the spring, and the rooftop and event program came together after that. Building a three-story concept is a big undertaking, so a staged opening let the team get each floor right before moving up to the next one.

The choice of Water Street also fits a larger pattern across the valley. Local operators have been betting on older, walkable cores instead of only chasing new suburban pads. The Arts District in Las Vegas has boomed on the same idea, filling old buildings with coffee shops, bars, and restaurants that draw people in on foot. Henderson's Water Street has the bones for the same kind of comeback, with wide sidewalks, historic buildings, and a central location. A flagship of this size is a strong signal that operators believe the district is ready.

What Happens Next

With all three floors coming online, the next chapter is about building routines. Coffee shops live and die on regulars, and the Water Street flagship will spend the coming months turning first-time visitors into daily customers. Watch for the rooftop event calendar to fill up as word spreads, especially heading into the cooler fall and winter months when a rooftop in Southern Nevada is at its best.

Expect the flagship to become a hub for downtown Henderson events. Water Street already hosts festivals, markets, and community gatherings, and a venue with a full rooftop bar is a natural partner for that programming. If the city keeps pushing downtown activity, this location is set up to benefit and to help draw crowds that spill into nearby shops and restaurants.

There is also the ripple effect on jobs. A three-story operation with a coffee house, a bakery, a full bar, and an event program needs baristas, bakers, cooks, bartenders, and event staff. That means steady local hiring in the heart of Henderson, and those workers often live and spend nearby. For a downtown trying to build momentum, new jobs and new payroll are just as important as the foot traffic they bring.

The bigger question is what this means for the rest of Water Street. A successful three-story flagship could pull more operators into downtown, from restaurants to boutiques to more nightlife. If the coffee-by-day, cocktails-by-night model works here, do not be surprised to see other local brands follow with their own multi-use concepts. Success tends to attract company.

Keep an eye on how the flagship handles the summer heat and the shift into the fall. A rooftop is a tough sell in July, when valley temperatures push well past 100 degrees, but it becomes a prized seat from October through spring. The team will likely lean on the shaded ground floor and indoor bar during the hottest months, then push rooftop programming hard once the weather turns. How well they balance those seasons will shape how the location performs in its first full year.
