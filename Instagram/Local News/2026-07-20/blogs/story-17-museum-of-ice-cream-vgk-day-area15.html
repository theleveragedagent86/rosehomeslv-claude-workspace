Museum of Ice Cream Hosts "VGK Day" at AREA15 in Las Vegas

The Museum of Ice Cream threw a one-day "VGK Day" party for the Vegas Golden Knights on Monday, July 13, 2026, at its brand-new AREA15 location just west of the Strip. It mixed hockey and ice cream in a way that only Las Vegas could pull off.

The big draw was a new hockey-themed mini-golf attraction called "Fortress Feast," where guests putted with real hockey sticks. Chance, the Golden Knights mascot, and members of the Vegas Vivas were on hand for meet-and-greets and photos. For hockey parents looking for a fun summer day with the kids, this was the sweetest ticket in town.

What Happened

The Museum of Ice Cream turned its newest attraction into a Vegas Golden Knights celebration for a single day. The event ran on Monday, July 13, 2026, at the museum's AREA15 home, which sits at 3072 Rigel Avenue in Las Vegas. The location only opened its doors on July 3, so VGK Day landed during the museum's very first weeks in the valley.

The centerpiece was "Fortress Feast." This is a co-branded, hockey-themed mini-golf course built with the Golden Knights in mind. Instead of a standard putter, guests grabbed a hockey stick and worked their way through the holes. The name plays off the team's "fortress" nickname for its home ice, and it tied the putting game right back to the sport fans love.

Star power showed up too. Chance, the official Vegas Golden Knights mascot, was expected for meet-and-greets throughout the day. Members of the Vegas Vivas, the team's entertainment and dance crew, were also set to join for photos and fan moments. Families could snap pictures, say hello, and soak up the team spirit without ever leaving the air conditioning.

The event was first reported by FOX5 Vegas on July 8, 2026, a few days before the party. VGK Day gave the new museum a splashy reason to bring in local families and hockey fans during the slow, hot stretch of summer when many people are looking for indoor things to do.

The timing was smart on its own. Mid-July is a quiet window for hockey, with the season months away and the playoffs long over. By putting the Golden Knights front and center at a fresh attraction, the museum kept the team on people's minds during the off-season. It also gave lifelong fans and brand-new ones a low-key way to connect with the club away from the ice. You did not need a game ticket or a jersey to feel like part of the team for a day.

Background and History

The Museum of Ice Cream started as a pop-up in New York back in 2016 and grew into a chain of permanent, Instagram-friendly attractions across the country. The Las Vegas spot is the biggest one yet. It spans about 30,000 square feet and packs in 14 immersive installations built around dessert, color, and play.

The highlights are hard to miss. There is the Golden Scoop Buffet, billed as the world's largest all-you-can-eat ice cream buffet with more than 20 flavors. There is the famous Iconic Sprinkle Pool, complete with two-story slides. There is even an ice cream tasting magic show called Big Top Taste. The whole place is designed to be walked through, tasted, and photographed.

That photo-first design is a big part of why the museum chose Las Vegas for its largest site. This is a city where people already come to celebrate, dress up, and share every moment online. An attraction built around bright colors and playful rooms fits right in. Layering a Golden Knights theme on top only sharpens that local appeal, since VGK gear and colors are already a familiar sight around the valley.

The Vegas Golden Knights have their own strong roots. The team joined the National Hockey League in 2017 as an expansion club and shocked the sports world by reaching the Stanley Cup Final in its very first season. They play at T-Mobile Arena on the Strip and won the Stanley Cup in 2023. That deep, fast bond between the team and the city is exactly why a mini-golf course themed around the Golden Knights makes sense at a brand-new ice cream museum.

Pairing the two is a classic Las Vegas move. This is a city built on mash-ups and spectacle, where a dessert museum and a hockey team can share a marquee for a day and nobody blinks. VGK Day is a small example of a much bigger pattern in how local brands team up to draw a crowd.

Mascot and entertainment crews like Chance and the Vegas Vivas have become community fixtures in their own right. They show up at schools, charity drives, youth hockey games, and family festivals across Clark County all year long. Bringing them to a new attraction is a natural fit, because fans already see them as friendly faces of the team. That kind of goodwill is hard to buy and takes years to build, and the Golden Knights have leaned into it since day one.
