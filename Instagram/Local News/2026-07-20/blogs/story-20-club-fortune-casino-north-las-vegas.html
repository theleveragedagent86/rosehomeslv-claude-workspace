Club Fortune Casino Sets July Opening Dates in North Las Vegas

The old Poker Palace in North Las Vegas is coming back to life. Club Fortune North, the renamed and remodeled version of that longtime locals casino, set a soft opening for July 19, 2026 and a full opening for July 22, 2026. New owner Truckee Gaming bought the property in October 2025 for about $20 million and closed it for a full remodel.

This matters to North Las Vegas because a neighborhood casino is more than a place to play slots. It is a job center, a spot to grab a meal, and a familiar hangout for people who have lived near Nellis Air Force Base for years. For nearly a year the building sat dark. Now it reopens under a new name with a fresh look.

There is one big change longtime regulars will notice right away. The remodeled casino will not have a poker room or table games. Instead it leans into slots and a new race and sportsbook run by Boomer's Sportsbook. Below is what happened, why it matters to your part of the valley, and what to watch as the doors open this week.

What Happened

Truckee Gaming, a Nevada gaming company, purchased the former Poker Palace in October 2025 for roughly $20 million. The company then shut the property down and started a full renovation. The plan all along was to reopen it under the Club Fortune name, tying it to the operator's existing local casino brand in the valley.

The reopening comes in two steps. A soft opening is set for Sunday, July 19, 2026. A soft opening lets the staff test the floor, the machines, the food service, and the systems with real guests before the big day. The full grand opening follows on Wednesday, July 22, 2026, when the casino runs at full speed.

The biggest change is what the new casino leaves out. The Poker Palace was known for its poker room. Club Fortune North will not have a poker room, and it will not offer table games like blackjack or craps either. That is a clear shift away from the property's old identity and toward a slots and sports focus.

In place of the card tables, the new owners are adding a race and sportsbook operated by Boomer's Sportsbook. A race and sportsbook is where guests can bet on horse racing and on sports like football, basketball, and baseball while they watch games on screens. This gives North Las Vegas locals a new spot to place a bet without driving to the Strip or to a bigger resort. The remodel also refreshed the gaming floor and the look of the building after years of wear.

The timing is worth noting. The soft opening on July 19 lands right in the middle of summer, a slower stretch for the valley before football season kicks in. That gives the staff time to settle in before the fall rush, when a fresh sportsbook usually draws its biggest crowds. By the time football weekends arrive, Club Fortune North should be running smoothly and ready to compete for local bettors.

It is also a fairly quick turnaround for a project this size. The property changed hands in October 2025 and reopens in July 2026, which is under a year from purchase to grand opening. Buying an aging casino, gutting and updating it, and relaunching it under a new brand in that window shows the new owners moved with purpose. For a corner of North Las Vegas that watched the building sit closed, the wait is nearly over.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

For people who live in North Las Vegas, a reopened casino is real local news. Neighborhood casinos, often called locals casinos, are built for the people who live nearby, not for tourists. They usually offer cheaper food, looser slot deals, and a friendly place to meet neighbors. When one closes, the area loses a gathering spot. When one reopens, that spot comes back.

Jobs are a big piece of this. A casino needs dealers for its machines, cage workers, cooks, servers, cleaners, security, and managers. When Club Fortune North opens fully on July 22, it brings paychecks back to a corner of the valley that leaned on the Poker Palace for work over many years. For families near Nellis Air Force Base and along North Las Vegas Boulevard, those local jobs stay close to home.

There is a housing angle too, and it is one I watch closely as a local agent. Buyers and renters care about what sits near a home. An empty, closed building can make a block feel tired and can weigh on nearby home values. A clean, busy, remodeled business can do the opposite. It brings foot traffic, lights, and a sense that the area is moving forward again.

The new sportsbook adds another draw. Sports betting is popular across Southern Nevada, and having a local book run by Boomer's Sportsbook means North Las Vegas residents can watch games and place bets without a long drive. That keeps entertainment dollars in the neighborhood instead of sending them to the Strip. For a part of the valley that often gets overlooked, that is a small but real win.

There is a trade-off for some longtime guests, and it is fair to name it. Losing the poker room and table games means the people who loved sitting down for a hand of cards will have to go elsewhere. Poker players are loyal, and the Poker Palace name carried real meaning for that crowd. The new owners are betting that a slots and sports focus will bring in more people overall, even if it turns away some of the old card room regulars.

For everyday residents, the simplest impact is having a familiar place open again. When a business on your route to work or school sits empty for months, the whole drive feels a little more run down. When it lights back up with cars in the lot and staff at the door, the block feels alive again. That kind of everyday change adds up for the people who live and drive nearby.

Background and History

The Poker Palace was one of the oldest gaming spots in North Las Vegas. Records tie the site at 2757 North Las Vegas Boulevard back to the early 1950s, and the casino grew over the decades into a true locals hangout near Nellis Air Force Base. Generations of North Las Vegas residents knew it as a low-key place to play cards and slots close to home.

Like many older, smaller casinos, the Poker Palace faced pressure over the years. Big resort brands, newer locals casinos, and changing habits made it harder for aging properties to keep up. The building needed work, and the old model of a card-room-first casino no longer fit the way many people gamble today, with slots and sports betting pulling much of the crowd.

That is where Truckee Gaming stepped in. The company already runs the Club Fortune brand in the valley, so buying the North Las Vegas property and reflagging it as Club Fortune North fit its playbook. Paying about $20 million and then closing the doors for a full remodel was a bet that the site still had life if it was updated for how people play now.

What Happens Next

The next few days are the big ones. The soft opening on July 19 is the test run, so expect the crowd to be smaller and the pace to be slower while staff work out any early issues. The full opening on July 22 is when Club Fortune North truly launches, with the whole gaming floor and the Boomer's Sportsbook up and running.

After opening week, the questions become simple ones about daily life. Will the food be a good deal? Will the slots feel fresh? Will the sportsbook pull in a steady crowd on game nights? Locals casinos live or die on regulars, so the early weeks will show whether North Las Vegas residents make Club Fortune North part of their routine.

The bigger test comes with football season in the fall. That is when a race and sportsbook proves its worth, since game days fill the seats and drive the biggest crowds of the year. If Boomer's Sportsbook can turn Club Fortune North into a go-to spot for local fans, it will lock in the kind of steady traffic that keeps a neighborhood casino healthy for the long run.

It is also worth watching how the property fits into the wider North Las Vegas growth story. The area is adding new homes at places like Tule Springs, and more rooftops usually mean more demand for nearby dining, gaming, and gathering spots. A busy, well-run casino on North Las Vegas Boulevard could benefit from that growth and give the corridor another anchor.

Keep an eye on the little details that signal how the launch is going. Things like hours, food and drink deals, promotions for the sportsbook, and whether the lot stays full on weekends will tell the real story. Locals casinos build their reputations on value and consistency, not on flash. If Club Fortune North nails those basics, word will spread fast among North Las Vegas regulars.
