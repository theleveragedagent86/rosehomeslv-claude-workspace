Henderson Home Prices Cool Slightly Year Over Year in 2026

Henderson home prices are drifting down. As of July 2026, the median list price in Henderson sits in the range of roughly $535,000 to $555,000, which is down about 3% from a year earlier. Homes are also taking longer to sell, spending a median of about 67 days on the market.

That is a real shift from the fast, bidding-war pace many people remember. It does not mean prices are crashing. It means the market is calming down and, for the first time in a while, buyers have a little more room to breathe. If you have been waiting on the sidelines in Henderson, this is the kind of news worth understanding.

What the Numbers Show

Here is the simple version. Henderson home prices are down a few percentage points compared to last summer, and homes are sitting on the market longer before they sell. According to Redfin market data pulled in July 2026, the median list price in Henderson is somewhere around $535,000 to $555,000. That is a dip of about 3% year over year.

The price per square foot tells the same story. It is now near $269, and that number is also down from a year ago. When both the total price and the price per square foot slip at the same time, it is a clear sign that sellers are adjusting their expectations. They are no longer able to name any number they want and expect a line of buyers at the door.

Days on market may be the most important number here. Homes in Henderson are now taking a median of about 67 days to sell. A couple of years ago, well-priced homes were going under contract in days, sometimes hours. When homes take more than two months to sell, sellers start offering price cuts, covering closing costs, and paying for repairs. That is a meaningful change in who holds the power.

It is worth noting that different sources report slightly different figures depending on whether they track list prices or sold prices, and whether they count single-family homes only or all property types. Some reports show the median closer to $540,000 and days on market closer to 57. The exact number moves around, but the direction is the same across the board. Prices are softening and the pace has slowed.

There is another number that matters just as much as the price, and that is the gap between list price and sold price. In a red-hot market, homes sell for more than the asking price. In a balanced or cooling market, they sell for a little under. Right now in Henderson, more homes are closing below their original list price, and a growing share of active listings have already taken at least one price cut. That is exactly what you would expect when homes are sitting on the market for two months or more.

Put all of it together and the picture is easy to read. Prices are down a few percent, price per square foot is down, homes are taking longer to sell, and sellers are cutting prices more often. None of those signals is dramatic on its own. Stacked together, they tell you the same thing: Henderson has shifted from a strong seller's market toward something much closer to balanced.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Henderson is one of the most popular places to live in all of Southern Nevada. It is the second largest city in the state, with well-planned neighborhoods, strong schools, and easy access to the Strip without living on top of it. When Henderson prices move, a lot of local families feel it. So this cooling matters to almost everyone thinking about a move.

If you are a buyer, this is good news. For the last several years, buying in Henderson meant competing against multiple offers, waiving inspections, and paying over asking price just to have a chance. A slower market flips that. You get time to think. You get time to schedule an inspection and actually use it. You can ask a seller to fix things or lower the price, and they are far more likely to say yes now than they were in 2022 or 2023.

If you are a seller, this is not a reason to panic, but it is a reason to be realistic. Your neighbor who sold two summers ago got a different market than the one you are in today. Homes that are priced right and show well are still selling. Homes that are overpriced are sitting, collecting days on market, and eventually cutting their price anyway. The lesson is to price it correctly from day one instead of chasing the market down.

For renters and first-time buyers, a cooler market plus longer days on market can be the opening you have been waiting for. Less competition means you are not forced to make a rushed decision on the biggest purchase of your life. Pair that with down payment help programs, and a Henderson home may be more within reach than it felt a year ago.

Commuters and growing families have reason to pay attention too. When homes sell in a day, you rarely get to be picky about location, layout, or condition. A slower market lets you hold out for the home near the right school, the shorter drive to work, or the extra bedroom you actually need. In a market like this, patience is finally rewarded instead of punished, and that is a real change for anyone who felt boxed out over the last few years.

Background and History

To understand why a 3% dip feels like a big deal, you have to remember where Henderson prices came from. During the pandemic years, prices in Henderson and across Clark County shot up at a pace almost nobody expected. Low mortgage rates, remote work, and buyers moving in from higher-cost and higher-tax states all pushed prices to record highs. Bidding wars became normal, and many buyers felt like they would never catch up.

Then mortgage rates climbed. Higher rates made monthly payments more expensive, which cooled buyer demand. When fewer buyers can afford to compete, homes take longer to sell and price growth slows or reverses. That is the basic force behind the softer numbers we are seeing in Henderson right now. It is less about Henderson losing its shine and more about the math of monthly payments.

It also helps to zoom out and look at the wider Southern Nevada picture. Henderson is cooling slightly at the same time that Las Vegas luxury home prices are still rising fast and Nevada is posting the highest foreclosure rate in the country. Those facts can seem to contradict each other, but they do not. The market is splitting. The high end and the everyday market are moving at different speeds, and headline numbers rarely tell the whole story of any one neighborhood.

Henderson has also added a lot of new supply. Master-planned growth in areas like West Henderson, Cadence, and Inspirada has given buyers more choices than they had a few years ago. More homes for sale, combined with pickier buyers, naturally pushes prices down a little and stretches out how long homes sit before they sell.

It also helps to remember how far prices climbed before this small pullback. A home in many Henderson neighborhoods is still worth far more today than it was five or six years ago, even after this year's dip. A 3% step back does very little to erase the gains longtime owners have built. For most homeowners, the equity they have is still very real, and that matters if they ever want to sell, refinance, or borrow against their home.

Henderson's appeal has not changed either. The city is still known for award-winning parks, strong schools, low crime relative to the region, and quick access to both the Strip and the airport. Those quality-of-life factors are a big part of why homes here have held their value so well over time. A cooling market does not change what makes people want to live in Henderson in the first place. It just changes the terms of the deal.

What Happens Next

Nobody can promise exactly where prices go from here, but a few things are worth watching. The biggest one is mortgage rates. If rates ease even a little, buyer demand tends to pick back up quickly, and that can firm prices right back up. If rates stay high, expect the slower pace and small price dips to continue through the rest of 2026.

Keep an eye on days on market and price cuts. Those two numbers usually move before the median price does. If you start seeing homes sell faster and fewer price reductions, that is an early sign the market is heating up again. If days on market keep climbing, buyers will keep gaining leverage. These are the signals a good local agent tracks every single week.

Also watch inventory. If a wave of new listings hits Henderson this fall, more supply gives buyers even more options and keeps a lid on prices. If listings dry up because sellers decide to wait, low supply can push prices back up even without a jump in demand. The balance between buyers and sellers is what really sets the direction, and right now that balance is closer to even than it has been in years.

Seasonality plays a role too. The Henderson market usually slows a bit heading into the fall and winter, when fewer families want to move in the middle of a school year. That normal seasonal cooldown can layer on top of the current softness and make the second half of 2026 feel even quieter. Smart buyers sometimes use those slower months to find motivated sellers, while sellers who can wait for spring often see more foot traffic. Timing your move to the season can be just as important as timing it to the market.

What You Can Do

If you are thinking about buying in Henderson, start by getting pre-approved so you know your real budget in today's rate environment. Then watch the specific neighborhoods you want, not just the citywide average. Henderson is big, and West Henderson, Green Valley, Anthem, and Inspirada can each behave a little differently. A citywide dip may look very different once you zoom into one community.

If you are thinking about selling, get an honest, current comparison of what similar homes near you have actually sold for in the last 30 to 60 days, not what they listed for last year. Ask about days on market and price cuts in your immediate area. That data will tell you far more than any national headline about whether now is a smart time to list and what number to start at.

Buyers should also take advantage of the tools that make a cooler market even more affordable. Nevada has down payment assistance programs that can hand qualified buyers help toward the upfront cost, and FHA loans in Clark County still allow as little as 3.5% down. When you combine a softer market, longer days on market, and a down payment program, the path to owning in Henderson can look very different than it did during the frenzy years. It is worth running the numbers before you assume you cannot afford it.

One more tip for both buyers and sellers: do not let a single national headline drive a six-figure decision. Reports about foreclosures leading the nation or luxury prices soaring can scare you or excite you, but neither one describes your specific street in Henderson. Local data, pulled for your exact neighborhood and price range, is the only thing that should guide a move this big. Zip codes just a few miles apart can tell completely different stories.
