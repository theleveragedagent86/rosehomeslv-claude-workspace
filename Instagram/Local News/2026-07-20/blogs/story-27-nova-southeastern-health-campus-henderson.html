Nova Southeastern to Open a Health Training Campus in Henderson

Nova Southeastern University is coming to Henderson. The Florida-based university announced a partnership with U.S. Anesthesia Partners-Nevada to open the NSU Health Nevada Regional Campus, and its first group of 26 students will begin a 27-month Certified Anesthesiologist Assistant program.

This is a big deal for Henderson. A new health training campus means skilled jobs, more medical education close to home, and another sign that the city is growing into a real hub for health care. The news appeared in the July 2026 edition of Nevada Business Magazine.

What Happened

Nova Southeastern University, known as NSU, said it will open a health training location in Henderson called the NSU Health Nevada Regional Campus. The campus is the product of a partnership between the university and U.S. Anesthesia Partners-Nevada, a group tied to the anesthesia care field. The two sides are teaming up to bring a full training program to Southern Nevada.

The first program on offer is a Certified Anesthesiologist Assistant program. This is a graduate-level track that runs for 27 months. The very first class will have 26 students. That is a small, focused group, which is common for programs that train people for hands-on medical roles.

A Certified Anesthesiologist Assistant, often shortened to CAA, is a trained health professional who helps deliver anesthesia care. These providers work as part of a care team and practice under the direction of a licensed anesthesiologist. They help keep patients safe and comfortable before, during, and after surgery. It is a demanding role that calls for strong science training and steady hands.

The 27-month timeline is worth noting. That is a little over two years of full-time study. Programs of this length are built to move students from the classroom into real clinical settings step by step. Students usually start with heavy science and lab work, then move into supervised hands-on training in hospitals and surgery centers. By the end, a graduate is ready to sit for national certification and join a care team.

The partnership itself is the engine behind all of this. U.S. Anesthesia Partners is a large anesthesia care organization, and its Nevada arm gives the new campus a direct link to working anesthesia teams. That connection can shape everything from who teaches the courses to where students do their clinical hours. For a brand-new program, that kind of real-world backing is a strong foundation.

The announcement ran in Nevada Business Magazine's July 2026 edition. The exact campus address, the precise start date for the first class, and the tuition figures were not spelled out in that announcement. Those details are the kind of thing prospective students will want to confirm directly with the university before applying. Anyone serious about the program should reach out to Nova Southeastern for the full picture before making plans.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Henderson has spent years working to be more than a bedroom community for the Las Vegas Strip. A new health campus fits that goal. It brings advanced education to the city, and it points to more high-skill jobs down the road. For families already living in Green Valley, Anthem, Inspirada, and Cadence, that is welcome news.

Health care is one of the steadiest job fields in the country. When a program trains anesthesia providers, it is feeding workers into hospitals and surgery centers that always need trained staff. Southern Nevada has a growing and aging population, and that means more surgeries, more clinics, and more demand for skilled medical hands. Training those workers here means fewer of them have to leave the state to learn their craft.

Keeping trained workers in Nevada is a real challenge, and it starts with where people go to school. Students who train out of state often build their lives and careers there, and many never come back. When the training happens locally, graduates are far more likely to stay, take local jobs, buy homes, and raise families here. That is a quiet win for the whole region, and it is one of the strongest arguments for building programs like this one at home.

There is also a simple money angle. Students in a 27-month program live somewhere, eat somewhere, and spend money at local shops. Faculty and staff need housing too. A campus, even a small one, sends a steady flow of people and dollars into the surrounding neighborhoods. Over time, that supports local restaurants, coffee shops, and service businesses in Henderson.

For renters and buyers, campuses tend to firm up nearby housing demand. Graduate students often rent close to where they study, and health professionals who finish training and take local jobs frequently put down roots. That kind of steady, well-paid demand is exactly what keeps a neighborhood stable. It is not a boom, but it is a healthy, lasting kind of growth.

There is a bigger picture here too. For years, Nevada has leaned heavily on tourism and gaming to power the economy. When those industries slow down, the whole state feels it. Adding education and health care jobs helps spread the risk. A more balanced economy is better for everyone, including homeowners, because it means the local job market does not rise and fall on the fortunes of the Strip alone.

Henderson has been a leader in that shift. The city has courted health, tech, and logistics employers, and it has built the roads, parks, and schools that skilled workers look for. A health training campus adds one more reason for young professionals to choose Henderson over other Western cities. Every time that happens, it strengthens the base of buyers and renters who make the local market work.

Background and History

Nova Southeastern University is a large private university based in Fort Lauderdale, Florida. It is well known for its health sciences programs and has trained doctors, dentists, pharmacists, and other health workers for decades. Bringing an NSU-branded campus to Nevada is a sign the school sees real demand in the Southwest.

Certified Anesthesiologist Assistant programs are still fairly rare across the country. They exist in a limited number of states, and Nevada building one is a step forward for the state's medical training scene. The role has grown in recent years as hospitals look for more ways to staff anesthesia care teams safely and efficiently. A program in Henderson puts Nevada on that map.

Henderson itself has been on a long run of growth. The city has added master-planned communities, retail centers, and health facilities over the past two decades. Water Street in the older downtown is seeing new life, and the outer edges keep filling in with new homes. A health training campus is another building block in that story, and it lines up with the city's push to attract education and medical employers.

The tie to U.S. Anesthesia Partners-Nevada matters here too. When a university links arms with a real-world medical group, students often get closer access to clinical training and job pipelines. That kind of partnership can make a program more attractive to applicants, because it hints at a clearer path from classroom to career.

It also helps to understand the demand behind a move like this. The country has faced ongoing shortages of trained medical staff, and anesthesia care is no exception. Surgeries cannot happen safely without trained anesthesia providers on hand. As Southern Nevada's population grows and ages, the number of procedures keeps climbing. Training providers locally is one of the most direct ways to meet that need, and it keeps talent from leaving the state to study elsewhere and never coming back.

Nevada has worked for years to grow its own medical training. The region has added and expanded medical schools, nursing programs, and allied health tracks. Each new program chips away at the state's reliance on out-of-state talent. A Certified Anesthesiologist Assistant program is a specialized piece of that puzzle, and it fills a gap that few other Nevada schools have addressed.

What Happens Next

The next milestone is the first class of 26 students starting the 27-month program. Once that group begins, the campus becomes a working part of Henderson's education landscape. Watch for the university to share a firm start date, a campus location, and application details as the launch gets closer.

If the first class goes well, programs like this often grow. A single track can expand into more health training over time, and class sizes can rise as the campus proves itself. That is the pattern many regional campuses follow. It starts small and focused, then adds programs once the model works.

Residents should also watch for ripple effects nearby. New campuses sometimes spark interest in nearby retail, housing, and services. Property owners and small businesses close to the eventual site may see fresh attention. None of that is guaranteed, but it is the kind of thing worth keeping an eye on as the plans firm up.

It is also fair to keep expectations realistic. A first class of 26 students is a modest start. This is not a giant medical center opening overnight with hundreds of jobs. It is a focused program with room to grow. The smart move is to treat it as a seed. If it takes root and thrives, the effects on jobs and the local economy will build over the coming years rather than all at once.

Keep an eye, too, on how the campus fits into Henderson's wider plans. The city continues to add housing, retail, and health facilities across its footprint. A training campus can complement nearby clinics and hospitals, and it may attract related businesses that want to be close to a source of fresh graduates. Watching how these pieces connect will tell you a lot about where Henderson is headed next.
