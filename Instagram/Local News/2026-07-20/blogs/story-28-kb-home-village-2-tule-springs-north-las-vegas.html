New Homes at Tule Springs: North Las Vegas Advances KB Home's Village 2

North Las Vegas is getting hundreds of new homes, and some of them will start at a price many local families can actually reach. The North Las Vegas Planning Commission approved KB Home's Village 2 inside the Villages at Tule Springs master plan, opening the door for more than 700 new homes near North 5th Street and the 215 Beltway.

This matters because the prices land below the valley's record median. Townhomes in Village 2 are planned to start around $340,000, and detached single family homes range from about $380,000 up to roughly $550,000 depending on the section. For first time buyers who feel priced out of Summerlin and Henderson, this is one of the more attainable new construction options on the map right now.

What Happened

The North Las Vegas Planning Commission signed off on KB Home's Village 2, a large new section within the Villages at Tule Springs community. The site sits in the northern part of the city, close to North 5th Street and the Interstate 215 Beltway. That location puts new residents near a major freeway connection while keeping them on the edge of open desert.

The largest piece of the approval covers 403 lots spread across about 35 acres. Combined with the other sections in the plan, Village 2 is expected to bring more than 700 new homes to this corner of North Las Vegas over time. That is a big jump in housing for an area that has been growing fast for years.

The pricing is the headline for most buyers. KB Home plans townhomes priced from roughly $340,000 to $385,000. The detached single family homes start around $380,000 and climb to about $550,000 for larger floor plans in certain sections. The mix gives buyers a range of choices, from a first home or a smart downsize to a full sized family house with more square footage.

That price mix is worth pausing on. A community that offers both townhomes and larger detached homes tends to draw a wider range of neighbors, from young first time buyers to growing families to people trading up. That variety can make a neighborhood feel more balanced and stable over time. It also gives a buyer room to move up or down within the same community as their needs change, without leaving the area they have grown to like.

KB Home is one of the national builders active in the Villages at Tule Springs, alongside other big names that have been building there since the community broke ground. The approval of Village 2 is another step in a long, phased plan that will keep adding rooftops in North Las Vegas for years to come.

It helps to understand what an approval like this really means. The Planning Commission is the body that reviews the layout of a project, the size of the lots, the road connections, and how the homes fit the larger master plan. Getting Village 2 through that step clears a major hurdle for KB Home. It signals that the design meets the city's rules and that the builder can start turning raw land into finished streets and houses. For buyers, it is the difference between a rumor about future homes and a project that is genuinely moving forward.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

For anyone trying to buy a first home in the valley, price is everything right now. The Las Vegas median home price has been sitting near a record $490,000. When a new community offers townhomes starting around $340,000, that is a real gap below the median. It means a monthly payment that more working families can plan around.

North Las Vegas has quietly become one of the best value spots in the valley for new construction. Land is more available here than in built out areas like Summerlin, so builders can still offer newer homes at lower entry prices. Village 2 fits that pattern. It gives renters a path to owning without leaving Clark County or stretching past their budget.

The 215 Beltway location also helps daily life. A quick freeway connection makes it easier to reach jobs across the valley, from the airport area to the west side. Families care about commute time, and being near the Beltway keeps this part of North Las Vegas connected to the rest of the metro instead of feeling cut off.

More homes also mean more neighbors, and that shapes everything from schools to shopping. New rooftops bring demand for grocery stores, restaurants, and services. Over time, that kind of growth tends to fill in the gaps around a community and make the area feel more complete. For current North Las Vegas homeowners, steady new demand can also support local property values.

There is a bigger housing story here too. Clark County has spent years talking about a shortage of homes and a shortage of land to build them on. Every new section that gets approved adds a little more supply to a tight market. More supply, especially at attainable price points, is one of the few things that can slow the runaway price growth that has locked so many renters out. Village 2 will not fix the shortage on its own, but hundreds of new homes moving through the pipeline is a step in the right direction.

Renters feel this pressure the most. When there are not enough homes to buy, more people stay stuck renting, and that keeps rents high too. Adding for sale housing gives some of those renters a way out and can ease the squeeze across the whole market. That is why local buyers should care about a Planning Commission vote even if they never plan to live in Tule Springs. The decisions made on these projects ripple through the entire valley.

Background and History

The Villages at Tule Springs is one of the largest master planned communities taking shape in the north end of the valley. The overall plan covers more than 1,200 acres just south of the 215 Beltway, and it is designed to hold roughly 8,600 homes once it is fully built out. That scale puts it among the most ambitious housing projects in North Las Vegas.

The community broke ground back in 2017 and has been building in phases ever since. Several national builders have been part of the effort, including KB Home, Lennar, and Tri Pointe Homes. The plan calls for a wide mix of housing, from townhomes to single story and two story homes, with sizes running from about 1,800 square feet to 3,800 square feet. A portion of the community, around 2,000 homes, is set aside for active adults age 55 and older.

Tule Springs was never meant to be just houses. The master plan includes a large regional park and close to 97 acres of public park space overall, along with fitness areas, picnic spots, and resident pools. It also sets aside room for two elementary schools, a fire station, a police station, and a commercial shopping center. The goal is a full community where people can live, learn, and shop close to home.

The setting is part of the appeal. Future phases border the Eglington Preserve, and the plan includes trailheads that connect residents to the nearby Tule Springs Fossil Beds National Monument, a 22,650 acre protected area known for its Ice Age fossils. That mix of new homes next to open, protected desert is rare in a growing metro like Las Vegas.

North Las Vegas as a whole has changed a lot over the last decade. For years it carried an unfair reputation as the overlooked corner of the valley. Then the jobs started coming. Large employers and logistics operations moved into the north and set off a wave of demand for nearby housing. Master plans like Tule Springs are part of the city's answer, giving those workers new places to live close to where they work. The growth in this area is not an accident. It follows the jobs.

What Happens Next

With Village 2 approved, KB Home can move ahead on the next steps toward building. That usually means finishing detailed site work, pulling permits, and grading the land before vertical construction starts. Buyers who want in early often watch for a sales office or a model home to open, since that is when floor plans, lot maps, and firm pricing become available.

Prices and floor plans can shift as the builder gets closer to opening. The ranges shared so far, townhomes from about $340,000 and detached homes up to roughly $550,000, are a strong starting guide. Still, final numbers depend on the section, the lot, and the options a buyer picks. It is smart to treat those figures as a helpful preview rather than a locked in quote.

Beyond Village 2, expect the larger Villages at Tule Springs plan to keep rolling out over the next several years. As each new section opens, the area will keep adding homes, parks, and services. Anyone thinking about North Las Vegas should watch how quickly these phases fill in, because early buyers in a growing master plan sometimes benefit as the community matures around them.

Keep an eye on the amenities timeline as well. In a big master plan, the parks, schools, and shopping do not all arrive at once. They tend to follow the homes as more families move in. Buying early can mean lower prices, but it can also mean living in a construction zone for a while before the fun parts of the community open. It is a tradeoff worth thinking through, and a good agent can help you read where a given phase sits in that cycle.

Ryan's Take

I get excited about projects like Village 2 because they hit a need the valley has been missing, which is attainable new construction. So much of the new home talk in Las Vegas centers on Summerlin land selling at record prices and luxury homes climbing fast. Meanwhile, first time buyers keep asking me where they can actually get a brand new home without a giant budget. North Las Vegas and Tule Springs are a big part of that answer.

My honest advice is to look past the North Las Vegas reputation of years ago and judge this area on where it is heading. The Beltway access, the parks, the schools in the master plan, and the wall of open desert next door add up to a genuinely appealing place to own. A townhome starting near $340,000 in a growing, well planned community is the kind of entry point that can build real equity over time. If you are renting and tired of watching prices rise, this is worth a serious look.

I always tell buyers to run the numbers on a new build the same way they would on a resale home. Factor in the homeowners association dues, the property taxes, and any special district fees that come with a master planned community. Those costs are normal, but they add to your monthly payment, so it pays to know them up front. A brand new home also means fewer repairs in the early years and a builder warranty, which can offset some of that. When you weigh the full picture, an attainable new home in a growing area can be one of the smartest first moves a buyer makes in this market.

What You Can Do

If Village 2 sounds like a fit, the first step is to get on KB Home's interest list for the Villages at Tule Springs so you hear when sales open. Builders often reward early buyers with first pick of lots and, at times, incentives on rate or closing costs. Getting in early can make a real difference in which home and lot you end up with.

Before you fall in love with a floor plan, get your financing lined up. Talk with a lender about what payment fits your budget and ask about first time buyer programs and down payment help available in Nevada. Knowing your real number keeps you focused and makes you a stronger buyer when it is time to sign. It also helps you compare a new build against resale homes in the same price range.

It is also smart to tour more than one section and more than one builder before you commit. Villages at Tule Springs has several neighborhoods and multiple builders, and each one offers different floor plans, lot sizes, and price points. Walking a few model homes helps you feel the difference between a townhome and a detached plan, and it gives you a better sense of what your money buys. Take notes on ceiling heights, yard sizes, garage space, and how close the home sits to future amenities.

You can also stay involved in how North Las Vegas grows. Planning Commission and City Council meetings are open to the public, and they decide which projects move forward and how neighborhoods take shape. If new housing, traffic, and parks matter to you, showing up or sending comments is one of the clearest ways to be heard. These meetings are where the future of your neighborhood really gets decided, and residents who pay attention have more say than they might think.
