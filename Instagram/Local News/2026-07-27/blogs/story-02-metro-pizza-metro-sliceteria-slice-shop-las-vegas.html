Metro Pizza Founder Opens His First Slice Shop, Metro Sliceteria, in Las Vegas

John Arena, the pizza legend who helped build Metro Pizza into one of the most loved pizza names in Las Vegas, has opened his first ever slice shop. It is called Metro Sliceteria, and it started serving slices and whole pies on or about July 21, 2026, at 8720 W. Sunset Road, Suite D-150, in the southwest valley. This matters because it brings a beloved 46 year old Las Vegas food name back to life in a brand new way, just months after Metro Pizza closed its longtime shop on East Tropicana Avenue.

For a lot of Las Vegas families, Metro Pizza is not just a restaurant. It is birthday parties, team dinners after Little League, and late nights after work. So when the East Tropicana location closed in late 2025 after 37 years, many people felt like they lost a small piece of the city. Metro Sliceteria is the happy next chapter. Instead of only selling whole pies at a sit down spot, this new shop lets you walk up, point at a slice, and eat right away. It keeps the old flavors people trust while trying a fresh, faster format. In this article we will break down what opened, where it is, what is on the menu, and why a new slice shop is a bigger deal for your neighborhood than it might seem at first.

What Happened

John Arena, who co-founded Metro Pizza in Las Vegas back in 1980, quietly opened Metro Sliceteria in the southwest part of the valley. The shop sits inside a newer shopping and dining area known as The Bend, near the IKEA store, at 8720 W. Sunset Road, Suite D-150. Reports say the doors opened to the public on or about July 21, 2026. This is a first for Arena and for the Metro brand. In all its years, Metro Pizza had never run a true grab a slice counter. Now it does.

The idea behind a slice shop is simple. Whole pizzas are great for a group, but they take time and they cost more. A slice shop lets one person, or a small family, get a hot piece of pizza in minutes without waiting for a full pie to bake. Metro Sliceteria is built around that idea. According to reports, the shop offers pizza by the slice pulled from many different pies, so you can mix and match instead of picking just one flavor. Coverage of the concept says the menu is planned to pull from around 15 different types of pie, which would give customers a wide range of choices in one visit. The menu is not only classic New York style. Reports about the concept mention several pizza styles that could show up, including Roman style, Detroit style, grandma style, and Sicilian style. These are all different in how thick the crust is, how the cheese and sauce are layered, and how the edges get crispy. On top of pizza, coverage says the shop plans to offer Italian favorites, gelato for dessert, and local craft beer. That mix turns a quick slice stop into a small sit and stay option too. [NOT VERIFIED: full drink and dessert lists and daily specials.]

It is worth being clear about names. John Arena started Metro Pizza with his cousin, Sam Facchini. The two came to Las Vegas from New York and opened their first shop in 1980 under the name The Original New York Pizza before it grew into the Metro Pizza brand people know today. Metro Sliceteria is Arena's new project, and it carries the Metro name forward into a slice first format.

The location choice is part of the story too. The Bend is a newer shopping and dining spot in the southwest valley, and it has been drawing fresh restaurants and stores as more homes fill in that part of town. Putting a slice shop there, close to a busy anchor like IKEA, means steady foot traffic and lots of nearby families who are hungry for quick, quality food. It is a smart place to test a new idea, because the built in crowd gives the shop a real shot at finding its regulars fast.

Both groups end up feeling more connected to the community. That shared pride is one of the quiet things that turns a group of houses into a real neighborhood.

Background and History

Metro Pizza has deep roots in this city. John Arena and his cousin Sam Facchini both grew up around pizza in New York, working in a family pizzeria as kids. In 1980, they moved to Las Vegas and opened their first shop, first called The Original New York Pizza. Over the years the business grew into Metro Pizza, with locations around the valley and a reputation for taking pizza seriously. Arena became known not just as a shop owner but as a true student of pizza, someone who studied recipes, taught classes, and cared deeply about the craft.

For 37 years, one of the most loved Metro Pizza shops sat at 1395 E. Tropicana Avenue. Generations of Las Vegas families made memories there. So in late 2025, when that location closed after 37 years, it was a genuinely emotional moment for a lot of people. Closings like that often happen for many reasons at once, from rising rent to changing traffic patterns to lease decisions. Whatever the mix, the loss of a 37 year old shop hit hard because Metro was woven into so many people's lives.

That is what makes the timing of Metro Sliceteria stand out. Rather than fade away, the brand is reinventing itself. The slice shop idea has been in the works for a while. Local food coverage discussed the Sliceteria plan for The Bend well before it opened, describing a slice first spot that would bring the Metro name to the growing southwest valley. Now that plan is real. The story is not about an ending. It is about a beloved institution learning a new trick and coming back to feed the city in a fresh format.
