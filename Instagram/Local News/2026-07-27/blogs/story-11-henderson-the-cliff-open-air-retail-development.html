Henderson Approves $50M "The Cliff" Open-Air Retail and Dining Destination

The City of Henderson has approved plans for The Cliff, a roughly $50 million open-air retail and dining destination in the Green Valley Ranch area. The project will turn about 10 acres of aging office space near Paseo Verde and St. Rose parkways into a 100,000 square foot gathering place with 25 shops, an outdoor dining lounge, fire pits, live performance space, and public art. Groundbreaking is expected in 2026, and it will be the biggest new retail project in Henderson in about 20 years.

If you live in Henderson, you have probably driven past a lot of tired office buildings and wished they were something better. That is exactly what The Cliff is trying to fix. Instead of another enclosed mall or another strip of drive-through windows, the developers want to build a walkable outdoor spot where people can eat, shop, and hang out. Some news outlets have even called it "Southern Nevada's first anti-mall." That is a fun nickname, but the real story is what it means for home values, traffic, and daily life in one of the fastest growing parts of the Las Vegas Valley.

Below, I break down what was approved, who is behind it, why it matters for people who own homes near Green Valley Ranch, and what neighbors should watch for as construction gets closer. I will keep it simple and stick to the facts we can confirm.

What Happened

The Henderson City Council voted to approve development plans for The Cliff, an open-air retail and dining project valued at about $50 million. The site sits in the Green Valley Ranch part of Henderson, near the corner of Paseo Verde Parkway and St. Rose Parkway. Right now that land holds older office buildings. The plan is to redevelop roughly 10 acres of that office park into a modern shopping and dining plaza.

The project is being led by two development groups, Partners Capital and CAST Capital Partners. Their plan calls for about 100,000 square feet of space spread across the site. That space will hold around 25 retail spots for stores and restaurants. The design is built around the outdoors instead of a closed building. Plans include an outdoor dining lounge, fire pits, areas for live performances, public art installations, and open zones where people can meet up and relax.

To make the project happen, the council had to approve some changes to the rules for that land. That included zoning changes and updates to the city's master plan. In plain terms, the old office park land was not set up for a big retail and entertainment center, so the city had to give it a new label and new guidelines. Reports describe the approval as unanimous, meaning the council members who voted were all in favor. [NOT VERIFIED: the exact approval date is not confirmed across all available sources; treat this as a 2026 approval per the project timeline.]

The developers have said The Cliff would be the largest retail development in Henderson since The District at Green Valley Ranch opened back in 2004. That is a big claim, because Henderson has added a lot of stores and restaurants over the years. But most of those came in smaller pieces. The Cliff is one large, planned project meant to feel like a single destination.

The "anti-mall" nickname is worth explaining, because it tells you what the developers are going for. A traditional mall is a big closed box. You walk inside, the stores face a hallway, and the whole thing feels sealed off from the world. The Cliff is the opposite. The shops open to the outdoors, the walkways are meant for strolling, and the gathering spaces are the main event instead of an afterthought. The goal is to make a place people want to hang around in, not just run into and out of. In the desert, that means shade, water features, and evening events when the temperatures drop.

Groundbreaking is expected in 2026, with construction to follow. [NOT VERIFIED: the exact groundbreaking month has been reported differently in different places, with some sources pointing to late 2026 and an opening target in the following year. Treat any single date as an estimate until the developer confirms it.] What we can say for sure is that the approval clears the biggest hurdle. Once a project gets its zoning and master plan changes, it can move toward permits and dirt work.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

New retail is not just about having more places to shop. For homeowners, projects like The Cliff can change the value and feel of a whole neighborhood. When a walkable dining and shopping spot opens nearby, homes around it often become more attractive to buyers. People like being close to good restaurants and easy errands without a long drive. That demand can support home prices over time.

Green Valley Ranch is already one of the most popular master planned areas in Henderson. It has parks, trails, schools, and The District, which has been the main gathering place there for more than 20 years. Adding a second big destination gives residents more options and spreads out the crowds. It also signals that developers still see strong demand in this part of the valley. Money follows people, and $50 million is a real vote of confidence in the area.

There are everyday benefits too. An outdoor center with fire pits, live music, and public art gives families a place to walk to on a nice evening. It can become a spot for date nights, kid friendly weekends, and casual meetups with neighbors. Those things add to what real estate agents call "lifestyle value." Buyers may not put a dollar figure on it, but they feel it when they tour a home and realize they can stroll to dinner.

Jobs matter as well. Building the project takes construction workers. Once it opens, the shops and restaurants need staff. That means more local hiring, from servers and cooks to store clerks and managers. New businesses also pay into the local tax base, which can help fund city services like roads and parks. For a growing city like Henderson, steady commercial growth helps balance the budget.

Of course, growth brings tradeoffs. More stores can mean more cars and more traffic at nearby intersections. Some neighbors worry about noise from live performances or crowds late in the evening. Those are fair concerns, and they are worth watching as the design gets finalized. The good news is that redeveloping an old office park usually adds less brand new traffic than building on empty desert, because the land already had daily workers coming and going.

It also helps that this is infill development. Infill means building inside an area that is already developed, instead of pushing further out into open land. Infill projects tend to be popular with cities because they use roads, sewer lines, and power that already exist. That saves money and keeps sprawl in check. For nearby homeowners, infill can be a quiet win. It replaces something dated with something fresh, and it usually comes with landscaping and design upgrades that lift the look of the whole corner.

Background and History

Henderson has grown from a small industrial town into the second largest city in Nevada. For decades it was known mostly for factories and quiet suburbs. Over the last 25 years, that changed fast. Master planned communities like Green Valley Ranch, Anthem, Inspirada, and Cadence brought thousands of new homes and families. As the rooftops went up, retail followed.

The District at Green Valley Ranch, which opened in 2004, was a turning point. It showed that an open-air, walkable center could work in the desert. People liked being able to park once and stroll between shops and restaurants instead of hopping in and out of the car. The District became a model that other Nevada developers copied. The Cliff builds on that same idea, but with a fresh design focused on gathering spaces and outdoor experiences.

At the same time, the office market has been changing. Many companies now let workers stay home part of the week, so some older office parks sit half empty. That leaves cities and owners looking for new uses for the land. Turning a dated office park into retail and dining is a smart way to reuse space that already has roads, power, and water in place. It is often faster and greener than building on raw desert land at the edge of town.

This is part of a bigger trend across the Las Vegas Valley. West Henderson is booming with new projects. The Inspirada community landed a 24-acre mixed-use plan with shops and apartments. Developers keep pointing to Henderson's strong population growth and high demand for places to eat and shop. The Cliff fits right into that wave. It takes a proven concept, an open-air center, and drops it into one of the most established neighborhoods in the city.

What Happens Next

With the plans approved, the project moves into the design and permitting stage. That is where the developers finalize building drawings, work out details with the city, and line up the construction crews. It also means signing tenants. A center like this usually announces some of its stores and restaurants before it opens, to build excitement and prove demand to lenders. [NOT VERIFIED: no confirmed tenant list has been released for The Cliff as of this writing.]

Groundbreaking is the next big milestone to watch. Once crews start clearing the old office buildings and prepping the site, you will see fencing, heavy equipment, and dirt work near Paseo Verde and St. Rose parkways. That stage can bring short term dust, noise, and some traffic changes for nearby drivers. Cities usually require the developer to manage those impacts, but neighbors should expect a busy site during construction.

After the structures go up, the interior build-out begins. Restaurants take longer to finish than simple retail because they need kitchens, plumbing, and inspections. That is why an opening date can slip a few months even when construction is on track. The smart move is to watch for official announcements from the developers and the City of Henderson rather than relying on early guesses.

For homeowners near the site, this is a good time to pay attention. Big projects like this can affect resale timing. Some buyers love being close to new amenities and will pay for it. Others may want to buy before the construction traffic starts, then enjoy the finished center later. Either way, knowing the timeline helps you plan. If you are thinking about selling in the next year or two, the story of The Cliff could become a selling point for your listing.
