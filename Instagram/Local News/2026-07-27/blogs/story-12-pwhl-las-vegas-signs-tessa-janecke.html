PWHL Las Vegas Signs No. 3 Overall Pick Tessa Janecke

PWHL Las Vegas signed its top 2026 draft pick, forward Tessa Janecke, on July 14, 2026. She was taken third overall out of Penn State, and she is now a big part of the team's plan for its first season. This is a huge move for the new club, and it is a big deal for hockey fans across the Las Vegas Valley.

Las Vegas is now getting a second pro hockey team. The Vegas Golden Knights already play in the National Hockey League, and the Henderson Silver Knights play in the American Hockey League. Now the Professional Women's Hockey League, or PWHL, is coming to town too. The new team is called PWHL Las Vegas, and it will play its home games at T-Mobile Arena. Its first season starts in the 2026-27 season.

Signing a player like Tessa Janecke sends a clear message. The team wants to win, and it wants to bring top talent to the valley right away. Janecke is one of the best young players in the sport. She also just helped Team USA win gold at the 2026 Winter Olympics. For a brand new team, landing a player like that is about as strong a start as you can get. In this article, we will break down what happened, why it matters to people who live here, and what comes next for hockey in Southern Nevada.

What Happened

On July 14, 2026, PWHL Las Vegas made it official. The team signed forward Tessa Janecke, who was the third overall pick in the 2026 PWHL Draft. Janecke played her college hockey at Penn State, where she became one of the most talked about players in the country. She was the kind of pick that fans and reporters had circled for months, and the new Las Vegas team made her a top target.

Janecke is known for her speed, her smart play, and her ability to score big goals. At Penn State she set records and earned major awards. She was named a First Team All-American, which is one of the highest honors a college player can win. She also made history at her school as a top scorer. On top of all that, she played for Team USA at the 2026 Winter Olympics and came home with a gold medal. That is a lot of success for a player who is still early in her career.

The signing was not the team's only big move. PWHL Las Vegas also added veteran forward Hilary Knight. Knight is one of the best known names in women's hockey. She came to the team through something called an Expansion Foundational Offer. That is a special rule the league uses to help brand new teams get a strong, well known player to build around. Knight brings years of experience and a long list of awards. Pairing a proven veteran like Knight with a rising young star like Janecke gives the team both leadership and fresh energy.

All of this roster building is happening under General Manager Dominique DiDia. A general manager, often called a GM, is the person in charge of picking players and shaping the team. DiDia has the job of turning a blank roster into a group that can compete right away. Signing a top draft pick and a foundational veteran in the same window shows the plan is moving fast. The team is not waiting around. It wants to be ready to play winning hockey when the puck drops on the 2026-27 season at T-Mobile Arena.

For fans, these moves also make the team feel real. A new franchise can seem like just an idea until players start signing their names. Now there are real faces to cheer for. When people can point to a star like Janecke and say she plays for our city, that is when a team starts to feel like it belongs. That is exactly what PWHL Las Vegas is trying to build in these early months.

It is worth remembering how young Janecke is compared to how much she has already done. Many players her age are still hoping to make a name for themselves. She has already stood on an Olympic podium and set records at a major college program. Teams love that kind of mix, because it means she should keep getting better for years to come. For a first year franchise, having a player who can grow along with the club is a real gift. She could become the face of the team for a long time, and that helps fans form a bond that lasts well beyond one season.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might wonder why a hockey signing matters to people who live here, even if they do not follow the sport. The answer is that pro sports teams do more than play games. They bring jobs, visitors, and money into the local economy. They also give a city a stronger sense of pride and identity. A second pro hockey team means more of all of those good things for the Las Vegas Valley.

Start with the events at T-Mobile Arena. A full PWHL season means many more game nights on the arena calendar. Each of those nights brings people out of their homes and into the area around the arena. Fans buy tickets, food, drinks, and team gear. They park their cars, ride shuttles, and often eat at nearby restaurants before or after the game. All of that spending supports local workers and local businesses. More events also mean more hours for the people who work at the arena, from ticket takers to security staff to cleaning crews.

There is also the housing side of this, which matters to me as a local real estate agent. When a city keeps adding pro teams and big events, it becomes a more exciting place to live. That draws new people to the area. Players, coaches, team staff, and league workers all need places to live. Some will rent, and some will buy homes. A growing sports scene is one of the many things that makes people want to plant roots in a place. Over time, that steady interest helps support home values across the valley.

Then there is the family side of things. A women's pro hockey team gives young girls in Las Vegas real role models to look up to. Kids who play youth hockey now have local stars to watch and copy. That can grow the number of families who sign their children up for the sport. More youth players means more demand for ice rinks, gear shops, and skating lessons. It all adds up to a bigger, healthier hockey community in Southern Nevada.

Finally, a second pro hockey team helps the whole valley stand out. Las Vegas is already known around the world for its shows and its nightlife. In recent years it has also become a true sports town. Adding the PWHL to that list gives fans one more reason to be proud of where they live. It also gives visitors one more reason to come and spend time here. For a community that depends so much on tourism, that is a real and lasting benefit.

Background and History

The Professional Women's Hockey League is fairly new, but it has grown fast. The league played its first season in 2024 with a small group of teams. Fans showed up in big numbers, and the games drew strong crowds and lots of attention. That early success led the league to look for new cities where it could expand. Las Vegas turned out to be a natural fit, thanks to its love of hockey and its top notch arena.

Las Vegas already had a proven hockey story before the PWHL arrived. The Vegas Golden Knights joined the National Hockey League in 2017 and became one of the most popular teams in the sport almost overnight. They reached the championship in their very first season, and later they won the Stanley Cup. Their success showed the whole hockey world that a desert city could be a passionate hockey market. The Henderson Silver Knights soon followed in the American Hockey League, giving fans even more hockey to watch close to home.

T-Mobile Arena has been at the center of that hockey story. It opened in 2016 near the south end of the Las Vegas Strip. The arena seats thousands of fans and hosts hockey games, concerts, boxing, and other big events all year long. It is already home to the Golden Knights, so it has the ice, the staff, and the fan base that a hockey team needs. Placing PWHL Las Vegas in that same building makes a lot of sense. The pieces for pro hockey are already in place.

Tessa Janecke fits right into this history of building strong teams. Her Penn State career and her Olympic gold medal make her one of the most promising young players to enter the league. By drafting her early and signing her fast, PWHL Las Vegas is following the same play that worked for other teams in town. Get top talent, put it in a great arena, and let a hungry fan base do the rest.

What Happens Next

The next step for PWHL Las Vegas is to keep filling out its roster. Signing Tessa Janecke and Hilary Knight is a strong start, but a full hockey team needs many more players. General Manager Dominique DiDia and his staff will keep working to add forwards, defense players, and goalies. Fans can expect more signings to be announced in the weeks and months ahead as the team gets ready for its first season.

After the roster comes training. The players will need time to practice together and learn how to play as one unit. New teams often take a little while to find their rhythm, since the players are meeting each other for the first time. A strong training camp will help the group build chemistry before real games begin. Coaches will use that time to set their systems and decide who plays in which role.

Fans should also watch for news about tickets, schedules, and the team's full look. The club will need to share when home games take place at T-Mobile Arena and how people can buy seats. Season ticket plans and single game tickets are the kind of details that fans will be eager to see. Local media outlets like the Las Vegas Review-Journal and the league's own website will be good places to follow along for updates.

Then comes the biggest moment of all, the first home game. When PWHL Las Vegas plays its opening night at T-Mobile Arena in the 2026-27 season, it will be a milestone for the whole valley. That night will show how much support the team can draw. If the crowds are anything like what the Golden Knights have seen, the new team should feel right at home. It will be a proud day for hockey in Southern Nevada.
