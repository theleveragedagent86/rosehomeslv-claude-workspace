Acclaimed Aroma Latin American Cocina Reopens in Chinatown

Aroma Latin American Cocina is back. Chef-owner Steve Kestler, a 2024 James Beard Award finalist, moved his highly rated restaurant from Green Valley Parkway in Henderson to the heart of the Las Vegas Chinatown corridor, reopening by late May 2026 at 3355 Spring Mountain Road, Suite 35, right next to the Sand Dollar Lounge.

This is a big deal for local food lovers. Aroma was already a Top 100 restaurant on the Las Vegas Review-Journal list, and it earned that spot while tucked away in a Henderson shopping center. Now it sits in the busiest dining district in the whole valley. When a chef who has been nominated for one of the top honors in American cooking picks Chinatown as his new home, it tells you something about where the best food in Las Vegas is being made these days.

For years, people drove out to Henderson for Aroma. Regulars filled the small dining room for pupusas, ceviche, and creative tacos that mixed Latin flavors with a few surprises from Asia and beyond. The move changes that drive for many fans, but it also puts the restaurant in front of a much larger crowd. Spring Mountain Road is where curious eaters go to try something new. Now Aroma is part of that scene. Below, we break down what happened, why the move matters for the neighborhoods around Chinatown, and what it could mean for the people who live nearby.

What Happened

Steve Kestler opened Aroma Latin American Cocina in 2021 at 2877 North Green Valley Parkway in Henderson. The restaurant started small and quiet, but the food spoke for itself. Kestler leaned on his Guatemalan roots and his years in some of the most respected kitchens in Las Vegas. He had worked at Bazaar Meat, Bouchon, and EDO Tapas before striking out on his own. That training showed up on every plate.

Word spread fast. Diners praised the pupusas, the Peruvian ceviche, the avocado tartare with plantain chips, and tacos that ranged from classic carne asada and al pastor to shrimp with mango salsa. The menu was never a plain Mexican menu or a plain Latin menu. It pulled from many countries across Latin America and added careful touches from Asian and other world cuisines. That mix helped Aroma stand out in a city packed with restaurants.

The recognition followed. Kestler was named a 2024 finalist for Best Chef: Southwest by the James Beard Foundation, one of the highest honors a cook in this region can earn. Aroma also landed on the Las Vegas Review-Journal Top 100 list for both 2024 and 2025. Those are rare wins for a small independent spot in a suburban strip mall.

In early 2026, Kestler announced he was closing the Henderson location to make a move. The Green Valley Parkway spot served its last meals, and the team packed up for a new address in Chinatown. The new home at 3355 Spring Mountain Road, Suite 35, sits beside the Sand Dollar Lounge, a well-known live music bar. The restaurant reopened by late May 2026, bringing its full menu of Latin American comfort food to the west side of the valley. The core idea stayed the same. The food is built for the adventurous eater who wants bold, honest flavors, not a watered-down version made to please everyone.

It helps to picture what sets the food apart. Aroma is not built to be a quick, familiar meal. The kitchen treats Latin America as one giant, connected region and pulls dishes from many of its corners. A single visit might include Salvadoran pupusas, Peruvian ceviche, and tacos that borrow a bright mango salsa or a splash of Asian seasoning. Diners have long praised the chicken revuelta pupusa for tasting truly homemade, and the avocado tartare with plantain chips has become a signature starter. That range is what earned the loyal crowd, and it is exactly what Kestler is bringing to Spring Mountain Road.

For Kestler, the move was about reaching more people and being part of a district that celebrates food the way he does. Chinatown draws diners from all over Clark County who are hunting for the next great meal. Aroma now shares that stage with dozens of other beloved kitchens. That kind of company tends to push a good restaurant to be even better, because the crowd on Spring Mountain Road has tried a lot and expects a lot.

Background and History

To understand why this move matters, it helps to know how Chinatown became the food capital of Las Vegas. The district began in 1995 with the opening of a single shopping center on Spring Mountain Road. At the time, few people expected it to grow into anything major. But it filled with restaurants serving food from across Asia, and diners kept coming. Over the years the corridor stretched for miles and added kitchens from many cultures, not just Asian ones.

Today Chinatown is where Las Vegas chefs go when they want to be part of a true food scene. Rent has often been friendlier than the Strip, the crowds are hungry and open-minded, and the neighbors are other serious cooks. That mix has pulled in talent that reaches far beyond Chinese food. You can find Korean barbecue, Japanese ramen, Thai street food, Mexican seafood, and now a growing number of Latin American spots. Aroma fits right into that trend.

Steve Kestler's own story mirrors the district's spirit. He grew up connected to Guatemalan cooking, then trained in top professional kitchens around Las Vegas. When he opened Aroma in Henderson in 2021, he was betting that local diners would embrace real Latin American food with a personal twist. The bet paid off with a James Beard nod and two straight years on the Review-Journal Top 100 list. Moving to Chinatown is the next logical step for a chef who has outgrown a quiet suburban corner and wants to cook where the action is.

The choice of address is fitting, too. Sitting next to the Sand Dollar Lounge places Aroma in a spot known for music and late nights. It is a corner of the corridor that stays lively, and it gives the restaurant a built-in flow of people looking for a good meal before or after a show.

What Happens Next

With the doors open by late May 2026, the next few months will show how the Chinatown crowd takes to Aroma. Early signs point to a warm welcome. The restaurant already had a loyal following in Henderson, and many of those fans will happily make the drive. On top of that, the constant foot traffic on Spring Mountain Road should introduce the menu to a whole new set of diners who never made it out to Green Valley Parkway.

Expect the core menu to stay strong. The pupusas, ceviche, avocado tartare, and creative tacos that built Aroma's name are the heart of what it does. A new, larger audience may also push the kitchen to grow. Chinatown restaurants often expand their hours, add late-night items, or roll out special dishes to match the district's busy rhythm.
