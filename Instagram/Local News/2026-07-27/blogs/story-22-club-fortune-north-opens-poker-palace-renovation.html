Club Fortune North Opens After $2.8M Renovation of Former Poker Palace

The old Poker Palace in North Las Vegas is open again, this time as Club Fortune North, after a $2.8 million makeover by Truckee Gaming. The company held a ribbon-cutting on Wednesday, July 22, 2026, and opened the doors to a fully redesigned locals casino that now includes the 14th Nevada location of Boomer's Sportsbook. This brings a long-empty gaming property back to life, adds jobs, and gives the north end of the valley a fresh place to play close to home.

For a lot of people who live near Owens Avenue and Las Vegas Boulevard, the Poker Palace was more than a casino. It was a landmark. It sat on that corner for decades and became part of the routine for folks who wanted a quick bite, a few hands, or a spot to watch a game. When it went dark, the corner felt a little quieter. Now the lights are back on, and the building looks nothing like the tired room people remembered. Truckee Gaming stripped it down, rebuilt the inside, and gave the outside a whole new look with new lighting and signs.

This story matters to more than gamblers. When a boarded-up building on a busy corner reopens, it changes how the whole neighborhood feels. It means new jobs, more foot traffic, and a signal to other business owners that this part of North Las Vegas is worth investing in. For homeowners nearby, that kind of momentum can be a quiet plus. Below is a plain look at what happened, why it matters, and what could come next for this corner of the valley.

What Happened

Truckee Gaming bought the longtime Poker Palace property, shut it down, and put $2.8 million into a full renovation. After about nine months of construction, the company reopened the building as Club Fortune North. There was a soft opening on July 19, 2026, so the staff could work out the kinks with real guests, and then an official grand opening with a ribbon-cutting on Wednesday, July 22. Local news outlets, including Fox5 Vegas, covered the debut and reported strong early turnout from curious locals.

The changes go from the parking lot to the back wall. Outside, the crew added new LED perimeter lighting that wraps the building, put up fresh signage with the Club Fortune North name, and cleaned up the landscaping so the property looks sharp from the street. Inside, the old floor plan is gone. The team redesigned the entire casino floor with new carpet, new machines, updated seating, and a brighter, more open feel. The goal was to make it feel like a modern locals casino instead of a dated room.

One of the biggest additions is Boomer's Sportsbook. Club Fortune North is the 14th Nevada location for the brand. The sportsbook lets guests bet on sports and horse racing. For horse racing bettors, the book offers a rewards perk. According to reporting on the opening, parimutuel bettors can earn 4 percent back on their wagering handle, which they can then use for food and drinks at the property. That kind of perk is aimed squarely at regulars who want their loyalty to pay off.

There is one notable thing the new casino does not have, and it is right there in the old name. Club Fortune North does not have a poker room. The original Poker Palace was known for its poker tables, but the new owners chose to skip the poker room and lean into slots, a fresh sportsbook, and food and drink instead. So while the address and the corner are the same, the mix of games under the roof has changed. People looking for a live poker room will need to go elsewhere, but people who like slots and betting on games now have a clean, updated place to do it.

The Club Fortune name is the other big piece of this. Truckee Gaming already runs a Club Fortune Casino in Henderson, so calling this property Club Fortune North ties the two together under one brand that valley residents can recognize. That matters because a familiar name carries trust. When people see a brand they already know landing on their corner, they are more likely to walk in and give it a try. It also lets the company share marketing, loyalty programs, and know-how across both spots instead of starting from scratch on the north end of town.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Locals casinos are a different animal from the big resorts on the Strip. The Strip is built for tourists. Locals casinos like Club Fortune North are built for the people who live here. They serve neighbors who want a short drive, a fair meal, a place to watch the game, and a room where the staff might know their name. When one of these places closes, the neighborhood loses a gathering spot. When one reopens, the neighborhood gets it back.

The most direct benefit is jobs. A renovated casino needs dealers, cage staff, cooks, servers, cleaners, security, and managers. Those are steady paychecks that stay in North Las Vegas. Many of the people who work at a locals casino also live nearby, so the money tends to circle back into the same community through rent, groceries, and other spending. In an area that has fought hard to bring in steady employers, a reopened casino with a payroll is a real win.

There is also the ripple effect on the corner itself. A busy, well-lit casino draws traffic at all hours. That traffic supports the gas stations, restaurants, and shops around it. A dark, empty building does the opposite. It can drag down the feel of a block and make people worry about safety and decline. Swapping a shuttered property for a bright, active one is the kind of change that people can see and feel when they drive by, and it tends to make the whole stretch feel more cared for.

Locals casinos also tend to be steadier than Strip resorts through ups and downs. Tourist spending rises and falls with the economy and with how many visitors are flying in. Neighborhood casinos lean on the people who live nearby, and that base holds up better. A regular who stops in after work does not stop coming just because airfares went up. That steadiness is part of why a locals casino can be a dependable anchor for a corner, giving nearby businesses foot traffic they can count on week after week.

For homeowners, this is where things get interesting. Home values are tied to more than just the house itself. They are tied to the neighborhood. Buyers care about what is nearby, whether the area is on the way up or on the way down, and how safe and lively it feels. A revived local casino with jobs and steady activity is one more mark in the plus column when someone is deciding whether to buy in North Las Vegas. It is not a magic switch that raises prices overnight, but it is part of the bigger picture of an area that keeps drawing investment.

Background and History

The Poker Palace had a long run in North Las Vegas. For decades it was one of the small, no-frills locals spots on the north end of the valley, known for its poker tables and its steady base of regulars. It was the kind of place that did not chase tourists and did not need to. It served the people who lived around it, and it became a familiar part of the landscape near the Las Vegas Boulevard corridor in North Las Vegas.

Over time, the property aged, and the old model of a small poker-focused casino got harder to sustain. Eventually it closed. When Truckee Gaming stepped in, the plan was to reopen the site under the Club Fortune brand. Club Fortune Casino is a locals brand already known in the valley for its Henderson location, so bringing a "Club Fortune North" to the other end of town gave the company a second anchor and a recognizable name to build around.

The road to reopening was not a straight line. Reporting on the project noted that the renovation grew well beyond its original scope, and the opening date moved more than once. Earlier updates pointed to a spring reopening, then a summer target, before the property finally opened its doors in July 2026. Renovations often stretch out like this once crews open up an older building and find work that needs doing. The $2.8 million figure and the roughly nine-month construction timeline reflect a project that turned into a bigger job than expected. The choice to drop the poker room says something about where the locals market is headed. Sports betting has boomed across the country, and a modern sportsbook plus a strong slot floor can pull in more everyday guests than a poker room. By reopening as Club Fortune North with Boomer's Sportsbook instead of poker tables, Truckee Gaming lined the property up with what today's locals players are actually looking for.

What Happens Next

Now that the doors are open, the near-term focus will be on building a steady base of regulars. A grand opening draws a crowd out of curiosity. The real test is whether people come back on a normal Tuesday. Expect Club Fortune North to lean on promotions, food deals, and the sportsbook rewards to turn first-time visitors into repeat guests. The 4 percent back for parimutuel horse racing bettors is one early example of that push.

Boomer's Sportsbook opening its 14th Nevada location here also hints at more growth for that brand across the state. Each new book adds another spot for the company to draw handle and build a following. If Club Fortune North performs well, it strengthens the case for the whole locals-focused model that Truckee Gaming is betting on, and it could encourage more updates or additions at the property down the road.

For the neighborhood, the thing to watch is the ripple. When an anchor property reopens on a busy corner, nearby owners often take notice. A cleaned-up, active casino can nudge neighboring lots toward new tenants, fresh paint, or their own upgrades. It can take time, and there is no guarantee, but that is often how a corner starts to turn. Local leaders in North Las Vegas have been pushing hard to attract investment, and a revived gaming property fits that story.

Homeowners and buyers in the area should simply keep an eye on the direction of the corridor. One reopening does not decide a neighborhood. A pattern of reopenings, new businesses, and steady jobs does. Club Fortune North is one more data point suggesting North Las Vegas is moving in the right direction, and it is worth tracking alongside the other projects popping up across the north end of the valley.
