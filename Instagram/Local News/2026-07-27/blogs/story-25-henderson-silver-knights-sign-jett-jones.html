Henderson Silver Knights Sign Forward Jett Jones

The Henderson Silver Knights signed forward Jett Jones, 23, to a one-year American Hockey League contract for the 2026-27 season, the team announced on July 20-21, 2026. Jones returns to Henderson as a reigning Kelly Cup champion, adding scoring depth and a familiar face to the roster ahead of new head coach Joel Ward's first year behind the bench.

If you follow hockey in southern Nevada, this is the kind of move that keeps a roster steady. Jones is not a stranger to the Silver Knights. He played parts of two seasons in Henderson from 2023 to 2025 before spending the 2025-26 season with the ECHL's Florida Everblades, where he helped win a championship. Now he is coming home to Lee's Family Forum. For families who live near the arena, or who are thinking about moving to Henderson, a move like this is a small but real sign of a sports community that keeps growing. As a local real estate agent, I pay attention to these things because a lively arena and a stable team are part of what makes a neighborhood feel alive. Let's walk through what happened, why it matters, and what comes next.

What Happened

The Henderson Silver Knights announced that they had signed forward Jett Jones to a one-year AHL contract for the 2026-27 season. The news came out around July 20 and 21, 2026, and was reported by Fox5 Vegas along with the team's own release. Jones is 23 years old and plays forward, and the deal keeps him with the organization for at least one more season.

This is a return, not a brand new arrival. Jones already knows the building, the staff, and the city. He suited up for the Silver Knights across parts of two seasons from 2023 through 2025. Then, for the 2025-26 season, he moved down a level to play in the ECHL with the Florida Everblades. That turned out to be a strong year for him. The Everblades won the Kelly Cup, which is the championship trophy of the ECHL, and Jones was part of that title run.

So the player coming back to Henderson is not the same player who left. He is a forward with a championship on his resume and a full season of playoff hockey under his belt. Winning a title takes more than skill. It takes long playoff series, tight games, and the kind of steady effort that coaches trust. Bringing that experience back to the Silver Knights locker room gives the younger players someone to lean on.

The timing matters too. This signing landed during the summer, which is when AHL teams build their rosters for the coming season. Contracts get announced, players get moved, and coaching staffs plan out who fits where. A one-year deal is common at this level. It gives both the team and the player a chance to see how the season goes without a long commitment. For Jones, it is a chance to prove he belongs. For the Silver Knights, it is a low-risk way to add a known, useful player.

It is also worth remembering how the Silver Knights fit into the bigger hockey picture. The Henderson Silver Knights are the AHL affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights, the NHL team that plays across the valley. The AHL is the top minor league, one step below the NHL. Players who do well in Henderson can get called up to play for the Golden Knights. That connection is a big part of why local hockey fans keep such a close eye on the Silver Knights roster.

Background and History

The Henderson Silver Knights began play as an expansion team in the AHL for the 2020-21 season. They were created to serve as the top development affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights, giving the NHL club a nearby place to grow young talent. Having the affiliate right in the valley is unusual and valuable. Most NHL teams keep their AHL clubs in far-off cities, so the close bond between Henderson and Vegas is a nice perk for local fans and for the players who move between the two.

The team's home is Lee's Family Forum, a modern arena in Henderson built with hockey in mind. The building seats about 5,567 people for hockey and cost roughly 84 million dollars to construct. It was built in partnership between the Vegas Golden Knights ownership group and the City of Henderson. The first Silver Knights game there was played on April 2, 2022. The venue has carried a few names over the years. It opened as The Dollar Loan Center and was renamed Lee's Family Forum in April 2024 as part of a partnership tied to the Lee family and their local business. That path, moving down to earn a bigger role and then coming back up, is a common and healthy route for young pros. The organization clearly saw enough in his growth to bring him back. Coach Joel Ward, entering his first season leading the Silver Knights, now inherits a player who already knows the system and carries a winning attitude.

What Happens Next

With the signing done, the next step is training camp and the buildup to the 2026-27 season. Jones will report with the rest of the group and work to earn his spot in the lineup. A one-year deal means he has real motivation to play well. A strong season could lead to a longer contract, more ice time, or even a look from the Vegas Golden Knights. A quiet season could send him back down. That is the nature of pro hockey at this level, and it tends to bring out a player's best effort.

For coach Joel Ward, this offseason is about shaping the roster and setting a tone in his first year. Signings like this one, along with the re-signing of players like defenseman Samuel Mayer and new staff hires, show a plan taking shape. Fans should expect more roster news through the summer as the team fills out its group. Each move gives a clearer picture of what the 2026-27 Silver Knights will look like.

For fans, the practical next steps are simple. The team has announced its 2026-27 regular season schedule, so now is the time to plan which games to attend. Season tickets, single game tickets, and promotional nights will all be part of the year at Lee's Family Forum. Watching how Jones performs early in the season will be one storyline worth following. If he picks up where his championship run left off, he could become a fan favorite again in Henderson.

It is also fair to keep expectations grounded. One player does not make or break a season, and a one-year AHL deal is a modest move by design. Still, depth signings are how good teams stay competitive over a long year. Injuries happen, call-ups happen, and having reliable, experienced forwards in reserve keeps a club steady. Jones gives the Silver Knights exactly that kind of insurance.

Keep an eye on how the roster comes together as camp nears. AHL rosters can shift right up to opening night, with two-way contracts and Golden Knights assignments changing the mix. Jones knowing the system should help him settle in fast, which matters in a season that runs deep into the spring. If the Silver Knights build a group that plays well together early, home games at Lee's Family Forum could become one of the better weeknight tickets in the valley. For local fans, the summer is the slow build, and the payoff comes when the puck drops in the fall.
