Vegas Jr. Golden Knights Summer Training Camp Returns to Summerlin

The Vegas Jr. Golden Knights are holding a summer training camp from July 27 through July 31, 2026, at City National Arena in Summerlin. The camp is a five-day development program for young hockey players who want to get better before the fall season starts. It is open to kids at different skill levels, and it takes place at the same rink the Vegas Golden Knights use for their own practices.

This matters to a lot of families in Las Vegas. Youth hockey has grown fast in Southern Nevada over the last several years, and the Jr. Golden Knights are the biggest youth program in the region. When a program like this runs a summer camp, it gives local kids a real chance to work on skating, stickhandling, and game sense with trained coaches. It also gives parents a place to send active kids during the hot summer months, inside a cool building that is built for hockey.

The timing lines up with the club's 2026-27 tryouts. Summer camp is often the first step for players who want to make a team in the fall. For newer players, it is a low-pressure way to test the sport. For kids who already play, it is a chance to sharpen skills and get noticed by coaches. Either way, the camp keeps young athletes moving during a stretch of the year when many other sports slow down. In this article, we will walk through what the camp offers, why it is a big deal for Las Vegas families, how youth hockey grew here, and what parents can do if they want to sign a child up.

What Happened

The Vegas Jr. Golden Knights scheduled a summer training camp for July 27 through July 31, 2026. The camp runs at City National Arena, which sits in the Downtown Summerlin area on the west side of the Las Vegas valley. The arena has two full-size ice sheets, which means a camp can run several groups at once without crowding the ice. That setup lets coaches split players by age and skill so each group gets drills that fit their level.

A summer camp like this usually mixes on-ice work with skill teaching. Players spend time on skating power, edge control, and quick turns. They practice passing and receiving the puck, shooting, and reading the play around them. Many camps also add small games, or scrimmages, so kids can use what they learned in a real setting. Coaches watch closely and give feedback so each player leaves with a few clear things to work on. Since the Golden Knights arrived, interest in hockey has climbed across Clark County. Kids who might never have tried the sport now have real programs, real coaches, and real places to play. The Jr. Golden Knights camp is a clear sign of how far youth hockey has come in Southern Nevada.

For parents, a summer camp solves a practical problem. Las Vegas summers are hot, often well above 100 degrees. Outdoor sports can be tough and even risky in that heat. An ice rink is one of the few places where kids can be very active and stay cool at the same time. A five-day camp gives families a safe, structured way to keep children busy, moving, and learning a new skill during a slow part of the calendar.

The camp also matters for what it teaches beyond hockey. Team sports help kids build confidence, discipline, and the habit of showing up and working hard. Hockey adds skating, which is a full-body skill that takes real practice. Kids who stick with it learn how to fall down and get back up, both on the ice and in life. Those lessons stay with a child long after the season ends.

Background and History

The Vegas Golden Knights joined the National Hockey League as an expansion team in 2017. Their arrival changed hockey in the desert almost overnight. The team's early success drew huge crowds and turned many locals into fans. That wave of interest reached kids, and youth programs grew to meet the demand.

City National Arena opened in 2017 as the Golden Knights' practice home. Built in Summerlin, it gave the valley a top-quality place to skate. Before facilities like this, ice time in Las Vegas was harder to find. With two sheets of ice under one roof, City National Arena made room for pro practices, public skating, adult leagues, and youth programs all in the same building. That kind of space is what a growing hockey community needs.

The Jr. Golden Knights program grew alongside the pro team. As the region's flagship youth development organization, the club built out teams across many age groups. It gave beginners a place to start and gave more advanced players a path to real competition. Summer camps and seasonal tryouts became a regular part of the yearly rhythm. Over time, more Las Vegas kids have grown up playing organized hockey, something that was rare here not long ago.

The region's hockey scene now stretches beyond just youth and the NHL club. The Henderson Silver Knights play in the American Hockey League as the Golden Knights' top minor-league affiliate, giving fans another team to follow. There is even a professional women's hockey presence tied to the market. Each layer adds to a hockey culture that keeps building in Southern Nevada. Youth camps like this one sit at the base of that whole pyramid, feeding the sport with new players every year.
