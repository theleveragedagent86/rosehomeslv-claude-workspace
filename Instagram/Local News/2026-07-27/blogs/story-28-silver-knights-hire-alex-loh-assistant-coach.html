Silver Knights Hire Alex Loh as Assistant Coach

The Henderson Silver Knights hired Alex Loh, 41, as an assistant coach. General Manager Tim Speltz announced the move on July 15, 2026, and it completes the coaching staff for the team's first season under new head coach Joel Ward.

Loh is not a new face inside the Vegas Golden Knights family. He has worked in the organization since 2022, and most recently he was the head coach of the Tahoe Knight Monsters. Now he moves to Henderson, where he will run the forward group and take charge of the power play. That is a big job on any hockey team, and it puts Loh at the center of how the Silver Knights score goals this season.

For people who live in Henderson, this is more than a sports headline. The Silver Knights play at Lee's Family Forum, a city-owned arena that sits right in the middle of our community. When the team builds a strong coaching staff, it means better hockey, more sold-out nights, and more energy in the neighborhoods around the building. This article breaks down who Alex Loh is, what his hiring means, and why a hockey coaching move matters to home life in southern Nevada.

What Happened

On July 15, 2026, the Henderson Silver Knights made it official. General Manager Tim Speltz announced that the team had hired Alex Loh as an assistant coach. Loh joins the staff of Joel Ward, who is stepping into his first year as the Silver Knights head coach. With this hire, the coaching staff for the 2026-27 season is now complete.

Loh is 41 years old, and he brings real experience to the bench. He has been part of the Vegas Golden Knights organization since 2022. That means he already understands how the Golden Knights want their teams to play, from the top pro club all the way down through the minor league levels. Before coming to Henderson, Loh served as the head coach of the Tahoe Knight Monsters. Being a head coach is a demanding role, so moving into an assistant job with a higher-level team shows that the Silver Knights value what he can teach.

His main duties are clear. Loh will primarily handle the forward group and the power play. In hockey, forwards are the players who lead the attack and score most of the goals. The power play is the special situation when the other team has a player in the penalty box, giving the Silver Knights a one-player advantage on the ice. Teams that win the power-play battle often win the game, so handing this job to Loh tells you the team trusts his eye for offense.

The Henderson Silver Knights are the American Hockey League affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights. That connection matters. Players who develop well in Henderson can get called up to play in the National Hockey League with the Golden Knights. So the coaching staff in Henderson is not just trying to win games. They are helping young players grow into pros. A coach like Loh, who knows both the head coaching side and the details of offense, fits that mission.

The announcement came directly from the team through its official channels and the ECHL and AHL news networks. It was reported as a straightforward roster and staff move, part of the normal offseason work every hockey club does before training camp. There was no drama here, just a club quietly putting the right people in place. When you add a completed coaching staff to a returning core of players, you get a team that can hit the ground running when the season starts.

It also helps to understand the role of an assistant coach. The head coach, in this case Joel Ward, sets the overall plan and makes the final calls on game day. Assistant coaches take charge of specific parts of the team. By giving Loh the forwards and the power play, Ward frees himself to focus on the whole picture while trusting an experienced hand to sharpen the attack. Good teams share the load this way. When each coach owns a clear area, players get more focused teaching and fewer mixed messages. That is why the details of a hire like this one matter more than they might seem at first glance.

It is owned by the city of Henderson and operated by Black Knight Sports and Entertainment, the same parent company behind the Vegas Golden Knights. When the Golden Knights invest in strong leadership for their Henderson affiliate, it shows they are committed to keeping pro hockey in our city for the long haul. A team that keeps hiring quality coaches like Alex Loh is a team that plans to stay.

A good coaching hire helps keep those game nights fun and worth attending.

It is worth remembering how much Henderson has grown alongside its sports scene. Not long ago, southern Nevada had no pro hockey at all. Now there is an NHL team in Las Vegas and a top minor league club right here in Henderson. That growth has matched a wave of new homes, new schools, and new shopping in the area. Sports and housing rise together because both depend on the same thing, which is people choosing to build their lives here. Background and History

The Henderson Silver Knights joined the American Hockey League in 2020 as the top minor league affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights. The idea was simple. The Golden Knights wanted a nearby team where young players could develop before jumping to the NHL. Having that affiliate right in Henderson, instead of in another state, means players and coaches move between the two clubs easily.

The team's home arena has a short but busy history. It opened as a new building in Henderson, and the first Silver Knights game there was played on April 2, 2022. On April 2, 2024, exactly two years later, the arena was renamed Lee's Family Forum. Today it is home to more than just hockey. The Vegas Knight Hawks of the Indoor Football League, the Vegas Thrill of the Pro Volleyball Federation, and the Las Vegas Desert Dogs of the National Lacrosse League all play there too. That mix makes the building a true year-round hub for Henderson sports.

Alex Loh's path fits the way the Golden Knights build from within. He joined the organization in 2022, the same year the Henderson arena opened its doors to hockey. Since then he worked his way through coaching roles, including his most recent job as head coach of the Tahoe Knight Monsters. Coaches who stay inside one organization learn its systems deeply, and that helps players move up smoothly when they get promoted.

This offseason has been about setting up a new chapter. Joel Ward is taking over as head coach for the first time, and the front office spent the summer filling out his staff. Hiring Loh to run the forwards and power play was one of the final pieces. With Tim Speltz leading as general manager, the club has focused on experienced hockey people who already speak the Golden Knights language.

What Happens Next

With the coaching staff complete, the focus now turns to the season ahead. Alex Loh will get to work with the forward group during training camp, teaching the systems that Joel Ward wants his team to play. Camp is where coaches and players first come together, test line combinations, and set the tone for the year. Loh's early job will be to figure out which forwards fit best together and how to build a power play that scores.

The power play is worth watching closely. It is one of the clearest ways to judge a coach's offensive plan. If the Silver Knights climb the league rankings on the power play this season, fans will know Loh's fingerprints are on that success. If the unit struggles early, expect him to keep adjusting the setup until it clicks. Either way, this is the part of the game he owns, and results there will tell the story.

Beyond the ice, the arena will keep its busy calendar. Silver Knights home games at Lee's Family Forum will share the building with football, volleyball, and lacrosse dates through the year. For residents, that means plenty of chances to catch a game close to home. The regular season schedule gives families a steady stream of affordable entertainment nights right in Henderson.

There is also the bigger development picture. Because the Silver Knights feed players up to the Golden Knights, a strong coaching season in Henderson can shape the NHL club for years. If Loh helps a young forward break out, that player might be scoring goals in Las Vegas before long. That pipeline is exactly why the team invests in coaches who understand both winning now and building for the future.
