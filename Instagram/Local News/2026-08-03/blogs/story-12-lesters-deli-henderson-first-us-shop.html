Lester's Deli Opens First US Shop in Henderson

Lester's Deli, a 75-year-old smoked-meat institution from Montreal, is coming to Henderson at 1590 W. Horizon Ridge. This is the brand's first location outside Canada and its very first shop in the United States, which makes it a big deal for local food fans and for the neighborhood around it.

For three quarters of a century, people in Montreal have lined up for Lester's hand-cut smoked meat piled high on rye bread. Now that same deli is setting up in the southeast part of the Las Vegas Valley. A kitchen hood permit was filed in early July, which is one of the first real signs that a restaurant is getting a space ready to cook. There is no firm grand-opening date set yet, so exactly when you can walk in and order is still up in the air. But the paperwork tells us this is moving from rumor to reality.

If you live in Henderson or anywhere in the valley, this is more than just another restaurant opening. It is a well-known name choosing our area for a history-making first. In this article I will walk through what we know so far, why it matters for the neighborhood and for home values nearby, a little history on the deli itself, what happens next, and simple steps you can take if you want to follow along or plan a visit.

What Happened

Here is the short version. Lester's Deli, the Montreal smoked-meat brand that has been around for 75 years, is opening a shop in Henderson. The address is 1590 W. Horizon Ridge Parkway. This spot will be the first Lester's location outside of Canada, and the first one anywhere in the United States. That is the headline that has food lovers talking.

The clue that tipped people off was a kitchen hood permit filed in early July. A kitchen hood is the big metal vent that hangs over a commercial stove and pulls smoke, steam, and grease out of the air. Cities require a permit and an inspection before a restaurant can run one. When a business files for a hood permit, it usually means they have signed a lease and are starting to build out the kitchen. It is one of the clearest early signs that a real restaurant, not just an idea, is on the way.

What we do not have yet is a firm grand-opening date. Restaurant build-outs take time. There are permits to pull, equipment to install, health inspections to pass, and staff to hire and train. Any of these steps can move faster or slower than planned. So while the hood permit tells us the project is active, it does not lock in a day when the doors open. Anyone who tells you the exact opening date right now is guessing.

The location itself sits in a busy part of Henderson. Horizon Ridge Parkway is a main road that runs through the Green Valley and MacDonald Ranch areas. It is lined with shopping centers, offices, and homes. This is not a far-off corner of the desert. It is a spot where lots of people already drive, shop, and eat every day. Placing a first-in-the-country restaurant here says something about how the brand sees Henderson: as a place with enough steady traffic and enough food-loving residents to support a name that has real history behind it.

It is also worth being clear about what smoked meat is, since not everyone in the valley grew up with it. Montreal smoked meat is a cured and smoked beef, usually brisket, that gets seasoned with a blend of spices and then steamed until it is tender. It is often compared to New York pastrami, but fans will tell you the spice mix and the texture are different. The classic order is a stacked sandwich on rye with yellow mustard, a side of fries or coleslaw, and a pickle. That simple plate is what built Lester's reputation over 75 years.

Background and History

Lester's Deli has been serving Montreal since around 1951. That is 75 years of feeding families, workers, and travelers. In a restaurant world where many places do not last five years, lasting 75 is rare. It means generations of customers have grown up on the same sandwiches. Grandparents brought their kids, and those kids brought their own kids. That kind of loyalty is what turns a deli into an institution.

Montreal has a deep smoked-meat culture. The city is famous for it, the same way some cities are known for barbecue or deep-dish pizza. A handful of delis there are treated almost like landmarks. Lester's is part of that group. Over the decades the brand has built a name not just for the meat itself but for the whole experience: the counter, the rye, the mustard, the pickles, and the no-fuss feel of a place that knows exactly what it does well.

What makes the Henderson news stand out is that Lester's has stayed a Canadian story for all of those 75 years. Plenty of famous restaurants expand quickly once they catch on. Lester's did not rush across the border. So when the brand finally chooses to open its first shop outside Canada, and that shop lands in Henderson, it is a genuine milestone. Our valley is not getting a copy of a chain that is already everywhere. We are getting the first step of a brand's move into a whole new country.

It is a pattern we have seen more of lately in the valley. Well-known out-of-town names keep picking Southern Nevada for big first steps. Just this summer, a famous Los Angeles taqueria chose a Las Vegas food hall for its first spot outside California. These moves show that operators see our market as a place worth planting a flag. Steady population growth, a strong tourism base, and a crowd that loves to try new food all make the valley attractive. Lester's joining that list is one more sign of the same trend.

What Happens Next

The honest answer is that we are in the waiting stage. The kitchen hood permit from early July tells us the project is real and moving, but a lot still has to happen before opening day. Here is the general path most restaurants follow, so you know what to watch for.

First comes the build-out. Crews frame walls, run plumbing and electrical, and install the kitchen equipment, including that hood. Next come inspections. The city checks the building work, the fire safety systems, and the health standards. A restaurant cannot legally serve food until it passes these. After that comes hiring and training. A deli lives or dies on getting the smoked meat right, so training the kitchen staff to hit the brand's standard takes real care. Only then does a soft opening or grand opening happen.

Because no firm grand-opening date has been set, I would not circle a day on your calendar just yet. Timelines slip all the time in the restaurant world. A single failed inspection or a delayed piece of equipment can push things back weeks. On the flip side, sometimes a build-out goes smoothly and a place opens sooner than expected. The smart move is to follow the signs rather than trust a rumored date.

Good signs to watch for as opening gets closer include window signage or a banner at 1590 W. Horizon Ridge, a hiring push on job sites and social media, and the deli setting up its own local pages to announce a date. Restaurants almost always ramp up their social media right before opening because they want a crowd on day one. When you see them posting photos of the space and the food, the opening is usually near.

I will keep an eye on this one myself. When a firm date lands, or when the deli starts hiring, that is the moment to plan your first visit. Until then, treat any exact date you hear as a maybe, not a promise.
