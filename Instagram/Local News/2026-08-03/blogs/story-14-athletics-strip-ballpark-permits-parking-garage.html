Athletics File New Permits for Parking Garage and Utility Plant at Las Vegas Strip Ballpark

The Athletics took another big step toward opening their new ballpark on the Las Vegas Strip. On July 30, 2026, the team filed permits to build a six-story parking garage, a central utility plant, and a plaza at the stadium site. This matters because it shows the roughly $2 billion project is moving from planning into real, on-the-ground construction. For anyone who lives here, works near the Strip, or owns a home in the area, these filings are a clear sign that a major league baseball team is coming to Southern Nevada.

The stadium will sit at Las Vegas Boulevard and Tropicana Avenue, in unincorporated Clark County. It is planned to hold about 33,000 seats. The team is aiming to open the ballpark in February 2028. That is a tight timeline, and every permit that gets filed brings the project closer to reality. The new filings cover the pieces of the project that most fans never think about but that no stadium can open without. You cannot host tens of thousands of people without a place to park, power to run the building, and a plaza to move crowds in and out safely.

What Happened

On July 30, 2026, the Athletics submitted a fresh set of construction permits for their Las Vegas Strip ballpark. The filings, first reported by Fox5 Vegas and News3LV, cover three separate parts of the project. The first is a six-story parking garage. The second is a central utility plant. The third is a plaza area outside the stadium. Together, these three pieces make up the next phase of infrastructure work at the site.

Let us break down what each part actually does. A six-story parking garage gives fans a place to leave their cars on game days. With a 33,000-seat stadium, parking is one of the biggest challenges any team faces. A garage that tall can hold thousands of vehicles in a small footprint, which matters a lot on the Strip where land is expensive and space is tight. Building up instead of out is the smart way to handle parking in a crowded area.

The central utility plant is the heart of the building's systems. This is where the power, heating, cooling, and water systems come together to run the whole stadium. In a place like Las Vegas, where summer temperatures often climb past 110 degrees, a strong cooling system is not a luxury. It is a must. A central utility plant keeps fans comfortable and keeps the stadium running during long, hot games. Without it, the building simply cannot open its doors.

The plaza is the open space where fans gather before and after games. Think of it as the front porch of the stadium. It is where crowds enter, where people meet up, and where the energy of game day builds. A well-designed plaza helps move large numbers of people safely and quickly, which is a real concern at a busy Strip location.

These filings mark the next infrastructure phase at the site. Earlier work already cleared the way for the main stadium structure. Now the team is adding the support systems that make a stadium function. Filing permits is not the same as finishing the work, but it is a required legal step. Clark County has to review and approve the plans before crews can build. So these filings tell us the team is staying on schedule and pushing the project forward piece by piece toward that February 2028 goal.

Background and History

The Athletics have a long history in Major League Baseball, and their path to Las Vegas has been closely watched for years. The team spent decades in Oakland before deciding to move. The choice to relocate to Southern Nevada put Las Vegas on the map as a growing hub for professional sports. The valley already had the Vegas Golden Knights in hockey and the Raiders in football at Allegiant Stadium. Baseball adds another major league team to that lineup.

The chosen site sits at Las Vegas Boulevard and Tropicana Avenue, one of the most famous corners in the world. This spot is in the heart of the Strip, which means the ballpark will be right in the middle of the action. That location is a big part of why the project has drawn so much attention. A stadium in this spot can pull in both locals and the millions of tourists who visit Las Vegas each year. It also fits the city's growing image as a place that hosts big events, from concerts to championship games.

The project has moved through many steps to reach this point. Plans for a roughly $2 billion stadium with about 33,000 seats had to clear reviews, approvals, and financing hurdles. Each phase brings its own set of permits and paperwork. The July 30 filings for the garage, utility plant, and plaza are part of that ongoing process. They follow earlier work on the main stadium and show that the project keeps advancing through the required legal and building steps. The February 2028 target date has stayed in place, which suggests the team is working to keep the timeline on track.
