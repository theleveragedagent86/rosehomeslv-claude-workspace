PWHL Las Vegas Builds Its First Roster and Opens Season Ticket Memberships

Professional women's hockey is coming to Las Vegas, and the new team is now signing its first players and taking season ticket deposits for just $50. The Professional Women's Hockey League, known as the PWHL, is building the inaugural roster for its Las Vegas club. Early signings include goaltender Nicole Hensley along with forwards Katy Knoll, Maureen Murphy, and Hayley Scamurra. The team will wear green and gold, and it plans to play its home games at T-Mobile Arena on the Strip.

This matters because it gives the valley a second major pro hockey team to rally behind, right alongside the Vegas Golden Knights. For families, it means another affordable night out on the ice. For the city, it is one more sign that Las Vegas keeps growing into a true sports town. And for anyone thinking about putting down roots here, a new team is a small but real boost to the local economy and to neighborhood pride.

One thing is still missing. The team does not have a name yet. That has not slowed down the excitement. Fans are already picking their favorite players, guessing at the name, and locking in their spot for the first season with a small deposit. Below, I break down what has happened so far, why it matters for people who live here, and exactly what you can do if you want in.

What Happened

The PWHL is putting together the roster for its brand new Las Vegas team. A roster is just the list of players who will suit up and play for the club. Building one from scratch takes time, so the team is adding names in waves rather than all at once. The first group of announced players gives fans an early look at who they will be cheering for.

Nicole Hensley is one of the first names on the list. She is a goaltender, which is the player who guards the net and stops shots. Hensley is well known in women's hockey and has played at a high level for years. Having a strong goalie in place early is a smart way to start a team, because good goaltending can keep a young club in almost every game.

The team also added three forwards. Forwards are the players who focus on scoring and creating chances near the other team's net. Those forwards are Katy Knoll, Maureen Murphy, and Hayley Scamurra. Each brings her own style and experience. Together with Hensley, they form the early core that the rest of the roster will be built around. More signings are expected as the season gets closer.

At the same time, the team opened season ticket memberships. A season ticket membership lets you claim your seats for the whole season instead of buying one game at a time. To get started, fans put down a $50 deposit. That deposit holds your place in line and gives you first access when full pricing and seat choices are released. It is a low-cost way to show interest and to be among the first fans in the door.

The team has also revealed its colors. The club will wear green and gold. Team colors might sound like a small detail, but they matter. Colors show up on jerseys, hats, banners, and all over social media. They help build an identity long before the first puck drops. Green and gold give the new team a bold, easy to spot look that fans can wear with pride.

The one big piece still missing is the team name. As of now, the club has not announced what it will be called. Leagues often hold the name back on purpose to build interest and to let fans feel part of the reveal. Expect the name, along with a logo, to be a major moment when it finally drops. For now, the green and gold colors and the first players are what fans have to go on, and that has been more than enough to get people talking.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

For a long time, Las Vegas was seen as a place people visited, not a place with its own deep sports roots. That has changed fast. The Golden Knights, the Raiders, the Aces, and the incoming Athletics baseball team have all helped turn the valley into a real sports market. Adding a PWHL team keeps that trend going and puts women's pro hockey right in the middle of it.

For families, the biggest draw may be cost. Major sports tickets can get pricey, and a night at a big game adds up quickly once you count parking, food, and drinks. Newer leagues and teams often price their tickets to be more welcoming, especially early on. A $50 deposit to reserve a season membership is a low bar to clear. For parents who want to get their kids to live games without spending a fortune, this could be one of the more affordable options in town.

There is also the youth angle. Las Vegas has a growing hockey community, with kids of all ages learning the game at local rinks. Having a women's pro team in town gives girls who play a clear path to look up to. When young players can watch pros who look like them and dream of doing the same thing, it can change how they see their own future. That kind of role model effect is hard to measure but very real.

Then there is the neighborhood and economy side, which is where I pay close attention as a local agent. Pro teams bring people out. Game nights fill nearby restaurants, bars, and shops. Hotels near the arena get more bookings on game days. Part-time and seasonal jobs open up at the venue. None of this makes or breaks the valley on its own, but each new team adds another reason for people and money to flow into the area.

Sports also shape how people feel about where they live. A shared team gives neighbors something to talk about, a reason to wear the same colors, and a common thing to celebrate. That sense of pride is a quiet part of what makes a place feel like home. For folks who are new to the valley or thinking about moving here, a growing sports scene is one more sign that Las Vegas is a full community, not just a vacation stop. All of that adds up, and it is why a story like this is worth more than a passing glance.

Background and History

The PWHL is a young league. It brought together the top players in women's hockey under one roof and played its first season in early 2024. Before that, women's pro hockey in North America had bounced between smaller leagues that struggled to stay funded. The PWHL was built to fix that, with steady backing and a plan to grow city by city. In its short life, it has drawn strong crowds and set attendance records that surprised a lot of doubters.

The league started with a handful of teams in the United States and Canada. After its early success, it began looking at new markets to expand into. Las Vegas is one of the cities now part of that growth. The pick makes sense. The Golden Knights proved that hockey can thrive in the desert, drawing big, loud crowds from day one. If NHL hockey works here, league leaders clearly believe women's pro hockey can find a home too.

Playing at T-Mobile Arena also puts the new team on a big stage. That arena sits right on the Strip and already hosts the Golden Knights and major events. It is one of the most well known venues in the country. Starting out in a building of that size and profile is a strong statement about how seriously the league is treating this market. It tells fans this is a real, major league effort, not a small side project.

It is worth being clear about what is still up in the air. The team name and logo have not been announced. The full roster is not set. Exact ticket prices, the game schedule, and the season start details will come out over time. As those pieces are confirmed, the picture will get sharper. For now, the core facts are the early players, the green and gold colors, the $50 season deposits, and the T-Mobile Arena home. Anything beyond that should be treated as still to come. What Happens Next

The next big steps are easy to spot. First, expect the team name and logo reveal. This is usually a planned event with plenty of buildup, and it often lets fans take part in some way. When it lands, it will give the club its full identity and likely spark a wave of new interest and merchandise. Keep an eye out, because this is the moment a lot of casual fans jump on board.

Second, look for more roster moves. The four early players are just the start. The team will keep signing skaters and goalies until it has a full lineup. Some names may be stars you already know from national teams or past pro seasons. Others will be up and coming players trying to make their mark. Each signing is a chance to learn more about the style and identity the team wants to build.

Third, watch for full ticket details. Right now the entry point is the $50 deposit. Later, the team should release seat locations, membership levels, and real prices. Fans who put down a deposit early usually get first pick and the best access. If you care about where you sit or want a specific price range, getting in early tends to pay off.

Finally, the season itself. Once the schedule is set, fans will know when the first home game is and who the team plays. That opening night at T-Mobile Arena will be a milestone for the franchise and for the city. Between now and then, expect a steady drip of news. The smart move is to follow the team's official channels so you hear each update as soon as it drops, instead of finding out after tickets are gone.
