Cadence and Summerlin Rank Among the Nation's Top-Selling Communities in 2026

Two Southern Nevada neighborhoods just landed near the top of a national list. In its mid-2026 report, the real estate advisory firm RCLCO ranked Cadence in Henderson as the fifth top-selling master-planned community in the United States, with 657 home sales. Summerlin in Las Vegas came in eighth, with 547 sales. That matters because it shows builders and buyers are still betting big on Las Vegas, even while home prices, mortgage rates, and inventory keep shifting across the valley.

These rankings are not just bragging rights. They tell you where new homes are going up, where builders are spending their money, and where a lot of families are choosing to plant roots. When a community sells hundreds of homes in half a year, it usually means there is fresh construction, new schools, new parks, and new stores on the way. For anyone thinking about buying, selling, or just tracking home values in Clark County, that is useful information. In this article, I will break down what the numbers say, why Cadence went one way and Summerlin went the other, and what it all means if you live here or want to.

What Happened

Every year, RCLCO, a national real estate consulting firm, tracks how many homes sell in the biggest master-planned communities across the country. A master-planned community is a large neighborhood that is designed all at once, with homes, parks, schools, trails, and shops laid out together under one long-term plan. RCLCO puts out a report in the middle of the year and another at the end of the year. Builders, investors, and city planners watch these lists closely because they show which communities are pulling in the most buyers.

In the mid-2026 report, Cadence in Henderson ranked fifth in the whole country. It recorded 657 home sales in the first half of the year. That is a strong number, but it was down about 9% compared to the same period a year earlier. So Cadence sold a lot of homes, just a little fewer than before.

Summerlin in Las Vegas ranked eighth nationally with 547 home sales. Unlike Cadence, Summerlin's sales went up about 6% year over year. So Summerlin sold fewer total homes than Cadence, but it was moving in the opposite direction, climbing instead of dipping.

Put those two numbers side by side and you get an interesting picture. Both communities are still among the top handful of best-selling neighborhoods in the entire United States. That is rare. Most metro areas would be thrilled to have one community on this list. Las Vegas has two in the top ten, one in Henderson and one on the west side of town. That says a lot about how much demand there is for new homes here.

It is worth noting what these figures do and do not tell us. They count new home sales inside the community, so they mostly reflect brand-new construction, not resales of older homes. A 9% drop at Cadence does not mean home values fell. It usually means fewer new homes closed, which can happen when a builder runs low on ready-to-sell lots, raises prices, or waits on the next phase of construction. A 6% gain at Summerlin means more new homes changed hands, which often lines up with fresh neighborhoods opening. The Las Vegas Review-Journal reported the rankings and framed both results as a sign of steady builder demand in Southern Nevada.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might wonder why a national ranking should matter to you if you are not shopping for a new build. The answer is that these communities shape the whole local market. When Cadence and Summerlin sell hundreds of homes, they set the tone for prices, competition, and even traffic across the valley.

First, think about home values. New home sales help set the going rate for the area. When builders price a new three-bedroom home at a certain level, nearby resale homes tend to follow. So if you own a home near Cadence in east Henderson or near Summerlin on the west side, what happens in those big communities can nudge your own value up or down. Strong sales usually support steady prices, which is good news for owners.

Second, think about choice. Both communities are still building, which means buyers have real options. If you want a brand-new home with a warranty, modern layout, and energy-efficient features, these are two of the biggest places in the country to find one. That is a real advantage for local families, first-time buyers, and folks moving here from out of state.

Third, think about the ripple effects. Big communities bring more than houses. They bring schools, parks, grocery stores, medical offices, and jobs. When a community keeps selling, the developer keeps investing in those extras. That can raise the quality of life for everyone nearby, not just the people who buy the new homes. It can also add traffic and fill up schools, so it cuts both ways.

Fourth, the split between the two communities is a useful signal. Cadence dipping while Summerlin climbs shows that the market is not one single thing. Different price points, different parts of town, and different buyer groups can move in different directions at the same time. If you are trying to time a purchase or a sale, that is a reminder to look at your specific neighborhood, not just the valley-wide headline. The overall Las Vegas market has seen rising inventory and mortgage rates ticking up toward the mid-6% range in 2026, so where you buy really does matter.

There is also a jobs angle worth keeping in mind. Communities that keep selling hundreds of homes support a lot of local work. Construction crews, landscapers, roofers, painters, and suppliers all stay busy when builders keep breaking ground. Real estate agents, lenders, title officers, and moving companies get work too. So when you read that Cadence and Summerlin are among the busiest communities in the country, that also means paychecks for a lot of your neighbors. A healthy building pipeline is one of the quiet engines of the local economy, and these two communities are a big part of it.

Background and History

To understand these rankings, it helps to know the two communities. Summerlin sits on the western edge of Las Vegas, up against the foothills near Red Rock Canyon. It is one of the largest master-planned communities in the country and has been in development for decades. In fact, 2026 marks its 36th year of development, and it continues to open new neighborhoods in its western villages. Over the years, Summerlin has grown into a collection of villages with parks, trails, golf, and its own downtown shopping and entertainment district. Because it has been at this for so long, Summerlin has appeared on the national top-selling lists many times.

Cadence is younger. It is a master-planned community in Henderson, on the eastern side of the valley. Cadence was built on land that was cleaned up and redeveloped, and it has grown quickly by focusing on parks, trails, and a strong sense of community events. It offers a range of home styles and price points, and it has become known as a spot where buyers can sometimes find newer homes at more attainable prices than the far west side. Local market reports have pointed to Cadence as one of Henderson's entry points, with pricing that tends to run below the city's overall median. That value story has helped Cadence climb the national rankings fast.

RCLCO's rankings go back many years and are widely seen as the standard scorecard for this kind of large-scale community. Making the top five or top ten nationally is a real achievement, and Las Vegas has become a regular presence on the list. That reflects the region's long run of population growth and its steady flow of new residents from higher-cost states.

What Happens Next

So where does this go from here? The mid-year report is a snapshot, not the final word. RCLCO will put out an end-of-year 2026 report, and that will show whether these trends held up through the second half of the year. Watch to see if Cadence steadies its sales or keeps easing, and whether Summerlin holds on to its growth.

A few things will shape the next report. Mortgage rates are one. In 2026, the 30-year rate has been hovering in the mid-6% range, higher than many buyers would like. If rates ease, new home sales in both communities could pick up. If rates climb, sales could cool. Builders often respond by offering incentives, like rate buydowns or help with closing costs, to keep homes moving. That is something buyers should ask about directly.

Supply is another factor. Cadence's dip may partly reflect where it is in its building schedule. As new phases open, sales can jump again. Summerlin keeps opening neighborhoods in its western villages, so it has plenty of new product coming. More available homes usually means more sales, as long as buyers keep showing up.

Finally, keep an eye on the broader valley. Single-family inventory has been rising, giving buyers more to choose from than they had a couple of years ago. If that continues, buyers gain a little more power to negotiate, and even top communities have to compete harder for each sale. That competition tends to be good for buyers and something sellers need to plan around.

It is also smart to watch the second-half rankings alongside the wider list. RCLCO tracks dozens of communities across states like Texas, Florida, Arizona, and the Carolinas, many of which compete with Las Vegas for the same out-of-state buyers. If those Sun Belt markets speed up or slow down, it can shift where the top spots land. Las Vegas holding two places in the top ten through a year of higher rates would be a strong showing. If one of our communities slips a few spots, that will not mean the local market is in trouble, only that a different city had a busy stretch. Read the rankings as a trend line over several reports, not a single grade on one test.

Ryan's Take

Here is how I read this as a local agent. Two communities in the national top ten is a genuinely strong sign for Las Vegas. It tells me demand for new homes here is real and durable, not a short-term blip. People still want to move to Southern Nevada, and builders still believe in that future enough to keep pouring foundations.

The split between Cadence and Summerlin is the part I find most useful. Cadence dipping 9% is not a warning sign to me. It looks like a normal swing tied to lot supply and pricing, and Cadence remains a great value play in Henderson for buyers who want more home for the money. Summerlin rising 6% shows that even a mature, higher-priced community can keep growing when it keeps opening fresh neighborhoods with strong amenities.

My honest advice: do not buy a home because it is in a top-ranked community, and do not skip one for the same reason. Rankings measure sales volume, not whether a specific house fits your life and budget. Focus on your commute, your schools, your monthly payment, and the resale strength of the exact street you are considering. Both of these communities can be smart buys. So can plenty of neighborhoods that will never make a national list. The ranking is a starting point for your search, not the finish line.

What You Can Do

If this news has you thinking about a move, here are some practical steps. First, get clear on your budget before you fall in love with a floor plan. Ask a lender what your monthly payment would look like at today's rates, and ask builders in Cadence and Summerlin whether they offer rate buydowns or closing-cost help. Those incentives can be worth thousands of dollars.

Second, compare new construction with nearby resale homes. A brand-new home has a warranty and modern features, but an established home may cost less and come with a finished yard and window coverings. Look at both before you decide.

Third, if you already own in or near these communities, use this news as a reason to check your home's value. Steady sales in top-selling neighborhoods often support pricing, and you may have more equity than you think. That equity could open the door to a move, a remodel, or an investment.

Fourth, tour more than one village or phase. Cadence and Summerlin both have several sub-neighborhoods, and prices, lot sizes, and vibes vary from one to the next. Walk them at different times of day and picture your real routine.
