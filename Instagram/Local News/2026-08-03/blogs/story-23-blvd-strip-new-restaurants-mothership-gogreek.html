Four New Restaurants Are Coming to BLVD on the Strip, Including Mothership Coffee and GoGreek

BLVD, the big new shopping and dining project on the Las Vegas Strip, just named four more food and drink tenants. The new lineup includes local favorite Mothership Coffee, Greek fast-casual spot GoGreek, the Japanese teppan chain Pepper Lunch, and the TENSHOU Collection with its Bar Centifolia. These new spots are targeted to open in the first few months of 2027. That matters because it gives both locals and visitors more everyday, walk-up dining choices right on the Strip, and it is another sign that the center of the Strip is filling in fast.

For years, much of the Strip has been built around big casino resorts. If you wanted a quick cup of coffee or a simple plate of food without walking through a casino floor, your choices were limited. BLVD is trying to change that. It is an open-air center packed with stores, restaurants, and gathering spaces, all facing the street. The addition of a homegrown coffee roaster like Mothership sends a clear message. The people behind BLVD want the place to feel local and familiar, not just flashy and tourist-only. In this article, we will break down who is coming, what the timeline looks like, why it matters for people who live here, and how it fits into the bigger story of the Strip.

What Happened

On July 7, 2026, news broke that BLVD on the Strip had added several new tenants to its growing list. The reveal named the TENSHOU Collection, which comes with a spot called Bar Centifolia. It also named Pepper Lunch, Mothership Coffee, and GoGreek. All of these are targeted to open in the first quarter of 2027, which covers January, February, and March of that year. That means the doors are not open yet. But the deals are signed, and the plans are moving forward.

Let us walk through each one. Mothership Coffee is the name that jumped out to a lot of locals. Mothership is a Las Vegas coffee brand that roasts its own beans and has built a loyal following in the valley over the years. Bringing a homegrown roaster to the Strip is a big deal. Many coffee spots on the Strip are national chains or hotel cafes. A local roaster stepping into that space is a chance for visitors to taste something made right here in Southern Nevada.

GoGreek is a fast-casual Greek concept. Fast-casual means you order at a counter, your food is made fresh, and you can eat there or take it to go. Think gyros, grilled meats, fresh salads, pita, and rice bowls. This style of food fits the Strip well because it is quick, it travels easily, and it works for people on the move. Greek food also gives folks a lighter, fresher option compared to heavy buffet or steakhouse meals.

Pepper Lunch is a Japanese chain known for its sizzling hot iron plates. At Pepper Lunch, your food often arrives on a very hot metal plate, and you finish cooking it yourself right at the table. It is fun to watch, and it is popular with families and younger diners. The chain has locations around the world, and its arrival at BLVD adds an interactive, hands-on choice to the mix.

The TENSHOU Collection with Bar Centifolia rounds out the newly named group. [NOT VERIFIED] The exact food and drink concept for TENSHOU and Bar Centifolia was not fully spelled out in the reveal, so we will not guess at the menu. What we do know is that it adds another distinct name to the roster and helps round out the dining choices at the center.

Taken together, these four names cover a lot of ground. You have coffee for the morning, a fast Greek meal for lunch, a hot sizzling plate for dinner, and a bar-style collection for later in the day. That range is on purpose. A good street-facing center wants to give people a reason to stop by at any hour. Morning coffee runs, midday bites, and evening drinks all pull different crowds. When one center can serve all of those moments, it stays busy from open to close instead of only filling up at night. That steady flow is what keeps rents strong for the businesses and keeps the whole block feeling alive.

You might think Strip restaurant news only matters to tourists. That is not true. What happens on the Strip shapes jobs, traffic, and the value of homes across the whole valley. When a big project like BLVD signs new tenants, it means construction jobs now and service jobs later. Restaurants need cooks, baristas, servers, cleaners, and managers. Those are real jobs for real people who live in Clark County. More openings mean more places to work close to the heart of the city.

There is also the local pride angle. When a homegrown brand like Mothership Coffee lands a spot on the Strip, it shows that local businesses can compete on the biggest stage in the city. That is good for the whole small business community. It tells other local owners that there is room for them too. It also gives residents a reason to visit the Strip. Many locals avoid the Strip because it feels built only for visitors. A familiar coffee brand and an easy Greek counter can make the area feel more welcoming to the people who actually live here.

Then there is the housing angle, which is my main focus as a local agent. Dining and shopping options are a big part of what makes an area desirable. Buyers often ask how close a home is to good food, coffee, and things to do. As the center of the Strip fills in with walk-up restaurants and shops, the nearby condos and high-rise units become more attractive. People who want a walkable, city-style life pay attention to projects like BLVD. More amenities near the Strip can support demand for homes in that corridor over time.

Finally, there is the practical, everyday value. If you work near the Strip, live in a nearby high-rise, or just pass through for events, having a real coffee shop and quick, fresh food nearby is a small daily win. It saves time. It gives you options that are not tied to a casino. And it makes the Strip a place you can use for normal life, not just for a special night out.

Background and History

To understand why BLVD matters, it helps to know what came before it. For a long time, the Strip was built around a simple idea. Get people inside a resort, and keep them there. Restaurants, shops, and shows were tucked inside casinos. The sidewalks were mostly for walking from one big building to the next. That worked for decades, but tastes have changed. Today, many travelers want open-air spaces, local flavor, and easy places to grab a bite without a big commitment.

BLVD is part of a newer wave of Strip projects built to face the street. Instead of hiding everything inside a casino, these centers put stores and restaurants right along the sidewalk. The goal is to catch foot traffic and give people a reason to slow down and stay a while. This is a shift toward the kind of open shopping and dining districts you see in other major cities. It is a bet that people want to stroll, sit outside, and choose from a mix of familiar and new names.

The dining roster at BLVD has been coming together in stages. Rather than announcing everything at once, the project has been revealing tenants over time. The July 2026 reveal of the TENSHOU Collection, Pepper Lunch, Mothership Coffee, and GoGreek was described as rounding out the development's dining lineup. That word "rounding out" tells us the list is getting close to complete. It signals that the project is moving from the planning stage toward the building and opening stage.

This slow, staged approach is common for large projects. Naming tenants a few at a time keeps the public interested over many months. Each reveal is a fresh piece of news that gets people talking and tagging friends online. It also gives each brand its own moment in the spotlight instead of getting lost in one giant list. For the developers, spreading out the announcements builds steady buzz right up until the doors open. By the time the first quarter of 2027 arrives, the goal is that plenty of people already know the names and are ready to visit.

What Happens Next

The clear next milestone is the opening window. These four new tenants are targeted for the first quarter of 2027. That is the January through March stretch. Keep in mind that "targeted" is not the same as "guaranteed." Opening dates for restaurants and big centers often shift. Construction can run long. Permits and inspections take time. City and county reviews can add weeks. So the smart move is to treat the first quarter of 2027 as a goal, not a promise. As the date gets closer, expect the project to share firmer opening dates for each spot.

Between now and then, watch for a few things. First, look for hiring news. Big restaurant openings usually post jobs weeks or months ahead. If you or someone you know wants to work near the Strip, those postings are worth tracking. Second, watch for build-out photos and menu teases. Brands often share peeks at their space and their food as they get close to opening. Third, keep an eye on any more tenant reveals. If the July news was about "rounding out" the roster, the remaining announcements may be small, but there could still be a few surprises.

It is also worth watching how these openings line up with the broader Strip calendar. The center of the Strip has a lot happening, from new attractions to major sports and event plans. A wave of new dining spots opening in early 2027 could arrive right as foot traffic in the area grows. That timing could help the new tenants find crowds quickly. It could also make the whole corridor busier, which is something nearby residents and drivers will want to plan around.
