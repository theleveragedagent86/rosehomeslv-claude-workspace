Nevada Police and Fire Games Bring 3-on-3 Ice Hockey Tournament to City National Arena in Summerlin

The Nevada Police and Fire Games are bringing an adult 3-on-3 ice hockey tournament to City National Arena in Summerlin from August 6 through August 8, 2026, with games open to first responders across multiple skill levels. This matters to Las Vegas because it puts the people who protect our valley on the ice for a fun, competitive event that neighbors can rally behind. It also shines a light on Summerlin's ice rink, a spot that a lot of locals drive past but have never stepped inside.

If you have ever wondered what your local police officer, firefighter, or paramedic does on a day off, this is one answer. Many of them lace up skates and chase a puck. The Nevada Police and Fire Games are part of a bigger movement of "public safety games" that happen all over the country. These events let first responders compete in sports the same way the Olympics work, just for the men and women who wear a badge or a fire helmet. Hockey is one of many sports on the schedule, and it fits right in with a city that has fallen hard for the game since the Vegas Golden Knights arrived.

Below, I will walk you through exactly what is happening, why it is a big deal for our community, the history behind these games, what comes next, and how you can take part or cheer from the stands. I will also share my honest take as a local who loves seeing Las Vegas show up for its own.

What Happened

The Nevada Police and Fire Games scheduled an adult 3-on-3 ice hockey tournament at City National Arena, the practice home of the Vegas Golden Knights, located in Downtown Summerlin. The tournament runs across three days, from Thursday, August 6, through Saturday, August 8, 2026. Games are set up with multiple skill divisions, which means players of different experience levels can join and still get a fair, fun matchup. The event is open across skill levels, so both longtime hockey players and newer skaters have a place to compete.

Let me explain what 3-on-3 hockey actually means, because it is a little different from the game you see on TV. A normal ice hockey team puts five skaters plus a goalie on the ice at once. In 3-on-3, each team only has three skaters on the ice at a time. With fewer players and the same big rink, there is a lot more open space. That means more speed, more scoring, and less standing around. It is fast, it is high energy, and it is honestly a blast to watch. The National Hockey League even uses 3-on-3 during overtime because it creates so much action.

The players in this tournament are first responders. That group usually includes police officers, firefighters, sheriff's deputies, corrections officers, paramedics, emergency medical technicians, and other public safety workers. The Nevada Police and Fire Games are built to bring these workers together to compete, stay active, and build friendships across different departments and agencies. Hockey is just one of the many sports offered during the games. Others often include things like running, shooting competitions, golf, basketball, and more, though the ice hockey tournament is the piece happening at City National Arena on these dates.

Because the games use multiple skill divisions, the organizers can group teams so that a beginner squad is not forced to play against a team full of former college players. This keeps the games close and keeps everyone safe. It also makes the event welcoming. A firefighter who only started skating a couple of years ago can still sign up, find the right division, and enjoy real competition without feeling out of place.

City National Arena is a strong choice for this kind of event. It is a real, pro-quality facility with two full ice sheets. It is clean, it is climate controlled, and it has seating so fans can watch. During the brutal Las Vegas summer, an indoor ice rink is one of the coolest places in the whole valley, both in temperature and in vibe. Hosting the first responder tournament here gives the event a big-league feel while keeping everyone out of the August heat.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might be thinking, "That sounds fun, but why does a hockey tournament matter to me?" It matters for a few real reasons that touch daily life in the valley.

First, it builds trust between residents and the people who protect them. When you see a police officer or firefighter laughing, skating, and competing like a regular neighbor, it changes how you see them. It reminds all of us that first responders are human. They have families, hobbies, and a sense of humor. Events like this help close the gap between the badge and the community. That kind of connection is good for every neighborhood, from Summerlin to Henderson to the east side.

Second, it is a free or low-cost thing to do during a slow, hot part of the summer. Las Vegas in early August is rough. It is often over 105 degrees. Families are looking for indoor activities that do not cost a fortune, especially right before school starts. An ice rink is one of the best answers. Coming out to watch first responders play hockey gives kids and adults something active and local to do, and it introduces a lot of people to a sport they might not have tried before.

Third, it supports our local sports economy in a quiet but real way. Every event at City National Arena brings people through Downtown Summerlin. Those visitors might grab a meal, do some shopping, or fill up on gas nearby. Youth hockey and adult leagues in the valley depend on rinks staying busy and healthy. When a big group event like this books ice time, it helps keep the whole hockey ecosystem strong. That is good news for the growing number of local kids who want to learn to skate and play.

Fourth, it shows how far Las Vegas has come as a hockey town. Not that long ago, ice hockey felt like an outsider sport in the desert. Then the Golden Knights arrived in 2017 and went to the Stanley Cup Final in their very first season. They won the Cup in 2023. Since then, interest in hockey has exploded. Youth programs have grown. Adult leagues have filled up. Now a women's pro team, the Professional Women's Hockey League club in Las Vegas, is on the way too. A first responder tournament is one more sign that hockey is now stitched into the fabric of our city.

For homeowners, all of this feeds into something I care about a lot. Strong community events and good local amenities help make neighborhoods more desirable. When an area has active rinks, parks, and public events, families want to live there. That demand supports home values over time. So even a hockey tournament, in a small way, is part of what makes Summerlin and the wider valley a place people want to call home.

Background and History

To understand this event, it helps to know where "public safety games" come from. Around the world, police officers and firefighters have long held their own sporting competitions. The idea is simple. First responders train hard and stay fit for their jobs, so friendly athletic competition is a natural fit. These games also honor the service and sacrifice of public safety workers, and they raise money and awareness for good causes.

State-level events like the Nevada Police and Fire Games give local first responders a place to compete without traveling across the country. They bring together agencies from all over the state, from small-town departments to big-city forces. Hockey has become a popular part of these kinds of games in cities with strong ice cultures, and Las Vegas now clearly qualifies as one of those cities.

City National Arena itself has an interesting story. It opened in 2017 as the official practice facility for the Vegas Golden Knights. It sits in Downtown Summerlin, near the shops, restaurants, and the ballpark. The building has two NHL-size ice sheets, a pro shop, a restaurant, and public seating. Beyond Golden Knights practices, it hosts youth leagues, adult leagues, learn-to-skate programs, camps, and public skating. It has become the heart of grassroots hockey in Southern Nevada.

The timing of the Golden Knights changed everything for local hockey. Before 2017, the valley had a smaller ice scene. After the team's magical first season and its 2023 championship, kids all over the valley wanted to play. Rinks added programs. More families bought gear. That growth is the backdrop for why a first responder tournament in Summerlin makes so much sense today. The interest, the facilities, and the community pride are all in place. What Happens Next

The tournament plays out over three days, August 6 through August 8, at City National Arena. Teams in the different skill divisions will work through their games during that window. In most 3-on-3 tournaments, teams play a set of shorter games, and the top teams in each division move on to playoff or medal rounds. That format packs a lot of action into a short time and keeps things exciting for players and fans alike.

After the tournament wraps, the Nevada Police and Fire Games usually recognize winners in each division. Public safety games often hand out medals, much like other amateur sporting events. For the first responders involved, bragging rights across departments can last all year. Do not be surprised if a fire station or police precinct proudly displays a trophy from the ice for months to come.

Looking further ahead, events like this tend to grow when a community shows up. If fans fill the seats and departments see how much fun their people had, the hockey tournament is likely to return and even expand next year. More divisions, more teams, and maybe more sports could follow. The health of the event depends a lot on community support in these early years, which is why coming out to watch actually helps shape the future of the games.

It is also worth watching how this connects to the bigger hockey calendar in Las Vegas. The Golden Knights season ramps up in the fall. The new women's pro team is building its roster and selling season ticket deposits. Local youth leagues start their seasons too. A first responder tournament in early August is like an opening act for a busy hockey year across the valley.
