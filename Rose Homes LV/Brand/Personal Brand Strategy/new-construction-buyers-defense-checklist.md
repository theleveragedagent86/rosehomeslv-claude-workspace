# The Las Vegas New Construction Buyer's Defense Checklist

### The questions builders hope you don't ask, and the costs they don't put on the sign.

A new build feels safe. Clean walls, warranty, a friendly person at the model home. But that friendly person works for the builder, not for you. This checklist puts you on even footing. Print it, bring it, and check every box before you sign anything.

> One thing to know up front: in almost every Las Vegas new build, the **builder pays the buyer's agent**. Bringing your own agent usually costs you nothing and gives you someone on your side. The catch is timing (see the very first item).

---

## 1. Before you walk into the model home

- [ ] **Bring your own agent on your FIRST visit.** Most builders pay your agent's commission, so it costs you nothing. But many builders require your agent to be with you (or registered) on visit one. Walk in alone and you can lose the right to bring an agent later, with no discount for going without one.
- [ ] Understand that the on-site sales rep represents the builder's interests, not yours. They are nice. They are also not your advocate.
- [ ] Do not sign the builder's registration card or any form before you have decided on representation.

## 2. The contract (it was written to protect the builder)

- [ ] Read every page, or have your agent or an attorney review it. Builder contracts are drafted by the builder's lawyers.
- [ ] Find the **delay clause.** If the home is finished late, what happens? Can you walk? Do you get anything? Many builders owe you nothing for delays.
- [ ] Find the **cancellation terms.** Can the builder cancel on you? What part of your deposit is at risk, and what triggers losing it?
- [ ] Confirm whether your **price is firm.** Ask in writing if material or tariff cost increases can be passed to you. (Tariffs have been adding roughly $7,500 to $17,500 per home in 2026.)
- [ ] Get every promise (incentives, upgrades, credits) written into the contract. A verbal promise at the model home is worth nothing at closing.

## 3. The money behind the price (the costs not on the sign)

- [ ] **SID or LID bonds. This is the big Las Vegas one.** Ask if the community has a Special Improvement District (common in Summerlin and Skye Canyon) or a Local Improvement District (common in Henderson). These are bond assessments billed on top of your property taxes. Get in writing:
  - The annual amount (commonly $500 to $3,000 or more)
  - How many years are left (often 10 to 20)
  - That it is billed twice a year, transfers to you as the owner, and carries roughly a 3% penalty if you pay it off early
  - That an unpaid SID/LID can lead to foreclosure
- [ ] **HOA.** Get the monthly amount, exactly what it covers, and any history of special assessments. Ask for the financial disclosures (Nevada's transparency rules require them).
- [ ] **Property taxes.** Good news for Nevada: homes are assessed at 35% of taxable value, the effective rate is roughly half a percent, your primary residence increase is capped at 3% a year, and there is no surprise supplemental tax bill like California has. Still, confirm your estimated number for this specific home.
- [ ] **Lot premium.** If you are paying extra for the lot, ask exactly what for, and whether that premium tends to hold value at resale.

## 4. The lender trap (incentives with strings attached)

- [ ] Know that builder incentives (rate buydowns, closing-cost credits, design-center dollars) are usually tied to using the **builder's preferred lender.**
- [ ] Get the builder lender's full **Loan Estimate**, then get at least one quote from an outside lender. Compare the rate AND the fees, not just the headline rate.
- [ ] Understand buydowns. A 2/1 or 3/2/1 buydown lowers your payment only temporarily. Ask what your payment becomes once it ends, and make sure you can afford the permanent rate.
- [ ] Make sure the incentive is real money, not a markup somewhere else. If the price quietly went up to fund the "free" credit, it is not free.

## 5. Inspections (yes, even on a brand-new home)

- [ ] Know this: about 9 out of 10 new homes have at least one significant issue at inspection. New does not mean flawless.
- [ ] Get a **pre-drywall inspection** (roughly $200 to $400) before the walls are closed up. This is your one chance to see the wiring, plumbing, and framing.
- [ ] Get a **final independent inspection** (roughly $310 to $730) before closing, separate from the builder's own walkthrough.
- [ ] Do a blue-tape walkthrough and put every item in writing before you sign off.

## 6. The warranty (know what is actually covered)

- [ ] Understand Nevada's **1-2-10 warranty:** Year 1 covers workmanship, Year 2 covers systems (plumbing, electrical, HVAC), and Years 3 through 10 cover the structure.
- [ ] Know that Nevada law (NRS Chapter 40) gives the builder a 90-day window to respond to a defect claim and requires mediation first. The structural protection runs 10 years.
- [ ] Get the warranty in writing and know exactly who to call (the builder directly, or a third-party warranty company).
- [ ] Take it seriously: the average structural warranty claim tops $40,000. This is real protection, not paperwork.

## 7. Built for the desert? (Vegas-specific build items)

- [ ] No basements here. The caliche soil layer makes them impractical, so plan your storage and square footage accordingly.
- [ ] Attics can hit 140 to 155F in summer and your AC runs 10 to 11 months a year. Ask about insulation, ductwork, and the AC efficiency rating.
- [ ] The water is very hard (around 17 grains per gallon). Budget for a water softener loop or system.
- [ ] Grass is banned in new yards (since April 2022). Plan for desert landscaping. The water authority offers turf rebates.
- [ ] New for 2026: an EV-ready garage circuit is now required, the federal solar tax credit ended January 1 (so price any solar carefully), and updated building codes took effect January 11.

## 8. Help paying for it (programs worth asking about)

- [ ] **Home Is Possible** — down payment assistance that is forgiven after three years, with a dedicated version for teachers.
- [ ] **Worker Advantage** — assistance for essential workers (a newer program). Great fit if you are a teacher, nurse, or first responder.
- [ ] **VA loan** — 0% down and no PMI if you are eligible.
- [ ] Other options to ask about: Home At Last, Rural Rocks, and the Clark County Welcome Home program. Ask which of these can stack on top of the builder's incentives.

---

## Before you sign, you should be able to say:

- [ ] I had my own representation from visit one.
- [ ] I read the contract, including the delay and cancellation clauses.
- [ ] I know my SID/LID and HOA numbers in writing.
- [ ] I compared the builder's lender against an outside quote.
- [ ] I got an independent pre-drywall and final inspection.
- [ ] I have the warranty and every incentive in writing.
