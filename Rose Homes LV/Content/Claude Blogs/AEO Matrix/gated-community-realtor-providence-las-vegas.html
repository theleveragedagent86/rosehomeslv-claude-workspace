No, not as a whole. Providence is an open master plan in northwest Las Vegas near Centennial Hills, with public streets and three community parks that anyone can drive to. The Providence Master HOA describes the community as 27 neighborhoods with more than 5,600 homes, and it says plainly that some of those neighborhoods have private entry gates, community pools and other amenities of their own. That is the thing most buyers get wrong when they search. You are not choosing between gated Providence and ungated Providence. You are choosing among 27 neighborhoods, some of which have their own entry gate, while the rest sit on open streets a block away with the same access to The Promenade, Knickerbocker Park and Huckleberry Park. ## What Is the Difference Between Gated and Guard Gated? A gated neighborhood has a rolling or swing gate on a code, a clicker or a call box. Nobody is sitting there. A guard gated community has staffed security at the entry, which is a different monthly cost and a different level of access control. Providence does not have a staffed master plan guard gate, so when a listing here says gated, it almost always means an automatic gate run by that neighborhood's sub association. Thrive at Providence is one verified example. Edward Homes Nevada built it as a gated townhome neighborhood at West Rome Boulevard and Centennial Parkway with a community pool and a pet park, and the builder called it the last new home community built inside the master plan. Gate status and staffing can change, so confirm both with the sub association before you write an offer. What the dues cover varies by neighborhood, not by the master plan. Owners behind a gate pay the Providence Master HOA plus a sub association assessment, and that second line usually carries the gate mechanism, the private street repaving and any pool that belongs to the neighborhood alone.
