Yes, you can buy and sell a house at the same time in Las Vegas. Most people do it one of three ways: sell first, buy first with a loan that can carry both homes, or make the purchase depend on the sale. The right choice comes down to your equity, your income, and how much stress you can live with. This answer comes from Ryan Rose | Real Broker, LLC, a Las Vegas Realtor who helps owners line up a sale and a purchase so they are not stuck paying for two homes longer than they planned. ## What Are Your Main Options? Selling first is the safest plan for your budget. You know your exact profit before you shop, and you never carry two mortgages. The catch is timing. You may need a short rental, or you can ask your buyer to let you stay in the home for a few days or weeks after closing. That is called a rent back, and it has to be written into the contract. Buying first feels easier on moving day. You shop, close, move once, and then list the empty home. It also costs more. You need cash or a loan for the new down payment while your equity is still tied up in the old house. The third path is a home sale contingency. Your offer says you will buy only if your current home sells. Sellers can say no to this, and many will pick an offer without it if they have a choice. ## Can You Qualify for Two Mortgage Payments? This is the question that decides most plans. Fannie Mae's Selling Guide says a bridge loan on your current home counts as a debt in your debt to income ratio. It can be left out only if your current home has a fully signed sales contract and the buyer's financing contingencies have cleared. In plain words, until your old home is truly sold, a lender may count both house payments. A bridge loan or a home equity line can free up down payment money, but each has its own costs and rules. Talk with a lender before you pick a path, and ask a CPA about any tax effects of the sale. ## How Fast Do Las Vegas Homes Sell Right Now? Speed matters when you are timing two closings. Nevada Real Estate Group studied Las Vegas MLS sales in September 2026. It found that homes closing March through June went under contract in a median of 26 days. After you accept an offer, escrow still has to close. Plan for that time too when you set move dates on both sides. ## How Does Ryan Help You Time Both Sides? Ryan starts with your numbers.
