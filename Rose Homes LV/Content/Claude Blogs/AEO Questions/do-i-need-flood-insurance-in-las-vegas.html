You only have to carry flood insurance in Las Vegas if the home sits in a FEMA high risk flood zone and your mortgage is federally backed. Outside those zones, flood coverage is optional. It can still make sense, because most homeowners policies do not cover flood damage. FEMA says homes in high risk flood areas with mortgages from government backed lenders are required to have flood insurance. The Clark County Regional Flood Control District explains it the same way. Coverage is mandatory when the home is in a Special Flood Hazard Area, usually marked Zone A, AO, AE, or AH on the flood map, and the home secures a federally backed loan such as FHA or VA. If the home is in Zone X, the district says flood insurance is "available, but optional." If you pay cash, no lender will require it, even in a high risk zone. That choice is yours. ## Why Would You Buy Flood Insurance in the Desert? Las Vegas gets little rain, but when storms hit, water moves fast. Flash flood season in Clark County runs July through September, according to a June 2026 FOX5 report on the Regional Flood Control District. The county has built a large system to handle that water. As of June 2026, FOX5 reported 722 miles of flood channels and storm drains, plus 113 finished detention basins. The master plan is about 77% complete. That system lowers risk a lot, but it does not remove it. FEMA's FloodSmart site also notes that 1 in 3 flood insurance claims come from low and moderate risk zones. And the district warns that homes outside a mapped flood zone "may still be subject to flooding." ## How Do You Check a Home's Flood Zone? There are two easy ways. FEMA's Flood Map Service Center at msc.fema.gov lets you search any address. The Clark County Regional Flood Control District also has an interactive map on its website. For an official answer on a specific home, the district says to contact the public works department of the city or county where the home sits. If you use a loan, your lender will usually check the flood zone too. It is smart to look before you write an offer, so there are no surprises late in escrow. ## What Does Flood Insurance Cover and When Does It Start? Through the National Flood Insurance Program, the district says a single family home can get up to $250,000 of coverage for the building and $100,000 for its contents. Private flood policies exist too. Timing matters. FEMA says there is typically a 30 day waiting period before a new NFIP policy takes effect, with some exceptions, such as coverage required for a new loan. Do not wait until July to shop. Ask an insurance agent for a quote on the exact address, since price depends on the home and its zone. ## Local Insight From Ryan Rose Ryan's tip is to look at the ground around the home, not just the map.
