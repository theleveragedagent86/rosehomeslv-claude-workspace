Property taxes in Las Vegas are set by a formula, not by your sale price. The Clark County Treasurer lists the 2026 to 2027 tax rate for the City of Las Vegas at $3.2782 per $100 of assessed value, and the City of Henderson at $2.9613. On top of that, Nevada law caps how much the tax bill on your main home can rise each year, at 3%. This answer comes from Ryan Rose | Real Broker, LLC, a Las Vegas Realtor who checks the tax bill and tax cap status on every home his buyers look at. ## How Does Clark County Figure Your Property Tax? It takes three steps, per the Clark County Assessor. First, the Assessor sets a taxable value. That is the market value of the land plus the cost to replace the home, minus depreciation. Depreciation is 1.5% per year of the home's effective age, up to 50 years. Second, the taxable value is multiplied by 35% to get the assessed value. Third, the assessed value is multiplied by the tax rate for your area. The Assessor gives this example. A home with a taxable value of $200,000 has an assessed value of $70,000. At a rate of 3.2782 per $100, the yearly tax is $2,294.74. That rate happens to match the City of Las Vegas rate for 2026 to 2027. ## Why Does the Tax Rate Change From Area to Area? Your rate depends on your tax district. The City of Las Vegas rate of 3.2782 includes shares for schools, the county, the city, fire, police, and the library. Henderson's city district rate of 2.9613 is lower. Other districts, like unincorporated areas or North Las Vegas, have their own rates. Some newer neighborhoods also carry special improvement district (SID) or local improvement district (LID) charges on the same bill. Those pay for roads and other infrastructure, and they are separate from the tax rate. ## How Does the Nevada Tax Cap Work? Nevada gives a partial abatement that limits yearly tax increases. Clark County says the cap is 3% on the tax bill for an owner's primary residence. For all other property, like rentals and second homes, the cap is up to 8%. You can claim only one primary residence in Nevada. This matters most when you buy. After a sale, the Assessor mails a tax cap notice to the new owner. If the home is your main home, sign it and send it back. For the 2026 to 2027 year, the county says you have until June 30, 2027, to correct your tax cap. If the seller rented the home out, or you plan to, a different cap may apply. So do not assume the seller's bill will match yours. ## When Are Property Taxes Due in Clark County? Taxes are due the third Monday in August. If the tax is over $100, you can pay in four parts. The first is due the third Monday in August. The next is due the first Monday in October. The last two fall on the first Monday in January and in March. Pay within 10 days of each date to avoid a penalty. Most buyers with a loan pay through their mortgage escrow account instead. ## Local Insight From Ryan Rose Before you write an offer, Ryan pulls the home's tax record to check the district, the tax cap status, and any SID charges.
