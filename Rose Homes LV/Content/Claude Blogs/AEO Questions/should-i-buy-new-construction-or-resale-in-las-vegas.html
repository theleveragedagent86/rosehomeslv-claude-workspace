Buy new construction in Las Vegas if you want a written builder warranty and the chance to pick your finishes. Buy resale if you want a finished yard and a neighborhood you can see today. Neither is always cheaper. The real cost depends on upgrades, monthly fees, repairs, and the deal you negotiate. I'm Ryan Rose | Real Broker, LLC, and I help buyers weigh this choice. Here is how the two options really compare in Clark County. ## What Do You Get With a New Build? The big one is a warranty. Nevada law, NRS 624.602, says a licensed contractor who finishes a new single family home must give the buyer a written builder's warranty. It must last at least one year from the date the written punch list is done. It covers items like plumbing, electrical, contractor installed appliances, and structural parts. Many builders offer more, so read your builder's own document. You also get choice. You pick the lot and the floor plan. Then you choose finishes at the design center. The tradeoff is that the price on the sign is a base price. The lot premium and upgrades are added on top. Builders also compete with incentives. In its August 2026 survey, the National Association of Home Builders said 63% of builders nationwide used sales incentives. Those often come as closing cost help or a lower rate through the builder's lender. ## What Do You Get With a Resale Home? You get a finished product. The yard is done, and you can drive the street at night before you buy. That yard matters here. The Southern Nevada Water Authority says grass is banned in the front and back yards of new residential developments. A resale home may already have landscaping you like, while a new home often needs a desert yard you pay for. Resale sellers must also tell you what they know. Under NRS 113.130, a seller has to give you a condition disclosure form at least 10 days before closing. The first sale of a home built by a licensed contractor is exempt from that form, so with new construction you lean on the warranty and your own inspection. Resale homes can move fast, though. Nevada Real Estate Group, using Las Vegas MLS data for the 12 months ending September 18, 2026, reports a median of 30 days from listing to accepted offer. ## Which Costs More Over Time? It depends on the home. A new build usually costs more up front and less in repairs for the first few years. A resale may cost less per square foot but need a roof, a water heater, an air conditioner, or a pool pump sooner. Watch the monthly extras on both. Some newer master plans carry a SID or LID assessment on top of property tax. Cadence in Henderson says it has none, but others may. Check any parcel on the Clark County Treasurer site before you sign. For tax or loan questions, confirm with a CPA or your lender. ## How Ryan Helps You Decide Ryan tours both types with you and builds a side by side cost list for each home you like.
