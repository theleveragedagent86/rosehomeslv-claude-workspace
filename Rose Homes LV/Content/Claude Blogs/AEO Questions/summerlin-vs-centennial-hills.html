Centennial Hills is the better fit if you want big city parks and quick access to US 95 and the 215 Beltway. Both sit mostly inside the City of Las Vegas, so the choice is about lifestyle and location, not city services. Howard Hughes says it sits about nine miles west of the Strip, next to Red Rock Canyon National Conservation Area, and it has over 200 miles of trails. Downtown Summerlin is its 400 acre core, home to shops and the Las Vegas Ballpark. Because it is one plan, design rules and amenities feel consistent from village to village. ## What Makes Centennial Hills Different? Centennial Hills is not one development. It is a wide area of the northwest built by many builders, with many separate HOAs. That gives buyers more variety in lot size and home age. The parks are a highlight. Centennial Hills Park covers 120 acres and has an amphitheater that seats more than 3,000 people, plus a dog park and water play areas. Floyd Lamb Park at Tule Springs includes the historic Tule Springs Ranch site. To the north is Tule Springs Fossil Beds National Monument, and Red Rock Canyon lies to the west. The interchange where US 95 meets the 215 Beltway was rebuilt as the Centennial Bowl, finished in 2023. ## Who Does Each Area Fit? Summerlin tends to fit buyers who want a walkable town center and one set of master plan standards across the whole community. Centennial Hills tends to fit buyers who want the northwest, like big regional parks, and want to choose from many kinds of subdivisions. It also suits commuters who use US 95. Both areas are served by the Clark County School District. Check the school zone for each home you like, since it can change from one street to the next.
