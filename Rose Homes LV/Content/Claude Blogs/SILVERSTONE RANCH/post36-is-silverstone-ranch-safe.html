# Is Silverstone Ranch Safe? (Area Crime & Safety) Silverstone Ranch is widely considered a safe place to live, though no crime statistics are published for the community by name. The surrounding Centennial Hills area ranks among the safer parts of the city of Las Vegas. ## Is Silverstone Ranch safe compared to the rest of Las Vegas? When people ask whether Silverstone Ranch is safe, the honest answer starts with a caveat: Metro Police and rating services do not break out numbers for this specific community. The best available picture comes from the wider Centennial Hills area, which surrounds Silverstone Ranch in northwest Las Vegas. Area data from services like CrimeGrade and AreaVibes places Centennial Hills around the 79th percentile for safety, with crime running roughly 30 to 40 percent below the citywide average. Treat those figures as area level context, not exact community numbers. Because Silverstone Ranch sits inside this broader area, buyers often feel comfortable with the general safety profile. Still, safety varies street by street, so the smart move is to check current, address specific data directly with Metro Police before you buy. ## What security features does Silverstone Ranch offer? Beyond area crime trends, Silverstone Ranch has structural features that some residents value for peace of mind. Three of its nine neighborhoods are gated, and one of them, The Palms, is guard gated with a 24/7 staffed entry. An active master homeowners association helps keep common areas maintained and consistent, which many buyers associate with a well kept, watchful community. None of this guarantees safety, and gates are not a substitute for good habits. What they do offer is an added layer of access control and a community that tends to look out for itself. If a gated section matters to you, focus your Silverstone Ranch search on those three neighborhoods. ## How can buyers check safety for a specific address? For a specific home, go past the headlines. Pull the most recent Metro Police crime maps for the exact block, drive the streets at different times of day, and talk to neighbors. Ask about the homeowners association, lighting, and whether the section is gated.
