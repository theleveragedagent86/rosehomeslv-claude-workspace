Golden Knights Erase Three-Goal Deficit, Take 3-0 Series Lead Over Avalanche in Western Conference Final

The Vegas Golden Knights trailed 3-0 after one period but rallied with five straight goals to beat the Colorado Avalanche 5-3 on May 24, 2026, moving one win away from the Stanley Cup Final. It was one of the most stunning comebacks in T-Mobile Arena history, and Las Vegas fans inside that building witnessed something the hockey world is still talking about the next day.

What Happened

Game 3 of the Western Conference Final started as a nightmare for Vegas. Colorado came out flying in the first period, scoring three times to build a lead that would have broken most teams. The T-Mobile Arena crowd went quiet. The Avalanche looked like they were ready to turn the series around after dropping the first two games on the road.

Then Mark Stone happened. Just 19 seconds into the second period, Stone picked up a loose puck and put it past the Colorado goalie to make it 3-1. The building erupted. Stone had been out with an injury and this was his return to the lineup. The timing could not have been written any better. Nineteen seconds. That was all it took for the entire energy of the game to shift.

Vegas kept pushing. The Golden Knights outskated and outhustled Colorado through the middle frame, adding a second goal before the period ended. Heading into the third period trailing just 3-2, the momentum had completely flipped. The Avalanche, who had looked so sharp and confident through the first 20 minutes, now appeared rattled. Their defensive zone coverage broke down. Their transition game, which had been sharp early, started to slow.

Tomas Hertl delivered the go-ahead goal at 8:21 of the third period, and T-Mobile Arena went into a full frenzy. The Golden Knights added a fourth goal to put some breathing room between themselves and Colorado. Brett Howden capped the scoring with an empty-net goal to make the final 5-3. Carter Hart was outstanding in net all night, stopping 32 of the 35 shots he faced, including several huge saves in the first period when Colorado was at its best and Vegas needed to stay close. Vegas leads the series 3-0 and can clinch a trip to the Stanley Cup Final in Game 4 on May 26, also at T-Mobile Arena.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Sports teams shape the identity and energy of a city. When the Vegas Golden Knights launched as an expansion team in 2017, a lot of people across the country dismissed Las Vegas as a real sports town. They said fans here would not show up. They said loyalty would be thin. The Knights and their fans have spent almost a decade proving every one of those doubters wrong, and moments like Game 3 against Colorado are exactly why.

A win in Game 4 on May 26 would send the Golden Knights back to the Stanley Cup Final. They won the Cup in 2023 and have been one of the best teams in the Western Conference every single season since the franchise was born. For residents who live and work in the Las Vegas Valley, the playoff run brings real energy to the city. Bars and restaurants near the Strip fill up on game nights. The mood downtown is lighter. People talk hockey at the grocery store, at school drop-off, in the office. Sports do that to a place, and right now the Golden Knights are doing it in a big way.

There is also an economic piece worth noting. Playoff hockey brings visitors to Las Vegas who are here specifically for the games. T-Mobile Arena seats around 17,500 people. When those seats fill up with a mix of locals and traveling fans, the spending ripples out across the whole Strip and into the surrounding neighborhoods. Hotels, restaurants, parking, merchandise, bars watching the game on TV screens, all of it gets a boost during a deep playoff run. That is good news for the broader Las Vegas economy, including the real estate and service businesses that make their living here.

For families and longtime residents, there is also something simpler and more personal at stake. Watching your hometown team make a run at a championship is one of those shared experiences that brings a community together. Kids growing up in the Las Vegas Valley right now are getting to watch a Golden Knights team that competes at the highest level year after year. That kind of consistent winning builds a sports culture that sticks with a city for generations.

Background and History

The Vegas Golden Knights entered the NHL in 2017 as an expansion team, which is normally when a franchise is expected to struggle. Expansion teams lose. They are built from cast-offs and players other teams did not want to keep. They take years to develop and find their identity. Las Vegas did not follow that script at all. The Golden Knights went to the Stanley Cup Final in their very first season, losing to the Washington Capitals. The hockey world was shocked. Las Vegas was not.

The franchise has been built on a combination of smart management, savvy trades, and a culture inside the locker room that prioritizes toughness and accountability. Mark Stone, the captain who scored the comeback goal 19 seconds into the second period of Game 3, is the face of that culture. He was acquired in a trade with Ottawa back in 2019 and has been the heart of this team ever since. When he is healthy and in the lineup, the Golden Knights are a different team. His return from injury in Game 3 was not just a storyline, it was a turning point.

Carter Hart joined the team and has given Vegas a true number-one goalie. His performance in Game 3, stopping 32 shots while his team dug out of a 3-0 hole, is the kind of effort that playoff runs are made of. Great goaltending covers mistakes, keeps teams in games, and gives everyone on the ice confidence that they can come back. Hart did all of that on Saturday night.

Colorado, for their part, is not a pushover. The Avalanche won the Stanley Cup in 2022 and have one of the most talented rosters in the Western Conference. Coming into this series they were a genuine threat to advance. But Vegas has simply been the better team through three games, and a comeback from three goals down only adds to the growing sense that this Golden Knights group is built for moments exactly like this one.

What Happens Next

Game 4 is set for Monday, May 26, 2026, at T-Mobile Arena on the Las Vegas Strip. Vegas needs one more win to close out the series and advance to the Stanley Cup Final. Colorado will not go quietly. Teams with the talent the Avalanche have do not quit after one bad game, even if that game included blowing a 3-0 lead at home for the opponent. Expect the Avalanche to push hard in the opening period of Game 4, trying to establish the same fast start they had in Game 3. Whether Vegas can handle that pressure and respond the way they did on Saturday will define how Game 4 unfolds. But everything about this Golden Knights team right now suggests they are ready to close it out in front of their home crowd on May 26.

If Vegas wins Game 4, they will wait to see who emerges from the Eastern Conference Final. The Stanley Cup Final would then begin in early June. T-Mobile Arena would host home games if the Golden Knights hold the better record, meaning Las Vegas could see the Cup played for right here in the city. That is what 17,500 fans and thousands more watching from home are dreaming about this week.
