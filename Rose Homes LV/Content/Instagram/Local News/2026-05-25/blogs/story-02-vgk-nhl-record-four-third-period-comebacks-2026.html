Vegas Golden Knights Set NHL Record with Four Third-Period Comeback Wins in One Playoff Run

The Vegas Golden Knights made NHL history on May 24, 2026, when their fourth third-period comeback win of the 2026 Stanley Cup Playoffs broke the all-time record for comeback victories in a single postseason run. No team in league history had ever done it four times in one playoff run, and the Knights did it in Game 3 against the Colorado Avalanche, proving this team simply will not quit when the stakes are highest.

What Happened

Game 3 of the Western Conference Final between the Vegas Golden Knights and the Colorado Avalanche, played on May 24, 2026, became one of the most memorable nights in Las Vegas sports history. The Knights trailed heading into the third period, a spot that would have broken many teams. But this team is not like most teams.

Vegas scored in the third period to complete the comeback and win the game, putting the Golden Knights up 3-0 in the series and one game away from the Stanley Cup Final. The victory also produced a piece of NHL history that will be written into the record books permanently. It was the fourth time in this single postseason run that the Knights had come back to win after being behind heading into the final period of regulation. No team had ever accomplished that four times in a single playoff run in the history of the National Hockey League.

To put that in context, consider that every other team still alive in the 2026 playoffs had at most two third-period comeback wins for their entire postseason. The Golden Knights had four. That is not just a record; it is a statement about the kind of team coach John Tortorella has built in a remarkably short amount of time.

The comeback against Colorado was especially striking because the Avalanche are not a team that collapses easily. Colorado had posted a 45-0 record when leading after two periods in the regular season before the Knights broke that streak in Game 2 of this very series. Now, just one game later, Vegas did it again. The Avalanche went from one of the most dominant close-the-door teams in the league to a team that could not hold leads against this Golden Knights squad in back-to-back games.

Mark Stone, the Knights' captain who had been sidelined with an injury and returned to the lineup for Game 3, scored on his first shift back. That kind of moment captures exactly what this team is: resilient, deep, and capable of rising to any moment. Stone's return gave the entire team a visible lift, and the crowd at T-Mobile Arena fed off that energy in a way that turned the building into something electric in the third period.

The win was also a reminder of how quickly the momentum of this series shifted. Colorado came in as a legitimate contender with championship experience. After three games, they are staring down elimination and trying to figure out how a team that was 18-4-1 in its last 23 games under a new coach managed to rewrite the record books at their expense.

Background and History

The Vegas Golden Knights were founded in 2017 as an NHL expansion franchise, and they immediately became one of the most remarkable stories in North American professional sports history. In their very first season, they reached the Stanley Cup Final, something no other expansion team had ever done. They lost that series to the Washington Capitals, but the message was clear: this franchise was built to compete.

The Knights won their first Stanley Cup championship in 2023, defeating the Florida Panthers in the Stanley Cup Final. That title validated everything the organization had built and cemented Las Vegas as a legitimate hockey market. The fanbase, which had already been passionate and loud from game one, grew even larger after the championship. T-Mobile Arena became one of the most intense home-ice environments in the league.

The 2025-26 season brought unexpected turbulence. Head coach Bruce Cassidy, who had been with the team since 2022, was replaced on March 29, 2026, with the regular season still in progress. John Tortorella, one of the most respected and intense coaches in NHL history, took over the bench. Tortorella had won a Stanley Cup with the Tampa Bay Lightning in 2004 and was known for building teams with tough, defensive, never-give-up identities.

The results under Tortorella were immediate and dramatic. The Knights went 18-4-1 in their final 23 regular season games combined with their playoff games under his direction through Game 3 of the Western Conference Final. That winning percentage would be extraordinary for any stretch of the season, let alone one that includes some of the most important games on the schedule.

The third-period comeback identity did not come from nowhere. Tortorella is famous for preaching process, staying present, and not panicking when the scoreboard looks bad. His teams have historically been hard to put away because he does not let his players quit. The four comeback wins this postseason are a direct reflection of that coaching philosophy taking hold in a locker room that was already talented enough to execute it.

What Happens Next

The Golden Knights lead the Colorado Avalanche three games to zero in the Western Conference Final. In NHL playoff history, teams that take a 3-0 series lead have gone on to win the series the vast majority of the time. While no lead is mathematically insurmountable, the historical odds heavily favor Vegas advancing to the Stanley Cup Final.

Game 4 of the series will give the Knights the opportunity to close out Colorado and secure their spot in the championship round. If they do, they will face the winner of the Eastern Conference Final for the Stanley Cup. As of late May 2026, the Knights would be considered a strong contender based on their performance in this postseason.

For Tortorella, a potential second Stanley Cup championship would be a remarkable capstone to a coaching career that already features one title and multiple trips to the conference final with different franchises. For the players, many of whom were part of the 2023 championship team, it would mean back-to-back cups with a new coaching staff, which would further cement this core group's legacy in Las Vegas sports history.

The T-Mobile Arena faithful will be watching closely. A potential Game 4 clincher at home would be one of the loudest nights in arena history. The city's sports community, already buzzing from the record-setting comeback win, is ready to push this team all the way to another title.

Even if Colorado forces a Game 4 and beyond, the Golden Knights have already done something no team has done before. The record is set. The history is made. Whatever happens in the remaining games of this run, the 2026 Golden Knights postseason will be studied and referenced for years to come.
