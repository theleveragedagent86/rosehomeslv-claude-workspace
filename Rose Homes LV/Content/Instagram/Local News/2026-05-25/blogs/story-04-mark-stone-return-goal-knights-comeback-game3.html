Mark Stone Returns from Injury, Scores on First Shift to Ignite Golden Knights Comeback

Golden Knights captain Mark Stone came back from a 16-day injury absence on May 24, 2026, and scored a power-play goal just 19 seconds into his first shift of the night, lighting a spark that carried Las Vegas all the way back from a 3-0 hole to a 5-3 win in Game 3 of the Western Conference Final. The moment was one of the most electric in T-Mobile Arena history, and it shifted the entire tone of the series against Colorado.

Stone also added an assist in the win, finishing the night with a goal and a helper. Vegas now leads the series 2-1 heading into Game 4, with their captain back on the ice and the crowd louder than it has been all postseason.

What Happened

Mark Stone had not played since May 8, when he took a hit late in Game 3 of the second-round series against the Anaheim Ducks. The team listed him as day-to-day with a lower-body injury, but as the days piled up, those five missed playoff games started to feel like a serious problem.

Vegas went 3-2 in Stone's absence, meaning they managed to stay competitive without their best player. They advanced past Anaheim and opened the Western Conference Final against Colorado. But something was clearly missing. Without Stone, the power play lacked its usual precision, the defensive zone coverage was thinner, and the team just did not carry the same presence.

Then Game 3 arrived. The Golden Knights came out flat and found themselves down 3-0 after the first period at T-Mobile Arena. The crowd, which had been buzzing before the puck dropped, grew quiet. Colorado looked comfortable. A sweep felt like a real possibility.

Then the second period began. Stone jumped over the boards for his first shift of the game. Nineteen seconds later, on a power play, he redirected a shot past the Colorado goaltender to make it 3-1. The building erupted. From that point forward, Vegas outplayed Colorado for the rest of the game, eventually winning 5-3 to take a 2-1 series lead.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Las Vegas is a city that has fully embraced the Golden Knights since their first season in 2017. The team is not just a sports franchise here. It is a shared identity. When the Knights are in the playoffs, the energy in the city shifts. Restaurants put the games on every screen. Watch parties fill parking lots. People who have never followed hockey find themselves checking scores.

For longtime fans and newer arrivals alike, Mark Stone returning is about more than just one player. He is the captain. He is the leader in the locker room and on the bench. His number is one of the most popular jerseys you will see anywhere on the Strip or in local neighborhoods. When he went down in Game 3 against Anaheim, the worry was real.

His return matters to the city because it means the Knights are whole again heading into the most important stretch of the season. A Western Conference Final appearance is a big deal every year. But when your captain comes back from injury and scores 19 seconds into his first shift, in a game where you were down 3-0, that is the kind of story that people talk about for years.

Clark County is home to T-Mobile Arena and the surrounding sports and entertainment district that has grown up around the Knights and the Vegas Aces. Local businesses near the arena have seen a direct lift on game days throughout the playoffs. Hotels, restaurants, ride-share drivers, and retail shops all feel the impact when the team is winning and the home games are packed. A deep playoff run by the Golden Knights is genuinely good for the local economy.

Background and History

Mark Stone joined the Golden Knights in February 2019, acquired in a trade from the Ottawa Senators. He signed an eight-year, $76 million extension almost immediately after the trade, a signal that the organization considered him the cornerstone of the franchise going forward. He was named team captain ahead of the 2021-22 season.

In the years since, Stone has become one of the most complete two-way forwards in the NHL. He is strong on the power play, plays a physical game, and is known for winning important faceoffs and protecting the puck under pressure. He led the team during their Stanley Cup run in 2022-23, when Vegas defeated the Florida Panthers four games to one to claim the franchise's first championship.

Injury has been a part of Stone's story with the Knights, too. He has dealt with a recurring back injury throughout his tenure in Las Vegas, undergoing surgery and missing significant time on more than one occasion. Each time, his return has been closely watched by fans and the hockey world. He has developed a reputation as a player who fights through pain and shows up when the stakes are highest.

This playoff run follows a regular season in which Stone played through discomfort multiple times. When he went down on May 8 against Anaheim, the medical staff and the team kept details quiet, which is standard practice. The fact that he was able to return in 16 days for a Conference Final game speaks to both his recovery and his determination.

What Happens Next

With the series now at 2-1 in favor of Las Vegas, Game 4 is scheduled to be played in Las Vegas. The Golden Knights will have a chance to take a commanding 3-1 series lead with Stone back in the lineup.

Colorado is not going to fold. The Avalanche have some of the best offensive talent in the league and are experienced in playoff hockey. Winning three straight games to eliminate the Knights is absolutely within their ability. But momentum matters in the playoffs, and right now, that momentum belongs to Vegas.

Stone's health will be watched closely in the days between games. A lower-body injury that kept him out for 16 days during the playoffs is not something that disappears completely after one good game. The team will monitor him carefully, and there is no guarantee he plays in every remaining game of the series. But his return changes the calculus for Colorado's coaching staff in terms of matchups, line combinations, and penalty kill strategy.

If Vegas can close out this series and advance to the Stanley Cup Final, the city will once again become the center of the hockey world. A back-to-back championship would be extraordinary. Even a second Final appearance in three years would solidify the franchise's status as one of the premier organizations in the sport.

Local fans should expect Game 4 at T-Mobile Arena to be one of the loudest and most intense home games of the season. With Stone healthy, the power play clicking, and a chance to go up 3-1, the energy inside that building will be electric.

Ryan's Take

Stone's comeback is a great reminder of what Las Vegas has built with this franchise. When the Knights were announced as an expansion team back in 2016, a lot of people outside Nevada questioned whether a hockey team could actually work in the desert. The city answered that question quickly and loudly.

What strikes me most about this story is the timing. The team was down 3-0 in Game 3. The crowd was quiet. Colorado was in control. And then their captain, who had not played in over two weeks, jumps over the boards and scores 19 seconds later. That is not a coincidence. That is leadership, preparation, and the kind of competitive spirit that makes sports worth watching.

From a community standpoint, playoff hockey in Las Vegas brings the city together in a way few other things do. New residents from all over the country arrive here not necessarily knowing anything about hockey, and within a season or two, they are all-in Knights fans. That is a real community builder, and it connects people across neighborhoods from Henderson to Summerlin to North Las Vegas.

Stone's return also matters beyond the ice. It is a story about resilience and coming back when your team needs you. That resonates everywhere, not just in sports. Whether you have been watching every game this postseason or just catching highlights on your phone, this one is worth paying attention to. The Golden Knights are alive, their captain is back, and Game 4 is coming.
