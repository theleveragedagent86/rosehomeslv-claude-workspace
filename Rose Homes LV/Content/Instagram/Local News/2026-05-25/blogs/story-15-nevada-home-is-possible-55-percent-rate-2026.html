Nevada's Home is Possible Program Is Offering First-Time Buyers Rates as Low as 5.5% Right Now

Nevada's Home is Possible program is offering first-time homebuyers mortgage rates as low as 5.5% as of May 22, 2026, nearly a full percentage point below what most buyers are seeing on the open market. For Las Vegas families who have been sitting on the sidelines, this program could be the difference between qualifying for a home and staying in a rental another year.

When the average 30-year fixed rate is hovering around 6.43%, a 5.5% rate sounds small on paper. But over the life of a $350,000 loan, that difference can mean tens of thousands of dollars in savings. This is one of the most concrete pieces of good news for Las Vegas buyers in 2026, and not enough people know it exists.

What the Home is Possible Program Is Offering Right Now

The Nevada Housing Division updated its Home is Possible program rates on May 22, 2026. Here is what the program is currently offering.

The FHA and VA loan option comes with a rate of 5.5% with 4 discount points. A discount point is an upfront fee paid at closing, equal to 1% of the loan amount. So on a $350,000 loan, 4 points would cost $14,000 upfront. That sounds like a lot, but it buys down the rate significantly compared to what lenders are quoting right now. For buyers who plan to stay in their home for several years, the monthly savings will eventually make up for that upfront cost.

The standard first-time homebuyer option through the program offers a rate of 6.5% along with 2% down payment assistance. On a $350,000 purchase, that is $7,000 in down payment help from the state, which can cover a meaningful chunk of what buyers need to bring to closing. This path does not require paying points upfront, making it more accessible for buyers with limited savings.

Down payment assistance through Home is Possible comes in the form of a grant, not a loan. That means you do not have to pay it back. There are no second liens, no deferred payments, and no surprise bills down the road. The assistance simply reduces what you need to bring to the table on closing day.

To qualify, your income must be at or below $105,000 if you are a single borrower. If you are applying with a second borrower, the limit rises to $135,000. Those thresholds cover a wide range of Las Vegas households. A teacher and a nurse, a mechanic and a school administrator, a first-year nurse and a retail manager, all of these couples could plausibly fall under $135,000 and qualify. The Nevada Housing Division designed these limits to capture working households, not just the lowest income brackets.

The program works through participating lenders, not directly through the state. You apply the same way you would for a conventional or FHA mortgage, but with a lender that is approved to offer Home is Possible. The state provides the assistance and rate buy-down behind the scenes. A list of approved lenders is available on the Home is Possible website.

Why This Matters for Las Vegas Residents Right Now

Las Vegas has had a complicated year for buyers. Inventory is up, prices have softened a bit, and sellers are more willing to negotiate than they were two years ago. In April 2026, the Las Vegas Realtors reported declining prices and slowing sales. The conditions for buyers are better than they have been in years. The main obstacle still standing in the way for many households is the monthly payment.

At 6.43%, which is roughly where the 30-year market rate sits right now, a $350,000 loan carries a principal and interest payment of about $2,185 per month. At 5.5%, that same loan drops to about $1,987 per month. That is nearly $200 per month in savings, or about $2,400 per year, or close to $72,000 over a 30-year loan. For a family stretching to qualify, that difference can mean approval versus denial on an underwriter's desk.

The down payment assistance piece matters just as much for a different group of buyers: those who earn enough to afford the monthly payment but struggle to save for a down payment. In a city where housing costs have risen sharply and wages have not always kept pace, saving $20,000 to $30,000 in cash while paying rent is genuinely hard. A 2% grant reduces that burden without adding debt.

For renters who are thinking about buying in Henderson, Summerlin, Spring Valley, Centennial Hills, or North Las Vegas, this program is worth a serious look. The income limits are generous enough that most two-income working households will fall under the cap. And the minimum credit score requirements, while they vary by lender, are generally achievable for buyers who have been responsibly managing their finances.

The combination of buyer-friendly market conditions and a subsidized rate program does not come along very often. The program has existed for years but the rate difference between the Home is Possible option and the open market is unusually wide right now. That gap could close if broader rates drop, so the window where this program provides the most value may not be permanent.

First-time buyers who purchase in 2026 will also be entering a market where competition is lower than it was in 2021 or 2022. Multiple-offer situations still happen on well-priced homes, but the frenzied days of waiving inspections and offering $50,000 over asking are largely behind us. A qualified buyer with a Home is Possible pre-approval can negotiate from a much stronger position than a buyer in the same income bracket could have three years ago.

Background on the Home is Possible Program

Home is Possible is run by the Nevada Housing Division, a state agency under the Department of Business and Industry. The program has been around since the mid-2010s and has helped tens of thousands of Nevada residents become homeowners. It was designed specifically to address one of the persistent barriers to homeownership in Nevada: the cost of entry, meaning the down payment and the difficulty of competing with cash buyers or well-capitalized investors.

Nevada has historically had a lower homeownership rate than the national average. A combination of factors is responsible for that gap: the state's economy is heavily tied to hospitality, entertainment, and construction, all industries with variable income streams that can complicate mortgage underwriting. Large numbers of residents move to Nevada from other states and have not had time to accumulate local financial history. And during the 2008 housing crash, Nevada was one of the hardest-hit states in the country, which left a generation of residents wary of homeownership and with damaged credit.

The Home is Possible program has evolved over the years. It has added components for teachers (Home is Possible for Heroes, which offers additional assistance to first responders and educators) and has updated its income limits as Las Vegas median incomes have changed. The current limits of $105,000 for single borrowers and $135,000 for two-borrower households reflect a conscious effort to reach the middle of the market, not just the lowest income tiers.

The Nevada Housing Division funds the program through the issuance of mortgage revenue bonds, a financing tool that allows the state to offer below-market rates without drawing on the general fund. The bonds are backed by the mortgages themselves and are considered low-risk because the borrowers must meet qualification standards. This structure has allowed the program to survive multiple economic cycles without requiring ongoing legislative appropriations.

The 4-point buy-down option at 5.5% is a relatively new addition to the program's offerings. It gives more sophisticated buyers who have done the math a way to lock in a significantly lower rate in exchange for upfront cost. Not every buyer will choose this path, but for those who plan to stay in their home for a long time, the math often favors it.

What Happens Next and How Rates Could Change

Rates through the Home is Possible program are updated regularly based on market conditions and the Nevada Housing Division's bond issuance schedule. The 5.5% rate with 4 points and the 6.5% rate with 2% down payment assistance represent the program's offerings as of May 22, 2026, but those numbers can change. Buyers who are interested should move quickly, or at least check the current rates on the official website before making any decisions.

The Federal Reserve's policy direction will play a role in where rates go from here. If the Fed begins cutting rates later in 2026 or early 2027, market rates will likely fall, which could reduce the gap between the Home is Possible program rate and what a buyer could get on the open market. That would make the program less compelling, not because it got worse, but because the market caught up to it.

On the other hand, if rates stay elevated or move higher, the 5.5% option becomes even more valuable. The uncertainty around rate direction is itself a reason to look at this program now while the benefit is clear and measurable.

For the broader Las Vegas market, a higher volume of first-time buyers entering the market through programs like Home is Possible could provide some support to home prices in the mid-range segment. Homes priced between $300,000 and $450,000 are the most natural fit for buyers using this program, given the income limits and loan size constraints. Increased demand in that price range could slow the price declines the market has seen in early 2026.

The Nevada Housing Division typically holds informational workshops and works with housing counseling agencies to help buyers understand their options. Completing a HUD-approved homebuyer education course is required for most first-time buyer programs, and Home is Possible is no exception. That requirement is a feature, not a bug. Buyers who complete education courses default at lower rates and report higher satisfaction with their purchases.

What You Can Do Right Now

If you want to explore whether Home is Possible is a fit for you, here are the practical steps.

First, check the current rates on the official website at homeispossiblenv.org/todays-rates. Rates update regularly, so look at the current numbers before making any assumptions based on what you read here.

Second, review the income limits. Single borrower households at or below $105,000 and two-borrower households at or below $135,000 are the thresholds as of May 2026. If you are close to the limit, a participating lender can help you understand exactly how income is calculated.

Third, find a participating lender. The Home is Possible website has a searchable directory of approved lenders. Not all mortgage companies offer this program, so you will need to work with one that does.

Fourth, complete a homebuyer education course. This is required for the program and takes a few hours. HUD has a list of approved providers. The course covers budgeting, the buying process, and what to expect after closing. It is genuinely useful, not just a checkbox.

Fifth, get pre-approved. A pre-approval letter tells you exactly how much home you can buy and makes your offer credible to sellers. With the market conditions in Las Vegas right now, a well-prepared buyer with pre-approval and down payment assistance is in a strong negotiating position.
