Las Vegas Is Now the 4th Strongest Buyer's Market in the Country, Redfin Says

Las Vegas has more home sellers than buyers right now, and it's not even close. A March 2026 Redfin report found there were 101% more sellers than buyers in the Las Vegas metro, ranking the city 4th among all major U.S. markets for buyer power. That kind of gap gives buyers leverage they simply did not have two or three years ago.

For anyone thinking about buying a home in Las Vegas, Henderson, Summerlin, or anywhere in Clark County, this is the most favorable market environment in years. Sellers are competing for a shrinking pool of buyers. Prices are softening. Homes are sitting longer before going under contract. The power has shifted.

This report came out April 27, 2026, based on Redfin's analysis of supply and demand data across major U.S. metros. Redfin senior economist Asad Khan told the Las Vegas Review-Journal that the market is likely to stay in buyer's territory for the foreseeable future. That is not a short-term blip. It reflects real structural changes in the Southern Nevada housing market that have been building for more than a year.

Whether you are a first-time buyer who kept getting outbid in 2022, a move-up buyer waiting for the right time, or an investor looking at the numbers again, what Redfin found in March 2026 is worth understanding in detail.

What the Redfin Report Actually Found

Redfin measured buyer demand versus seller supply across the country's largest metros. The metric they used compares active listings to buyer activity signals like tours, offers, and saved searches. When sellers outnumber active buyers by a large margin, the market favors buyers. When buyers outnumber available homes, sellers hold the leverage.

In Las Vegas, the March 2026 data showed a 101% surplus of sellers over buyers. That means for roughly every one qualified, active buyer in the market, there were two sellers competing for that buyer's attention. That 101% figure placed Las Vegas 4th among all major U.S. metros. Only three cities had a wider seller-to-buyer gap.

To understand how significant that is, consider what the market looked like just a few years earlier. In 2021 and into early 2022, Las Vegas was one of the hottest seller's markets in the country. Homes were selling in hours, not days. Buyers were waiving inspections, going tens of thousands over asking price, and losing bidding wars six and seven times before landing a contract. The Las Vegas metro was consistently near the top of national lists for home price appreciation.

The 2022 rate hike cycle changed everything. The Federal Reserve raised rates aggressively starting in March 2022, and by late 2022 the 30-year fixed mortgage rate had climbed from around 3% to over 7%. That cooled demand fast. Buyers who could afford a $400,000 home at 3% interest suddenly could not qualify for the same price at 7%. The Las Vegas market, which had been driven heavily by out-of-state buyers relocating from California and other high-cost states, felt that shift hard.

Inventory started rising in late 2022 and has continued climbing through 2023, 2024, and into 2026. At the same time, many sellers who bought or refinanced at 2-3% rates have been reluctant to list because they would be trading a low rate for a much higher one on their next purchase. That "rate lock" effect kept supply somewhat constrained even as demand slowed. But by early 2026, inventory in Clark County had built to levels that clearly favor buyers, and the Redfin data confirms it with hard numbers.

Asad Khan, the Redfin senior economist quoted in the Review-Journal, said the imbalance is not expected to correct quickly. The combination of elevated mortgage rates, rising supply, and slower in-migration from California has created conditions that favor buyers and will likely persist through the rest of 2026 at minimum.

Why This Matters to Las Vegas Buyers and Sellers Right Now

The practical meaning of being the 4th strongest buyer's market in the country is that buyers in Las Vegas have real negotiating power today. That is not marketing language. It shows up in specific ways that affect what you can ask for and what you can expect when you make an offer.

First, sellers are accepting contingencies again. During the peak seller's market years, buyers were routinely pressured to waive inspection contingencies, appraisal contingencies, and financing contingencies just to be competitive. In the current market, buyers can include all of those protections. That matters enormously for your risk exposure. An inspection contingency lets you walk away or negotiate repairs if the inspection reveals problems. An appraisal contingency protects you if the home does not appraise at the purchase price.

Second, price reductions are common. Many sellers are listing at a price they hope to get and then reducing it after 30 to 60 days on market when offers don't materialize. Buyers who are patient and watching the market can catch homes that have been reduced once or twice and are now priced to sell. This is happening across price points, from entry-level condos in North Las Vegas to move-up homes in Green Valley and Henderson.

Third, sellers are paying closing costs again. In a hot seller's market, the idea of asking a seller to cover closing costs was almost laughable. Today, it is a standard negotiating point. A buyer can reasonably ask for two to three percent of the purchase price in seller-paid closing costs, which on a $450,000 home is $9,000 to $13,500 out of the seller's proceeds. That money goes directly toward reducing the buyer's upfront cash requirement.

For sellers, the picture is more challenging. If you need to sell in this environment, pricing correctly from the start is more important than ever. Overpriced homes are sitting. The data from the Las Vegas Realtors association shows days on market has been climbing for months. Buyers have options, and they are not in a rush. A seller who prices aggressively and presents the home well can still get a strong result. But chasing the market down by listing high and reducing every few weeks is a strategy that costs time and usually nets less than pricing right the first time.

Sellers who are not forced to move should also honestly evaluate whether this is the right time to list. If you can wait 12 to 18 months, market conditions may shift. But if you have a life reason to move, whether a job, a family change, or a financial need, the current market still has buyers. It just requires more patience and realistic expectations.

Background: How Las Vegas Got Here

Las Vegas has always been a volatile real estate market. It boomed in the mid-2000s, collapsed harder than almost any other city in the 2008-2012 foreclosure crisis, then recovered strongly in the 2010s, and absolutely exploded during the 2020-2022 pandemic era. Understanding that history helps explain why the current shift is real and not just a temporary softening.

The pandemic years were extraordinary for Las Vegas real estate. Remote work freed people from living near expensive coastal job centers. Las Vegas offered lower taxes, no state income tax, a lower cost of living compared to California, and a warm climate. Buyers from Los Angeles, the Bay Area, and Seattle poured into the market. Demand from local first-time buyers was also high, driven by ultra-low mortgage rates that made monthly payments affordable even as prices climbed.

Between January 2020 and June 2022, the median home price in Las Vegas rose by roughly 60%. That was not sustainable. When rates jumped in 2022, the out-of-state buyers slowed dramatically. Many of the move-up buyers who had planned to sell and trade up got stuck because their next purchase would cost so much more in monthly payments at the new rates.

Through 2023 and 2024, the market was in an awkward middle phase. Prices did not crash as some predicted, but they stagnated. Sellers who needed to move accepted less. Inventory grew slowly. Buyers who could still afford the higher rates were selective and cautious. By 2025, inventory in Clark County had climbed well above the tight pandemic-era levels, and by early 2026 the Redfin data captures just how far the balance has shifted.

The construction market adds another layer to this. Las Vegas homebuilders have been one of the biggest sources of competing inventory. New construction has continued even as demand slowed, creating additional supply that resale sellers have to compete against. Builders have been offering rate buydowns, free upgrades, and other incentives that pull buyers away from existing homes.

What Happens Next

Redfin's Asad Khan said buyer's market conditions are likely to persist in Las Vegas for the foreseeable future. That means the factors driving the imbalance are not expected to reverse quickly. But nothing in real estate is permanent, and it is worth understanding what could change the picture.

Mortgage rates are the biggest variable. If rates drop meaningfully, say from the current range near 6.5-7% down toward 5.5%, that would bring a wave of buyers back into the market. The buyers who have been sitting on the sidelines waiting for better affordability would activate quickly, and inventory levels would tighten. The Nevada Home is Possible program has already been offering rates near 5.5% for qualifying buyers, which has helped on the affordability side for first-time buyers specifically.

Population growth also matters. Las Vegas continues to attract residents from higher-cost states, though the pace has slowed from the pandemic peak. If in-migration accelerates again, demand would increase. The metro's job market, driven by hospitality, healthcare, construction, and growing tech sector activity, will influence whether new residents continue to choose Las Vegas.

Buyers who are comfortable with the complexity of buying distressed properties may find additional opportunities there.

For now, the consensus from Redfin and local market data points to buyer's market conditions holding through at least the end of 2026. The smart play for buyers is to take advantage of the current window, and for sellers to price accordingly and not wait for conditions that may not arrive soon.

Ryan's Take

I have been selling homes in Las Vegas for years, and market conditions shift more in this city than almost anywhere I have seen. The Redfin report confirming Las Vegas as the 4th strongest buyer's market in the country matches exactly what I am seeing on the ground every week.

Buyers are winning negotiations right now. I mean really winning, not just getting a $1,000 credit. I am seeing buyers get $10,000 to $20,000 in price reductions, full closing cost coverage, inspection repairs completed before close, and home warranties included. On specific homes that have been sitting, buyers are coming in well below asking price and getting it accepted. That simply did not happen in 2021 or 2022.

If you have been waiting to buy in Las Vegas because you kept losing bidding wars or prices felt too high, this is genuinely a different market. The inventory is there, the competition is lower, and sellers are motivated. The one thing working against buyers is that mortgage rates are still elevated compared to 2020 and 2021. But with the right down payment assistance program and a motivated seller covering closing costs, deals can be structured that work.

For my seller clients, my message is simple. You can still sell your home at a fair price in this market. Homes that are priced correctly and show well are moving. The days of pricing 10% over market and getting three offers in a weekend are gone for now. But the right strategy, right price, and right presentation still gets results. The worst mistake is to list too high, sit for 60 days, and then chase the price down. Buyers notice days on market. Price it right from day one and move on with your life.

What You Can Do Right Now

If you are a buyer, start by getting pre-approved. In a buyer's market, you have time to be thoughtful, but sellers still want to see a solid pre-approval before they take an offer seriously. Get your financing in order so you can move decisively when the right home comes up.

Next, define your non-negotiables versus your nice-to-haves. In a buyer's market, you can afford to be a bit more selective, but having a clear list of what you truly need helps you move when you find the right property instead of second-guessing.

Ask for everything. Request an inspection contingency, appraisal contingency, and financing contingency on every offer. Ask for seller-paid closing costs as a starting point in your negotiation. The worst a seller can do is say no, and in this market, most will say yes.

If you are a seller, get a real comparative market analysis done on your home before you list. Not just a Zillow Zestimate. An actual analysis that looks at recent comparable sales, active competition, and days on market trends in your specific neighborhood. Price based on that data, not on what your neighbor got two years ago.

Make sure your home shows at its best. Buyers have options right now. Clean, decluttered, well-staged homes that photograph well still attract attention. Homes that look tired or dated against clean competition are going to sit.

Finally, talk to a real estate agent who is actually active in the market right now, not one who is just pulling comps from behind a desk. The difference between a good outcome and a frustrating one in a market like this often comes down to having someone who knows what is actually happening week to week.
