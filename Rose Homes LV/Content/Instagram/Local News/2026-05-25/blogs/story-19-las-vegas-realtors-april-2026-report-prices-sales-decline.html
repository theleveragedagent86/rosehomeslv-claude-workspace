Las Vegas Realtors April 2026 Report: Home Prices and Sales Both Decline Year-Over-Year

The Las Vegas housing market pulled back in April 2026, with the median price of an existing single-family home dropping to $473,875, a 1.3% decline from April 2025. At the same time, total closed sales fell and available homes for sale kept climbing, signaling that the balance of power in the Las Vegas market is shifting toward buyers.

These numbers come from the Las Vegas Realtors (LVR) monthly housing report released May 6, 2026. The data covers all closed residential sales in Clark County tracked through the Multiple Listing Service (MLS). For anyone buying, selling, or simply watching the market, this report offers the clearest monthly snapshot of where Las Vegas real estate stands right now.

The short version: prices are cooling, fewer homes are selling, and buyers have more options than they have had in several years. That shift matters whether you are a first-time buyer trying to get in, a homeowner thinking about selling, or an investor tracking values across Southern Nevada.

What the April 2026 Numbers Show

The headline number is the median price. In April 2026, the median sale price for an existing single-family home in Clark County was $473,875. That is down 1.3% from April 2025, when the median was around $480,000. While a 1.3% drop may seem small, it represents a real change in direction after years of rising prices following the pandemic-era buying surge.

For context, Las Vegas home prices peaked in mid-2022. They softened in late 2022 and early 2023 as mortgage rates jumped. Prices then stabilized and ticked back up through 2024 and into early 2025. The April 2026 data suggests that upward push has stalled and reversed slightly.

On the sales volume side, the numbers are also softer. Total residential properties sold in April 2026 came in at 2,643. Within that total, single-family home closings fell 2.9% compared to April 2025. Fewer transactions means fewer families moving, fewer agents earning commissions, and fewer sellers collecting their equity gains.

Inventory is the most striking shift. Active single-family listings available at the end of April 2026 reached 6,689, up 7.7% from April 2025. More homes on the market means buyers have more choices and more leverage in negotiations. Sellers are competing harder for the same pool of buyers.

The months-of-supply figure tells the story clearly. At roughly 3.5 months of supply, Las Vegas has moved meaningfully away from the extreme seller's market conditions of 2021 and 2022, when supply was under one month. A balanced market is generally considered to be around five to six months of supply. At 3.5 months, Las Vegas is still technically a mild seller's market, but it is much closer to balance than at any point in the last four years.

Condos and townhomes also showed weakness. The condo and townhome segment typically moves in the same direction as single-family homes but sometimes with greater volatility. Buyers who were priced out of single-family homes during the peak years turned to condos as a more affordable entry point. As single-family prices soften, some of those buyers may shift their search, which could pressure the condo market further.

Days on market is another number worth watching. When the market was hot, homes often received multiple offers within days of listing. A longer average days-on-market figure signals that homes are sitting longer, giving buyers more time to think and negotiate. The LVR April 2026 report reflects that sellers can no longer expect a bidding war the moment they list.

Why This Matters to Las Vegas Residents

If you own a home in Las Vegas, a 1.3% price dip is not cause for panic. Homes that sold for $480,000 a year ago are selling for roughly $473,000 today. That is a difference of about $6,000 to $7,000. For most homeowners who bought several years ago, equity gains from 2020 through 2022 still make up a significant cushion. The concern would be if this trend accelerates and prices fall more steeply over the coming months.

For sellers, the current environment demands a realistic pricing strategy. Overpriced homes are sitting. Homes priced at or slightly below comparable recent sales are still moving. The days of pricing high and waiting for buyers to meet you there are less predictable now. Presentation matters more too. When buyers have 6,689 single-family listings to choose from, a home that shows well has a real advantage over one that does not.

For buyers, this is the most favorable environment in years. More inventory means more options. Slower price growth means less urgency to rush into a purchase. If you have been on the sidelines waiting for the frenzy to cool, the April 2026 data suggests the market has done exactly that. With mortgage rates still elevated compared to the 2020 and 2021 lows, affordability remains a challenge, but the price side of the equation is no longer working against you as aggressively as it was.

First-time buyers in particular stand to benefit from expanded inventory. During the peak seller's market years, first-time buyers were routinely outbid by cash investors or buyers waiving contingencies. More supply means competition for any given home is lower, and sellers are more likely to accept standard offers with inspection contingencies and financing terms.

Renters watching the for-sale market should also pay attention. If home prices soften further and mortgage rates eventually come down, the math on buying versus renting could shift. Las Vegas rents have also been elevated since 2021. A softening purchase market can eventually ease pressure on the rental side too, as more renters convert to buyers and reduce demand for rental units.

Investors who bought at peak prices in 2021 and 2022 may be watching more nervously. If they bought to rent, their cash flow math depended partly on continued appreciation. A market where prices are flat or slightly declining does not hurt cash flow directly, but it does affect exit strategy calculations. Investors looking to sell should factor in that buyers have alternatives and are negotiating harder.

Background and History: How Las Vegas Got Here

Las Vegas real estate has always been cyclical. The city grew rapidly through the 1990s and mid-2000s, then experienced one of the most severe housing crashes in the country during the 2008 to 2012 downturn. At the bottom, Las Vegas home prices fell roughly 60% from their 2006 peak. Foreclosures flooded the market. Entire neighborhoods sat half-finished.

Recovery was slow but steady through the 2010s. By 2019, Las Vegas prices had largely recovered to pre-crash levels. Then the pandemic hit.

From mid-2020 through mid-2022, Las Vegas home prices surged faster than almost anywhere in the country. Remote workers from California, Nevada, and other high-cost states relocated to Las Vegas attracted by lower taxes, lower prices, and a lower cost of living. Demand exploded while supply remained tight. Months of inventory fell below one. Bidding wars were common. Homes sold for well over asking price.

When the Federal Reserve began raising interest rates aggressively in 2022 to fight inflation, the dynamic shifted. Mortgage rates climbed from around 3% to over 7%. Monthly payments on a $450,000 home nearly doubled compared to 2021. Demand cooled sharply. Prices pulled back from peak levels in late 2022 and early 2023.

Through 2023 and into 2024, the market found a floor. Sellers who did not need to sell held onto their homes rather than give up their locked-in low mortgage rates. That kept supply constrained even as demand slowed, which helped prices stabilize. By mid-2024 and into 2025, prices had crept back up and even set new nominal highs in some segments.

The April 2026 report suggests that second attempt at price appreciation has now stalled. The combination of persistently high mortgage rates, growing inventory, and slightly softer demand has tipped the balance again. This is not a repeat of 2008. Foreclosure levels remain low compared to that era. But the direction of the market has clearly changed.

What Happens Next

Several factors will shape the Las Vegas housing market over the coming months. Mortgage rates are the biggest wildcard. If rates drop meaningfully, buyer demand could return quickly and put upward pressure on prices again. If rates stay elevated, the current soft trend is likely to continue or deepen.

Inventory will be the other key variable to watch. At 6,689 active single-family listings, supply is growing but not overwhelming. If inventory continues to climb toward 8,000 or 9,000 listings, price pressure on sellers will increase. If inventory levels off or falls back, the market could stabilize relatively quickly.

New home construction is also part of the picture. Las Vegas homebuilders had their worst first quarter for new home sales in years in early 2026, according to earlier reports. Builders who cut prices, offer incentives, and buy down mortgage rates are competing directly with the resale market. That competition adds supply pressure and can drag resale prices down.

On the employment side, Las Vegas remains dependent on hospitality, gaming, and tourism. Any significant economic slowdown that leads to local job losses would accelerate the softening trend. Conversely, if the broader national economy holds up and tourism stays strong, the local housing market has a better chance of finding stable footing.

The next LVR monthly report, covering May 2026, will be released in early June. That data will show whether the April trends are continuing or whether the market is beginning to stabilize. Watch the median price and months of supply especially. Those are the two indicators that most clearly signal direction.

Ryan's Take

I have been working in Las Vegas real estate for years, and I have seen this market move in both directions. The April 2026 report is not alarming, but it is a clear signal that conditions have changed. The seller's market that pushed prices to new heights a few years ago has largely run its course. What we have now is a more balanced environment, and that is actually healthy.

For sellers, the biggest mistake I see right now is pricing based on the market from 12 to 18 months ago. Buyers are well-informed. They see the same data. If your home is priced above what comparable homes are selling for today, it will sit. And a home that sits starts to lose appeal because buyers wonder what is wrong with it. The best strategy is to price correctly from day one, make the home show its best, and move quickly.

For buyers, I want to be clear: this is not a moment to expect a crash and hold out for rock-bottom prices. The fundamentals in Las Vegas are still solid. Population growth continues. Employment is diversifying beyond gaming. Infrastructure investment is happening across the valley. A 1.3% price dip in a single month is not the beginning of a collapse. It is the market finding equilibrium after an extraordinary run-up.

What I tell clients right now is simple. If you need to sell, price it right and move with purpose. If you need to buy, you have more options and more leverage than you have had in years. Take advantage of that. If you are in no hurry in either direction, use this time to get your finances in order and understand exactly what you want from your next home or your next move.

The data tells a story, but the story is not panic. It is adjustment. And adjustments, handled well, create opportunity on both sides of a transaction.

What You Can Do Right Now

If you are thinking about selling, get a current market analysis done now. Not one based on what your neighbor got last year. One based on what is actually selling today, in your specific neighborhood, at your specific price point. The market is neighborhood-specific. Some areas of Henderson and Summerlin are holding up better than others. Some price ranges are more affected than others. You need data that reflects your exact situation.

If you are a buyer, use the increased inventory to your advantage. Take your time viewing properties. Do not skip the inspection because you are worried about losing a bidding war. Ask for seller concessions. Many sellers will contribute to closing costs or buy down your mortgage rate to get the deal done. These were nearly impossible asks 18 months ago. They are realistic asks today.

If you are a homeowner with no plans to move, stay calm. Your equity gains from the past several years are still significant in most cases. One month of price softening does not erase years of appreciation. Monitor the trend, but do not make reactive decisions based on a single data point.

If you are an investor, revisit your underwriting. Make sure the numbers still work at current prices and current rental rates. If you bought with tight margins expecting continued appreciation, now is the time to stress-test your assumptions and make sure your investment can weather a flat or mildly negative price environment for 12 to 24 months.

Whatever your situation, having current, accurate information is the best starting point. The Las Vegas Realtors publish their monthly report every month. Read it, or work with someone who does.
