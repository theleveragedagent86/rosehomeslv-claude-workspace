Nellie's Southern Kitchen Closes at MGM Grand After Nearly Three Years

Nellie's Southern Kitchen closed permanently at MGM Grand on May 25, 2026, ending a nearly three-year run on the Las Vegas Strip. The restaurant was opened and operated by Denise and Kevin Jonas Sr., parents of the Jonas Brothers, and quickly became a fan destination for both Southern comfort food and celebrity memorabilia.

The closure is the latest in a string of Las Vegas Strip restaurant departures in 2026. It leaves a notable hole in the MGM Grand dining lineup, and the resort has stated there are no immediate plans for the space. For locals, tourists, and Jonas Brothers fans who visited specifically for the experience, the news landed with a mix of surprise and sadness.

This one gets personal for a lot of people. Nellie's was not just a restaurant. It was a story. A family's story. And for a lot of guests, that made it feel different from the hundreds of other dining options on the Strip.

What Happened

Nellie's Southern Kitchen officially opened its doors at MGM Grand in June 2022. The concept was built around the Jonas family's real roots. Denise Jonas, who goes by "Mama Jonas" to the band's massive fanbase, grew up cooking Southern food. The restaurant was named after her. The menu reflected her actual cooking, not a corporate version of it. That authenticity mattered to guests from the start.

The menu featured dishes like chicken and waffles, fried catfish, cheddar-chive biscuits, collard greens, and other Southern comfort staples. Prices were reasonable by Strip standards. The vibe was warm and welcoming, decorated with Jonas family memorabilia, old photos, and mementos that made it feel more like a family home than a hotel restaurant. Kevin Jonas Sr. was often on-site, greeting guests and sharing stories about the food and the family.

For fans of the Jonas Brothers, a trip to Nellie's became part of the Las Vegas experience. The band performed at Allegiant Stadium and at various residencies and one-off shows over the years, and fans would make reservations at Nellie's to round out the trip. The restaurant leaned into that connection without making it feel cheesy. The memorabilia was tasteful. The food was the centerpiece.

According to the Las Vegas Review-Journal's NEON, the restaurant served its last guests on May 25, 2026. No major advance notice was given publicly. The closure was confirmed by MGM Grand, which released a brief statement saying there are no immediate plans for the space. The Jonas family has not made a detailed public statement about the reasons for the closure as of the time of this writing [NOT VERIFIED whether a statement was later issued].

The timing lines up with a broader pattern on the Strip. Several high-profile restaurant concepts have closed or downsized in the first half of 2026. The MGM Grand Buffet, a 30-year institution, closed earlier this year. Heart Attack Grill, a Fremont Street fixture for 15 years, also shuttered. Las Vegas is not losing its dining culture, but it is going through a very visible reshuffling. What replaces these familiar spots matters a lot for the character of the city.

MGM Grand holds a large dining portfolio across its property, including celebrity chef partnerships and longtime tenant restaurants. The company routinely rotates concepts based on performance, lease terms, and strategic direction. Nellie's occupied a space that could eventually be redeveloped into something new, though the resort has not announced a timeline.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might wonder why a Strip restaurant closure matters to people who live here. Most locals do not eat on the Strip regularly. The prices are higher, parking is a hassle, and there are dozens of excellent independent restaurants in neighborhoods across the valley. But Strip restaurant news affects the local economy in ways that ripple outward.

MGM Grand and its sister properties employ tens of thousands of workers in the Las Vegas metro area. When a restaurant closes, those jobs disappear or shift. Front-of-house staff, kitchen workers, dishwashers, hosts, and managers all need to find new positions. In a city where service industry employment is a major economic driver, these transitions matter. A single restaurant closure might affect 30 to 80 workers depending on size and staffing [NOT VERIFIED exact Nellie's headcount].

There is also the question of what the Strip's dining scene signals to tourists deciding whether to visit. Las Vegas markets itself aggressively as a world-class food destination. When notable restaurants close in a short stretch, it can affect perception. Visitors research dining options before they book. If a restaurant they planned to try is suddenly gone, that can influence decisions about where to spend money and even where to stay.

For the Jonas Brothers fanbase, which skews heavily toward millennial and Gen Z women, Nellie's held a specific emotional charge. It was one of the rare places on the Strip where the connection between the celebrity brand and the experience felt genuine rather than licensed. The Jonas Brothers have been vocal about their faith, their family, and their Midwestern and Southern roots. Nellie's embodied all of that. Fans traveled to Las Vegas specifically to eat there. That kind of experience-based tourism is increasingly important to the city's economy, and losing a venue that served that purpose has real consequences.

Southern food also has a relatively small footprint on the Las Vegas Strip compared to steakhouses, sushi bars, and Italian concepts. Nellie's was one of very few spots offering genuine Southern comfort food in a large-scale Strip setting. Locals and tourists who wanted that style of cooking had a dependable option at MGM Grand. Finding a comparable replacement will not be easy.

The closure also feeds a larger conversation happening in Las Vegas right now about which restaurants and businesses can sustain themselves long term on the Strip. Rents are high. Labor costs have risen sharply in recent years. Competition from new celebrity-backed concepts is constant. Nellie's ran for nearly three years, which is not a failure, but it is also not the decade-long run that some Strip institutions have achieved.

Background and History

The Jonas Brothers connection to Las Vegas runs deep. Nick, Joe, and Kevin Jonas grew up in a household shaped by their father's Christian ministry and their mother's cooking. Kevin Jonas Sr. served as a musician and pastor, and the family relocated several times during the brothers' childhood, spending time in New Jersey and other parts of the East Coast. Their mother Denise was known among family and friends for her Southern cooking, a tradition she brought with her from her upbringing.

When the Jonas Brothers reestablished themselves as a group after their 2013 breakup, they reconnected with their family story in a visible way. The brothers have been open about how much their parents influenced them, and the idea of giving their mother a restaurant bearing her name was a natural extension of that. Kevin Jonas Sr. became the operating face of the business, handling the day-to-day alongside restaurant professionals hired to manage the kitchen and service teams.

MGM Grand was a logical partner. The property had existing relationships with celebrity-connected dining concepts, and it draws a demographic that overlaps with the Jonas Brothers' fanbase. The casino portion of MGM Grand had been a backdrop for Jonas Brothers performances over the years, and the brand alignment made sense. Nellie's opened in June 2022 with immediate buzz, drawing both food critics and entertainment reporters who covered the celebrity angle heavily.

Reviews of the food were generally positive. Critics noted that the dishes tasted home-cooked rather than hotel-kitchen mass-produced, and that the service felt warm and personal. The chicken and waffles received consistent praise. The cheddar-chive biscuits became something of a signature item mentioned across review platforms [NOT VERIFIED specific review scores or ratings]. Kevin Jonas Sr. was noted frequently for his visible presence in the dining room, which added to the authenticity of the experience.

Nearly three years is a meaningful run for a celebrity-backed concept on the Strip. The history of such restaurants is mixed. Some thrive for years; others close within months. Nellie's outlasted several flashier, bigger-budget celebrity dining ventures that came and went on the Strip during the same period. Whatever the business reasons for the closure, the restaurant earned a real reputation during its time open.

Tell people about it. Leave a review. The space at MGM Grand will not sit empty for long. The resort has strong incentives to activate every square foot of its property. What comes next will be announced when MGM is ready. Until then, the space that housed Nellie's Southern Kitchen closes this chapter of its story.
