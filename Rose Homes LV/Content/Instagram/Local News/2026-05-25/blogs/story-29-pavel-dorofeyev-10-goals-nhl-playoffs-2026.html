Pavel Dorofeyev Is Leading the Entire NHL Playoffs in Goals. Here Is the Story Behind Vegas Golden Knights' Breakout Star.

Pavel Dorofeyev is the top goal scorer in the 2026 Stanley Cup Playoffs, with 10 goals and 14 total points through the Western Conference Final. That number does not just lead his team. It leads every player in the entire NHL postseason, making the 24-year-old Russian winger the undisputed offensive engine of the Vegas Golden Knights' deep playoff run.

For most of the first two rounds, casual fans outside Las Vegas were still asking, "Who is this guy?" By the time he put one in overtime against the Anaheim Ducks in Game 5 of the second round, the hockey world had its answer. Pavel Dorofeyev is no longer a prospect or a depth piece. He is a legitimate playoff star, and right now, he is the most dangerous forward in the postseason.

What Happened: A Playoff Performance Unlike Anything VGK Has Seen

The 2026 playoff run for the Vegas Golden Knights has been a team story from the start, built on comeback wins and resilient defense. But one player has separated himself from everyone else on the ice, and his name is Pavel Dorofeyev.

Dorofeyev entered the playoffs with something to prove. He had shown flashes during the regular season, posting solid numbers but never becoming a household name in a lineup that includes veterans and former All-Stars. Once the puck dropped in April, something clicked. In the first round against the Utah Hockey Club, Dorofeyev erupted with a hat trick in a single game, becoming the first Vegas player to score three goals in a playoff game in several seasons. That performance set the tone for everything that followed.

He did not slow down in the second round against the Anaheim Ducks. Dorofeyev continued to find the back of the net in ways that looked almost effortless. His shot release is quick and heavy, and he has a knack for getting to the right spots in front of the goal. When overtime arrived in Game 5 with the series hanging in the balance, it was Dorofeyev who ended it. His overtime winner sent T-Mobile Arena into a frenzy and pushed the VGK into the Western Conference Final.

Through the Western Conference Final against the Colorado Avalanche, he continued to produce, finishing with 10 goals and 14 points when the analysis published on May 12. According to the Las Vegas Sun, those numbers represent the best individual playoff performance from any skater remaining in the tournament at that point in the postseason.

To put that in context, scoring 10 goals in an NHL postseason is rare even when a player makes it to the Stanley Cup Final. Most playoff scoring leaders finish with 8 to 12 goals total. Dorofeyev had already reached 10 before the conference final was even complete. If the VGK advance to the Stanley Cup Final, he has a realistic shot at challenging the single-season NHL playoff goal records that have stood for decades.

Head coach Bruce Cassidy has been careful not to put too much pressure on his young winger publicly, but the numbers speak for themselves. The coaching staff has leaned into Dorofeyev's hot streak, giving him consistent linemates and quality ice time in high-leverage situations. That trust has been repaid with goals at the most important moments of the run.

Background and History: Who Is Pavel Dorofeyev?

Pavel Dorofeyev was born in Russia in 2001 and began his professional hockey career in the Kontinental Hockey League before the Vegas Golden Knights drafted him in the third round of the 2019 NHL Entry Draft. He was taken 79th overall, a spot that typically produces solid depth pieces rather than franchise cornerstones. The fact that he is now leading the entire NHL postseason in goals is a remarkable development by any measure.

He spent time developing in the AHL with the Henderson Silver Knights, Vegas's primary development affiliate, before earning a more permanent roster spot with the big club. Those who watched him closely in the AHL saw a player with an unusually quick release, soft hands in traffic, and an instinct for getting to the right areas of the ice. His skating improved over time, and so did his confidence.

During the regular season heading into the 2026 playoffs, Dorofeyev had posted respectable but not explosive numbers. He was a reliable contributor, not yet a player opposing coaches built their defensive game plans around. The playoffs changed that. In the pressure of postseason hockey, with defensive structures tightened and physical play ramped up, Dorofeyev found another gear.

His hat trick against Utah in the first round was the moment the broader hockey world started to pay attention. A hat trick in the playoffs is a rare and memorable event. Three goals in a single playoff game is something that sticks in highlight reels for years. For Dorofeyev, it was a statement that he belonged at this level and that he could deliver when the stakes were highest.

The Vegas Golden Knights organization has a history of developing players who bloom under the bright lights of the postseason. It is a culture built around accountability, preparation, and the understanding that playoff hockey rewards the players who want the puck in big moments. Dorofeyev has clearly internalized that culture, and it is showing up in the most public way possible: goals on the scoreboard when the season is on the line.

What Happens Next: The Stanley Cup Final Is in Sight

As of mid-May 2026, the Vegas Golden Knights were competing in the Western Conference Final against the Colorado Avalanche. The outcome of that series will determine whether Dorofeyev gets a chance to add to his already historic goal total on the game's biggest stage.

If the VGK win the Western Conference Final, they advance to the Stanley Cup Final, which typically begins in early June. That means potentially two more rounds of games, giving Dorofeyev a realistic path toward one of the most impressive individual playoff seasons in NHL history. The record for goals in a single playoff year is 19, set by Reggie Leach in 1976. That bar is well out of reach, but a finish somewhere between 14 and 17 goals would still rank among the greatest single-playoff performances in the modern era.

The VGK's upcoming schedule depends entirely on how the conference final plays out. Home games at T-Mobile Arena on the Las Vegas Strip will bring tens of thousands of fans into the building and tens of thousands more into the bars, restaurants, and watch parties around the city. Each game in this run is an event.

Beyond the immediate playoff picture, the larger question is what Dorofeyev's breakout means for his long-term future with the organization. He is a restricted free agent after the season, which means the Golden Knights hold his rights but will need to negotiate a new contract. A 10-goal playoff run that leads the entire NHL will dramatically change that conversation. Players who deliver in the postseason get paid accordingly, and the VGK front office will face a decision about how much they are willing to invest in their new star.

Dorofeyev himself has been characteristically low-key in interviews, deflecting credit to his linemates and focusing on the team's goals rather than his own statistics. That mentality tends to play well in Las Vegas, where the culture under coach Cassidy has always been about the group over the individual.
