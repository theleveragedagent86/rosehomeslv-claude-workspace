Carolina's Jordan Staal Wins Conn Smythe Over Vegas, Sets Age Record

The 2026 Conn Smythe Trophy went to Carolina Hurricanes captain Jordan Staal, who became the oldest player in NHL history to win the award for playoff MVP. At 37 years and 277 days old, Staal passed the old record set by Tim Thomas back in 2011. He earned the honor after his Hurricanes beat the Vegas Golden Knights in the Stanley Cup Final, four games to two. For Vegas fans, the news stings twice. Not only did our team come up short of a second Cup, but the league handed its biggest individual playoff award to the captain of the team that beat us. It is a tough way to close out a season that was full of hope.

What Happened

Jordan Staal had the kind of postseason run that players dream about. Across the six games of the Stanley Cup Final, he scored six goals and added one assist. That is a huge total for a Final series, and it is even more impressive coming from a player who is closer to 40 than he is to 30. Staal has built his long career as a defensive forward, a player known more for shutting down the other team's best lines than for lighting up the scoreboard. So watching him pour in goals against Vegas at this stage of his career caught a lot of people by surprise.

The Conn Smythe Trophy is given to the most valuable player of the entire playoffs, not just the Final. Voters look at the whole run, from the first round all the way to the last game. Staal's leadership and clutch scoring made him the clear choice. He becomes the oldest winner ever, breaking the mark Tim Thomas set in 2011 when Thomas was a goalie for the Boston Bruins. That makes Staal's award a piece of NHL history, even if it is history many Vegas fans would rather not have witnessed in person.

What makes this even harder to swallow is how close the Golden Knights came. Vegas led all playoff teams with 66 goals during the run. That is an offense that was clicking, a team that could score in waves. They simply ran into a Carolina squad that played tight, smart hockey when it mattered most. The series went six games, which means it was a real fight, not a sweep. Vegas pushed Carolina, but the Hurricanes had just enough to close it out and lift the Cup.

The fact that the Conn Smythe went to the captain of the winning team is normal. Most years the award goes to a player on the championship side. But for the opposing fan base, it adds a layer of frustration. It is one thing to lose the series. It is another thing to watch the man who scored six times against your goalies hold up the trophy and give a speech. Staal handled the moment with class, praising his teammates and the city of Raleigh. Still, every Vegas fan watching knew those goals came at our expense.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Las Vegas may be a desert city, but it has become one of the best hockey towns in the entire league. When the Golden Knights arrived in 2017, plenty of people doubted that a market known for slot machines and pool parties would care about ice hockey. Those doubters were proven wrong almost overnight. The team made the Stanley Cup Final in its very first season, and the whole valley fell in love. By the time Vegas won the Cup in 2023, the Golden Knights were not just a team. They were part of the local identity, a thing that brought neighbors together.

So a result like this Conn Smythe news matters here in a way it might not in other places. Vegas fans take their hockey seriously. Bars across the valley fill up on game nights. Kids wear gold jerseys to school. Families plan their evenings around puck drop. When the team loses in the Final, and then the league hands the MVP award to the other side, the whole city feels it. The mood in the valley shifts. People talk about it at the grocery store, at work, and at the gym.

For someone like me who helps families buy and sell homes here, I see how sports tie into the feel of a neighborhood. A strong, fun pro team is one of the things that makes a place feel like home. When the Golden Knights are winning, there is an energy in the air. People feel proud to live here. That pride is real, and it shows up in how folks talk about the city. Even after a painful playoff loss, the love for the team does not fade. If anything, fans get more fired up for the next season.

The Golden Knights also help drive a lot of activity in the valley. Game nights bring people to restaurants, bars, and shops near T-Mobile Arena and beyond. That activity supports local jobs and small businesses. When a season ends, even a tough one, fans start looking ahead to what comes next. The team has shown it can bounce back. That belief is part of what keeps the city connected to its hockey club through the highs and the lows.

It is worth remembering that getting all the way to the Final is no small thing. Most teams never make it that far. Vegas reaching the championship round again shows the franchise is still among the best in the league. That kind of steady success is rare, and it is something Vegas fans should hold onto even on a hard day like this one.

Background and History

The Conn Smythe Trophy has been around since 1965. It is named after Conn Smythe, a builder of the Toronto Maple Leafs and a major figure in hockey history. Each year the Professional Hockey Writers Association votes on which player deserves the honor for the playoffs. The list of past winners reads like a who's who of the sport, with names like Wayne Gretzky, Mario Lemieux, and Sidney Crosby. To join that list is one of the highest honors a hockey player can earn.

Before this year, the oldest winner was Tim Thomas. In 2011, Thomas was a goalie for the Boston Bruins, and he stood on his head game after game to carry Boston to the Cup. He was 37 years old at the time. That record stood for 15 years until Jordan Staal broke it in 2026. Staal was 37 years and 277 days old when he won, just enough to pass Thomas and take the top spot for oldest winner ever.

Staal himself has a long and respected career. He came into the league as a teenager and won a Stanley Cup early on with the Pittsburgh Penguins. He later joined the Carolina Hurricanes and became their captain, a steady leader who set the tone for a young, hard-working team. For years he was seen as a strong two-way center, not a star scorer. That is what makes his Final performance against Vegas so remarkable. At an age when many players have already retired, he saved some of his best hockey for the biggest stage.

The Golden Knights have their own short but rich history. Born in 2017, the team reached the Final in year one, won it all in 2023, and has stayed near the top ever since. That history is why a Final loss like this one feels so heavy in Vegas. Fans here are used to deep runs and big moments. They expect their team to contend, and the team usually delivers.

What Happens Next

With the season over, the Golden Knights now turn to the offseason. The first big question is the salary cap. As covered in a related story, Vegas is heading into a tight cap situation, which means the front office will have hard choices to make. Some players may have to be moved to fit everyone under the limit. Fans should expect a busy summer of trade talk and contract news as the team tries to keep its core together while staying within the rules.

The NHL Draft and free agency both come up in the weeks ahead. These are the tools teams use to reload. Vegas has a history of being aggressive, making bold moves to chase a championship. Do not be surprised if the team shakes things up to come back stronger next fall. The goal is always the same in Vegas: win the Cup, no matter what it takes.

For Jordan Staal and the Hurricanes, the focus shifts to celebration and then to the question of whether they can do it again. Repeating as champions is one of the hardest things in sports. Carolina will enjoy this win, then start the long climb all over. Staal, now an MVP and a record holder, has cemented his place in NHL history. At his age, every season could be his last, so the Hurricanes will want to make the most of the time they have with him.

Back in the valley, fans will spend the summer healing from the loss and getting excited for what comes next. Training camp will arrive before you know it. The schedule will drop, and the countdown to the next season will begin. Vegas hockey is not going anywhere. The team will be back, hungry to flip the script and write a happier ending next time around.
