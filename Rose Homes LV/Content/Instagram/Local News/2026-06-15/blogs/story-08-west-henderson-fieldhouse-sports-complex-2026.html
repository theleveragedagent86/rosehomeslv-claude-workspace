West Henderson Fieldhouse Indoor Sports Complex Approved by City Council

The Henderson City Council has approved a big new project for West Henderson. It is called the West Henderson Fieldhouse, and it will be a giant indoor sports and family fun complex. The building will be 160,000 square feet and stand two levels tall. It is going up behind the Chicken N Pickle near St. Rose and Maryland parkways. The whole project will cost about $70 million, and a company called KemperSports will put in $10 million of that. The city believes the Fieldhouse will create more than 200 permanent jobs and bring in around $1.5 million in tax money every year. The goal is to open the doors in fall 2026.

If you live in Henderson or anywhere in the Las Vegas Valley, this is news worth knowing. A project this large does not just add a place to play. It shapes the whole area around it. New jobs, new visitors, and new family activities all change how a neighborhood grows. In this post, I will walk you through what happened, why it matters for local families and homeowners, and what comes next for this part of West Henderson. I will also share my own take as someone who watches this market every day.

What Happened

During a meeting in June 2026, the Henderson City Council gave the green light to the West Henderson Fieldhouse. The vote moved the project forward after months of planning. Now the team behind it can keep building toward an opening later this year.

So what exactly is being built? The Fieldhouse will be a 160,000-square-foot indoor sports and family entertainment complex. That is a huge amount of space. To picture it, imagine almost three football fields of room all under one roof, spread across two levels. The building is going up behind the popular Chicken N Pickle spot near where St. Rose Parkway meets Maryland Parkway. That area has been growing fast, and this project adds one more major reason for people to visit.

The money side of the project is also worth looking at. The full cost is around $70 million. That is a serious investment. A company called KemperSports has agreed to contribute $10 million toward that total. KemperSports is known for running sports and recreation places, so they bring experience to the table. The rest of the funding comes together through the larger plan the city approved.

The city expects real benefits from the deal. Leaders project that the Fieldhouse will create more than 200 permanent jobs once it is up and running. Those are not just short-term construction jobs. These are lasting roles in areas like staffing, coaching, food service, event planning, and building care. On top of the jobs, the city believes the complex will produce about $1.5 million in tax revenue each year. That money can help pay for services that benefit everyone in Henderson, from roads to parks to public safety.

The timeline is quick. The opening is targeted for fall 2026. That means the building should be welcoming families before the year is out, if everything stays on track. For a project of this size, that is a fast turnaround, and it shows how much the team wants to get it open and running.

An indoor complex like this is a smart fit for our climate. Summers in the Las Vegas Valley get very hot. Playing sports outside in July can be tough, even dangerous. An indoor venue with cooled air lets kids and adults play year-round, no matter the weather. That alone makes the Fieldhouse a useful addition for local families who want active things to do without melting in the heat.

Background and History

To understand why this project matters, it helps to look at how West Henderson has changed. Not long ago, much of this area was open desert land. Over the past several years, builders and the city have worked to turn it into one of the most active growth zones in Southern Nevada. Master-planned communities, schools, shopping centers, and parks have all sprung up across the region.

The corner near St. Rose and Maryland parkways has become a hub of that growth. The arrival of Chicken N Pickle, a popular spot that blends food, drinks, and pickleball, helped draw crowds and energy to the area. The new Fieldhouse will sit right behind it, building on that momentum. Placing the complex next to an already popular destination is a smart move. It creates a cluster of activity where families can spend a whole day.

Henderson has long worked to be known as a family-friendly city. It has invested in parks, trails, and recreation for years. In fact, voters recently renewed a parks property tax, which shows how much the community values these kinds of spaces. The Fieldhouse fits that pattern. It is another step in a long history of Henderson choosing amenities that serve families and keep the city attractive to new residents.

Bringing in a partner like KemperSports also reflects how these projects often come together. The city does not build everything alone. Public and private partners team up to share the cost and the work. KemperSports adds $10 million and brings know-how in running recreation venues. That kind of partnership helps large projects move from idea to reality. It is a common path for big community spaces, and it has helped many cities add features they might not afford on their own.

Between now and then, the team will work to finish construction, set up the inside of the complex, and hire staff for those 200-plus permanent jobs. Expect to see steady progress at the site behind Chicken N Pickle in the coming months.

For families, the most exciting question is what the Fieldhouse will offer once it opens. Indoor sports complexes like this often host basketball, volleyball, indoor soccer, and similar activities. Many also include space for tournaments, leagues, camps, and family events. The two-level design suggests there will be plenty of room for a range of uses. As opening day gets closer, more details about programs and schedules should become available.

So the Fieldhouse could spark even more growth around St. Rose and Maryland parkways in the years ahead. That is good news for residents who want more options close to home.
