PWHL Las Vegas Completes Foundational Roster, Adds Hilary Knight

Las Vegas just took a giant step toward becoming a women's professional hockey city. On June 9, 2026, PWHL Las Vegas finished building the first piece of its team and added one of the most famous players in the sport. The new team will play at T-Mobile Arena and starts in the 2026-27 season. For families across the valley, this is more than a sports headline. It is a sign that Las Vegas keeps growing as a major sports town, and that means more games to attend, more role models for kids, and more reasons people want to live here.

What Happened

On June 9, 2026, PWHL Las Vegas completed Phase 2 of the Professional Women's Hockey League's Expansion Roster Distribution Process. This is the process the league uses to help brand new teams build a starting group of players. PWHL Las Vegas signed five foundational players. Their names are Hayley Scamurra, Erin Ambrose, Mae Batherson, Kendall Cooper, and Hilary Knight.

The signing that got the most attention was Hilary Knight. Knight is a five-time Olympian. She is also the all-time leading scorer in United States Olympic hockey history. That means no American has ever scored more in Olympic hockey than she has. She is one of the biggest stars the sport has ever produced. Knight signed a one-year deal and was the team's final foundational add. In simple terms, she was the last big piece the team locked in to finish this first stage of roster building.

Here is where the story gets interesting. The Knight signing was actually the first step of a sign-and-trade. A sign-and-trade is when a team signs a player and then trades that player to another team as part of the same deal. PWHL Las Vegas signed Hilary Knight, and then sent her to PWHL Detroit. In exchange, PWHL Las Vegas received Detroit's first-round pick in the 2026 PWHL Draft.

So even though Hilary Knight will not actually play in Las Vegas, the move still helped the new team in a big way. Draft picks are valuable. A first-round pick gives PWHL Las Vegas a chance to choose a strong young player early in the draft. Smart teams often trade a star for picks when they are just getting started. It helps them build a deeper roster for the long run instead of relying on just one player.

The other four foundational players give the team a real base to build on. Hayley Scamurra, Erin Ambrose, Mae Batherson, and Kendall Cooper now form the core that PWHL Las Vegas will grow around. The team will keep adding players through the draft and through other signings before the season starts. This June 9 announcement marked the end of the foundational phase, which is the most important early step for any expansion team.

The most exciting part for local fans is the timeline. PWHL Las Vegas will debut in the 2026-27 season. The team will play its home games at T-Mobile Arena, the same building where the Vegas Golden Knights play. Sharing that arena puts women's pro hockey right in the heart of the Las Vegas sports scene.

You might wonder why a new hockey team matters to your home and your neighborhood. The answer is that sports teams shape the way a city grows. When a city adds a pro team, it usually means more jobs, more events, and more visitors. All of that touches the local economy, and the local economy touches the housing market.

Think about what the Golden Knights did for Las Vegas. When they arrived in 2017, they helped change how people saw the city. Las Vegas went from a place with no major pro teams to a true sports town in just a few years. The Raiders came next, then the Aces in basketball, and now a women's pro hockey team is on the way. Each new team adds to the feeling that Las Vegas is a real big-league city, not just a vacation spot.

For families, a new team means more affordable entertainment options close to home. Not every family can take a trip out of town for a big sporting event. Having more teams in the valley means parents can take their kids to a game without a long drive or a hotel stay. PWHL Las Vegas gives families one more reason to spend a fun night together right here in town.

This is also a big moment for girls who play sports. When kids can watch women play hockey at the highest level in their own city, it shows them what is possible. A young girl who laces up skates at a local rink can now dream of playing in front of a packed T-Mobile Arena crowd. That kind of role model matters. It can keep kids active, build confidence, and give them goals to chase.

From a housing point of view, a growing sports scene helps make a city more attractive to new residents. People who move here often look at quality of life, not just home prices. They ask what there is to do on a weekend. They ask if there are activities for their kids. More teams and more events make Las Vegas an easier place to sell to families thinking about relocating. That demand supports home values across the valley over time.

It also helps neighborhoods near T-Mobile Arena and the wider valley. Game nights bring people to restaurants, shops, and hotels. That activity supports local businesses and the people who work in them. A strong local economy is good news for anyone who owns a home here, because steady jobs and steady spending help keep the housing market healthy.

Background and History

To understand why this is such a big deal, it helps to know about the Professional Women's Hockey League. The PWHL is a fairly young league. It launched in recent years as the top level of women's pro hockey in North America. The league was built to give the best women players in the world a stable place to compete and earn a living from the sport they love. Before the PWHL, women's pro hockey had a harder time finding steady footing. The league brought top players together under one banner and gave fans a clear product to follow.

The PWHL started with a small group of teams and has been growing. Adding an expansion team in Las Vegas is part of that growth. Expansion means the league believes the sport can succeed in new markets. Choosing Las Vegas shows the league trusts this city to support women's hockey. That trust did not come by accident. Las Vegas has already proven it can fill arenas and back its teams.

Women's hockey has been growing fast across the country. More girls are playing the sport at the youth level than ever before. International events like the Olympics and the world championships have drawn larger crowds and bigger television audiences. Stars like Hilary Knight helped build that following over many years. Her name on a signing announcement, even in a sign-and-trade, brings attention and energy to the new Las Vegas team.

Las Vegas itself has a short but powerful history as a sports city. For decades, the city had no major pro teams. Many people thought a transient town built on tourism could not support its own franchises. The Golden Knights proved that wrong in a hurry. The team reached the Stanley Cup Final in its very first season and later won a championship. Fans packed the arena and filled the streets. That success opened the door for the Raiders in football and the Aces in women's basketball, and the Aces have won titles of their own.

Each of those teams showed that Las Vegas locals show up. Visitors fill some seats, but the heart of the fan base lives right here in the valley. That loyal local support is exactly what a new team like PWHL Las Vegas needs. The city has built a reputation as a place where pro sports can thrive, and the PWHL clearly took notice.

T-Mobile Arena plays a key role in this story too. The arena sits near the Strip and has hosted hockey, concerts, fights, and big events since it opened. By placing the new women's hockey team in that same building, the league connects PWHL Las Vegas to a venue fans already know and love. Sharing a home with the Golden Knights also creates a natural bond between the two teams and their fans.

What Happens Next

The foundational signings are just the start. With the core in place, PWHL Las Vegas will turn its attention to the 2026 PWHL Draft. That extra first-round pick from the Detroit trade gives the team more chances to land young talent. Expect the front office to keep adding players through the draft and through free agent signings in the months ahead.

Fans should also watch for news about coaching, team branding, ticket plans, and the full schedule. New teams usually roll out these details step by step as the first season gets closer. Season ticket information and single-game plans will likely become a hot topic for local hockey fans who want to be there for the debut.

The biggest date on the calendar is the 2026-27 season opener. That is when PWHL Las Vegas will play its first real games at T-Mobile Arena. The early part of an expansion team's life is rarely smooth. New teams often need time to find their footing and build chemistry. Still, the buzz around the debut should be strong, especially with the city's track record of backing its teams.

For families, now is a good time to start following the team's announcements. Learning the players and the schedule early makes that first game even more fun. It also helps you plan ahead if you want to grab tickets before they sell out, which can happen fast for a new team in a sports-hungry town like Las Vegas.
