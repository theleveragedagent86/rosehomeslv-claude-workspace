Golden Knights Enter Offseason in a Cap Crunch With Key Free Agents

The short answer for Vegas Golden Knights fans is this. The team is about to face one of its trickiest summers in years. After losing in the Stanley Cup Final, the Knights begin the offseason with only about $4.6 million in space under the 2026-27 salary cap. That is not a lot of money in the National Hockey League. At the same time, a long list of important players can leave to sign somewhere else. General manager Kelly McCrimmon has to make hard choices, and some of the faces fans cheered for all season may not be back next year.

If you follow the Knights closely, you know this team has spent every penny it could to win. That all-in style brought a Cup to Las Vegas in 2023 and another deep run this spring. But spending big has a cost, and the bill is coming due. This summer is about math as much as it is about hockey. Let us walk through what is happening, why it matters to people who live here, and what you can do as a fan.

What Happened

Right after the Knights lost the Cup Final to Carolina, the calendar flipped to offseason mode. In the NHL, the offseason is not a quiet time. It is when teams sign players, trade players, and try to build a roster for next year. The biggest rule that shapes all of this is the salary cap. The salary cap is a hard limit on how much money a team can pay all its players combined. Every team gets the same limit. No team is allowed to go over it, even if the owner is willing to spend more.

Vegas is starting the summer with roughly $4.6 million in cap space. To put that in plain terms, that is the amount of room the team has left to add or keep players before it hits the ceiling. A single strong forward or defenseman can cost more than that on his own. So the Knights do not have much wiggle room at all.

The problem grows because of free agency. A free agent is a player whose contract has ended. There are two main kinds. An unrestricted free agent, or UFA, can sign with any team he wants. The current team has no special right to keep him. A restricted free agent, or RFA, is younger and still tied to his current team in some ways, but he can still cost more money or even leave in certain cases.

The Knights have a big group of pending unrestricted free agents. The list includes forwards Reilly Smith, Brandon Saad, and Colton Sissons, plus defensemen Ben Hutton, Jeremy Lauzon, Rasmus Andersson, and Dylan Coghlan. That is seven players who could walk out the door. On top of that, Vegas has two restricted free agents to handle. They are forward Pavel Dorofeyev and goalie Akira Schmid.

Each of these players filled a real role. Reilly Smith is a familiar name who has worn the Vegas sweater before and brings scoring and leadership. Brandon Saad is a steady veteran winger who can play up and down the lineup. Colton Sissons gives the team grit and reliable two-way play. Ben Hutton, Jeremy Lauzon, Rasmus Andersson, and Dylan Coghlan all add depth and toughness on defense, which is exactly what a team needs in a long playoff run. Dorofeyev is a young scorer with a bright future, and Schmid offers help in goal. Keeping all of them is simply not possible with $4.6 million. That is the core of the cap crunch. The Knights cannot afford their own roster.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might wonder why a salary cap story matters to people who live in the valley. The reason is simple. Las Vegas has become a real hockey town, and the Golden Knights are woven into the identity of the city. When the team plays well, the energy spreads far beyond the arena. Sports bars fill up. Jerseys show up at the grocery store and the gym. Kids wear the gold and steel gray to school. The Knights gave Las Vegas a shared sense of pride that the city did not always have when it came to pro sports.

Because the team matters so much here, roster changes hit close to home. Fans grow attached to specific players. When a familiar name leaves because of the cap, it can feel personal. The seven unrestricted free agents on this list are not just numbers on a spreadsheet. Many of them helped the team reach the Cup Final this spring. Losing several of them at once could change the feel of the whole roster.

There is also a money side that connects to daily life here. The Golden Knights are a major part of the local economy. Game nights bring people downtown and to the Strip area. Restaurants, hotels, rideshare drivers, and small shops all see a lift when the team is winning and the building is packed. A strong roster keeps season ticket demand high, and season ticket demand is part of what keeps the whole machine running. When fans believe the team is built to win, they renew their seats and they show up loud.

For people who have moved to Las Vegas in recent years, the Knights are often one of the first things that made the city feel like home. The team started playing in 2017, and many newer residents have never known a Vegas without hockey. That sense of belonging has real value. It is part of why people choose to plant roots here. As someone who helps families find homes in this valley, I see how much a shared team identity matters when folks decide where they want to live and raise their kids.

Background and History

To understand this cap crunch, it helps to know how the Golden Knights got here. The team joined the NHL in 2017 as an expansion club. Most new teams struggle for years. Vegas did the opposite. In their very first season, the Knights reached the Stanley Cup Final. That stunned the hockey world and instantly made Las Vegas fall in love with the sport. The city had finally landed a major pro team, and that team was winning right away.

From the start, the Golden Knights chose an aggressive way of building. The front office, led by Kelly McCrimmon and team president George McPhee in the early years, was never afraid to make a big trade or sign a big contract. That approach paid off in 2023 when Vegas won its first Stanley Cup. The city threw a party that fans still talk about. The Knights proved that a team in the desert could be among the best in the league.

The downside of going all-in is that it makes the salary cap harder to manage over time. When you sign star players to large deals, those deals stack up. The cap rises a little each year, but not always fast enough to cover everything a winning team wants to keep. Many strong NHL teams hit a moment where they simply cannot pay everyone. Vegas is at that moment now. The very players who helped the team reach the Cup Final have earned the right to be paid, and the team does not have room to pay them all.

This is also why the NHL Draft matters so much right now. The 2026 NHL Draft runs June 26 and 27 in Buffalo. The draft is how teams add young talent for the future. Drafted players start on cheap contracts, which is a huge help for a team fighting the cap. Vegas holds a pick in the first round, a pick in the fourth round, and two picks in the sixth round. Those picks let the team bring in fresh players who will not cost much money for years. Smart drafting is one of the best tools a cap-strapped team has. The Knights have leaned more on trades and free agency than on the draft over their history, so these picks carry extra weight this time.

It is worth remembering that the Knights have faced tough cap summers before and still found ways to stay strong. The front office has a track record of creative moves. That history gives fans reason to trust the process, even when the math looks scary on paper.

What Happens Next

The next few weeks will be busy. First comes the draft on June 26 and 27. Watch how Vegas uses its four picks. The team could keep them to add cheap young talent, or it could package picks in a trade to clear cap space or bring in a player who fits better. Either move tells you something about the plan.

After the draft, free agency opens. This is when the seven unrestricted free agents can sign with any team. The Knights will likely try to keep one or two of them at a price that works, while letting others go. Expect McCrimmon to make at least one tough goodbye. He may also look to trade a current contract to free up money. Sometimes a team moves a useful player just to create the space it needs to keep someone else.

The two restricted free agents, Pavel Dorofeyev and Akira Schmid, are a different kind of puzzle. The team wants to keep both, but new deals for young players can still eat into that thin $4.6 million. Getting those contracts done at a fair number is one of the most important jobs of the summer.

By the time training camp opens in the fall, the roster could look noticeably different from the team that reached the Cup Final. Some new faces will arrive. Some familiar ones will be gone. The goal for the front office is to stay competitive without breaking the cap. That balance is the whole challenge.

Ryan's Take

I have lived and worked in this valley long enough to see how much the Golden Knights mean to people here. So I get why a cap story can feel stressful. But I want to offer some calm. This team has been in tight spots before and has always found a way to stay in the hunt. A $4.6 million crunch is real, but it is not the end of anything.

What I love about following this team is how it mirrors the choices we all make at home. You only have so much room in the budget. You cannot keep everything you want, so you decide what matters most and you build around it. The Knights are doing that on a big stage. As a fan, I trust the front office to make smart moves, and I plan to be in the stands cheering no matter which jerseys are on the ice. A winning culture is bigger than any one player, and Vegas has built that culture for real.

What You Can Do

If you are a fan who wants to stay close to the action this summer, here are a few simple steps. First, follow the draft on June 26 and 27. It is fun to watch even if you do not know every prospect, and it gives you a first look at the future of the team. Pay attention to which young players Vegas picks and whether the team trades any picks away.

Second, keep an eye on free agency news right after the draft. Reliable hockey reporters will post quick updates when players sign or get traded. Following the team on its official channels is the easiest way to get news you can trust instead of rumors.

Third, if you have been thinking about season tickets or even single-game tickets, this is a good time to plan. Season ticket demand stays strong in Las Vegas, and seats can move fast when the team looks ready to compete again. Going to a game in person is the best way to feel why this city loves its team. The noise inside that building is something special.

Fourth, support the local spots that thrive on game nights. The restaurants, bars, and small shops near the arena and around the valley all benefit when fans turn out. Being a fan is also about showing up for your community.

And if any of this has you thinking about life in Las Vegas in a bigger way, whether that means moving closer to the action or finding a home in a neighborhood full of Knights fans, I am always happy to talk. The team is part of what makes this city feel like home, and helping families find that home is what I do.
