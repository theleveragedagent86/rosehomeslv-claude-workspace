Las Vegas Condo and Townhome Listings Drop Sharply

The number of condos and townhomes for sale in Southern Nevada fell hard in May 2026. Only 951 of these attached homes were listed for sale across the Las Vegas Valley. That is a 7.4 percent drop from April and a steep 13.8 percent drop from the same time last year. These numbers come straight from Las Vegas Realtors, the group that tracks home sales in our area. If you have been hoping to buy a condo or townhome as your first home, this news matters a lot to you.

Condos and townhomes have always been the cheapest way to step into owning a home in Las Vegas. They cost less than a regular single-family house with its own yard. So when fewer of them go up for sale, the front door to ownership gets tighter. This is happening at the worst possible time. Single-family home prices just hit a record high in our valley. That leaves a lot of buyers stuck between two hard choices. Let us walk through exactly what is going on and what it means for you.

What Happened

In May 2026, just 951 condos and townhomes were listed for sale in Southern Nevada. To put that in plain terms, listings are the homes that owners have put on the market for buyers to see and bid on. When the listing count goes down, there are fewer homes to choose from. That 951 number is 7.4 percent lower than April 2026. It is also 13.8 percent lower than May 2025, which was just one year earlier. So the supply of these homes has shrunk both month over month and year over year.

The price side of the story is mixed. The median price of a condo or townhome that sold in May was $295,000. The word median means the middle price. Half of the homes sold for more than that, and half sold for less. That $295,000 figure was up 1.7 percent from April. So prices ticked up a little from the month before. But compared to May 2025, prices were actually down 3.9 percent. And the current price still sits below the record high of $315,000 that condos and townhomes hit back in October 2024.

Sales also slowed down. A total of 509 condos and townhomes actually sold in May 2026. That is 8.9 percent lower than the number sold in May 2025. So fewer homes were listed, and fewer homes changed hands. When you put all these pieces together, you get a clear picture. The attached-home market in Las Vegas is getting smaller and quieter, not bigger and busier.

Why does this happen? When owners do not list their homes, buyers have less to pick from. Some owners may be waiting for prices to climb back to that 2024 record. Others may be holding on to low mortgage rates they locked in years ago. If they sell, they would have to buy again at today's higher rates. So they stay put. That keeps homes off the market and pushes the listing count down. The result is a tight, slow market where buyers fight over a small pool of homes.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

This story hits some groups of people harder than others. First-time buyers feel it the most. A condo or townhome is often the only home a first-time buyer can afford. These homes cost less than a single-family house because you share walls with neighbors and you do not get a big private yard. The trade-off is a lower price. So when these homes vanish from the market, the easiest path into ownership starts to close. A first-time buyer who waited too long may now find only a handful of choices.

Downsizers feel the squeeze too. Many older Las Vegas residents own a large family home and want to move into something smaller and easier to care for. A condo or townhome is perfect for that. There is less yard work and less space to clean. But if there are only 951 of these homes for sale across the whole valley, a downsizer may have to wait months to find the right fit. Some may give up and stay in a home that is too big for them.

The biggest reason this matters comes down to affordability. The median price of a single-family home in Las Vegas just set a record at $490,000. Compare that to the $295,000 median for a condo or townhome. That is a gap of nearly $200,000. For many working families, that gap is the difference between owning and renting. The condo and townhome market is the affordable entry point. It is the rung on the ladder that lets people start building wealth through their home. When that rung gets weak, the whole ladder shakes.

There is one small bright spot for buyers. Prices are down 3.9 percent from last year, and they sit below the 2024 record. So today's condo and townhome prices are not at their peak. A patient buyer who can find the right home may pay less than they would have a year ago. The challenge is simply finding that home in such a thin market. Less choice means buyers must move fast and come prepared when the right home shows up.

Background and History

To understand today's market, it helps to look back a little. Condos and townhomes have long played a special role in Las Vegas. As our valley grew over the past few decades, builders put up thousands of these attached homes near jobs, shopping, and the freeways. They gave new workers, young couples, and retirees an affordable way to own instead of rent. For a long time, that worked well. Supply kept up with demand, and prices stayed within reach for everyday families.

The market peaked back in October 2024. That is when the median price of a condo or townhome hit its all-time high of $315,000. After that, prices cooled off a bit. By May 2026, the median had slipped to $295,000. That is a drop from the peak, which sounds like good news for buyers. But the real story is not just about price. It is about how few homes are out there to buy.

The shrinking supply did not happen overnight. Higher mortgage rates have played a big part. When rates jumped, many owners who had locked in low rates years ago decided to stay put. They did not want to sell and then borrow again at a higher cost. This is sometimes called the lock-in effect. It keeps homes off the market. At the same time, builders have focused more on single-family homes in many new communities. That means fewer brand-new condos and townhomes are being added to the supply. Put those two forces together over time, and you get the sharp listing drop we see today. The 13.8 percent year-over-year fall is the latest chapter in a longer trend of tightening attached-home supply.

What Happens Next

So where does the market go from here? No one can predict the future for certain, but a few things seem likely. As long as mortgage rates stay around current levels, many owners will keep holding on to their homes. That means the lock-in effect could continue, and listings may stay low for a while. If rates drop, more owners might feel free to sell and move up or downsize. That could finally bring more condos and townhomes onto the market. So mortgage rates are the key thing to watch.

Prices are harder to call. They rose a little from April to May, but they are still down from last year and below the 2024 record. If supply stays tight and buyers keep competing for the few homes available, prices could slowly climb again. On the other hand, if buyers pull back because homes feel too expensive or rates feel too high, prices could stay flat or dip. The balance between low supply and cautious demand will decide which way prices move.

For first-time buyers and downsizers, the months ahead call for patience and readiness. Because there are so few homes for sale, the good ones may not last long. Buyers who get their financing lined up early and who know exactly what they want will have the best shot. Those who wait and hope for a flood of new listings may be disappointed. The smart move is to prepare now so you are ready to act the moment the right condo or townhome appears.
