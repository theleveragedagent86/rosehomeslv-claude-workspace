32 CCSD Seniors Named National Merit Scholarship Winners

Thirty-two seniors from the Clark County School District have been named 2026 National Merit Scholarship winners. This is one of the highest academic honors a high school student in America can earn. These students stood out from more than a million test takers across the country, and now they get to carry that recognition with them as they head off to college. For a school district that has spent much of the year in the news for budget cuts and tough financial choices, this is a bright and welcome moment worth celebrating.

The recognition comes in three different forms. Ten of the CCSD students earned the $2,500 National Merit Scholarship, which is awarded to a select group chosen from more than 15,000 national finalists. One student from Advanced Technologies Academy, often called A-Tech, won a corporate-sponsored scholarship. The remaining 21 students received college-sponsored awards, which are funded by some of the 138 colleges and universities that take part in the National Merit program. Together, these 32 students represent a strong showing for Southern Nevada.

What Happened

The National Merit Scholarship Corporation released its final list of 2026 winners, and 32 of them attend schools right here in Clark County. FOX5 Vegas reported the news on June 8, 2026, sharing how these local students earned their spots in a program that recognizes academic talent at the national level. The path to becoming a winner is long and demanding, and these students worked toward it for years.

The journey begins in junior year, when students take the PSAT/NMSQT. This is the test that decides who qualifies. Out of roughly 1.3 million students who take it each year, only about 16,000 become semifinalists. From there, students must complete a detailed application, write an essay, keep up strong grades, and earn a confirming score on the SAT to move on to finalist status. Only after all of that do a smaller group of finalists become actual scholarship winners. The 32 CCSD seniors made it through every step.

The 10 students who won the $2,500 National Merit Scholarship were chosen from more than 15,000 finalists nationwide. That is an incredibly competitive pool. These awards are given based on a student's record, abilities, skills, and accomplishments, and they are not tied to any one college. The money can be used wherever the student decides to enroll.

The single corporate-sponsored winner came from A-Tech, a magnet school in downtown Las Vegas known for its focus on technology and rigorous coursework. Corporate-sponsored scholarships are funded by companies and businesses that partner with the National Merit program. Many of these awards go to students whose parents work for the sponsoring company, or to students who plan to study a field the company cares about, such as engineering or computer science.

The largest group, 21 students, earned college-sponsored scholarships. These awards are paid for by the colleges and universities the students chose to attend. A total of 138 higher education institutions across the country fund these scholarships. When a finalist tells a participating college that they plan to enroll there, that school may offer a renewable scholarship that helps cover tuition over several years. This can add up to thousands of dollars in support.

What makes this news feel even bigger is the timing. CCSD has faced a hard year filled with talk of budget cuts and staffing changes. A story like this reminds the community that strong teaching and strong students are still very much alive across the valley, even during difficult times.

Background and History

The National Merit Scholarship Program has been around since 1955. It was created by the National Merit Scholarship Corporation, a private nonprofit group based in Illinois. The goal was simple: find and reward the brightest high school students in America and help them afford college. Over the decades, the program has handed out hundreds of thousands of scholarships and has become one of the most respected academic honors in the country.

The program works the same way every year. Students take the PSAT/NMSQT during their junior year of high school. The scores set a cutoff, and that cutoff is different in each state because it is based on how students in that state perform. The top scorers become semifinalists, and from there the field narrows to finalists, and finally to scholarship winners. Earning the title of National Merit Scholar is a goal that ambitious students and their families plan for years in advance.

Clark County School District is one of the largest school districts in the entire United States. It serves around 300,000 students across hundreds of schools, stretching from the heart of Las Vegas out to Henderson, North Las Vegas, Summerlin, and the surrounding desert communities. Running a district this size is no small task, and CCSD has faced many challenges over the years, including crowded classrooms, teacher shortages, and budget pressure. Yet within that huge system, certain schools and programs have built strong reputations for academic excellence.

Magnet schools and select programs have long been a source of pride for the district. A-Tech, the school that produced this year's corporate-sponsored winner, is a good example. It draws students from across the valley who want a challenging education focused on technology. Other strong programs, including advanced placement courses and gifted programs spread across many CCSD high schools, give talented students the chance to push themselves and prepare for tests like the PSAT.

This year's group of 32 winners did not appear out of nowhere. They are the result of years of teaching, study, and family support, all happening inside a public school system that often gets more attention for its struggles than its wins. That is why this milestone feels especially meaningful. It shows the depth of talent that exists in our schools and the dedication of the educators who help bring it out.

It is also worth noting that recognition like this tends to build on itself. Schools that produce National Merit winners often attract more high-achieving students in the years that follow. Success draws success, and over time that can lift the reputation of an entire campus and the neighborhood around it.

What Happens Next

For the 32 winning seniors, the next step is college. Most of them have already chosen where they will enroll, and many will carry their scholarship money with them to help pay for tuition, books, and other costs. The college-sponsored winners will see their awards renewed year after year as long as they keep up their grades, which means the support can follow them through all four years of school.

For CCSD as a whole, this news offers a chance to highlight what is going right. After a year of difficult headlines, district leaders are likely to point to these students as proof that the schools are still doing important work. Expect to see these winners honored at school events and possibly at school board meetings, where they can be recognized in front of their families and communities.

Looking further ahead, the current junior class is already preparing for the same path. Students who took the PSAT this past fall are waiting to learn if they qualified as semifinalists for next year's cycle. The teachers and counselors who helped this year's group will be working with the next wave of students, hoping to repeat the success. Strong programs build momentum, and a good year like this one can inspire younger students to aim higher.

For families thinking about the future, this is a reminder to pay attention to which schools and programs are producing results. If you have younger children, the choices you make now about where to live and which schools to target can shape the opportunities your kids have later. It is never too early to start thinking about the path ahead.
