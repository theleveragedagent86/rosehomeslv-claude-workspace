About 26 Percent of Las Vegas Homes Bought With Cash in March

Here is the short answer. About 26.1 percent of all homes sold in the Las Vegas Valley in March 2026 were bought with all cash. That means more than one out of every four homes sold here was paid for without a mortgage loan. This number comes from a study by the real estate company Redfin, and it was reported by the Las Vegas Review-Journal on June 3, 2026. Our local cash number sits just below the national average of 28.8 percent. It is also down a little from last year. Cash purchases dropped 1.8 percent compared to the same time the year before.

If you are a buyer who needs a home loan, this story matters a lot to you. For years, many people felt like they could not win a home unless they had piles of cash. That is starting to change. Let us walk through what these numbers really mean for everyday families in our valley.

What Happened

Redfin is a national real estate company that studies home sales across the country. They looked at every home sale in many big cities, including ours. When they finished counting, they found that 26.1 percent of homes sold in the Las Vegas Valley in March 2026 were all-cash deals. An all-cash deal means the buyer paid the full price without borrowing money from a bank. No mortgage. No monthly loan payment. Just money straight to the seller.

That number is big, but it is not the biggest in the country. The national average was 28.8 percent. So Las Vegas was a touch below what was happening across the rest of America. Our cash share was close to normal, not wild or strange.

Here is the part that surprised some people. The number of cash buyers actually went down. All-cash purchases in our area fell 1.8 percent compared to the year before. That may sound small, but it points to a real shift. Fewer people are paying cash now than were paying cash a year ago.

Why would cash buying drop? Redfin gave a clear reason. The market has flipped into what we call a buyer-friendly market. That means there are far more sellers than buyers right now. When there are tons of homes for sale and fewer people shopping, sellers have to compete for attention. Homes sit longer. Sellers drop their prices. They become more willing to deal.

In a market like this, you no longer need a cash offer to win. A few years back, things were the opposite. There were way more buyers than homes. People got into bidding wars. Ten or fifteen families would fight over one house. Sellers could pick whoever they wanted, and they almost always picked the cash buyer. Cash deals close fast and have fewer risks for the seller. So if you needed a loan, you often lost.

Now that the crowd of buyers has thinned out, sellers cannot be so picky. A buyer with a home loan can win the house. They do not have to beat a cash offer to get the keys. Redfin said this levels the field for buyers who need financing. That is great news for first-time buyers and working families who do not have hundreds of thousands of dollars sitting in the bank.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Let us be honest about something. Most people do not have enough cash to buy a whole house outright. The typical home in our valley costs a lot of money. To pay cash, you would need a giant savings account or money from selling another home. Most regular families simply cannot do that. They need a mortgage to make the dream of owning a home come true.

So for the past few years, the rise of cash buyers felt scary to a lot of locals. It felt like the deck was stacked against them. They would find a home they loved, make a strong offer, and then lose it to someone waving cash. That pattern wore people down. Some folks gave up the search entirely.

This new report gives those buyers reason to hope. With cash buyers slipping and the market turning friendly for buyers, a financed buyer now has a real shot. You can walk into a deal knowing the seller will actually consider your offer. You can ask for repairs. You can ask for help with closing costs. You can take your time during inspections. None of that was easy when cash was king.

This shift also matters for our neighborhoods as a whole. When a large share of homes goes to cash buyers, many of those buyers are investors. Investors often turn homes into rentals or fix them up to sell again. That can be fine, but it can also push out families who want to live in the home themselves. When more financed buyers can win, more of those homes go to people who plan to move in and stay. That helps build steady, rooted neighborhoods where people know their neighbors.

There is one more reason this matters. It affects how you should think about your own offer. If you have been told you need to act crazy fast or offer way over the asking price, that advice may be out of date. The market has changed. The pressure has eased. You can make a smart, careful offer and still win. Knowing that can save you stress and money.

Background and History

To understand today, it helps to look back. The Las Vegas housing market has gone through some wild swings over the years. After the crash around 2008, home prices here fell hard. Many homes sat empty. During that time, investors swooped in with cash and bought thousands of cheap homes. Cash buying spiked because deals were everywhere and banks were nervous about lending.

Then came the boom years. Around 2020 through 2022, mortgage rates dropped very low. Lots of people wanted to buy. At the same time, builders had not built enough homes. So we had a shortage. Too many buyers chasing too few houses. That is when bidding wars took over. Homes got dozens of offers. Prices shot up month after month.

In that boom, cash became a superpower. A cash offer told the seller, I can close fast and nothing will fall apart. Sellers loved that. So cash buyers kept winning, and the share of cash deals stayed high. Regular buyers with loans got squeezed out again and again. It was a tough time to be a first-time buyer in Las Vegas.

Things began to cool when mortgage rates climbed. Higher rates made monthly payments bigger. That priced some buyers out and made others wait. The crowd of shoppers shrank. At the same time, more sellers decided to list their homes. Slowly the balance tipped from a seller market toward a buyer market. That is where we are now in 2026.

It is worth knowing how cash share works as a number. Cash share is just the slice of all sales that were paid with cash. When the total number of sales changes, the cash share can move too. If financed buyers come back into the market in big numbers, the cash slice gets smaller even if the same amount of cash buyers are still shopping. So a falling cash share can also be a sign that everyday buyers are returning. That is a healthy thing.

Las Vegas has often tracked close to the national trend, and this report shows that again. Our 26.1 percent sat right near the national 28.8 percent. We are not an outlier. We are part of a broad story playing out across the whole country, where buyers are slowly getting back some power they lost during the boom.

What Happens Next

So where do things go from here? No one can promise the future, but we can look at the signs. If the market stays friendly to buyers, with lots of homes for sale and fewer shoppers, then financed buyers should keep doing well. Sellers will keep needing to compete, and that competition helps you when you make an offer.

Mortgage rates will play a huge role. If rates ease up, more buyers can afford payments and will jump back in. That could shrink the cash share even more, because so many of the new buyers would use loans. But it could also bring back some competition, since more shoppers means more demand. It is a balance to watch.

Cash buyers and investors will not disappear. They are always part of the market. But their grip has loosened for now. As long as the valley has plenty of homes for sale, the days of needing cash just to compete look like they are fading. That is a good window for families who have been waiting on the sidelines.

Keep an eye on monthly reports from groups like Redfin and our local Realtor association. These numbers shift month to month. One report is a snapshot, not the whole movie. Watching the trend over several months tells you far more than any single figure ever could.

What You Can Do

If this report has you thinking about buying, here are some steps you can take right now. First, get a mortgage pre-approval letter from a lender. This shows sellers you are serious and that a bank has already checked your money. In a buyer-friendly market, a clean pre-approval makes your offer strong, even without cash.

Second, do not be afraid to negotiate. Since there are more sellers than buyers, you have room to ask for things. You can ask the seller to fix problems found in the inspection. You can ask them to help pay your closing costs. You can ask for a fair price instead of overpaying. Sellers are more open to deals than they were a couple of years ago.

Third, take your time and do your homework. You do not have to rush an offer the same day a home hits the market. You can tour homes, sleep on it, and look at the numbers carefully. That breathing room is one of the best gifts of a buyer market. Use it.

Fourth, learn your neighborhood. Look at how long homes are sitting and whether prices are dropping. A home that has been listed for a while often comes with more room to negotiate. Knowing these details helps you make a confident offer.

Finally, lean on a local expert. The numbers in this story are valley-wide. Your street, your zip code, and your price range may look different. A local agent can show you exactly what is happening where you want to buy. That knowledge can save you money and stress.
