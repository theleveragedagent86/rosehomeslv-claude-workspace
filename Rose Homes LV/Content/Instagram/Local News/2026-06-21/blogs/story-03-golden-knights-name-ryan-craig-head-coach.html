Vegas Golden Knights Name Ryan Craig as New Head Coach

The Vegas Golden Knights have a new head coach, and his name is one a lot of local hockey fans already know. On June 17, 2026, general manager Kelly McCrimmon named Ryan Craig the fifth head coach in team history. Craig, who is 44 years old, signed a three-year deal. He takes over from John Tortorella, who held the job before him. This is the first time Craig has ever been a head coach in the NHL, and it caps a long climb that ran right through our own backyard.

What makes this hire stand out is where Craig comes from. For the past three seasons, he was the head coach of the Henderson Silver Knights, the team's American Hockey League club that plays right here in our valley. Before that, he spent six years as a Vegas Golden Knights assistant coach, from 2017 to 2023. In other words, the team did not go hunting for a big name from far away. They promoted from within, and they promoted from Henderson. For anyone who lives in Henderson or follows the local sports scene, that is a story worth understanding.

What Happened

Here are the plain facts. On June 17, 2026, the Vegas Golden Knights announced that Ryan Craig would be their next head coach. General manager Kelly McCrimmon made the call. The deal is for three years. Craig becomes the fifth person to lead the team since it started play in 2017, following in the steps of the coaches who came before him, the most recent being John Tortorella.

Craig is not a stranger to the organization. He served as a Golden Knights assistant coach from 2017 through 2023, which means he was on the bench during the early years of the franchise and helped shape how the team played. After that, he moved down to lead the Henderson Silver Knights in the American Hockey League. Over three seasons in Henderson, his teams won 28 games, then 29 games, then 39 games. That steady climb in the win column showed the front office that his teams were getting better year over year.

This is his first head coaching job at the NHL level. He has spent years learning the craft as an assistant and then running his own bench in the minor leagues. Now he gets the top seat with the big club. The promotion ties the Golden Knights and the Silver Knights together at the very top of the organization, which is exactly the kind of pipeline the team built Henderson to create.

It is worth pausing on the choice itself. When an NHL team needs a head coach, the front office can go in a lot of directions. They can hire a famous name with a long resume, they can poach a rising star from another club, or they can promote from inside their own system. Each path carries different risks and rewards. The Golden Knights went with the inside option, and they did it knowing Craig had never run an NHL bench before. That is a vote of confidence in the person and in the system that shaped him. It says the team trusts what it has built more than it craves a splashy headline.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might be wondering why a coaching change matters to anyone who is not a die-hard hockey fan. The answer is that pro sports teams are now part of how this region works. They shape jobs, draw visitors, and give neighborhoods an identity. When the team makes a move like this, the ripple effects reach further than the ice.

Start with Henderson. The Silver Knights play their home games at Lee's Family Forum in Henderson, an arena that opened to give the valley a second pro hockey venue. When the head coach of that Henderson team gets promoted to lead the Golden Knights, it puts a spotlight on Henderson as a place that grows talent, not just a place that hosts games. That kind of attention helps the city's reputation, and reputation matters when people decide where to live and where to buy a home.

There is also the simple matter of the fan economy. Game nights fill restaurants, bars, and hotels near T-Mobile Arena on the Strip and near the arena in Henderson. People buy jerseys, grab dinner before the puck drops, and book rides home after. A stable, winning team keeps those crowds coming. A homegrown coach who fans can root for adds to that energy. When the team does well, the businesses around it tend to do well too, and that activity is part of what makes neighborhoods near these venues feel alive.

For homeowners, the link is indirect but real. Areas near major arenas and entertainment districts often see steady interest from buyers who want to be close to the action. Henderson and Summerlin, two of the valley's most popular places to live, both benefit from the wider sports and entertainment boom that hockey helped kick off. None of this means a coaching hire will move your home value on its own. It does mean the team's health is woven into the larger story of why people keep moving here.

There is one more angle worth naming, and it is about identity. Before 2017, this valley did not have a major league team to rally around. Now it has several, and hockey was the one that broke the ice, so to speak. When relocating buyers ask me what makes Las Vegas feel like home and not just a vacation spot, the answer is often the everyday things, the youth leagues, the watch parties, the local pride in a team that belongs to us. A homegrown coach feeds that pride. It is a small detail in the big picture of a home search, but identity and belonging are part of why people choose one community over another, and that is the soil good neighborhoods grow in.

Background and History

To understand why this hire feels like a full-circle moment, it helps to look back at how the Golden Knights and the Silver Knights came to be. The Vegas Golden Knights joined the NHL in 2017 as the first major pro sports team to call the Las Vegas Valley home. They were an instant hit. The team reached the championship round in its very first season, which is almost unheard of for a brand-new franchise, and that early success helped lock in a passionate local fan base that has stayed loyal ever since.

Ryan Craig was part of that ride from the start. He joined the coaching staff as an assistant in 2017, the team's first year, and stayed through 2023. During those six seasons, he helped run a team that became one of the most consistent winners in the league. He saw up close what it takes to build a culture from nothing, and he carried those lessons with him when he took the next step in his career.

That next step was Henderson. The Henderson Silver Knights were created as the Golden Knights' top minor league affiliate, the place where young players develop and prove they are ready for the NHL. The whole point of an affiliate team is to build a pipeline, a steady flow of talent moving up from the minors to the big club. Craig spent three seasons running that Henderson team, and his squads got better each year, climbing from 28 wins to 29 to 39. Players he coached in Henderson have gone on to contribute in Las Vegas.

So when McCrimmon chose Craig, he was not just picking a coach. He was rewarding the pipeline the organization built. Craig knows the Golden Knights system because he helped run it as an assistant. He knows the young players because he coached many of them in Henderson. That kind of continuity is rare, and it is part of why the front office felt comfortable handing him his first NHL head job.

It also says something about how the Golden Knights see Henderson. The affiliate is not just a stop along the way for prospects. It has become a proving ground for coaches too. By choosing the man who ran the Silver Knights, the team sent a clear message that Henderson is a real part of how the franchise grows, top to bottom. For a city that has worked hard to build its own identity in the shadow of the Strip, having its hockey program produce the next head coach of the NHL club is a point of genuine local pride.

What Happens Next

With a new coach in place, attention turns to the offseason and the season ahead. The Golden Knights head into the 2026 NHL Draft on June 26 and 27 in Buffalo, and they do so with limited draft capital. The team's first selection is the second-to-last pick of the third round, after they traded away their first-rounder and gave up a second-round pick tied to a 2026 rules matter. That means Craig will not have a fresh wave of high draft picks to work with right away, so his ability to develop players already in the system will matter even more.

This is where Craig's Henderson background could pay off. A coach who already knows the young players coming up from the affiliate has a head start. He understands their strengths and where they need to grow because he watched them every night in the minors. With tight cap space and few draft picks, getting the most out of the existing roster and the prospects in Henderson will be a big part of how the team performs.

For fans, the next steps are the usual rhythm of an NHL offseason. Free agency, trades, and roster decisions will all unfold over the summer, and Craig will help shape who stays and who goes. By the time training camp opens in the fall, we will start to see how his version of the team takes shape. Expect the front office to lean on the Silver Knights pipeline to fill gaps, which keeps Henderson front and center in the story.

Locally, the practical things to watch are simple. Keep an eye on the schedule for both the Golden Knights at T-Mobile Arena and the Silver Knights in Henderson. Strong seasons from both clubs keep the energy high in the entertainment districts and keep game-night traffic flowing to nearby businesses. A coaching change does not promise wins, but a smooth, homegrown transition gives the team its best shot at staying steady.
