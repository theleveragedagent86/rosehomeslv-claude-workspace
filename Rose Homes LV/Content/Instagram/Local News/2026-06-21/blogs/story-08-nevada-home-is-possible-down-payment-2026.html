Nevada's Home Is Possible Program Offers Down Payment Help in 2026

Here is the short answer. Nevada runs a program called Home Is Possible that can hand qualified buyers somewhere between 2 and 4 percent of their loan amount to use toward the down payment and closing costs. That help comes as a forgivable second mortgage, which means you do not have to pay it back if you stay in the home for at least three years. The program runs through the Nevada Housing Division and pairs with a regular 30-year fixed loan. For 2026, the household income limit sits near $105,000 and the home price cap is $832,750. There is even a special version for teachers that offers a flat $7,500 in help.

If you have been sitting on the sidelines because the down payment felt out of reach, this is the kind of program that can change the math. Below I will explain how it works, why it matters for people trying to buy here in the Las Vegas Valley, where the program came from, and the exact steps you can take to see if you qualify. The goal is to give you a clear, calm picture so you can decide if this path fits your life.

How the Program Works

Home Is Possible is run by the Nevada Housing Division, a state agency that helps Nevadans buy and keep homes. The program is built to solve one of the biggest roadblocks for new buyers, which is coming up with cash for the down payment and the closing costs. Even when a buyer can handle the monthly payment, that upfront pile of cash is often the wall that stops them.

Here is the basic structure. You take out a regular 30-year fixed-rate mortgage through an approved lender. On top of that loan, the program gives you a grant of roughly 2 to 4 percent of your total loan amount. You can put that money toward your down payment, your closing costs, or both. So if you borrow $300,000, a 3 percent grant would be about $9,000 in help. That money can be the difference between buying this year and waiting another two or three years to save.

The help arrives as a forgivable second mortgage. That phrase can sound scary, so let me break it down. A second mortgage is just a smaller loan that sits behind your main loan. The word forgivable is the key part. As long as you live in the home as your main residence for three years, the state forgives that second loan and you owe nothing on it. If you sell or move out before the three years are up, you may have to pay some or all of it back. After you cross the three-year mark, that help is yours to keep.

There are a few rules you have to meet to qualify. For 2026, the household income limit is around $105,000. That figure can shift over time, so always check the current number before you count on it. The maximum home price you can buy with the program is $832,750. You also need to meet credit and lender guidelines, and most buyers must finish a short homebuyer education course. That class teaches you the basics of budgeting, the loan process, and how to keep your home once you own it.

There is also a special version made just for educators. The teacher program offers a flat $7,500 toward down payment and closing costs for eligible licensed Nevada teachers. This is the state's way of helping the people who teach our kids put down roots in the communities where they work. If you teach in a Clark County classroom, this version is worth a close look.

One important note on numbers. The income limit, price cap, and exact grant percentages can change as the program updates each year, and they can also vary a little by the loan type you choose. Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

For a lot of valley families, the down payment is the single biggest thing standing between them and a home of their own. Home prices in Las Vegas have climbed for years, and the cash needed to buy has climbed right along with them. A program that covers even a few percent of the loan can shrink that wall down to something a working family can actually clear.

Think about what 3 percent of a loan really means in real dollars. On a typical valley starter home, the loan amount might land somewhere in the low to mid $300,000s. A grant of 2 to 4 percent on that loan could be in the range of $6,000 to $13,000. For many buyers, that is one to two full years of hard saving handed to them up front. That is time you get back, and it is time the market does not sit still while you wait.

This matters even more in a city like ours, where rents take a big bite out of every paycheck. When you are paying rent, it is hard to save for a down payment because the rent itself eats the money you would set aside. Programs like Home Is Possible help break that cycle. They let renters who have steady income but thin savings make the jump to ownership without waiting years to build a giant cash cushion first.

The income limit also matters in a way that surprises people. At around $105,000 for a household, the program reaches well past the lowest earners. A two-income family with two regular jobs in the valley can easily fall under that ceiling. So this is not only a program for the lowest paid. It is built for the broad middle of working Las Vegas, including the very families who feel priced out the most.

And for teachers, the stakes are personal. Clark County has worked hard for years to recruit and keep classroom teachers. When a teacher can buy a home near the school where they work, they are more likely to stay, build a life here, and keep teaching our kids year after year. The $7,500 teacher version is a small but real push in that direction.

Background and History

To understand why this program exists, it helps to know who runs it. The Nevada Housing Division is a state agency that has worked for decades to make homeownership reachable for more Nevadans. It does this by partnering with banks and lenders to offer loans and grants that a buyer could not get on the open market alone. Home Is Possible is one of its best-known programs.

The idea behind the program is simple and old. For most American families, owning a home has long been the main way they build wealth over a lifetime. But the down payment has always been the hardest part of getting started. Down payment assistance programs exist to lower that very first hurdle, because once a family is in a home, time and steady payments tend to do the rest of the work.

Nevada launched Home Is Possible to put a state-backed tool in the hands of regular buyers. Over the years the program has grown and changed. The income limits and price caps get updated as the cost of housing rises, so the program keeps pace with the real market instead of falling behind. The teacher version was added to target a specific need the state cared about, which was keeping educators rooted in their communities.

The reason a program like this feels so needed right now goes back to the past few years of the housing market. During the pandemic, mortgage rates fell to historic lows and a buying rush pushed Las Vegas home prices up fast. Then rates more than doubled and prices stayed high. Buyers today face both high prices and high borrowing costs at the same time. That double squeeze is exactly the kind of problem down payment help is designed to soften.

It is worth knowing that Home Is Possible is not the only assistance program out there, but it is one of the most widely used statewide options. Some cities and counties run their own separate help programs, and federal loan types like FHA already allow lower down payments on their own. Home Is Possible can stack a grant on top of a solid loan, which is why so many first-time buyers in the valley end up looking at it.

What Happens Next

The most useful thing to know about Home Is Possible is that it is an ongoing program, not a one-time event with a deadline. As of 2026, it is open and available to buyers who qualify. That means you do not have to rush in a panic, but you also should not assume the exact terms will stay frozen forever.

Each year the Nevada Housing Division reviews and updates the program. Income limits, the price cap, and the grant details can shift to match the market and the funding the program has on hand. The 2026 numbers, an income limit near $105,000 and a price cap of $832,750, are the current figures. If you are reading this and planning a purchase, the smart move is to confirm the live numbers before you start shopping, because they may have nudged up or down by the time you apply.

Funding is the other thing to watch. Programs like this run on a pool of money, and the most popular options can fill up or pause when demand is high. That does not mean the program is going away. It means timing can matter. If you are close to ready, it is worth starting the conversation with an approved lender sooner rather than later, so you are positioned to act while funds are flowing.

Looking ahead, the broader Las Vegas market is the backdrop. Most forecasters expect mortgage rates to stay near 6.5 percent through the rest of 2026, and valley home prices have stayed high while inventory creeps up a little. In a market like that, down payment help becomes more valuable, not less, because it offsets at least one part of the cost squeeze buyers face. As long as the program stays funded, it will keep being a real tool for valley families.

Ryan's Take

I have walked a lot of first-time buyers through the down payment wall, and I want to be honest about how much a program like this can help. The down payment is almost always the thing that stops good, hard-working people from buying. They have the income. They have the steady job. What they do not have is a fat savings account, because rent and life keep eating it. Home Is Possible is built for exactly those buyers.

That said, I always tell my clients to look at the full picture, not just the free money. A grant that covers your down payment is wonderful, but you still need to be comfortable with the monthly payment, the property taxes, the insurance, and any HOA fees. The program gets you in the door. Your budget keeps you there. I would rather see you buy a home you can comfortably afford with help than stretch into a home that keeps you up at night.

Here is the other thing I want you to hear. The three-year forgiveness rule is a fair trade, not a trap. The state is basically saying, plant roots here for three years and the help is yours free and clear. For most buyers, that is an easy promise to keep, because they are not planning to flip the house in a year anyway. Just know the rule going in so you are not surprised if your plans change.

I also want teachers to pay special attention. If you teach in Clark County, the $7,500 version is a real benefit you have earned. I have seen educators assume they could never buy near where they work, then learn there is help made specifically for them. Do not count yourself out before you check. The worst case is you find out you do not qualify. The best case is you get a meaningful boost toward a home of your own.

My bottom line is this. Free or forgivable down payment help is one of the few tools that can genuinely move a family from renting to owning years earlier than they thought possible. If the down payment is your wall, this program deserves a serious look. Just pair it with an honest budget and a lender who knows the program well.

What You Can Do

If Home Is Possible sounds like it might fit your situation, here are practical steps to take next.

First, check the current numbers. Visit the official Home Is Possible website at homeispossiblenv.org or contact the Nevada Housing Division to confirm the live income limit, price cap, and grant details. Programs update, so you want the numbers that apply to you today, not last year.

Second, find an approved lender. Home Is Possible works through a list of approved lenders, not every bank in town. An approved lender can run your numbers, tell you which version of the program fits, and explain how the forgivable second mortgage works alongside your main loan. This is the single most useful step you can take.

Third, get pre-approved. Before you fall in love with a home, get pre-approved so you know your real budget and your real grant amount. Pre-approval also makes your offer stronger when you find the right house, because sellers take prepared buyers more seriously.

Fourth, plan for the homebuyer education course. Most assistance programs require a short class. It is not hard, and it actually helps. The course walks you through budgeting, the loan process, and how to keep your home once you own it. Treat it as a useful step, not a chore.

Fifth, if you are a teacher, ask specifically about the educator version. The $7,500 teacher help has its own rules, so tell your lender up front that you teach in Nevada and want to explore that path. Bring your license details so they can check your eligibility quickly.

Sixth, look at your full monthly budget, not just the down payment. Add up the loan payment, taxes, insurance, and any HOA fees, then compare that to your income. The grant solves the upfront cost. A solid monthly plan keeps you safe and happy in the home for years to come.

Finally, talk to someone who knows the valley and these programs. A short conversation about your income, your savings, and the neighborhoods you like can tell you quickly whether Home Is Possible is the right tool for you, or whether a different program fits better. Every buyer is different, and the right plan depends on your own numbers, not a one-size-fits-all answer.
