PWHL Las Vegas Drafts Penn State's Tessa Janecke Third Overall

PWHL Las Vegas made the first draft pick in team history on June 17, 2026, and the choice was a forward from Penn State named Tessa Janecke. The new women's pro hockey team used the third overall pick at the 2026 PWHL Draft in Detroit to grab her. That pick did not come out of nowhere. Las Vegas got it earlier in a trade that sent away star player Hilary Knight. So the very first player the team ever drafted is now tied to one of the biggest moves the franchise has made so far.

For people who live here, this matters more than a single hockey pick might seem to. A brand new pro sports team is putting down roots in our city. That brings jobs, crowds, and energy to whatever arena ends up hosting the games. It also adds one more reason for families to want to live in the Las Vegas Valley. As your local real estate agent, I keep an eye on news like this because new teams and new venues shape neighborhoods over time.

What Happened

The 2026 PWHL Draft took place on June 17 in Detroit. The PWHL is the Professional Women's Hockey League. It is the top pro league for women's hockey in North America. This was the league's entry-level draft, where teams pick young players who are ready to turn pro. For PWHL Las Vegas, it was the first draft the team had ever taken part in. That made it a milestone moment for the franchise.

Las Vegas held the third overall pick. With it, the team selected Tessa Janecke, a forward who played college hockey at Penn State. A forward is a player whose main job is to score goals and create scoring chances. By taking Janecke at number three, Las Vegas signaled that they see her as a building block for the years ahead. She becomes the first player the team ever drafted, which puts her name in the record books for the franchise no matter what happens next.

The third overall pick itself has a story behind it. Las Vegas did not have that pick at the start. The team got it in a trade. Earlier, Las Vegas had star forward Hilary Knight on the roster. Knight is one of the most well known names in women's hockey. The team made the choice to trade her away, and in return they received the top-three pick they used on Janecke. So the draft pick and the trade are linked. One move set up the other.

That kind of trade is a bet on the future. When a team trades a famous veteran for a high draft pick, it usually means the team wants to build around younger talent for the long run. It is a sign that PWHL Las Vegas is thinking past its first season. They wanted a player they could grow with, and they used the Knight trade to get the chance to pick one near the top of the draft.

Background and History

To understand this pick, it helps to know a little about the PWHL itself. The league launched as the top home for pro women's hockey in North America. It brought together many of the best players in the world under one banner. The goal was to give women's hockey a stable, well run league with real teams, real schedules, and real fan bases. Adding a team in Las Vegas was a big step in spreading the sport to new markets out west.

Las Vegas earning a PWHL team fits a pattern we have watched play out over the last several years. This city went from having no major pro teams to having several in a short window. The Golden Knights arrived in 2017 and reached the Stanley Cup Final in their very first season. The Raiders moved to town and opened Allegiant Stadium. The Las Vegas Aces of the WNBA won titles and built a loyal following. Each new team made it easier for the next one to come.

PWHL Las Vegas is the newest chapter. Before a team can play, it has to build a roster. That is where moves like the Hilary Knight trade and the Janecke pick come in. Signing a star like Knight gave the team instant name value and helped sell the idea of pro women's hockey in the desert. Trading her for a top draft pick then let the team pivot toward younger talent. Both moves were about setting up the franchise for the road ahead, not just the first game.

Tessa Janecke comes from Penn State, a respected college hockey program. Players who reach the top of a pro draft usually stood out in college through their scoring, their speed, or their all around play. Being picked third overall means scouts and team leaders saw her as one of the very best young forwards available. For a franchise making its first ever pick, choosing a highly rated college forward is a careful, forward looking decision.

It is worth pausing on what it means to be a franchise's first ever draft pick. Every team has one, and that player carries a special place in team history forever. Fans remember the first pick the way they remember the first goal or the first win. For Janecke, that label sticks with her no matter how her career unfolds. She is the cornerstone the team chose to start building its homegrown identity around.

The Hilary Knight piece of this is worth a closer look too. Knight has been one of the faces of women's hockey for a long time. When a young franchise signs a player of that profile, it gets a quick jolt of attention and credibility. People who had never thought about pro women's hockey suddenly had a name they recognized. That kind of buzz helps a new team sell tickets and build a fan base before it has even played a full season. So the Knight signing did its job even though it ended in a trade.

Then comes the turn. Trading that star for a top-three pick is a classic move for a team that wants to control its own future. A veteran star has tremendous value right now, but younger players are the ones a team can grow with for many seasons. By cashing in the Knight value for a high pick, Las Vegas gave itself the chance to draft a player it can develop on its own terms. The decision shows a front office that is thinking several years down the road, not just chasing one headline.

This blend of star power and youth is something Las Vegas sports fans have seen before. The Golden Knights leaned on smart roster building to compete fast. The Aces paired experienced leaders with rising talent on the way to titles. PWHL Las Vegas appears to be reading from a similar playbook. Get attention early, then build a roster that can last. For a city that has fallen in love with winning teams, that approach should feel familiar and welcome.
