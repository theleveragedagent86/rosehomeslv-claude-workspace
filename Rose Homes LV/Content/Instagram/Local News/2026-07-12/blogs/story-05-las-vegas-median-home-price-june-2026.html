Las Vegas Housing Market Normalizes

Las Vegas Median Home Price Holds at Record $490,000 in June as Single-Family Sales Jump 18% From a Year Ago

The median price for a single-family home in the Las Vegas valley held at a record $490,000 in June 2026, and single-family home sales jumped about 18 percent compared with a year earlier. That is the headline number local buyers and sellers keep quoting, and it tells a clear story: prices are strong, and people are still buying.

The data comes from Las Vegas Realtors, the local trade group that tracks nearly every home sold through the MLS. A record price is one thing. A record price paired with a big jump in sales is another. It means demand did not blink, even with mortgage rates parked in the mid-six-percent range.

What Happened

Las Vegas Realtors reported that the median sales price for an existing single-family home reached $490,000 in June 2026. That figure matched the record set earlier in the year, so the market did not cool off during the hot summer months the way some people expected. For a valley that sat closer to the $470,000 range not long ago, holding at $490,000 is a meaningful mark.

The bigger surprise was the sales volume. The number of single-family homes sold climbed roughly 18 percent compared with the same month a year earlier. When you see prices at a record and sales rising at the same time, that is a sign of real buyer demand and not just a quiet market propped up by a few high-end deals. Buyers are showing up, making offers, and closing.

George Kypreos, the president of Las Vegas Realtors, pointed to steady demand and tight supply as the drivers. The group tracks sales across Clark County, from Summerlin and Centennial Hills in the northwest to Green Valley and Inspirada down in Henderson. When Las Vegas Realtors releases a monthly number, it is the most reliable local read we have, because it is built from actual closed sales rather than estimates or online guesses.

The single-family number stands in sharp contrast to the attached market. Condos and townhomes in the valley slipped to a median of about $292,000 in June, down roughly 4.3 percent from a year earlier. So the two halves of the Las Vegas market are moving in different directions. Houses set a record while condos softened. That split matters a lot depending on what you are trying to buy or sell.

It helps to put the $490,000 figure in context. A year ago, the valley median sat lower, and just a few years back it was closer to the $470,000 range. Holding a record through June, which is deep into the busy summer selling season, shows the market has real staying power. June is usually one of the strongest months of the year, with families racing to close before the school year starts, and this June delivered on both price and volume.

The sales surge is worth a second look as well. An 18 percent jump in closings is not a small bump. It means hundreds more homes changed hands than in the same month a year earlier, and that kind of movement does not happen unless buyers, lenders, and sellers are all active. In a slow market, homes sit and price cuts pile up. In June, the opposite happened across much of the valley.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

If you own a single-family home in the valley, this news is good for your net worth. A record median means the equity in your house likely grew again. Homeowners in areas like Summerlin, Skye Canyon, Mountains Edge, and Southern Highlands have watched their values climb for years, and June kept that trend alive. If you have been thinking about selling, the market is rewarding sellers with strong prices and quick sales.

For buyers, the picture is tougher but not hopeless. A $490,000 median means half of the single-family homes sold for more and half sold for less. There are still homes below that line, especially in North Las Vegas, parts of the east valley, and older neighborhoods closer to the center of town. The 18 percent jump in sales tells you that plenty of buyers are finding a way to make the numbers work.

Renters feel this too. When home prices stay high and keep climbing, some would-be buyers stay in rentals longer, which keeps pressure on rents. At the same time, new apartment projects like the 300-unit Ainsley at Tivoli Village near Rampart Boulevard and the 80-unit Beals-Henderson Pointe affordable community in northwest Las Vegas are adding supply. More rental options can ease that pressure over time.

Families trying to move up or down within the valley need to watch both sides of the trade. If you are selling a condo to buy a house, the math got harder, because condo values dipped while house values held at a record. If you are downsizing from a house into a condo or townhome, the timing may actually work in your favor right now.

First-time buyers feel the squeeze the most. At $490,000, a 20 percent down payment is nearly $98,000, which is a heavy lift for a young family. That is one reason the condo and townhome market, sitting near $292,000, is worth a close look for anyone trying to get a foot on the ladder. A softer attached market can be a real opening for a buyer who is priced out of a single-family home in Henderson or the northwest.

Sellers should not read a record median as a green light to overprice. Buyers today are sharp, and they have the same online tools you do. Homes that are priced right and show well are the ones getting the quick sales and the multiple offers. Homes that reach for a number the comps do not support still sit, even in a strong market. The record price is a tailwind, not a magic wand.

Background and History

The Las Vegas housing market has been on a long climb since the recovery that followed the 2008 crash. Back then, the valley was one of the hardest-hit markets in the country, with foreclosures everywhere and prices cut in half. The comeback has been steady, and prices have pushed to new records again and again over the past few years.

What makes the current run different is how it is happening. Instead of the frenzy of the 2021 boom, when rock-bottom rates sent prices soaring almost overnight, this market is grinding higher on tight supply. The valley showed about 3.5 months of housing supply in June. A balanced market usually sits closer to five or six months, so we are still short on inventory, and that shortage keeps prices firm.

Demand from outside the valley plays a big role. A recent Realtor.com report found that California buyers, led by the Los Angeles metro at about 23.7 percent, drive close to a third of the out-of-market demand for Las Vegas homes. Californians keep coming here for lower prices, no state income tax, and more space for the money. Las Vegas also ranked as the 5th most-searched housing market in the country in a Redfin report, which shows how much national interest the valley pulls in.

Cash buyers remain a real force too. Roughly 23 percent of June sales in the valley were cash deals, and distressed sales sat near historic lows. That mix of strong cash demand and very few foreclosures is a big reason prices have stayed so sturdy even as mortgage rates hover in the mid-six-percent range.

The supply story goes back years. Las Vegas is boxed in by federal land on almost every side, so there is a limit on how fast the valley can spread out. New home builders have been busy in places like Skye Canyon, Cadence in Henderson, and the far southwest, but they cannot build fast enough to fully meet demand. That land squeeze has been quietly holding prices up for a long time and will keep doing so.

What Happens Next

The number everyone will watch is mortgage rates. Freddie Mac's most recent weekly survey showed the 30-year rate ticked up to 6.49 percent. At the $490,000 median, small rate moves change the monthly payment by real money. A buyer putting 20 percent down on a $490,000 home is financing $392,000. Every quarter-point change on that loan shifts the payment by roughly $50 to $60 a month, which adds up fast over the life of a mortgage.

If rates ease later in the year, expect even more buyers to jump in, which could push prices higher and keep the sales pace strong. If rates climb, some buyers may step back, and the market could cool a bit. Either way, the tight supply gives sellers a cushion. As long as inventory stays near 3.5 months, a sharp price drop is unlikely.

The national picture gives helpful contrast. US existing-home sales dipped 2.4 percent in June, while the national median hit a record $440,600. So the country as a whole saw sales slow even as prices rose. Las Vegas went the other way on volume, with single-family sales climbing about 18 percent from a year earlier. That gap is a reminder that the valley marches to its own beat, driven by out-of-state demand and a tight, land-locked supply that most markets do not share.

Watch the neighborhood splits too. Recent data showed North Las Vegas leading the valley in appreciation while Summerlin topped the valley on price at about $697,000. Those gaps tell you where value shoppers and luxury buyers are each finding their spots. The next few monthly reports from Las Vegas Realtors will show whether the record holds through the rest of the summer selling season.

Keep an eye on inventory as fall approaches. If more sellers list their homes in the coming months, buyers will get a little more room to negotiate. If listings stay tight, the seller's edge holds. The 3.5 months of supply figure is the number to track, because it is the clearest sign of which way the balance of power is tilting between buyers and sellers in the valley.

There is also plenty of new construction and development in the pipeline that could shape the market over the next year. Projects like the 1,350-acre Redevelopment Area 3 plan the city is advancing, along with new apartment and affordable housing communities, will slowly add homes to the valley. None of it changes the June record, but over time more supply is the healthiest path to keeping Las Vegas livable for working families.

Ryan's Take

As someone who works this valley every day, the June numbers line up with what I am seeing on the ground. Well-priced single-family homes in strong neighborhoods are moving, and good listings still draw multiple offers. The 18 percent jump in sales is the part that stands out to me, because it proves buyers are not sitting on the sidelines. They adjusted to the mid-six-percent rates and got back to work.

My honest read is that this is a healthier market than the 2021 frenzy. It is supported by real demand and low distress, not cheap money and panic buying. If you are a seller, price it right and your home will sell. If you are a buyer, do not wait for a crash that the fundamentals just do not support. Focus on the right neighborhood and a payment you can live with. The house matters more than trying to time the exact bottom, which almost nobody catches anyway.

I also tell clients to zoom in past the valley-wide median. Your outcome depends on your specific neighborhood, your floor plan, and your timing far more than on a single headline number. A well-kept home in a desirable pocket of Henderson or the northwest can command a premium, while an outdated home a few miles away may sit. The record median is a helpful gauge, but real strategy happens street by street, and that is where a local set of eyes pays for itself.

What You Can Do

If you are thinking about selling, start by getting a real read on your home's value. The median is a valley-wide number, and your specific street, floor plan, and condition can put you well above or below it. A local market analysis will show you what similar homes near you actually sold for, so you can price with confidence and not guess.

If you are buying, get pre-approved before you shop. In a market with tight supply and quick sales, sellers take offers from ready buyers more seriously. Know your comfortable monthly payment at today's rates, and build in a little room in case rates move. Then focus your search on neighborhoods that fit your budget, whether that is North Las Vegas for value, Henderson for schools and parks, or Summerlin if you are stretching for the top end.

It also pays to run your real payment before you fall in love with a home. Take the $490,000 median as a starting point, plug in today's 6.49 percent rate, and add property taxes, insurance, and any HOA dues that come with the community. Communities in Summerlin and Henderson often carry HOA fees, so factor those in early. Knowing your true monthly cost keeps you from stretching past your comfort zone once you are under contract.

Either way, lean on real local data instead of national headlines. The Las Vegas market has its own story, and the single-family and condo halves are moving in opposite directions right now.
