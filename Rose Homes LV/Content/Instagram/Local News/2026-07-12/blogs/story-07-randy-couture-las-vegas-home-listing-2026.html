MMA Legend Randy Couture Lists His Custom Las Vegas Home for $1.1 Million

UFC Hall of Famer Randy Couture just put his custom-built Southern Highlands home up for sale, and the asking price is $1.1 million. The six-time UFC champion is leaving Las Vegas to live full time on his private ranch in Arizona, so a home he built with his own hands is now on the open market.

The listing is more than a celebrity headline. It is a real look at what a well-built home in a guard-gated Las Vegas community is worth in the summer of 2026, and it comes with a twist most listings never offer. A buyer can take home a piece of Couture's fighting career along with the keys.

What Happened

Randy Couture, a six-time UFC champion and a member of the UFC Hall of Fame, listed his four-bedroom, four-bathroom house in Las Vegas for $1.1 million. The home sits inside The Foothills, a guard-gated community within Southern Highlands on the far south end of the valley. The listing went live in early July 2026 and was first reported by the Las Vegas Review-Journal on July 10.

The house measures 3,743 square feet and sits on a 0.38 acre lot. It was originally built in 2003 for an initial sale price of $456,355. That means the current $1.1 million ask is more than double the home's first price tag, which lines up with how much Las Vegas real estate has climbed over the past two decades.

The listing agent is Ariel Ives with The Barton Group, part of Keller Williams Las Vegas. Ives said the sale carries an interesting twist. Some of Couture's memorabilia will convey with the home through a separate bill of sale. In plain terms, a buyer can own a slice of Vegas fight history along with the property. That kind of add-on is rare in residential real estate, and it turns an already unique home into a true one-of-a-kind sale.

The features list reads like a custom build because it is one. The home has 52 solar panels that come with full property coverage, no inversion, and a $12,000 NV Energy credit. There is a heated pool with a fresh Pebbletek finish, smart home integration, and custom finishes throughout. Outside, the yard has a built-in natural gas fire pit, a real grass backyard, and a resort-style layout. Inside you will find a stone pillar front entry, a stone-built shower, and a wine cooler. Ives said Couture built the house himself from start to finish, and that shows in the detail work.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

A celebrity listing is fun to scroll through, but this one has real value for anyone watching the local market. The $1.1 million price on a 3,743-square-foot home in a guard-gated community shows where the upper-middle tier of Las Vegas real estate sits right now. That is roughly $294 per square foot, which is a useful benchmark for owners in Southern Highlands, Mountains Edge, and Southern Highlands-adjacent neighborhoods who want to know what their own home might fetch.

It also puts the valley's price growth in sharp focus. This home sold new for about $456,000 in 2003. Listing it now at $1.1 million means the value has grown by roughly 140% over 23 years, even after all the ups and downs the market has seen. For longtime owners, that is a reminder of how much equity can build in a home you hold for the long haul. For newer buyers, it is a sign of why getting in sooner rather than later has paid off for so many valley families.

The home is priced well above the current Las Vegas median. The June single-family median held at a record $490,000, so Couture's home sits in the luxury and near-luxury band that makes up a smaller slice of local sales. That segment behaves differently than the mid-market. Custom homes, guard-gated communities, and one-owner properties often take longer to sell and draw a specific kind of buyer who wants something you cannot find in a tract neighborhood.

There is a lifestyle angle too. The features that make this home stand out, like the paid-off solar array, the heated pool, and the real grass backyard, are exactly the things Las Vegas buyers ask about most. Energy costs and summer heat are top of mind here. A home with 52 solar panels and an NV Energy credit speaks directly to buyers who worry about power bills during a valley summer that can push past 105 degrees.

Background and History

Randy Couture is one of the most recognized names in mixed martial arts. He won UFC titles in two different weight classes and became a six-time champion across his career. He entered the UFC Hall of Fame and later moved into acting and business. Like many fighters and entertainers, he made Las Vegas his home base, and he did it in a hands-on way by building his own house from the ground up in 2003.

Southern Highlands has long been a draw for athletes, entertainers, and business owners who want privacy and space without leaving the valley. The community sits at the southern edge of the Las Vegas area, close to the I-15 and a quick drive to the airport and the Strip. Guard-gated pockets like The Foothills offer added security and a quieter feel, which is why they appeal to people who value their privacy.

Couture's listing is part of a busy stretch for high-profile Las Vegas homes. In recent weeks the market has seen a Summerlin mansion from a well-known attorney list for $22.5 million, a castle-themed Henderson compound from a former headliner magician come back to market, and a celebrity gossip columnist list a home for $4.25 million. Las Vegas has always attracted famous owners, and the steady stream of celebrity listings keeps the luxury end of the market in the news.

The reason for the sale is simple and relatable. Couture is leaving the valley to live full time on his private ranch in Arizona. Plenty of longtime valley owners reach a point where they want more land, more quiet, or a change of pace. His move is a high-profile version of a choice regular families make every year when they decide the next chapter looks different from the last one.

What Happens Next

The home is now on the open market, so the next step is finding a buyer who wants both the property and the story that comes with it. Custom homes in guard-gated communities often sit longer than typical listings because the pool of buyers is smaller. The memorabilia twist could speed things up by drawing interest from fight fans and collectors who might not otherwise shop in this price range.

Watch how the price holds. Luxury and near-luxury sellers sometimes start high and adjust after the first few weeks on market. If the home moves quickly at or near the $1.1 million ask, that is a strong signal for the upper-middle tier in Southern Highlands. If it lingers, it may point to buyers being pickier at that price point as mortgage rates hover in the mid-six-percent range.

The bigger thing to watch is what the sale says about the valley's luxury segment heading into the back half of 2026. Single-family prices hit a record in June, and demand from out-of-state buyers, especially from Southern California, remains strong. If celebrity and custom listings keep selling, it reinforces the idea that Las Vegas is still a destination market for people with money to spend and a desire to plant roots in the desert.

Ryan's Take

This listing is a great teaching moment for anyone trying to understand the Las Vegas market. The headline is Randy Couture, but the lesson is about equity and quality. A home that sold new for about $456,000 in 2003 is now asking $1.1 million. That is the power of buying and holding in a growing valley. I tell clients all the time that time in the market beats timing the market, and this home is proof of it.

The custom-build angle matters too. Buyers pay a premium for homes where the owner cared about every detail, and they should. Paid-off solar, a heated pool with a fresh finish, real grass, and smart home features are not just nice extras. They are the things that hold value and make a home easier to sell down the road. If you are thinking about upgrades before you list, focus on the improvements that solve real Las Vegas problems like heat, energy bills, and outdoor living. That is where you get your money back.

What You Can Do

If you own a home in Southern Highlands, Mountains Edge, or another south valley community, this listing is a good reason to check your own equity. Prices have moved a lot since many of these homes were built, and you may be sitting on more value than you think. A quick, no-pressure home value review can tell you where you stand and whether now is a smart time to sell or refinance.

If you are a buyer eyeing the luxury or near-luxury tier, use listings like this one to study the market. Look at price per square foot, days on market, and what features command a premium. A custom home with a story can be a great buy, but you want to understand what you are paying for and how it will hold value. Getting pre-approved and working with an agent who knows the guard-gated communities will put you in a strong spot when the right home comes along.

And if you are simply curious about what your neighborhood is doing, that is worth a conversation too. The valley market shifts month to month, and knowing your numbers helps you make smart moves whether you plan to sell next month or five years from now.
