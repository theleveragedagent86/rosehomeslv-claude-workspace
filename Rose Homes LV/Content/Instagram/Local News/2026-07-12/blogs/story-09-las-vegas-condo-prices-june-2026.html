Las Vegas Condo and Townhome Prices Slip to $292,000 as Attached Market Diverges

Las Vegas condo and townhome prices slipped to about $292,000 in June, down 4.3% from a year earlier, even as single-family houses held at a record $490,000. That is a big deal, because for years these two parts of the market moved together, and now they are pulling apart in a way that changes the math for first-time buyers and investors.

The numbers come from Las Vegas Realtors data reported by World Property Journal on July 9, 2026. In plain terms, houses keep setting records while attached homes are getting cheaper. If you have been priced out of a house and hoped a condo would be your way in, this new gap is worth understanding before you make a move.

What Happened

According to Las Vegas Realtors, the median price for a condo or townhome in Southern Nevada landed near $292,000 in June 2026. That is down about 4.3% compared to June of last year. At the same time, the median price for an existing single-family home held at a record $490,000, based on the same monthly data set.

George Kypreos, who leads Las Vegas Realtors, and other local market watchers have pointed to this split as one of the more interesting stories of the summer. Attached homes, which include condos, townhomes, and some lofts, are behaving very differently from detached houses right now. The two categories used to rise and fall side by side. In June, they clearly did not.

The gap between the two is now roughly $198,000. A house costs about $490,000 at the middle of the market, while a condo or townhome sits near $292,000. That is a spread of about 68%. A few years ago the difference was smaller in percentage terms, which meant the jump from a condo to a house felt more reachable. Today that leap is wider.

It helps to remember what a median is. It is the middle number, where half of the sales are higher and half are lower. So a $292,000 median does not mean every condo costs that much. Plenty sell for less, and plenty sell for more, depending on the community, the square footage, the age of the building, and whether it sits in Summerlin, the southwest valley, Henderson, or closer to the Strip. The median just tells us where the middle of the market landed for the month.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

For first-time buyers, this is actually a window worth watching. Condos and townhomes are often the entry point into ownership. They usually cost less than a house, they need less yard work, and they let people stop renting and start building equity. When condo prices soften while house prices climb, the entry door to ownership gets a little easier to open, at least on price.

But there is a catch that every Las Vegas buyer needs to know about. The sticker price is only part of the cost. Condos and townhomes almost always come with homeowners association fees, and attached communities sometimes carry higher monthly dues than a typical single-family neighborhood. A lower purchase price can be balanced out by a bigger monthly HOA payment. You have to look at both numbers together to know your real cost of ownership.

For renters thinking about buying, the softer condo market changes the buy-versus-rent question. With attached prices down 4.3% year over year, a condo payment may pencil out closer to what you already pay in rent, especially in communities where the HOA covers water, trash, roof maintenance, or exterior insurance. Every situation is different, so it pays to run the actual numbers for the specific building you are looking at.

For current condo owners, a 4.3% dip is not a reason to panic, but it is a reason to pay attention. If you bought recently and planned to sell soon, your timeline matters. Real estate is a long game, and one soft year does not erase years of gains. Still, if you are thinking about moving up into a house, the wider gap between condos and houses means the move up now costs more than it did a couple of years ago.

Background and History

For most of the last decade, condos and houses in Las Vegas moved in the same direction. When the valley boomed, both rose. When the market cooled, both softened. The two categories were tied together because they answered the same demand, which was people wanting to own a home in a fast-growing region with strong job creation and lots of people moving in from other states.

Several things pushed the two apart over the past year. Insurance is one. Attached communities have faced rising master insurance costs, and those costs flow into HOA dues. When dues climb, some buyers get nervous about the total monthly payment, which can soften demand and prices for that type of home. Lending rules are another factor. Some condo communities have trouble getting approved for certain loan programs, which shrinks the pool of buyers who can purchase there.

Meanwhile, single-family houses kept their strength. Las Vegas has seen steady demand from out-of-state buyers, including a large share from Southern California, and houses remain the top choice for families who want a yard, a garage, and space to grow. Tight supply of detached homes has helped push that side of the market to record levels, while the attached side has more competition and softer pricing.

Interest rates play a role too. With mortgage rates hovering in the mid-six-percent range, buyers are careful about every dollar of their monthly payment. When money is tight, a high HOA fee on a condo can tip the scale toward a house with a lower fee, even if the house costs more up front. All of these forces added up to the divergence we saw in June.

What Happens Next

The big question is whether this gap keeps growing or starts to close. If condo prices keep sliding while houses hold, the entry point into ownership stays affordable, and that could pull in more first-time buyers and investors looking for cash flow. More buyers competing for condos could eventually stop the price slide and even reverse it. Markets tend to find a floor when the value looks too good to pass up.

Watch the HOA and insurance story closely. If master insurance costs level off, and if lenders keep approving more condo communities, demand for attached homes could firm up in the second half of the year. On the other hand, if dues keep climbing, condo prices may stay soft for a while longer. The insurance picture is one of the most important things to track in this segment.

Keep an eye on the monthly Las Vegas Realtors reports through the rest of 2026. One month is a data point, not a trend. If we see the condo median dip again in July and August, that tells us the divergence is real and lasting. If it bounces back, June may turn out to be a short-term wobble. Either way, buyers and sellers should track the numbers rather than react to a single headline.

Ryan's Take

I see this as a real opportunity for the right buyer, and a caution flag for the wrong one. If you are a first-time buyer who wants to stop renting, a softer condo market plus a record house market means the attached side is where the deals are hiding right now. You just have to do your homework. Read the HOA budget, ask about the reserve fund, and get the master insurance details in writing before you fall in love with a unit.

For investors, a $292,000 median with rents holding steady can pencil out nicely, but the HOA is make or break. I always run the numbers with the full monthly cost, not just the mortgage. And I always tell clients that condos and houses are two different markets in Las Vegas now, so compare a condo to other condos, not to what the house down the street just sold for. Play the right game and this soft patch can work in your favor.

What You Can Do

If you are thinking about buying a condo or townhome in Las Vegas, start by getting the full financial picture on any unit you like. Ask for the HOA budget, the current monthly dues, the reserve study, and the master insurance policy. These documents tell you the real cost of owning there and whether the community is on solid footing. A great price on a shaky HOA is not a great deal.

Next, get pre-approved before you shop, and ask your lender specifically whether they will finance the community you are considering. Some condo projects are approved for certain loans and some are not, and finding out early saves you heartbreak later. Knowing your loan options up front makes you a stronger buyer when you find the right place.

Finally, compare the right things. Line a condo up against other condos in the same area, not against detached houses, since the two markets are diverging. Look at recent sales in the specific building or community, factor in the HOA, and decide based on your total monthly cost and how long you plan to stay.
