Developer Lands $40M to Build 300 Apartments Behind Tivoli Village

A developer just secured $40 million to build 300 new apartments right behind Tivoli Village near Rampart Boulevard in Las Vegas. The project is called Ainsley at Tivoli, and it is backed by the Calida Group with funding raised through the CanAm EB-5 program. If you live or shop in the Rampart and Summerlin area, this is going to add hundreds of new neighbors and change one of the last open parcels near the shopping village.

New apartments near a busy retail center always spark strong reactions. Some people see fresh rental options and more foot traffic for local shops. Others worry about traffic, parking, and what 300 more households will do to the streets they drive every day. Here is what the project involves, why it matters, and what happens next.

What Happened

The Las Vegas Review-Journal reported on July 2, 2026 that a developer landed $40 million in funding to move forward on a new apartment community behind Tivoli Village. The community will hold 300 units and sits near Rampart Boulevard on the west side of the Las Vegas valley. The name of the project is Ainsley at Tivoli.

The developer behind the project is the Calida Group, a Las Vegas based company that builds and manages apartment communities across the valley. Calida is not new to this part of town. The company has built and run several rental communities in the west valley and around Summerlin, so it knows the area and the renters who want to live there.

The $40 million came through the CanAm EB-5 program. EB-5 is a federal program that lets foreign investors put money into approved projects in the United States. In exchange, those investors can qualify for a path to a green card if the project creates enough jobs. CanAm is one of the larger firms that pools this kind of money and places it into real estate deals. For a big apartment build, this kind of financing helps close the gap between a bank loan and the full cost of construction.

Tivoli Village itself is a well known open air shopping and dining center near the corner of Rampart Boulevard and Alta Drive. It has restaurants, shops, offices, and an Italian inspired look that has made it a landmark on the west side. Building 300 apartments right behind it means the new residents will be steps from stores, food, and services that are already up and running. That is a big selling point for a rental community, and it is exactly the kind of location renters pay a premium to live near.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Las Vegas has a housing supply problem, and this project speaks right to it. The valley median single family home price sat at a record $490,000 in June 2026. That price has pushed a lot of would be buyers into the rental market, at least for now. When 300 new units come online near a desirable area like Rampart, it gives renters more choices and can take some pressure off rents across the west side.

For people who already live near Rampart Boulevard, the biggest day to day concern is traffic. Rampart is a busy road, and the stretch near Tivoli Village already sees heavy use during shopping hours and weekends. Adding 300 households means more cars pulling in and out, more trips to nearby stores, and more demand at the local intersections. How the project handles its entrances, parking, and traffic flow will matter a lot to the neighbors around it.

There is also a quality of life angle for renters who want to live near Summerlin without paying Summerlin home prices. Tivoli Village puts dining, coffee, and shops within walking distance. For a young professional, a downsizing empty nester, or someone new to the valley, being able to walk to a restaurant instead of driving is a real draw. That walkable setup is still rare in most of Las Vegas, so a community built around it will stand out.

Local shops and restaurants inside Tivoli Village stand to gain too. Hundreds of new residents next door means a built in base of customers who can pop over for dinner, a haircut, or a quick errand. Retail centers live or die on foot traffic, and a full apartment community right behind the shops can help keep those storefronts busy. That is good news for the small businesses that make the center feel alive.

Background and History

Tivoli Village opened in phases starting in the early 2010s. It was pitched as a European style village with shops on the ground floor and offices above. The center had a rocky start during the years after the last housing crash, when many storefronts sat empty and the project changed hands. Over the past decade it found its footing, added tenants, and became a steady part of the Rampart corridor.

The land behind and around the center has always been valuable because of its location. It sits close to Summerlin, close to the 215 Beltway, and inside one of the more established parts of the west valley. Open parcels in this pocket do not stay empty for long, and developers have eyed this area for housing for years. An apartment community is a natural fit next to an existing retail hub.

The Calida Group has been part of the valley apartment boom that took off as Las Vegas grew and home prices climbed. As buying a home got harder, demand for well built rentals went up. Calida and other local firms answered by building garden style and mid rise communities across the west and southwest valley. Ainsley at Tivoli fits that pattern, placing new rentals in a spot where people already want to be.

EB-5 money has also played a quiet but real role in Las Vegas construction. The program has helped fund hotels, mixed use projects, and housing across the valley for years. It is a way for developers to raise a large chunk of capital without leaning entirely on traditional lenders. Using CanAm EB-5 funding for this project shows how these deals still come together in Las Vegas, even in a higher rate environment where financing is tougher than it used to be.

What Happens Next

With the $40 million in hand, the next steps are the ones that turn a funded plan into a real community. That usually means finishing permits, breaking ground, and starting vertical construction. A project of 300 units is not built overnight. Communities this size often take a couple of years from the first shovel to the first move in, depending on weather, labor, and supply.

Neighbors should watch for construction traffic and road work near Rampart Boulevard as the site gets going. Big builds bring trucks, crews, and temporary lane changes. There may also be public meetings or notices tied to the site plan, landscaping, and traffic studies. If you live nearby, those are the moments to speak up about entrances, buffering, and how the project connects to the existing streets.

The other thing to watch is pricing. When the community gets closer to opening, the rents it sets will tell us a lot about the west valley market. If Ainsley at Tivoli leans toward higher end rents, it signals that developers still see strong demand near Summerlin. If it comes in more moderate, it could give renters a real new option in a pricey part of town. Either way, the first lease rates will be a useful read on where the rental market is headed.

Showing up or sending a comment is the clearest way to have a say in how the entrances, parking, and landscaping come together. Your input carries the most weight before the concrete is poured.

If you are a renter, keep an eye on Ainsley at Tivoli as it gets closer to opening. A brand new community near shops and dining does not come along often in this part of the valley, and the first leasing round is usually when the best selection is available. Signing up for updates from the developer can put you near the front of the line when units hit the market.
