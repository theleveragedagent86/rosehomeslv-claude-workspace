Where Vegas Prices Vary: North Las Vegas Leads Valley Appreciation, Summerlin Tops on Price

Home prices in Las Vegas are not one single number. Redfin data from July 2026 shows North Las Vegas leading the entire valley in how fast home values are climbing, while Summerlin sits at the top of the price ladder at about $697,000. That gap tells you almost everything about how the Las Vegas market really works.

The valley-wide median for a single-family home sits at a record $490,000 right now. But that headline number hides a huge spread. Some neighborhoods sell for well under it, and some sell for far above it. Where you buy changes your price, your monthly payment, and how much your home might be worth in five years.

What the Redfin Data Shows

The latest Redfin neighborhood data paints a clear picture of a split market. Summerlin, the master-planned community on the western edge of the valley, tops the list on raw price at roughly $697,000. That is more than $200,000 above the valley median. Summerlin has long been the premium address in Las Vegas, and the numbers back that up again in 2026.

North Las Vegas tells a different story. It does not top the price charts. Instead, it leads the valley in appreciation, meaning home values there are rising at the fastest pace. Buyers who were priced out of Summerlin, Henderson, and the southwest have been turning to North Las Vegas for years, and that steady demand keeps pushing values up.

This is the classic pattern of a healthy, spread-out market. The most expensive areas are not always the fastest-growing ones. Buyers chase value. When a neighborhood offers newer homes at a lower entry price, demand flows there, and prices catch up over time. North Las Vegas is doing exactly that right now.

It helps to put the two numbers side by side. Summerlin near $697,000 is a price point. North Las Vegas leading appreciation is a growth rate. A buyer with a smaller budget who wants their money to grow may look hard at North Las Vegas communities like Aliante. A buyer who wants a premium, established address and top-tier amenities looks at Summerlin. Both can be smart moves. They are just different goals.

Redfin builds these neighborhood numbers from actual closed sales, not asking prices or guesses. That is why they carry weight. When a data provider that tracks the whole country flags North Las Vegas as a valley appreciation leader, it is picking up on a real pattern of buyers competing for the same homes. And when it puts Summerlin at the top on price, it is confirming what locals already feel every time they drive west toward Red Rock. The data simply gives us the receipts.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

If you own a home in Las Vegas, your neighborhood is your net worth. A homeowner in North Las Vegas is watching their equity grow quickly right now, and that matters if they plan to move up, tap a home equity line, or sell in the next few years. Fast appreciation can turn a starter home into a real down payment on a bigger one.

For buyers, the split market is a road map. If you are shopping with a budget near or below the $490,000 valley median, Summerlin is likely out of reach for a standard single-family home. But that same budget stretches much further in North Las Vegas, parts of the east valley, and some southwest pockets. You get more square footage and, in North Las Vegas, strong upside.

Renters feel this too. When home prices in a neighborhood rise fast, rents usually follow. Families renting in North Las Vegas may see their housing costs climb as the area heats up. That pressure is one reason many renters try to buy sooner rather than later, so they can lock in a payment before prices move again.

It also shapes where families put down roots. School zones, commute times, and amenities all tie back to neighborhood price. A young family may accept a longer drive from Aliante or Skye Canyon to get a newer home and more yard. An empty-nester may pay the Summerlin premium to be near Downtown Summerlin, trails, and medical care. The price map is really a lifestyle map.

There is a timing lesson here too. A neighborhood that leads appreciation today is often one that was affordable yesterday. Buyers who moved into North Las Vegas a few years ago are the ones sitting on the biggest gains right now. That is how it usually works in this valley. The value pockets of one cycle become the strong-equity stories of the next. Paying attention to which areas are heating up can help you decide where to buy before the crowd catches on and prices jump again.

Background and History

Las Vegas has always been a market of master-planned communities, and that history explains the price spread. Summerlin, developed by the Howard Hughes Corporation, has spent decades building a premium brand with parks, trails, top-rated schools, and Downtown Summerlin as its retail and dining core. That reputation is baked into its prices.

North Las Vegas took a different road. For years it was seen as the more affordable corner of the valley, and it was hit hard during the last housing crash. But the northern valley has transformed. Communities like Aliante brought golf, parks, and newer homes. Skye Canyon and Providence, technically in the northwest, added master-planned amenities at prices below Summerlin. That value story is what drives today's appreciation.

The valley as a whole has been on a long run. The median single-family price recently hit a record $490,000, and sales surged about 18% in June. Strong out-of-state demand, especially from Southern California, keeps pressure on inventory. When buyers from higher-cost markets arrive, even Summerlin's near-$697,000 median looks like a relative bargain to them, which supports prices across the board.

At the same time, the attached market has cooled. Condo and townhome prices slipped to about $292,000 in June, down 4.3% year over year. That divergence matters for the neighborhood story. Some of the most affordable ways into the valley are attached homes, and their softness gives first-time buyers a different lane than the single-family race in North Las Vegas.

How the Neighborhoods Stack Up

Beyond the two headliners, the valley has clear tiers. On the higher end, Summerlin leads, and Henderson communities like Green Valley, Inspirada, and Cadence carry strong prices thanks to schools, parks, and newer construction. The southwest valley, including Mountains Edge and Southern Highlands, also sits above the valley median for many single-family homes.

In the middle and value tiers, North Las Vegas, Aliante, and parts of the central and east valley offer lower entry prices. Spring Valley and the central corridor give buyers a shorter commute to the Strip and airport, often at prices below the far-flung master plans. Centennial Hills and Skye Canyon in the northwest blend newer homes with prices that still trail Summerlin.

Location relative to jobs and the Strip also drives the tiers. Central and east valley homes near Sunrise Manor and the airport corridor often sell below the master plans, in part because they are older and closer to busy roads. But that same central spot means a shorter commute for service and hospitality workers, which keeps steady demand under those homes. Value in Las Vegas is never just about the house. It is about the drive, the schools, and what sits at the end of the street.

The takeaway is not that one area is better than another. It is that Las Vegas rewards buyers who match their goals to the right neighborhood. If growth is the goal, the fastest-appreciating areas like North Las Vegas deserve a hard look. If long-term prestige and amenities matter most, Summerlin and Henderson keep their crown. Knowing the map is how you shop smart. The worst outcome is stretching for the priciest zip code you can barely afford when a neighborhood one tier down would have grown your money faster and left room in your budget to breathe.

What Happens Next

Expect the price spread to hold, and possibly widen, through the rest of 2026. As long as out-of-state demand stays strong and inventory stays tight, Summerlin should keep its premium spot. Buyers priced out of the west will keep pushing into North Las Vegas and the northwest, which should keep appreciation there running ahead of the valley.

Mortgage rates are the wild card. Rates recently ticked up to about 6.49%. If they climb further, more buyers get pushed toward affordable neighborhoods, which could speed up appreciation in North Las Vegas even more. If rates fall, some of that pressure eases, and the higher-priced areas may see renewed demand from buyers who suddenly qualify for more.

New construction is another factor to watch. Builders are still active in North Las Vegas, the northwest, and the far southwest, and new-home incentives can pull buyers away from resale listings. When builders drop rates or throw in upgrades, it changes what a nearby used home can command. Keep an eye on which parts of the valley are still adding rooftops, because heavy building can cool appreciation in the short run even as it grows the community long term.

Watch the monthly Redfin and GLVAR neighborhood reports for the trend. If North Las Vegas keeps leading appreciation for several more months, the value gap with Summerlin will slowly close. That is worth tracking whether you plan to buy, sell, or simply want to know what your home is doing while you sleep. These reports are free, and reading them a few minutes each month makes you a sharper buyer or seller than most of the people you will be negotiating against.

Ryan's Take

Here is what I tell clients every week. Do not shop the valley median. Shop the neighborhood. A $490,000 average means very little when Summerlin is near $697,000 and North Las Vegas homes come in well below that. The number that matters is the one on the street you actually want to live on.

If you have a modest budget and you care about growth, North Las Vegas and Aliante are hard to ignore right now. You are buying into the fastest-appreciating part of the valley, which is exactly where I want a first-time buyer building equity. If you want the established, amenity-rich lifestyle and you can afford it, Summerlin has earned its price and holds value well. Both paths work. The mistake is guessing instead of pulling real neighborhood data before you write an offer.

I also want to gently push back on the fear I hear a lot. People see the $490,000 valley median and assume Las Vegas is out of reach. It is not, at least not everywhere. There are still single-family homes selling below that median in solid, growing neighborhoods with good schools and newer builds. You just have to know where to look and be willing to consider an area that is not the flashiest name on the map. Some of the best buys I help clients make are in the exact neighborhoods leading appreciation, because they get the growth and a payment they can actually live with.

What You Can Do

Start by getting a real read on your target neighborhoods, not the valley average. Pull recent sold prices for the specific area you want, whether that is Summerlin, Aliante, Skye Canyon, Green Valley, or Mountains Edge. Sold prices from the last 60 to 90 days tell you far more than any headline number.

If you already own, ask for a current value estimate on your home and compare it to where you bought. Homeowners in fast-appreciating areas like North Las Vegas may have far more equity than they realize, and that equity can fund a move up, a remodel, or an investment property. Do not leave money sitting unused because you never checked.

Buyers should get pre-approved before they fall in love with a neighborhood. Knowing your true budget tells you which tier of the valley is realistic, and it lets you move fast when the right home hits the market in a competitive area. In a valley where prices vary this much block to block, good local guidance is the difference between overpaying and buying smart.

Finally, spend a weekend actually driving the neighborhoods on your short list. Numbers on a screen cannot tell you how a street feels, how close the schools are, or how the commute lands during rush hour. Visit Summerlin, then visit Aliante or Skye Canyon, and pay attention to what you get for the price in each. That side-by-side reality check often surprises people, and it usually points them toward a smarter buy than the one they walked in expecting.
