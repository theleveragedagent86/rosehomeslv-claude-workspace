Las Vegas Ranks as the 5th Most-Searched Housing Market in the Country

Las Vegas is now the fifth most-searched housing market in the country. A new Redfin report tracked where house hunters are looking online in the first three months of 2026, and the valley landed in the national top five, behind only four Florida metro areas. That means more people are typing "Las Vegas homes" into a search bar than almost anywhere else in America.

This is a big deal for anyone who owns a home here or is thinking about buying one. Strong search interest is one of the earliest signs of housing demand, and it shows the valley is still pulling attention from buyers across the country. It also helps explain why local prices have held near record highs even as the rest of the market cools.

What Happened

The ranking comes from Redfin, the national real estate website. Redfin used what it calls its relocation methodology, which studies where people search for homes and where they are searching from. The company released the numbers on July 7, 2026, and they cover the first quarter of the year. The Las Vegas Review-Journal reported the findings the same day.

Orlando topped the entire list. It was followed by North Port, Florida, then Miami, then Cape Coral, Florida. Las Vegas came in fifth. So four of the five most-searched markets in the country are in Florida, and Las Vegas is the only metro outside that state to crack the top five. For a valley of about 2.3 million people, sitting shoulder to shoulder with the biggest Sun Belt destinations is a strong showing.

Redfin also tracked where the searches were coming from. Los Angeles was the top search origin for Las Vegas homes. In other words, more people looking at valley listings started their search in the LA area than anywhere else. That matches a separate Realtor.com report from the same week, which found the Los Angeles metro alone drives about a quarter of all out-of-market demand for Las Vegas homes, with California overall driving close to a third.

The report also mapped the bigger national picture. The cities people most want to move away from were New York, Seattle, Los Angeles, and San Jose. Florida was the top state house hunters wanted to move to, followed by Arizona, South Carolina, and Tennessee. The states people most wanted to leave were California, New York, Illinois, and Washington. Put simply, buyers are leaving expensive coastal metros and looking hard at more affordable Sun Belt markets. Las Vegas sits right in the middle of that migration path.

It is worth pausing on what "most-searched" actually measures. Redfin does not count how many homes sold. It counts how many people are actively looking at listings and mapping out a possible move. That makes the ranking a demand signal, a kind of early warning system for where buyers are headed next. A market can be busy with local buyers and still not rank high nationally. Las Vegas ranks high because people from other states keep searching here, which is a different and stronger kind of interest. It tells you the valley is on the shopping list for buyers who have options all over the country.

The timing matters too. This data covers the first quarter of 2026, the early part of the year when many buyers start planning summer moves. Families often begin searching in winter and spring so they can move before a new school year. Seeing Las Vegas rank fifth during that planning window suggests the valley will keep drawing serious buyers through the busy summer and fall selling seasons. That is the season when a lot of the actual sales get done.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

If you own a home in the valley, this ranking is good news for your equity. When demand stays high, prices tend to hold or grow, even when interest rates are stubborn. The Las Vegas median single-family home price held at a record $490,000 in June, and sustained search interest like this is a big part of why. Buyers are still lined up, and that supports the value of your home in Summerlin, Henderson, Spring Valley, or wherever you live.

If you are trying to buy, the picture is more complicated. Being one of the most-searched markets means you are competing with people from all over the country, not just your neighbors. A family in Los Angeles that sold a modest home for $900,000 can come to the valley with a large down payment and move fast. That kind of buyer can outbid a first-time local buyer who is stretching to afford a starter home. More eyes on the market means more competition for the homes that hit the listings.

Renters feel this too. When outside demand is strong, investors and out-of-state buyers scoop up homes that might otherwise go to local families. That keeps for-sale inventory tight and can push more people into the rental market, which puts pressure on rents. The ripple effect touches almost everyone in Clark County, whether you are buying, selling, or renting.

There is also a bigger economic angle. High housing demand tends to follow job growth, tourism strength, and population growth. Las Vegas keeps adding people and jobs, and this ranking is a reflection of that. A valley that people want to move to is a valley with a healthy long-term outlook, which matters for schools, roads, local businesses, and the overall economy that supports your household.

It also shapes how you should think about timing. If you have been sitting on the fence about selling, knowing that the valley is a national top-five search market gives you real leverage. You are not hoping a single local buyer walks through the door. You are marketing to a pipeline of out-of-state shoppers who are already looking at Las Vegas listings on their phones. That is a very different position than a slow, forgotten market where sellers have to cut prices just to get noticed.

For buyers, the flip side is a reminder to be ready. In a high-interest market, the good homes at fair prices tend to move quickest. If you are pre-approved and clear on your must-haves, you can act while a hesitant out-of-state buyer is still booking a flight to come see it in person. Local knowledge and speed are your two biggest advantages, and both are things you control.

Background and History

Las Vegas has been a magnet for out-of-state buyers for decades, but the pattern shifted after the pandemic. Remote work freed a lot of people from expensive coastal cities, and Nevada's lack of a state income tax, its lower home prices, and its sunny weather made it an easy sell. Southern California has long been the single biggest source of that demand, and it still is today.

Redfin's report explained why the migration engine keeps running. The company pointed to affordability as the main driver. Housing costs are near record highs because mortgage rates and sale prices remain high, and inflation has pushed up the cost of everyday life. That is pushing people to move from expensive areas to cheaper ones. A California buyer can sell high and buy in Las Vegas for far less per square foot, which is exactly why LA keeps topping the list of search origins for the valley.

The report also named a second trend it calls boomerang migration. Some Americans who moved during the pandemic are now moving again, sometimes back toward where they came from or on to a new spot that fits better. That churn keeps search activity high. Redfin found that about one in five house hunters, or 19.1 percent, looked to move from one part of the country to another in the first quarter of 2026. That was up slightly from 18.9 percent a year earlier, and it was the highest share in Redfin's records going back to 2021.

It helps to remember that Las Vegas has climbed these kinds of lists before. The valley regularly shows up in national reports on buyer interest, buying power, and relocation demand. This latest ranking is not a one-time blip. It is part of a long pattern of the country looking west to the desert when it wants more house for the money.

The pandemic years are still shaping the numbers, too. Redfin's records only go back to 2021, right in the middle of the remote-work boom, when relocation searches exploded. What is striking is that the share of house hunters looking to relocate is now even higher than it was then, at 19.1 percent. The moving wave did not end when offices reopened. It simply changed shape. Instead of tech workers chasing space, it is now families and retirees chasing affordability. Las Vegas fits that new profile well, with prices below the California coast and a lower overall cost of living.

What Happens Next

The one thing to watch is whether all this searching turns into actual buying. Redfin was careful to point this out. Home searches do not always add up to home purchases and real moves. The company noted that overall homebuying activity is sluggish right now, so even though a record share of house hunters want to relocate, the total number of people actually moving is likely lower than it was in 2021 or 2022. A smaller share of a bigger pool of buyers was relocating back then.

So the smart read is that interest is high but the trigger-pullers are being careful. Mortgage rates are still parked in the mid-six-percent range, and that keeps some buyers on the sidelines even when they love a market. If rates ease later in 2026, expect some of that pent-up search interest to convert into real offers, and expect competition in the valley to heat up fast.

For now, keep an eye on the local numbers that tell the real story. Watch the monthly median price from Las Vegas Realtors, the months of supply, and the share of cash buyers. The valley recently showed about 3.5 months of supply and cash buyers near 23 percent, both signs of a market that is busy but not overheated. If search interest stays this strong and supply stays tight, prices will likely keep their footing through the rest of the year.

Well-priced homes still sit, and there are real opportunities for buyers who move with a plan and a strong pre-approval. What You Can Do

If you are thinking about buying, the first step is to get a clear read on your budget in today's rate environment. Get pre-approved with a local lender so you know your real monthly payment at the current mid-six-percent rates. That single step puts you ahead of the out-of-state buyers who show up without financing lined up. It also helps you move fast when the right home appears.

If you are a homeowner curious about your value, now is a smart time to check where you stand. With demand this strong and the median holding at $490,000, your equity may be higher than you think. A quick, no-pressure home value review can show you what buyers would likely pay for your specific home in your specific neighborhood, whether that is Mountains Edge, Green Valley, Centennial Hills, or Aliante.

And if you are just trying to understand the market before you make any move, keep reading local data instead of national headlines. National reports are useful for the big picture, but your decision comes down to your street, your price range, and your timeline. That is where a local expert who watches the valley every day can save you money and stress.

It also pays to think about which part of the valley fits your goals. Search interest is spread across the whole metro, but the story is different in each community. Summerlin tends to draw move-up buyers and out-of-state families who want amenities and top schools. North Las Vegas has been leading the valley in appreciation, which can matter for buyers focused on future value. Areas like Spring Valley, Henderson, and the southwest each carry their own price points and pace. Knowing where the demand is landing helps you shop smarter, whether you want the safest value or the strongest upside.

Finally, do not let fear of missing out drive a rushed decision. A hot search ranking is a reason to be prepared, not a reason to overpay. The best move is to line up your financing, learn your target neighborhoods, and set a clear budget you are comfortable with. When you do that, national demand becomes something you can use to your advantage instead of something that pushes you into a purchase you regret.
