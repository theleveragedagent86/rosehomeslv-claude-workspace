Mortgage Rates Just Hit an 11-Month High. Las Vegas Prices Did Not Blink

The average 30 year fixed mortgage rate climbed to 6.55 percent on July 16, 2026, the highest level since August 2025. Las Vegas home prices did not react at all. The median price of an existing single family home in Southern Nevada held at a record $490,000 for the second straight month.

Those two numbers are supposed to pull against each other. When borrowing costs go up, the usual theory says buyers can afford less, demand cools, and sellers have to cut. That is not what happened in Clark County this summer.

Freddie Mac reported the 6.55 percent average in its weekly Primary Mortgage Market Survey, up from 6.49 percent the week before. The local number came from Las Vegas Realtors, which tracks sales through the local MLS. Their June report showed the median single family price unchanged from the all time high set in May, and up 1.0 percent from June 2025.

If you have been waiting on the sidelines for higher rates to crack local prices, this is your answer. It has not happened. The gap between the national headline and the Las Vegas reality is the entire story here, and it matters for anyone trying to time a purchase in the valley right now.

What Happened

Freddie Mac publishes its Primary Mortgage Market Survey every Thursday. It is the most widely quoted mortgage rate benchmark in the country. On July 16, 2026, the survey put the average 30 year fixed rate at 6.55 percent. That was up from 6.49 percent the prior week, and it was the highest reading in almost a year. You have to go back to August 2025 to find a higher weekly average.

Here is the part most headlines skipped. A year earlier, in July 2025, the same survey averaged 6.75 percent. So even at an 11 month high, today's rate is still lower than it was twelve months ago. The "high" in that headline is a high for the past eleven months, not a high for the past several years.

Now look at the local side. Las Vegas Realtors released its June 2026 report on July 7. The median price of an existing single family home sold through the Southern Nevada MLS was $490,000. That matched the record set in May, and it was 1.0 percent above June 2025. Total transaction value for single family homes topped $1.4 billion for the month.

Sales activity went up, not down. Las Vegas Realtors logged 2,823 closings across existing single family homes, condos and townhomes in June. Single family sales alone rose 18.3 percent compared to June 2025. That is a big jump in a month when the national picture was cooling off.

Supply stayed thin. At the end of June there were 7,147 single family homes listed without an offer, up 2.2 percent year over year. That works out to roughly three and a half months of supply. Most housing economists treat six months as a balanced market. Clark County is nowhere near that.

Not every part of the market moved the same way. The median price for an existing condo or townhome fell to $292,000 in June, down 4.3 percent from a year earlier. Attached home inventory grew faster than the single family side, with 2,690 units listed without an offer, up 4.9 percent year over year. So the softness that buyers keep hoping for does exist locally. It just shows up in condos and townhomes, not in houses.

Las Vegas Realtors president George Kypreos summed it up plainly in the June report, saying local home prices do not always follow national trends, but they have been lately. He credited tight supply and steady demand for holding the median at the record level.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Let us turn these percentages into a real monthly payment, because that is what actually hits your bank account. Take a $490,000 home with 20 percent down. That is $98,000 down and a $392,000 loan. At 6.55 percent on a 30 year fixed, principal and interest run about $2,491 a month. At last week's 6.49 percent, the same loan ran about $2,475. The jump that made national news costs you roughly $16 a month.

Now run the year over year comparison, and the picture flips. In June 2025 the Southern Nevada median was about $485,000, which is a $388,000 loan at 20 percent down. At the 6.75 percent average rate from July 2025, principal and interest came to roughly $2,517 a month. Today, at a record price and an 11 month high rate, that same buyer is looking at about $2,491. The payment is actually a little lower than it was a year ago.

Read that again, because it is the practical takeaway. A Clark County buyer who waited twelve months for prices to fall did not save money. Prices went up, rates went down slightly, and the monthly payment landed in almost the same place. The waiting bought nothing except twelve more months of rent payments. These figures cover principal and interest only, so property taxes, insurance and any HOA dues sit on top.

For sellers in Summerlin, Henderson, Centennial Hills and the southwest valley, this report is confirmation that pricing power has not slipped. With three and a half months of supply, a well priced and well presented house still draws real interest. That does not mean you can overprice it. Buyers at this payment level are careful, and they walk away from homes that need work or sit above the comps.

For renters thinking about buying, the math deserves a fresh look. Average apartment rent in Las Vegas sits around $1,464 and has drifted slightly lower over the past year, so renting is genuinely cheaper month to month than owning at the median price. The tradeoff is that a renter builds nothing while the median owner watched their home value tick up another 1.0 percent.

First time buyers should know the payment math above assumes 20 percent down, and most first time buyers are not putting down $98,000. At 10 percent down on a $490,000 home, the loan grows to $441,000 and principal and interest run about $2,802 a month at 6.55 percent. At 5 percent down, the loan is $465,500 and the payment climbs to roughly $2,957. Smaller down payments usually add mortgage insurance on top of that. This is why plenty of Clark County buyers end up shopping below the median rather than at it, and why the condo and townhome side keeps getting a second look.

For current homeowners, the story is simply stability. If you bought in 2021 or 2022 and worried about being underwater, the record median says otherwise. Clark County equity has held, and the luxury end has done better than that, with Las Vegas luxury prices up 13.7 percent year over year and ranking third fastest in the nation.

Background and History

To understand why rates and prices split apart here, you have to look at what happened to supply. Between 2020 and 2022, mortgage rates sat near historic lows and a wave of buyers locked in loans in the 2 and 3 percent range. Those owners have almost no reason to sell today. Moving means trading a 3 percent loan for a 6.55 percent loan on a more expensive house.

Economists call that the lock in effect, and Southern Nevada feels it hard. It is the main reason Clark County has been stuck around three to four months of supply for years now. Fewer listings hit the market each month than the valley's population growth would normally produce.

At the same time, demand kept arriving. Las Vegas has drawn steady in migration from California and other high cost states, plus job growth tied to tourism, health care, logistics and the sports economy. Even in a slower year, buyers keep showing up. When you combine steady demand with a supply pipeline that stays tight, higher rates do not automatically translate into lower prices. They mostly translate into fewer transactions.

The national numbers back this up rather than contradict it. The National Association of Realtors reported that U.S. existing home sales fell 2.4 percent month over month in June 2026 to a 4.09 million annual pace. Yet the national median sales price still hit a record $440,600, up 1.8 percent from a year earlier. That was the 36th consecutive month of annual price gains nationwide. So even on the national level, higher rates slowed the volume of sales without pushing prices down.

It is also worth knowing where Las Vegas now sits relative to the country. At $490,000, the Southern Nevada median for an existing single family home runs about $49,400 above the national median. Clark County is no longer the cheap alternative it was fifteen years ago. National supply sat at 4.6 months in June while Las Vegas sat near three and a half, which is exactly why local prices have been firmer than the national trend.

What Happens Next

Watch two calendars. Freddie Mac updates the Primary Mortgage Market Survey every Thursday morning, so the national rate number refreshes weekly. Las Vegas Realtors releases its monthly local report in the first week or so of each month, which means the July data for Southern Nevada should land in early August 2026.

The key question in that next local report is whether the median finally moves off $490,000. Two straight months at the same record number is unusual. It could break either way. Inventory has been creeping up, with single family listings without offers rising 2.2 percent year over year, and if that build continues through the fall, price growth should flatten. Late summer and fall are also typically slower selling months in the valley once school starts back up.

Keep an eye on the condo and townhome segment as an early signal. Attached homes are the one part of the Clark County market where prices have actually dropped, down 4.3 percent to a $292,000 median. Attached inventory is also building faster. If the single family side is going to soften, that softness will very likely show up in condos first.

Also watch the new construction side, because builders react to rates faster than individual sellers do. Local builders rolled out aggressive summer incentives, including a promotional 3.99 percent fixed conventional rate from one national builder on select homes, savings of up to $85,000 on select quick move in homes from another, and closing cost credits from a third. Those offers usually come with deadlines and require using the builder's affiliated lender. When builder incentives get this generous, it tells you they are working to keep sales moving in a higher rate environment.

One more thing to keep in perspective. A move from 6.49 percent to 6.55 percent is six hundredths of a percentage point. Weekly moves that small are normal noise in the mortgage market, and they get written up as news because the survey publishes every week. What actually matters for a Las Vegas buyer is the trend, not the weekly tick. Every weekly reading between August 2025 and this month came in below 6.55 percent, and the July 2025 average was higher at 6.75 percent. In other words, the market has been grinding sideways in a fairly narrow band for about a year, and a single week of movement is not a signal to change your plans.

Ryan's Take

I have this conversation almost every week. Someone tells me they are waiting for rates to drop, or waiting for prices to fall, and they have been waiting since 2023. This report is the cleanest evidence yet that the waiting strategy is not paying off in Clark County. Rates went to an 11 month high and the median did not move a dollar. Meanwhile the buyer who waited a year ended up with a payment that is basically identical, minus twelve months of equity.

The thing I would push back on is the idea that there is one Las Vegas market. There is not. Single family homes are at a record. Condos and townhomes are down 4.3 percent. Luxury is up 13.7 percent. Rents are slightly lower than a year ago. Those four facts are all true at the same time in the same valley. A national headline about mortgage rates cannot tell you which of those four situations applies to you.

My honest advice is to stop watching the Freddie Mac number and start running your own numbers. Rates are a national average built from thousands of loans nationwide. Your rate depends on your credit, your down payment, your loan type and which lender you use, and a builder buydown can put you hundreds of dollars a month below the headline. On that same $392,000 loan, 3.99 percent instead of 6.55 percent is roughly $620 a month in difference. That is the kind of gap worth chasing.

What You Can Do

Start by getting a real rate quote instead of relying on the weekly national average. Talk to two or three lenders in the same week, since rate sheets move daily and comparing quotes from different weeks is not a fair comparison. Ask each one for a written loan estimate so you can line up the rate, the points, the lender fees and the monthly payment side by side.

Next, price out both sides of the market. Look at existing single family homes near the $490,000 median, then look at attached homes near the $292,000 condo median, and then look at new construction with a builder buydown. Those three paths produce very different monthly payments for the same household budget. Plenty of Clark County buyers assume they are priced out of one and never check the other two.

Finally, verify the numbers yourself. Las Vegas Realtors posts its monthly market reports publicly, and Freddie Mac posts the weekly rate survey at freddiemac.com. Reading the local report next to the national one takes about ten minutes and will tell you more about your actual situation than any headline. If you are renting right now, run your renewal number against a real mortgage estimate before you sign another twelve month lease.

If you are already a homeowner, this is a good month to check your own position. Pull up what similar homes on your street have sold for this year. A median at a record level does not automatically mean your specific house went up, since values move differently by zip code, age and condition. Knowing your real number helps whether you plan to sell, refinance later, or simply stay put.
