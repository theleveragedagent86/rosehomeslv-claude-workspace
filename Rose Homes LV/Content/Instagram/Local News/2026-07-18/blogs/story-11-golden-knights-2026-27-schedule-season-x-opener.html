NHL Releases 2026-27 Schedule: Golden Knights Open "Season X" at Home vs. Chicago

The NHL released its full 2026-27 regular season schedule on July 16, and the Vegas Golden Knights open their 10th season at home against the Chicago Blackhawks on Tuesday, Sept. 29. Puck drops at 7:30 p.m. Pacific time at T-Mobile Arena, and the game airs nationally on ESPN.

There is a second headline buried in that schedule. This is the first 84-game regular season in NHL history. Every team, including Vegas, plays two more games than the league has scheduled for the past three decades. For Las Vegas fans, that means more nights of hockey on the Strip and a longer calendar to plan around.

The season wraps up at home too. Vegas closes the regular season on Saturday, April 10 against the Los Angeles Kings at T-Mobile Arena. Ten seasons after the franchise played its first game, the Golden Knights will open and close the year in front of their own crowd. The team is calling the milestone year "Season X," and that gold X you may have seen on billboards around the valley was the tease for exactly this.

What Happened

The league dropped all 32 team schedules at once on Thursday, July 16, 2026. The Vegas Golden Knights posted their full slate the same day through the team's official channels. Schedule release day has become its own small holiday for hockey fans, and this year the team built a week of suspense around it with mystery gold billboards placed at Circa, Park MGM, Fontainebleau, Paris Las Vegas, and AREA15.

Here are the dates that matter most. The home opener is Tuesday, Sept. 29 against the Chicago Blackhawks, 7:30 p.m. Pacific, at T-Mobile Arena, broadcast on ESPN. A national TV window for a Vegas home opener is not a small thing. It puts the arena, the Strip, and the crowd in front of a country-wide audience on the first real night of the NHL season.

The regular season finale is Saturday, April 10 at home against the Los Angeles Kings. A Saturday night finish against a division rival is about as good as a closing date gets, especially if playoff seeding is still on the line. Vegas fans who have sat through weeknight games in Canada at 4 p.m. local time will appreciate a home game to end things.

The schedule also hands Vegas two five-game homestands at T-Mobile Arena, one in November and one in March. Five straight home games is a real advantage in a league built on travel. No flights, no time zones, no hotel beds. For fans, it means five chances to catch a game inside a two-week window without hunting for tickets across a whole month.

And then there is Monday, Dec. 21. That is when the Carolina Hurricanes come to T-Mobile Arena. Carolina beat Vegas four games to two in the 2026 Stanley Cup Final in June, closing the series out with a 3-0 win in Game 6 on Las Vegas ice. The Hurricanes lifted the Cup in this building. The December date is the first time Vegas gets them back home. Circle it.

The 84-game format is the structural change underneath all of it. The NHL and the players association agreed to expand the regular season from 82 games to 84 games starting with this season, and to trim the preseason schedule in exchange. It is the first time the league has changed its regular season length in a generation.

The math is simple. An 84-game season splits into 42 home games and 42 road games. That is one extra home date and one extra road date for every club. It also changes the standings math a little. There are more points available over the year, which means a slow start is slightly easier to climb out of and a late slide is slightly harder to survive. Two games sounds small in July. It will not feel small in March.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

If you live in Clark County, an NHL schedule release is a traffic document as much as a sports document. Forty-two home games at T-Mobile Arena means 42 nights when Las Vegas Boulevard, Frank Sinatra Drive, and the parking structures around The Park fill up faster than usual. Two extra games this year means two more of those nights.

Locals who commute through the resort corridor already know the drill. A 7:30 p.m. puck drop pulls thousands of cars toward the Strip between 5:30 and 7 p.m. If your drive home from work crosses Tropicana Avenue or Harmon Avenue, the home schedule is worth saving to your phone. The same goes for the two five-game homestands. Five straight nights of arena traffic in November and again in March is a pattern worth planning around, especially with the I-15 and Tropicana work that has reshaped that stretch of road.

It also matters for household budgets. Golden Knights hockey is one of the few big-league experiences in this valley that locals genuinely treat as their own. Season ticket holders in Summerlin, Green Valley, Centennial Hills, and Mountains Edge build their fall and winter around this calendar. Knowing the dates in July gives families time to decide which games to buy, which to split with friends, and which to watch from the couch.

For the roughly one in three Clark County jobs tied to tourism and hospitality, 42 home dates is 42 nights of extra demand. Restaurants at New York-New York, Park MGM, and along The Park see it. So do rideshare drivers, bartenders, and hotel staff. An ESPN home opener on Sept. 29 is a national ad for the destination, and it lands right when the fall convention season is picking up.

Short-term rental owners and anyone renting out a room near the Strip should read the schedule as a demand calendar. Weekend home games and marquee dates pull visiting fans into town, and Chicago, Carolina, and Los Angeles all travel well. Those are the nights when nearby rooms and rentals get booked earliest. The two five-game homestands create the same effect over a longer stretch.

There is a quieter effect too. A pro sports calendar is one of the things that makes a fast-growing metro feel like a real hometown instead of a place people pass through. Ten seasons in, kids in Henderson and North Las Vegas have never known a Las Vegas without an NHL team. That matters more to how people feel about living here than any single game result.

Background and History

The Vegas Golden Knights played their first game in October 2017. Almost nobody expected what came next. That first team reached the Stanley Cup Final as an expansion club, and the run turned a brand new franchise into a civic institution in about eight months. The team won the Stanley Cup in 2023, which cemented the whole thing.

The 2026 season ended differently. Vegas got back to the Stanley Cup Final and lost to the Carolina Hurricanes four games to two. Game 6 was in Las Vegas, and the Hurricanes shut Vegas out 3-0 to take the Cup on T-Mobile Arena ice. Watching another team celebrate on your home rink is the kind of ending fans carry into the next season. That is a big part of why the Dec. 21 rematch has already become the most talked-about date on the calendar.

The "Season X" branding is the team's way of framing what comes next. Before the schedule dropped, gold billboards with a single X appeared around the valley at Circa, Park MGM, Fontainebleau, Paris Las Vegas, and AREA15. For about a week, people argued about what it meant. Some guessed a new casino. Some guessed a concert residency. Plenty of locals guessed correctly that it was the Golden Knights marking their 10th season, and the reveal came alongside the schedule announcement.

The arena itself is part of the story. T-Mobile Arena opened in 2016 next to The Park, one year before the Golden Knights played a game, and the district around it changed the south end of the Strip. Las Vegas has added the Raiders at Allegiant Stadium and the Aces at Michelob Ultra Arena since then, and a PWHL team is coming to T-Mobile Arena this fall. Hockey was first, and it proved the market.

The 84-game change has a longer backstory. The NHL has played an 82-game regular season since the mid-1990s. The move to 84 came out of the labor agreement between the league and the players, which also shortened the preseason. The trade is simple. Fewer exhibition games nobody buys tickets for, and two more real games that count in the standings. For a team like Vegas that fills its building, two more home dates is real revenue and two more nights for fans.

What Happens Next

Between now and Sept. 29, the calendar fills in fast. Training camp and preseason come first, though the preseason is shorter this year under the new agreement. Rosters get set, and the team holds its usual fan events at City National Arena in Downtown Summerlin, which is where the Golden Knights practice and where a lot of locals get their closest look at the players.

Ticket sales are the next thing to watch. Schedule release day is when season ticket holders lock in their seats and when single-game inventory starts moving. If you want opening night against Chicago, or the Dec. 21 Carolina game, or the April 10 finale against Los Angeles, those three dates will be the hardest to get all year. The Carolina game is a Monday night in December, which historically is a softer date, but this one will not be.

Watch the two five-game homestands as well. November and March both give Vegas an extended stretch at T-Mobile Arena. Teams that bank points during long homestands tend to control their own playoff position in April. If Vegas is going to make a run in its 10th season, those two windows are where it gets built.

T-Mobile Arena will be busier than usual this year for another reason. PWHL Las Vegas, the new pro women's hockey team, plays at the same building this fall and just signed No. 3 overall pick Tessa Janecke to a two-year deal. Two pro hockey teams sharing one arena on the Strip means more event nights, more scheduling puzzles for the building, and more hockey for local families to choose from.

One more thing to keep an eye on. The 84-game season is new for everyone, including the players. Two extra games across the same rough calendar window means slightly more compression and slightly less rest. How teams handle that is going to be a real story by February, and Vegas travels more than most clubs because of where the city sits on the map.

Ryan's Take

I have watched what a pro sports calendar does to a city, and it is bigger than people give it credit for. When I sit down with buyers moving here from California or the Midwest, the Golden Knights come up constantly. Not as a reason to buy a house, but as proof that Las Vegas is a real place to live and not just a place to visit. That perception moves people, and people moving is what moves this market.

On the practical side, I tell clients looking near the resort corridor to pull up the home schedule before they decide. If you are buying a condo near the Strip, 42 hockey nights plus concerts, conventions, and Raiders and Aces games is your reality. Some buyers love that energy and want to walk to games. Others realize they want to be in Summerlin, Henderson, or Southern Highlands with an easy drive in and a quiet street at home. Neither answer is wrong. It is just better to know before you sign.

Ten seasons in, this team has become one of the reasons people stay in Clark County instead of leaving after a few years. That is worth something to every homeowner here, whether you follow hockey or not.

What You Can Do

Start by pulling the full schedule from the team's official site and adding it to your phone calendar. The team publishes a downloadable version every year, and it takes about two minutes. If you drive through the resort corridor for work, that one step will save you real time between October and April.

If you want tickets, decide now which games matter to you. Opening night against Chicago on Sept. 29, the Carolina rematch on Dec. 21, and the April 10 finale against Los Angeles are the three premium dates. Weeknight games in January and February against non-rival teams are usually the best value, and that is where families can find affordable seats. Season ticket and partial plan information runs through the team directly.

If your kids are into hockey, the fall is when local youth programs fill up. City National Arena in Downtown Summerlin and Lifeguard Arena in downtown Henderson both run learn-to-skate and youth programs, and registration tends to open around the start of the NHL season. Ten years in, Southern Nevada now has a real pipeline of kids who grew up on the ice here, and the Henderson Silver Knights give families a second team to follow closer to home.

You do not need a ticket to be part of it. The Sept. 29 opener is on ESPN, so anyone with basic cable or a streaming package can watch. Neighborhood bars and restaurants across Summerlin, Green Valley, Centennial Hills, and the southwest valley run Golden Knights nights all season, and those rooms get loud. If you are new to Las Vegas and trying to meet neighbors, a home game on a Tuesday night at a local spot is an easier introduction than most.

Finally, if you are thinking about buying near the Strip, go stand outside T-Mobile Arena on a game night before you commit. Feel the traffic and the noise. It is the single best way to know whether that location fits your life.
