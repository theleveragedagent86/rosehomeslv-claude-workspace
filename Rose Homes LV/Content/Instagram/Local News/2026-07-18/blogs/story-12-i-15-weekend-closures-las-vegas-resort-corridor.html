Two Full Weekend Closures of I-15 Through the Las Vegas Resort Corridor

Southbound Interstate 15 through the Las Vegas resort corridor is closed for the entire weekend, from Friday, July 18 at 9 p.m. until Monday, July 21 at 5 a.m. The shutdown covers the stretch between Spring Mountain Road and Tropicana Avenue, which is one of the busiest pieces of freeway in Nevada.

This is the second of two full weekend closures the Nevada Department of Transportation scheduled for July. The first one already came and went. It closed northbound I-15 between Russell Road and Flamingo Road from Friday, July 11 at 9 p.m. through Monday, July 14 at 5 a.m.

Crews are paving and striping the new lanes. NDOT says this is the final major closure tied to the I-15 and Tropicana Project. That means the end is finally in sight for a construction zone that has slowed Strip traffic for years.

If you are driving anywhere near the Strip before Monday morning, you need a plan. Decatur Boulevard is the main detour NDOT is pointing drivers toward.

What NDOT Closed and When

NDOT scheduled two full weekend closures of I-15 in July 2026, back to back. Both shut down every lane in one direction through part of the resort corridor. This was not a lane reduction or a shoulder squeeze. It was a full stop.

The first closure ran on the northbound side. It started Friday, July 11 at 9 p.m. and reopened Monday, July 14 at 5 a.m. Crews closed northbound I-15 between Russell Road and Flamingo Road. That covers the stretch running past Mandalay Bay, Allegiant Stadium, and the Tropicana Avenue interchange.

The second closure is the one happening right now. Southbound I-15 is closed between Spring Mountain Road and Tropicana Avenue. It started Friday, July 18 at 9 p.m. and it does not reopen until Monday, July 21 at 5 a.m. That is roughly 56 straight hours, and it covers the busiest weekend hours in the city.

Both closures cover the same general area from opposite directions. The work is paving and striping. Crews lay down the final driving surface, then paint the lane lines on top of it. Paving needs a dry, uninterrupted work area. Fresh striping needs time to set. You cannot do either one safely with cars moving past at 65 miles an hour.

NDOT picked the 9 p.m. Friday start time on purpose. Closing that late lets the Friday evening rush clear out first. Reopening at 5 a.m. Monday puts the freeway back in service before the Monday morning commute builds.

A full closure means more than the mainline lanes. On ramps feeding into the closed direction shut down too, and some off ramps get blocked so drivers do not wander into the work zone. Message boards go up days in advance along I-15 and the surface streets, and crews stage barricades before the first cone goes out.

The first weekend gave everyone a preview of how this goes. Traffic backed up onto surface streets, the detour route ran slow at peak hours, and the freeway reopened on schedule Monday morning. Drivers who planned ahead barely noticed. Drivers who did not sat in it.

The main detour is Decatur Boulevard. Drivers heading south can exit ahead of the closure, run down Decatur, and rejoin I-15 south of the work zone. Valley View Boulevard, Industrial Road, and Las Vegas Boulevard also carry traffic through the corridor, but all three fill up fast on a weekend night.

Drivers making a longer trip have another option. The 215 Beltway can route you around the resort corridor entirely, and US 95 works for some north and south trips depending on where you start and where you end. Going around is often faster than going through, even when the map says you are driving more miles.

NDOT also confirmed this is the final major closure for the I-15 and Tropicana Project. For anyone who has been living with the orange cones, that is the real headline.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

I-15 through the resort corridor is not just a tourist road. It is the spine of the valley. People in Henderson, Green Valley, Anthem, and Southern Highlands use it to head north. People in Centennial Hills, Skye Canyon, and North Las Vegas use it to head south. Cut it in half and the whole valley feels it.

Weekend closures here are different from weekend closures in most cities. Saturday night in Las Vegas is peak traffic, not off peak. Hotel check ins, casino shift changes, concerts, sporting events, and airport runs all stack up at the exact hours the freeway is down.

Hospitality workers feel it first. Thousands of Clark County residents work swing and graveyard shifts on the Strip. A closure that starts at 9 p.m. Friday lands right on top of a shift change. If your normal drive is 25 minutes, give yourself 45 to 60 this weekend.

Rideshare and delivery drivers take a direct hit too. Those jobs pay by the trip, so a detour that adds 15 minutes to every run cuts income for the entire weekend. The same goes for anyone driving a service van or a box truck through the corridor on a Saturday afternoon.

Airport traffic is the other pressure point. Harry Reid International sits just east of the closure zone. Anyone driving from the north or west side of the valley to catch a flight should leave much earlier than usual, especially Saturday.

Then there is the surface street spillover. When a freeway closes, that traffic does not vanish. It moves onto Decatur, Valley View, Industrial, and Las Vegas Boulevard. Neighborhoods near those corridors get more cars, more noise, and slower left turns for the whole weekend.

Renters near the corridor deal with the noise. Paving is loud, and it runs around the clock during a closure like this one. If you live in an apartment near Spring Mountain, Twain, Flamingo, or Tropicana, expect equipment noise late into the night through Sunday.

Small businesses along the detour route see the other side of it. Some pick up extra walk in traffic from drivers stuck on Decatur. Others lose regulars who decide the trip is not worth the hassle this weekend. Either way, the numbers look different than a normal Saturday.

Visitors feel it too, and that matters to local paychecks. Tourism spending drives a huge share of the Clark County economy. One rough travel weekend does not sink those numbers, but it is a good reason NDOT works hard to schedule closures around major events and holidays.

For homeowners, this is a short term headache with a long term payoff. Freeway access is one of the first things buyers ask about in Southern Nevada. A finished interchange is a selling point. A permanent construction zone is not.

Background and History of the I-15 and Tropicana Project

The I-15 and Tropicana interchange has been a construction site for years. It sits at the doorstep of Allegiant Stadium, Mandalay Bay, Luxor, and Excalibur, and it carries an enormous share of the traffic moving in and out of the south Strip.

Traffic in that spot changed fast. Allegiant Stadium opened in 2020 and brought NFL crowds to the corridor. Formula 1 racing arrived on the Strip in 2023. The Super Bowl came to Allegiant Stadium in February 2024. Each of those pushed tens of thousands of extra cars through an interchange designed for a smaller Las Vegas.

NDOT's answer was a rebuild of the interchange along with widening and repaving work on the mainline freeway. That kind of project cannot happen all at once. It gets broken into stages across multiple years, with nightly lane closures, ramp closures, shifted traffic patterns, and a handful of full weekend shutdowns.

Drivers have been dealing with those stages the entire time. Narrow shoulders, reduced speed limits, closed on ramps, and confusing detours became a normal part of the drive. The two July weekend closures represent the last big push.

Paving in Las Vegas has its own rules. Summer surface temperatures make daytime work brutal on crews and can change how the asphalt behaves as it sets. That is one reason so much heavy work happens overnight, and part of why these closures start at 9 p.m. and push through the dark hours.

There is a strategy behind stacking two closures back to back, too. NDOT could have spread this same work across six or eight weekends of partial closures. Instead it took the whole freeway twice and got it finished. Concentrating the disruption is usually cheaper, safer for the crews, and shorter overall.

This is also familiar territory for NDOT. Project Neon, the massive rebuild of the I-15 and US 95 area near downtown, used the same playbook. Full weekend closures, heavy advance signage, and a hard reopening deadline. Locals grumbled for a couple of years, then the road opened and the drive got noticeably better.

What Happens Next

Southbound I-15 is scheduled to reopen Monday, July 21 at 5 a.m. NDOT builds buffer time into these closures, but weather and equipment problems can still push a reopening later. Check conditions before you drive Monday morning rather than assuming the lanes are back.

After this weekend, expect the work to shrink. Finishing a project this size usually means overnight lane closures, ramp work, permanent signage, landscaping, and a punch list of small items. Those are annoying, but they do not take the whole freeway away from you.

Pay attention to the word NDOT used. This is the final major closure. Major does not mean every cone disappears Monday morning. It means the era of losing an entire direction of I-15 for a full weekend should be behind us.

Watch for an official completion announcement. NDOT publishes project milestones on its website and shares them through local news outlets. If you commute through the corridor, that is the notice actually worth waiting for.

There is a bigger picture here as well. The resort corridor keeps adding demand. New resort projects, more convention traffic, and steady population growth in the south and southwest valley all push more cars onto the same freeway. A rebuilt interchange buys real capacity, but it does not freeze traffic in place forever.

One more thing to watch. Football season starts in September. Once the Raiders are back at Allegiant Stadium and 65,000 people are trying to leave at the same time, the rebuilt interchange gets its real test. That first sold out Sunday will tell everyone whether the years of orange cones bought what they were supposed to buy.

Ryan's Take

I have watched buyers rule out entire zip codes over a commute. It happens more than people think. Someone will fall in love with a house in the southwest valley, then drive the route at 5 p.m. on a Tuesday and quietly cross it off the list. Freeway access is not a small detail in this valley. It is a real part of what a home is worth.

So when a project like this finally wraps, it matters for more than the traffic report. Homes in Spring Valley, Enterprise, the southwest, and the neighborhoods feeding off Russell, Tropicana, and Flamingo all get a cleaner shot at the resort corridor and the airport. That is a quiet, permanent upgrade to daily life, and it is the kind of thing that shows up in what people are willing to pay a few years later.

The other piece is jobs. A huge share of Clark County works on or near the Strip. Shaving ten minutes off a shift commute, twice a day, five days a week, adds up to real hours back in someone's life. That part never makes it into a press release.

I also tell sellers not to panic about construction near their listing. Buyers do ask about it, but the question they really want answered is when it ends. Once you can tell them a project is finished, or close to it, the construction stops being an objection and starts being a feature. This weekend is the moment that flips for the resort corridor.

My honest advice for this weekend is simple. Do not fight it. Move your errands, leave early, or just stay on your side of the valley until Monday. Two days of patience is a fair trade for finally being done with this thing.

What You Can Do This Weekend

Start with the detour. If you are heading south, plan to exit ahead of Spring Mountain Road and use Decatur Boulevard to get around the closure, then rejoin I-15 past the work zone. Valley View Boulevard and Industrial Road are backups, but they load up quickly once everyone has the same idea at the same time.

Check live conditions before you leave the house. Nevada's 511 system at nvroads.com shows current closures, crashes, and travel times statewide. NDOT also posts closure notices on its website. A 30 second check can save you an hour of sitting still.

Give yourself real padding if you have a flight. Harry Reid International sits right next to the closure zone. Add at least an extra 45 minutes to whatever you would normally allow, and make it more if you are flying out on Saturday afternoon.

If you work on the Strip, talk to your crew about arrival times. Friday at 9 p.m. and the Saturday evening shift change are going to be the worst of it. Carpooling or getting dropped off can be simpler than fighting for a parking garage.

Sign up for alerts if you drive this corridor regularly. NDOT and Nevada 511 both offer notifications for closures and incidents. Getting a heads up on your phone before you leave the house beats finding out at the on ramp with nowhere to turn around.

If you live near Decatur, Valley View, Industrial, or Las Vegas Boulevard, expect heavier traffic on your street through Sunday night. Watch for pedestrians, give yourself extra room at the lights, and be patient with the out of town drivers who have no idea where they are going.

Finally, pass the dates along to someone who needs them. Plenty of people will not hear about this closure until they hit the barricade. A quick text to a friend who works nights on the Strip, or to a family member catching an early flight out of Harry Reid, saves them a genuinely bad hour.
