Henderson Silver Knights Release 2026-27 Schedule, Open Oct. 3 at Lee's Family Forum

The Henderson Silver Knights released their 2026-27 regular season schedule on July 9, and opening night lands on Saturday, Oct. 3 against the Bakersfield Condors at Lee's Family Forum. It is a 72-game season with 36 of those games played right here in Henderson.

That is the headline, but the details are what make this schedule interesting for local families. The Silver Knights play 11 of their first 15 games at home. They also added six Kids Day Sundays where fans 14 and under get a giveaway and can go on the ice after the game for a slapshot. If you live in Green Valley, Anthem, Inspirada, or Cadence, that is a lot of affordable weekend plans dropped on your calendar all at once.

This is also the start of a new era behind the bench. Joel Ward takes over as head coach for his first year in the role. He replaces Ryan Craig, who earned a promotion to the NHL job in Vegas. The front office kept building in July, adding Alex Loh as an assistant coach on July 15.

What Happened

On July 9, the Henderson Silver Knights announced their full regular season schedule for the 2026-27 American Hockey League season. This will be the seventh season in franchise history. For a team that arrived during a strange stretch of Las Vegas sports history, seven years is starting to feel like real roots.

The season opens Saturday, Oct. 3 at Lee's Family Forum against the Bakersfield Condors. Bakersfield is a familiar name for Silver Knights fans. The two clubs share the Pacific Division, so this is a division opponent right out of the gate and not a soft warmup night.

The full slate runs 72 games. Half of those, 36 games, are home dates in Henderson. That is a big number when you compare it to other pro teams in the valley. The Vegas Golden Knights play 41 home games at T-Mobile Arena in a full NHL season, so the Silver Knights are offering a schedule that is close in volume but sits in a smaller building much closer to most Henderson neighborhoods.

The front-loaded start is the piece that jumps out. Henderson plays 11 of its first 15 games at home. Teams almost never get a run like that. It means October and early November will be packed with home dates at Lee's Family Forum. For a new head coach installing a new system, that stretch is a gift. For fans, it means you do not have to wait until midseason to see the team play in person.

The club also built six Kids Day Sundays into the schedule. On those dates, fans 14 and under get a giveaway item, and they can go down on the ice after the final horn to take a postgame slapshot. That is the kind of promotion that turns a casual Sunday into a memory. A kid who gets to shoot a puck on the same ice the pros just used tends to remember it.

Behind the bench, the Silver Knights are starting fresh. Joel Ward is the new head coach, and this is his first year leading the club. He steps in after Ryan Craig was promoted to the NHL job in Vegas. That promotion says something good about the organization. When the parent club pulls your head coach up to the big league, it usually means the development pipeline is working.

Henderson kept filling out the staff after the schedule dropped. On July 15, the team announced the hiring of Alex Loh as an assistant coach. Six days after publishing the schedule, the coaching room was another piece more complete. That is a normal July for a pro hockey club, but it is a reminder that the work never really stops between seasons.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Thirty-six home games is not just a sports statistic. It is 36 nights of activity in Henderson, most of them on weekends and weeknights when families are looking for something to do that does not involve a drive to the Strip.

Think about what a normal Saturday looks like for a family in Green Valley or Anthem. You can fight traffic on the 215 and head to the resort corridor, pay to park, and spend a small fortune. Or you can drive a few minutes to Lee's Family Forum and watch pro hockey. The second option is easier on the schedule and easier on the budget. That convenience is the entire pitch, and it is a strong one.

The location matters more than people realize. Lee's Family Forum sits in Henderson, not downtown and not on the Strip. For residents in Inspirada, Cadence, Anthem, and the Green Valley area, this is a genuinely local venue. Parents can get kids home and in bed at a reasonable hour on a school night. That is not something you can say about most pro sports in this valley.

The six Kids Day Sundays deserve their own mention. Free giveaways and a postgame slapshot on the ice give parents a real reason to buy tickets for a specific date. It also gives kids a first-hand connection to the sport. Hockey is not the default youth sport in a desert town. Programs grow when kids touch the ice and decide they want to come back. Six Sundays a season is a small thing that adds up over years.

There is a housing angle here too, and it is worth being honest about it. Buyers who move to Henderson from colder states often ask what there is to do. A pro hockey team playing 36 nights a year a short drive from your front door is a real answer. It will not move a home price by itself. Nothing single-handedly does. But amenities like this are part of why Henderson keeps winning transplants over other parts of the valley.

The busy October also matters for local businesses. Restaurants and shops near the arena see traffic on game nights. When 11 of the first 15 games are home dates, that is a strong fall for anyone running a business nearby. Henderson has spent years building out its dining and retail scene, and a packed home schedule feeds right into it.

The front-loaded start helps families plan, too. Eleven of the first 15 games at Lee's Family Forum means you can pick two or three dates in October, spread them out, and still feel like you saw a real chunk of the season. Compare that to a schedule where all the good home dates land in one crowded stretch. Henderson families get options early, and options are what make a season feel manageable instead of overwhelming. That flexibility matters most for households with young kids, where a weeknight game only works if the drive home is short.

Background and History

The Silver Knights came to Henderson in 2020 as the AHL affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights. The timing was rough. The franchise launched into a pandemic season with limits on crowds and a lot of uncertainty about what live sports would even look like. The first couple of years were about surviving as much as building.

The team played early home games at Orleans Arena while its permanent home was built in Henderson. That building, now known as Lee's Family Forum, gave the club a real base of operations and a real identity separate from its parent club in Las Vegas. Having your own arena changes how a team feels to a community. It stops being a road show and starts being the local team.

The affiliation with Vegas is the core of the whole thing. Players who suit up in Henderson are often one phone call from an NHL debut at T-Mobile Arena. Fans who follow the Silver Knights closely get to watch prospects before the rest of the valley knows their names. That is a fun way to follow hockey, and it makes the AHL more interesting than a lot of people assume.

Ryan Craig's promotion is a good illustration of how that pipeline works in both directions. Craig coached in Henderson and earned the NHL job in Vegas. That is the same path players take. It also means Joel Ward inherits a program that has been producing results, not one that needs a full rebuild. First-year head coaches do not always walk into that.

Seven seasons in, the Silver Knights are past the novelty stage. The valley has grown up around them. Henderson keeps adding rooftops in places like Cadence and Inspirada, and those neighborhoods have thousands of families who were not here when the team arrived. Every year the potential audience gets bigger.

What Happens Next

The next milestone is training camp and the preseason ramp-up before opening night on Oct. 3. Rosters at the AHL level shift right up until the puck drops. Players get assigned down from Vegas, others get signed, and the group that starts the season is rarely the exact group anyone expects in July.

Joel Ward will spend the summer and fall setting up his system and his staff. The Alex Loh hire on July 15 is part of that work. Expect more roster and staff news between now and October. That is how the AHL calendar always runs, and it is worth watching if you follow the Golden Knights, because these are the players Vegas may call on during the season.

The coaching staff is worth watching too. Joel Ward and Alex Loh are both new to their roles in Henderson this season, so the staff has to build chemistry at the same time the roster does. Assistant coaches handle a lot of the daily development work at the AHL level, from special teams systems to one-on-one video sessions with young players. Loh joining on July 15 gives him a full summer and a full training camp to settle in before the Oct. 3 opener. That timing matters more than it sounds.

For fans, the practical next step is planning. The schedule is public, so you can look at the home dates and pick the ones that fit your fall and winter. The six Kids Day Sundays are the ones that tend to draw families, so those dates are worth circling early if you have kids at home.

The early home stretch is the thing to watch on the ice. Eleven of the first 15 games at Lee's Family Forum gives Henderson a real chance to bank points before the travel picks up. A strong October can set the tone for an entire season. A slow one puts a first-year head coach in a hole while the schedule gets harder. Either way, October will tell us a lot.

Ryan's Take

I look at this schedule and I see quality of life, which is a phrase real estate people overuse but actually means something here. Buyers moving to Henderson from Michigan, Minnesota, Colorado, or Canada ask me about hockey more often than you would think. Being able to say there is a pro team playing 36 home games a few minutes from Green Valley or Anthem is a genuinely useful answer.

Henderson has been quietly stacking these wins for years. New restaurants, new parks, a growing arena calendar, and master planned communities like Cadence and Inspirada that keep filling in. No single item on that list moves the market. Together, they are the reason Henderson keeps holding value better than a lot of the valley. Amenities are sticky. People pay to live near things they actually use.

The Kids Day Sundays are my favorite detail, and not because of anything to do with real estate. Six times a season, a kid gets to walk on pro ice and take a shot. That is the kind of thing that turns into a lifelong fan and, sometimes, a lifelong resident. Communities are built out of small moments like that, and Henderson keeps creating them.

What You Can Do

Start with the schedule itself. The Henderson Silver Knights posted the full 2026-27 slate on their team website, so you can see every home date and pick the games that fit your fall and winter. If you want the Kids Day Sundays, look those up first and plan around them, because family promotions tend to draw bigger crowds.

Mark Saturday, Oct. 3 on your calendar if you want to be there for opening night against Bakersfield. Season openers usually have the best energy of the year, and this one doubles as the first game of the Joel Ward era. That combination does not come around often.

If you have kids who want to play, use the season as a starting point. Take them to a game, see if it clicks, then look into local youth programs around the valley. A lot of hockey families in Henderson got started exactly this way, with one game and one kid who would not stop talking about it on the drive home.

Follow the team's news page through the summer for roster and coaching updates. Between now and October there will be more moves. If you are a Golden Knights fan, those updates matter to you too, because the Henderson roster is where a lot of Vegas depth comes from.
