Nevada's $20,000 Down Payment Program Has Only 59 Spots Left

Nevada is offering $20,000 in down payment help plus a 5.50 percent mortgage rate to essential workers buying a home, and the state's own rate sheet shows only 59 reservations left. That last number is why this story matters today instead of next month.

The program is called Worker Advantage. It runs through Home Is Possible, the home buyer assistance arm of the Nevada Housing Division. The 5.50 percent rate applies to FHA, VA and USDA loans. It comes with 4 percent in discount points, which is a real cost baked into the deal. The $20,000 is not a gift and it is not a grant. It is a second mortgage with no interest and no monthly payment, and it is not forgivable. You pay it back when you sell the home or refinance it.

To qualify you need a 640 credit score, a job in a qualifying essential worker category, at least six months of Nevada residency, and a purchase price under $832,750. Here is the part most people miss. You do not have to be a first-time buyer.

For context on the rate, Freddie Mac reported the 30-year fixed averaged 6.55 percent the week of July 16, 2026. A 5.50 percent rate sits roughly a full percentage point below that. On a real Las Vegas loan amount, a point of rate is not a small thing. We will run those numbers below.

One honest caution before you read further. The 59 count is a snapshot from the state's rate page on July 20, 2026. Reservation counts move fast, and the number can drop between the time this posts and the time you read it. Confirm the current count with a participating lender before you build a plan around it.

What Happened

The Nevada Housing Division posts a daily rate sheet at homeispossiblenv.org. The July 20, 2026 posting listed the Worker Advantage product at a 5.50 percent interest rate on FHA, VA and USDA loans, paired with $20,000 in down payment assistance. Next to that pricing, the sheet listed 59 remaining reservations.

That word, reservations, is the key to understanding the whole thing. State assistance money does not sit in an open pool that anyone can tap whenever they want. It comes in funded blocks. When a buyer gets under contract, an approved lender reserves a slice of that block in that buyer's name. Every reservation pulls the counter down by one. When the counter hits zero, that pricing and that $20,000 stop being available until the state funds and posts a new block. Nobody gets a warning email the day before it closes.

Now the structure of the money, because this is where headlines get it wrong. The $20,000 is a no-interest, no-payment, non-forgivable second mortgage. Break that apart. No interest means the balance never grows. No payment means it does not add anything to your monthly bill. Non-forgivable means it never goes away on its own. There is no five-year clock or ten-year clock that erases it. You borrowed $20,000 and you owe $20,000 until the day you sell the house or refinance the loan, and then it gets paid off out of that transaction.

The 4 percent discount points deserve the same plain talk. Discount points are money paid up front to buy the interest rate down. That is how a 5.50 percent rate exists in a 6.55 percent market. Four percent of a $400,000 loan is $16,000. That cost is real, it shows up in the closing figures, and you should ask your lender directly how it is handled on your specific file and whether a seller credit can help cover any of it. Anyone who tells you this program is free money has not read the fine print.

The eligibility rules as posted are straightforward:

  • Minimum 640 credit score
  • Employment in a qualifying essential worker category
  • At least six months of Nevada residency
  • Maximum purchase price of $832,750
  • FHA, VA or USDA financing
  • First-time buyer status is not required

The qualifying occupation list is set by the Nevada Housing Division, and job titles do not always match how people describe their own work. Do not assume you are in or out based on a guess. Have a participating lender check your actual employer and title against the current list before you decide this is not for you.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with the local price. Las Vegas Realtors reported a median existing single-family price of $490,000 for June 2026, a record high. On a $490,000 purchase with an FHA loan, the minimum 3.5 percent down payment is $17,150. The Worker Advantage assistance is $20,000. That means the help covers the entire minimum down payment on a median-priced Las Vegas home, with money to spare. Ask your lender how any remaining amount can be applied on your file, because that varies.

This matters because for most working families in Clark County, income is not the wall. Cash is. Plenty of people in this valley can carry a mortgage payment. Far fewer have $17,000 sitting in a savings account on top of closing costs, an earnest money deposit, an inspection, moving expenses and a deposit on the utilities. That gap is where the dream stalls out, year after year.

Saving that pile has gotten harder here, and the rent data shows it. A Zumper analysis reported by the Las Vegas Review-Journal found the median one-bedroom rent in the Las Vegas Valley went from about $1,053 in June 2019 to $1,235 in June 2026, a 17 percent jump. Los Angeles one-bedroom rent actually fell 1.3 percent over those same seven years. Try building a down payment while your rent climbs every single renewal. That is the treadmill this program is built to interrupt.

Then there is the rate itself. Here is the math with the assumptions stated plainly. Take a $400,000 loan amount on a 30-year fixed, and look only at principal and interest. At 5.50 percent the payment is roughly $2,270 a month. At 6.55 percent, the Freddie Mac average for the week of July 16, 2026, it is roughly $2,540 a month. That is a difference of about $270 every month, or somewhere near $3,240 a year. Those figures exclude property taxes, homeowners insurance and mortgage insurance, so your real payment will be higher. But the gap between the two rates is the gap, and it does not shrink.

The non-first-time-buyer rule is quietly one of the biggest features here. Most assistance programs slam the door on anyone who has ever owned. This one does not. If you owned a home in another state before you moved here, if you sold during a divorce, if you sold years ago and have been renting since, you are not automatically disqualified. That covers a lot of people in a city built on newcomers and second chances.

And look at the price cap. At $832,750, the ceiling sits far above the $490,000 valley median. This is not a program limited to the cheapest corner of the market. Henderson, North Las Vegas, the northwest, the southwest, the established central neighborhoods, almost the entire valley has inventory that fits under that cap.

Background and History

Home Is Possible is not new. It is the long-running home buyer assistance brand of the Nevada Housing Division, and it has helped Nevadans get to a closing table for years through a family of loan and down payment products. Worker Advantage is one of those products. It launched earlier in 2026 and was built specifically around essential workers. The news this week is not the launch. The news is that the runway is almost gone.

Programs like this exist because two things moved against buyers at the same time. Prices climbed and rates climbed with them. A buyer facing a $490,000 median price and a 6.55 percent market rate needs both a bigger pile of cash and a bigger monthly payment than a buyer facing the same purchase five or six years ago. Worker Advantage attacks both problems at once, one with the $20,000 and one with the bought-down rate.

It is worth knowing that even the experts disagree about where Las Vegas values are heading. Zillow's June 2026 market report put the typical Las Vegas metro home value at $427,825, down 3.1 percent year over year, while the national typical value rose 1.1 percent to $372,057. Las Vegas Realtors, meanwhile, reported that record $490,000 median sale price. Those two numbers measure different things, one an estimate across all homes and one the middle of what actually sold. Argue about which is right all you want. Neither one changes the size of the check a buyer brings to closing, and that is the problem this program solves.

The last piece of background is the funding itself. Assistance money is finite. It comes in blocks, it gets committed one buyer at a time, and the counter only moves in one direction until the state posts more. That is not a marketing tactic to create urgency. It is just how the mechanism works. A program can be genuinely great and genuinely out of money in the same week.

What Happens Next

The most likely next step is simple. The counter keeps falling. Fifty-nine reservations is a live number on a page the Nevada Housing Division updates, and it can reach zero on any given day once word spreads. It is also possible the state posts new funds, new pricing, or a different rate on that same page. Both outcomes are normal. The only way to know which one you are looking at is to check the current sheet, not a screenshot from a news story.

For a buyer, the sequence runs in a specific order and it is easy to get wrong. You do not reserve the assistance yourself. Your lender does it, and generally only once you are actually under contract on a home. That means the preparation work has to happen first. Getting pre-approved after the funds are gone is not a plan. If you want a real shot at one of these spots, you need a participating lender lined up, your credit verified at 640 or better, your employment confirmed against the eligible list, and a real offer ready to write.

Ask your lender two timing questions that most buyers forget. First, how long does a reservation hold once it is made. Second, what is the deadline to close before that reservation expires. Locking a spot and then blowing the closing timeline is a painful way to lose it, and those terms are set by the program, not by your agent.

If the 59 spots are gone by the time you call, do not assume you are out of options. Home Is Possible runs more than one product, and the Nevada Housing Division has posted new funding blocks in the past. Get on a lender's list, ask to be told when new pricing posts, and keep your file ready. The buyers who get these things are almost always the ones who were already prepared when the window opened.

Ryan's Take

I want to be straight with you, because this story is going to get shared with captions calling it free money. It is not free money. It is a $20,000 loan at zero percent with no monthly payment, and you will pay every dollar of it back when you sell or refinance. The 5.50 percent rate is bought down with 4 percent in points, and points cost real money. If you go in thinking somebody handed you $20,000, you will be surprised at the closing table and surprised again at the settlement statement when you sell.

Now here is why I still think it is one of the better deals available to a Las Vegas buyer right now. The actual barrier in this valley is not the monthly payment, it is the cash to get in the door. This program hits that barrier directly, and it pairs it with a rate about a point under market. That $270 a month I calculated above does not go away after a promotional period. It is baked into a 30-year fixed loan. Over the years you stay in the house, that adds up to real money you keep.

Two cautions from someone who watches these closings happen. Do not stretch into a house you cannot comfortably carry just because you qualified for a spot. And if there is any chance you are moving again inside a couple of years, run the sale numbers first, because that $20,000 second mortgage comes off the top of your proceeds. This is a tool for people planting roots in Clark County, not for people passing through.

What You Can Do

Start by checking the live rate sheet at homeispossiblenv.org and looking at the reservation count with your own eyes. Whatever number you see there beats any number in a news article, including this one. If the count still shows spots available, move the same day. This is not a decision to sit on over a long weekend.

Next, call a lender who participates in Home Is Possible. Ask three questions in this order. Does my job title fall inside the qualifying essential worker category. Am I at 640 or better on the score the program actually uses. Can you confirm the reservations remaining right now. That last one takes a lender thirty seconds and it saves you a week of hoping.

Then get your file ready. Confirm you can document at least six months of Nevada residency. Pull your credit early so any errors have time to get corrected. Get a full pre-approval, not a quick online estimate. And make sure you understand two things in writing before you commit: who is paying the 4 percent in discount points on your deal, and exactly when the $20,000 second mortgage becomes due. Those two answers change the real math more than anything else in the program.

Finally, be ready to write an offer. Reservations attach to buyers under contract, which means the assistance goes to people who are already looking, already approved, and already able to say yes on a house. If you have been waiting for a reason to start, 59 is a pretty good one.
