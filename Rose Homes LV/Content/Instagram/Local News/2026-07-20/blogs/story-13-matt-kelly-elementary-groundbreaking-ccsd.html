CCSD Breaks Ground on a $76 Million New Matt Kelly Elementary in the Historic Westside

The Clark County School District broke ground on Monday, July 13, 2026, on a brand new Matt Kelly Elementary School in the Historic Westside. The $76 million campus replaces a building that has served that neighborhood since 1960, and it is set to open for the 2027-2028 school year.

That is the short version. The longer version matters more.

Matt Kelly Elementary sits at 1900 North J Street, in the middle of one of the oldest Black neighborhoods in Las Vegas. Generations of families on the Westside went to school in that building. Parents walked those halls. So did grandparents. When the old bell rang for the last time in May 2026, it was not just a building closing. It was a landmark closing.

The new school will be two stories and will sit on the same lot as the original. It is paid for through CCSD's 2015 Capital Improvement Program. Principal Jerrell Hall told FOX5 that the design took roughly three to four years and pulled in ideas from teachers, families and community partners along the way.

"Matt Kelly was built in 1960 and has a long, deep, rich history right here in the heart of Historic Westside," Hall said. "Today, we are breaking ground on an innovative, new campus that's going to be more than these kids ever could dream of to help them meet their dreams for the future."

For a neighborhood that has spent decades being told to wait, a $76 million school going up on the same corner is a big deal. Here is what is actually being built, why the neighborhood history matters, and what it means for people who live nearby.

What Happened

District leaders, school staff, families and community members gathered at the site on July 13 for the official groundbreaking. The old building had already come down. Demolition started in June 2026, right after the school year ended.

Before that, students got a send-off. In May, kids lined up to ring the old school bell one last time. Assistant Principal Sherence Sathekge told FOX5 the point was to honor the old campus while getting kids excited about the new one.

"We know the impact this is going to have on our students to be in a state-of-the-art school where they have everything up-to-date to make sure that they have the best that all that everyone has," Sathekge said.

The replacement is not a copy-paste school. Most CCSD elementary schools are built from one or two standard floor plans. This one is not. KTNV reported that the design came out of years of community meetings, and the building reflects what the neighborhood asked for.

According to KTNV, the new campus will include:

  • A community resource center for parents
  • An outdoor amphitheater
  • A garden
  • A learning kitchen

The garden and the kitchen work together. Hall told KTNV that students will grow fruits and vegetables on site, then take them into the kitchen and learn to turn them into meals.

"We're also going to have a learning kitchen for kids where they'll be able to take those fruits and vegetables, the things that they grow in the garden, and learn how to prepare them into healthy snacks, healthy meals, smoothies," Hall said.

The community resource center is worth pausing on. That is space set aside inside a public elementary school for adults in the neighborhood, not just for students. Schools that do this well become the place where people go for help with paperwork, job leads, food assistance and parenting support. It is a small line item in a $76 million budget that can end up mattering a lot.

The money comes from CCSD's 2015 Capital Improvement Program. That is the district's long-running construction and renovation fund, and it is how CCSD pays for new schools, replacement campuses and major repairs across Clark County. Matt Kelly is one project inside that larger program.

KTNV also reported that the new school is part of a broader $1 billion investment across the Historic Westside. That figure covers many different projects and many different funding sources, not just the school. Still, it gives you a sense of the scale of what is being planned in that part of town.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with the families directly affected. When the 2026-27 school year begins on Monday, August 10, Matt Kelly students will not report to their own campus. They will attend Fife Elementary School, which CCSD uses as a swing school for exactly this situation. The plan is to bring them back to the new Matt Kelly building in the fall of 2027.

That is roughly a full school year at a different campus. For parents, that means new drop-off routines, new drive times and new logistics. It is a real disruption, and it is the price of getting a new building on the same lot instead of somewhere else.

There is a nice detail in it too. KTNV reported that students heading into 4th grade will be the first class to graduate out of the new building. They will spend a year away, come back to a school nobody has ever used, and then move on to middle school from it.

"They've never been to a new school before, so they're going to be pretty happy," parent Taneisha Winchester told KTNV.

Now zoom out. Matt Kelly is not the only investment landing in this part of town this month. On July 9, the James M. Cox Foundation announced a $200,000 grant to the Las Vegas-Clark County Library District Foundation to build a Creation Lab at the West Las Vegas Library.

That grant funds four things. There is a free six-week sewing and apparel program. There is upgraded software for the library's podcasting and media studio. There is game development technology for a student gaming club. And there are 5,000 take-home Curiosity Kits with books and STEM activities for children five and under.

Two separate investments, two separate funding sources, same neighborhood, same month. One is a school. One is a library. Both are aimed at kids and families in West Las Vegas. That is not a coincidence so much as a pattern, and it is a pattern worth noticing.

For the rest of Clark County, there is a simpler takeaway. CCSD's capital program is spending real money on replacement campuses, and Matt Kelly is proof that older schools do eventually get rebuilt. If your neighborhood school is aging, the district's capital plan is the document to watch.

Background and History

You cannot tell this story honestly without the neighborhood behind it.

The Historic Westside started before Las Vegas did. Surveyor J.T. McWilliams laid out a townsite on 80 acres west of the railroad's planned right of way in 1904, a year before the city was founded in 1905. That area became known as the McWilliams Townsite.

Black families began moving west of the tracks in the early 1930s. The bigger wave came in the 1940s and 1950s, when people arrived from the South for defense industry work. They found jobs. They also found a segregated city.

Local policy and practice at the time kept Black families from buying homes across most of Las Vegas. The Westside was one of the few places where they could. So the neighborhood grew dense with Black homeowners, churches, businesses and clubs, not because it was chosen freely, but because the rest of the valley was closed.

What the community built there anyway is the part people should know. Black entertainers who headlined on the Strip were not allowed to stay in the hotels they performed in, so they came to the Westside after their shows. Jackson Avenue had Black-owned clubs, and the music there was some of the best in the country on any given night.

The Moulin Rouge opened on May 24, 1955, as the first racially integrated hotel-casino in Las Vegas. It did not stay open long, but it mattered. In 1960, Dr. James McMillan, the first Black dentist in Las Vegas, led an NAACP effort that pushed local officials to desegregate Strip hotels and casinos. Nevada did not ban residential segregation until 1971.

Here is the timing that sticks with me. Matt Kelly Elementary opened in 1960. That is the same year the desegregation push reached the Strip. The school and the fight for equal access in this city are almost exactly the same age.

What Happens Next

The construction timeline is straightforward. Crews are working now. The building is scheduled to open for the 2027-2028 school year, which means students should be back in the fall of 2027.

In the meantime, Matt Kelly families report to Fife Elementary starting August 10, 2026. CCSD registration for the 2026-27 year is already open, and the district also rolled out new bell times that take effect that same day. If you have a student in the Matt Kelly zone, check your school start time before the first morning. It may not be what it was last year.

The wider Westside work continues too. The City of Las Vegas has been running a long-term plan for the neighborhood called the HUNDRED Plan, short for Historic Urban Neighborhood Design Redevelopment. It was first developed in 2016 and covers housing, streets, business development and public spaces across the Westside.

Projects tied to that broader effort are moving now. Marble Manor, a longtime public housing site, is being redeveloped in phases, with the first phase expected to finish in 2027. City leaders also broke ground in 2026 on a commercial office and entrepreneurial training center near West Owens Avenue.

None of these projects are the same thing. Different agencies, different budgets, different timelines. But they are all landing in the same square mile over the next few years, and the new Matt Kelly building will open right in the middle of that stretch.

One honest caution on timelines. Construction schedules move. A 2027-2028 opening is the plan, not a promise. If crews hit delays, the swing year at Fife could stretch. Families should watch CCSD's updates rather than assuming the date is locked.

Ryan's Take

I get asked about schools constantly. Buyers want to know if a new school will push values up in the surrounding blocks. My honest answer is that a new school is one signal among many, and it is a weaker signal on its own than people assume.

School quality does show up in home prices in most markets. But quality is not the same thing as a new building. A new building can help a school attract and keep good teachers, and it removes the maintenance problems that drag a campus down. That is real. It is also slow. You do not see it in a year.

What I would say about the Historic Westside is narrower and more careful. A $76 million public investment tells you that institutions are paying attention to a neighborhood that has often been skipped. That is meaningful information. It is not a signal to go treat somebody's community as a trade.

I also want to be straight about something. When money starts flowing into a historically Black neighborhood, longtime residents do not universally cheer. Some people are thrilled about the school and worried about their rent at the same time. Both of those things can be true in the same household. Anyone who tells you the Westside feels one single way about neighborhood change is not talking to enough people there.

So my take is simple. This is a community that has been asking for a proper school building for a long time, and it is finally getting one that the neighborhood helped design. That deserves to be reported as good news for the people who live there, full stop.

What You Can Do

If you have a student at Matt Kelly, confirm your Fife Elementary details before August 10. Check the bus route, the start time and the drop-off plan. CCSD changed bell times for the 2026-27 year, so do not assume last year's schedule carries over.

If you are registering a student anywhere in Clark County, registration for 2026-27 is open now. Doing it early makes the first week easier for everyone, including your kid's teacher.

If you live near the West Las Vegas Library, watch for the Creation Lab programs to open. The sewing and apparel classes are free, the Curiosity Kits are free for families with young children, and the podcasting studio is a free resource most people do not know exists.

If you want to understand the Historic Westside beyond a news clip, go to the source material. UNLV Special Collections maintains a public archive documenting the African American experience in Las Vegas. The City of Las Vegas also publishes its HUNDRED Plan documents online, which show what is planned block by block.

And if you are thinking about buying or selling anywhere in the valley, treat school news as one input among many. Look at the whole picture: commute, taxes, HOA, condition, comparable sales and how long you actually plan to stay. A groundbreaking headline is not a strategy.
