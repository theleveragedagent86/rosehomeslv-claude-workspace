United Way Is Handing Out $13 Million in Free Preschool Scholarships in Southern Nevada

United Way of Southern Nevada is giving out more than $13 million in preschool scholarships this year. The money pays for free preschool for about 1,592 children across Southern Nevada.

Here is the part that matters most to a parent. Do the division and $13 million spread across 1,592 children works out to about $8,165 per child. That is an average, not a promise. Some awards will be worth more and some less, depending on the center and the program. But it gives you a real sense of the size of what is on the table.

Think about that number next to what a family here actually pays. Full-time care for a preschooler in Clark County is a serious monthly bill. A scholarship worth roughly $8,000 for the year is not a coupon. For a lot of households it is the difference between one parent working and one parent staying home.

The money comes through the Nevada Ready! State Pre-K Program, a partnership between United Way of Southern Nevada and the Nevada Department of Education. Nearly 50 local preschool centers are taking part. Every one of them has to meet quality standards set by the state.

The starting point for families is uwsn.org/early-education. That page has an eligibility list and an interest form. Below, I walk through how the awards actually get handed out, who qualifies, and what to do this week if you want a seat.

What Happened

On July 16, 2026, FOX5 Vegas reported that United Way of Southern Nevada is distributing more than $13 million in preschool scholarships this year. The funding covers free, high-quality preschool for approximately 1,592 children across the region.

Samuel Rudd is the president and CEO of United Way of Southern Nevada. He said early education lays the foundation for lifelong success and helps families build more stable lives.

Nearly 50 local preschool centers are participating. All of them meet the state quality rating standards set by the Nevada Department of Education. That last detail is worth pausing on. These are not random daycares. They are centers the state has already reviewed and rated.

Now here is the piece most people miss. You do not apply to United Way and get a check. Families receive scholarships through the participating centers, based on recommendations and referrals from those centers.

That changes how you should approach this. Filling out the interest form on the United Way site is a good first step. But calling a participating center near you is just as important, maybe more so. The centers are the ones putting families forward.

The FOX5 story did not print the eligibility rules or the list of centers. I went and found both on United Way's own website, so you do not have to guess.

As of July 20, 2026, the Early Education page at uwsn.org lists 47 participating Nevada Ready! centers by name, address and phone number. That matches the "nearly 50" figure in the news story. Of those 47, I counted 35 in Las Vegas, 7 in North Las Vegas, and 5 in Henderson.

The eligibility rules are published there too. For the Nevada Ready! State Pre-K Program, a child must be 4 years old by August 1, 2026. On top of the age rule, a family may qualify if one or more of these things are true:

  • Yearly household income at or below 250 percent of the Federal Poverty Level
  • The family speaks a language other than English at home
  • The child has an Individualized Education Program, or IEP
  • The child is with a foster family providing temporary care
  • The family belongs to a historically underserved community
  • The family is currently unhoused
  • The family includes a current or former member of the military
  • The family lives in a rural area or on tribal land
  • The family has refugee or asylum status
  • The family is a single-parent household
  • The child lives with relatives or trusted family friends instead of their parents
  • A parent or guardian is currently in jail or prison
  • The family lives in a neighborhood where all nearby elementary schools have a 1-star or 2-star rating

Read that list again. Income is only one of thirteen paths. A lot of families who assume they earn too much still qualify through one of the others.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Let me put real dollars on that 250 percent income line, because "250 percent of the Federal Poverty Level" means nothing to most people.

The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services publishes poverty guidelines every year. For 2026, in the 48 contiguous states, the guideline for a family of four is $33,000 a year. Multiply that by 2.5 and you get $82,500.

Here is the full picture at 250 percent using the official 2026 numbers:

  • Household of 2: about $54,100
  • Household of 3: about $68,300
  • Household of 4: about $82,500
  • Household of 5: about $96,700
  • Household of 6: about $110,900

An $82,500 ceiling for a family of four is a lot higher than most people expect. That is a household with two working parents in ordinary Las Vegas jobs. Plenty of families who would never call themselves low income land under that line.

There is a second United Way program worth knowing about. It is called Pre-K for Nevada, and it is aimed at families who do not qualify for the state program. It serves both 3-year-olds and 4-year-olds, and it goes up to 300 percent of the Federal Poverty Level. That is about $99,000 for a family of four.

One honest note on that program. The United Way page currently says children must turn 3 or 4 on or before August 1, 2025. That date looks out of date for a 2026 school year. Do not let a possibly stale line on a web page stop you. Call and ask. [NOT VERIFIED whether the 2025 date is a typo or a real cutoff.]

The bigger reason this matters is simple math about household budgets. Childcare and preschool are a real monthly line item for young families in Clark County, and that line item competes directly with a mortgage payment. When a family tells me they cannot afford to buy yet, childcare is often a bigger factor than the down payment.

Take that $8,165 average and spread it over a year. That is close to $680 a month. In a lot of Las Vegas households, $680 a month is the gap between renting and qualifying for a loan. I am not going to oversell it, because a scholarship is not a mortgage approval. But it is real money that frees up real room in a budget.

There is a geographic point here too, and I want to be straight about it. Looking at the addresses on United Way's partner list, the centers cluster heavily in central Las Vegas, the east valley and North Las Vegas. Outer suburbs are thinner. If you live in Summerlin or the far southwest, check the list carefully before you assume there is a seat down the street.

Background and History

The Nevada Ready! State Pre-K Program is not new. United Way of Southern Nevada and the Nevada Department of Education have been running it together since 2015.

In that time, according to United Way, the partnership has provided more than 7,500 preschool scholarships. The program also pays for ongoing teacher training, so classrooms keep up with current teaching methods. That teacher training piece rarely makes the news, but it is part of why the state signs off on these centers.

Now for a number that did not appear in the July news coverage, and I think parents should see it.

In February 2026, United Way of Southern Nevada put out a press release announcing $15.9 million in scholarships. That round covered about 1,900 students at nearly 60 centers.

Compare the two announcements side by side:

  • February 2026: $15.9 million, about 1,900 students, nearly 60 centers
  • July 2026: more than $13 million, about 1,592 children, nearly 50 centers

That is roughly $2.9 million less, about 18 percent. It is about 308 fewer seats, about 16 percent. And it is roughly ten fewer centers.

Run the per-child math on both and something interesting shows up. The February round works out to about $8,368 per student. The July round works out to about $8,165. So the value of an individual scholarship held pretty steady, down only about 2 percent. What shrank was the number of children served.

United Way did not publish a year-over-year comparison and did not explain the change. I am not going to guess at the reason. But the practical takeaway for a parent is clear: there are fewer seats this round than last, so moving early matters more than it did a year ago.

What Happens Next

United Way has not published a deadline for the scholarships. There is no posted cutoff date and no posted application window. That is not me being vague, it simply is not on the page.

What we do know is the calendar pressure. The Clark County School District starts the 2026-27 school year on Monday, August 10, 2026. Preschool centers fill their fall rosters in the weeks leading up to that. Waiting until August is waiting too long.

Something else landed the very same week, and it is genuinely useful to see the two options next to each other.

On July 16, the same day as the United Way news, the city of Las Vegas opened online registration for its Strong Start GO! Mobile Pre-K Academy. It is a free program for children ages 3 to 5, and classes are held on converted school buses.

Sessions run Monday through Thursday. Mornings are 8:30 to 11 a.m. Afternoons are 1 to 3:30 p.m. Both time slots are offered at all four locations:

  • Chuck Minker Sports Complex, at Mojave Road and Stewart Avenue
  • Historic Westside School, 330 W. Washington Ave. at D Street
  • Derfelt Strong Future Youth Enrichment Center, at Twin Lakes Drive and Washington Avenue
  • Marble Manor, at McWilliams and M Street

For questions on the mobile program, call 702-229-5437 and choose Option 3, or email clvstronggoprek@LasVegasNevada.gov.

So a Las Vegas parent now has two free pre-K doors open at the same time. They are different programs run by different organizations, and they are not competing. If your child is 3, the mobile academy and Pre-K for Nevada are your paths, since Nevada Ready! is built around 4-year-olds. If your child is 4 and turns 4 by August 1, you may fit all three.

My honest advice is to pursue more than one at once. These are half-day and full-day programs with limited seats, and nobody has posted a waitlist policy.

Ryan's Take

The single most useful thing in this story is the eligibility list, and it is the part that got the least coverage.

Most parents hear "scholarship" and immediately do a gut check on their paycheck. Then they decide they earn too much and move on. That instinct costs families real money here.

Income is one of thirteen ways to qualify for Nevada Ready! A single parent qualifies. A family that speaks Spanish or Tagalog at home qualifies. A veteran's family qualifies. A child with an IEP qualifies. A grandmother raising her grandkids qualifies. None of those have anything to do with what you make.

And even the income path is more generous than people assume. An $82,500 ceiling for a household of four covers a huge slice of working Las Vegas.

The other thing I would flag is the referral structure. Awards flow through the centers, not straight from United Way. So the family that calls three centers and introduces themselves is in a better spot than the family that submits one online form and waits. That is not how it should work, maybe, but it is how it does work, and you should use that.

On the real estate side I will keep this short and honest. Childcare is one of the largest fixed costs a young family in Clark County carries, and it sits right alongside a house payment in the monthly budget. When that cost drops, buying power goes up. I am not telling you a preschool scholarship gets you a house. I am telling you it changes the math, and the math is where most first-time buyers here get stuck.

What You Can Do

Here is the order I would work through this week.

Start at uwsn.org/early-education. Read the eligibility list before you judge yourself against it. Then fill out the interest form on that page. It asks for your name, phone, email, your child's name, and the date care is needed.

Check eligibility a second way. United Way also points families to the First 5 Nevada website, which has its own eligibility checker at first5nevada.org.

Call a participating center directly. This is the step people skip and it may be the most important one. The partner list with names, addresses and phone numbers is on the same United Way Early Education page. Pick two or three near you, call, and tell them you are seeking a Nevada Ready! scholarship.

Do not disqualify yourself. If you are unsure whether you meet a rule, ask United Way instead of guessing. Their main line is 702-892-2300.

Register for the city program too. The Strong Start GO! Mobile Pre-K Academy is separate and free. Call 702-229-5437, Option 3, or email clvstronggoprek@LasVegasNevada.gov.

Move before August 10. That is when CCSD's year starts and when fall rosters lock up.
