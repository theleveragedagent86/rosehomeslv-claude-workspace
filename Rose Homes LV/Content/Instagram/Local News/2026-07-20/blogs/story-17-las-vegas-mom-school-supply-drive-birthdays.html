A Las Vegas Mom Turned Her Four Kids' July Birthdays Into a School Supply Drive

A Las Vegas mother has turned her children's birthdays into an annual school supply collection, and the donations are landing at the exact moment Clark County families need them. LaTaisha Cummings has four children, and all four were born in July. Instead of asking for birthday gifts, the family asks friends and relatives to bring school supplies.

Those supplies go to the FOX5 S.O.S. Back-to-School Drive, which fills backpacks for students in need across Southern Nevada. The first year the family tried it, they collected one backpack. This year is the biggest haul they have ever had.

The timing is what makes this worth your attention today. The Clark County School District starts the 2026-27 school year on Monday, August 10. That leaves about three weeks to fill backpacks before the first bell.

Here is the short version if you want to help. Supplies can be dropped off at Findlay Subaru of Las Vegas, 6455 W. Roy Horn Way. Drop-offs opened July 10 and are still going. If you would rather give money, text SOS2026 to 51555.

FOX5 will also broadcast live from three valley locations from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on July 22, July 23 and July 24. Donations are accepted at every site. Full addresses are further down this page.

On its face this is a small story. One family, one birthday tradition, a growing pile of notebooks. But it lands on top of a real number. Back-to-school is one of the most expensive months of the year for a household with kids, and in a county this size, a lot of families are quietly stretched thin in July.

What Happened

FOX5 Vegas reporter Talia Kirkland told the family's story on July 18. Cummings has four children, and every one of their birthdays falls in July. Rather than a stack of presents each summer, the family started asking the people who come to celebrate to bring school supplies instead.

The first year, that ask produced exactly one backpack. The second year brought a few backpacks and a stack of supplies. It has grown every year since, and 2026 is the largest collection the family has put together.

Cummings told FOX5 why she started it. "I used to be that kid that never had anything, so I don't want my kids to take for granted all of the things they do have," she said.

Her daughter, Kelsey Taylor, described what the tradition feels like from the kids' side. "It makes me feel happy that I give kids everything that they need," she said.

Everything the family collects goes to the FOX5 S.O.S. Back-to-School Drive. S.O.S. stands for Supply Our Students. FOX5 runs the drive with its Take 5 To Care partners, and all donations benefit the Salvation Army, which packs the supplies into backpacks and gets them to students.

How to donate, plainly

Drop off supplies: Findlay Subaru of Las Vegas, 6455 W. Roy Horn Way, Las Vegas, NV 89118. Drop-offs began July 10.

Give money instead: Text SOS2026 to 51555.

Live broadcast donation days, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. each day:

  • Wednesday, July 22: UFC Fit, 5651 Centennial Center Blvd., Las Vegas, NV 89149
  • Thursday, July 23: Findlay Subaru of Las Vegas, 6455 W. Roy Horn Way, Las Vegas, NV 89118
  • Friday, July 24: Albertsons, 201 S. Stephanie St., Henderson, NV 89012

Those three days are the push. If you are in the northwest, UFC Fit on Centennial Center is your stop. If you are central or southwest, Findlay Subaru works. If you live in Henderson or Green Valley, the Albertsons on Stephanie is the closest option and it is right off the 215.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with what back-to-school actually costs now. The National Retail Federation released its 2026 survey on July 14. Families with students in elementary through high school plan to spend an average of $863.86 this year, up slightly from $858.07 in 2025. Total national spending is expected to hit a record $43.3 billion.

Break that down and the supply line alone is real money. NRF found K-12 shoppers plan to spend an average of $146.45 on school supplies, on top of $250.29 for clothing, $174.01 for shoes and $293.11 for electronics. For a household with two or three kids, you are looking at a four-figure month before anyone has bought a single lunch.

NRF also found something telling about how families are coping. About 47 percent of shoppers say they plan to buy only the essentials for day one and then replenish supplies later in the year as they can afford to. That is a budgeting strategy, not a preference. It is also exactly the gap a supply drive fills.

Now put that next to the size of Clark County. CCSD is the fifth-largest school district in the United States. FOX5 reported in February 2026 that enrollment sits around 277,000 students, down roughly 47,000 from its 2018 peak. Even after that decline, this is a district serving more than a quarter of a million kids.

Here is a fact that says a lot without needing any editorializing. CCSD participates in the federal Community Eligibility Provision district wide. That means the district serves free breakfast and lunch to every student at participating schools without collecting household income applications at all. Districts qualify for that program based on the concentration of need in the community they serve.

School meals are covered. School supplies are not. That is the gap. A family can send a child to a CCSD campus knowing breakfast and lunch are handled, and still be short the $20 for a backpack and the $40 for everything that goes in it.

There is also a practical reason a drive like this exists in late July specifically. Teachers build their supply lists into the first week of instruction. A student who shows up without a composition book or a set of pencils is not just embarrassed. That student is behind on day one, in a classroom where the teacher is already stretched.

Background and History

The FOX5 S.O.S. drive is not new. It runs each summer through FOX5's Take 5 To Care community program, and the Salvation Army of Southern Nevada is the partner on the receiving end. FOX5 and the Salvation Army announced this year's version on July 7, with drop-offs opening July 10.

The Salvation Army's role is the part people tend to skip past. The organization takes what comes in and assembles it into filled backpacks for valley students. That is why organizers ask for supplies rather than a general "whatever you have" donation. A backpack with nothing in it does not do the job, and a box of loose pencils with no bag does not either.

That distinction matters when you decide how to give. Dropping off physical supplies is direct and tangible. You buy the notebooks, you hand them over, they end up in a backpack. It is also the easiest thing to do if you are already at Target or Walmart during the July sales.

Giving money works differently, and it has its own advantage. A cash donation lets the organization buy in bulk at prices a household cannot get, and it lets them fill whatever is actually short. Every drive ends up with too many of one item and not enough of another. Money fixes that imbalance in a way a shopping cart cannot.

Both are welcome here. The drive publishes a text-to-give number precisely so people who cannot make a drop-off window still have a way in. If you are torn, a reasonable split is to buy the fun stuff your own kid would want, crayons, colored pencils, a decent backpack, and text a donation for the boring gaps.

As for the Cummings family, they did not start any of this with an organization behind them. They started with a birthday party and one backpack. The scale came later, and it came from people who kept showing up.

What Happens Next

The next three days are the ones that count. July 22, 23 and 24 are the live broadcast days, and each site takes donations from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. After that, the drive still has drop-off at Findlay Subaru, but the broadcast push is when the volume comes in.

Then comes August 10. That is the first day of the 2026-27 school year for CCSD, and it also brings the district's new start times into effect. Registration for the year is already open, so if you have moved into a new attendance zone recently, that is worth handling now rather than the week before.

There is a second back-to-school resource landing in the same window, and it is easy to miss. The Southern Nevada Health District is running back-to-school vaccination clinics across the valley ahead of the August 10 start. The clinics focus on students entering kindergarten, seventh grade and 12th grade, which are the grades with required immunization checkpoints in Nevada.

Two details make those clinics worth a look even if your kids are already up to date. Families receive backpacks and school supplies at the clinics while supplies last. Sports physicals are available for $20, which is well under what most urgent care clinics charge for the same form.

Appointments for the SNHD clinics can be made at snhd.info/bts or by calling 702-759-0850. Slots tend to fill in the last two weeks of July, so earlier is better.

If you want to know what to buy for the S.O.S. drive, the safest bets are the items every classroom in the valley burns through. Backpacks, spiral notebooks, composition books, wide-ruled and college-ruled paper, No. 2 pencils, pens, big erasers, crayons, colored pencils, washable markers, glue sticks, safety scissors, pocket folders, pencil pouches, rulers and highlighters.

One caution. That list is a general one, not an official CCSD list. Individual schools publish their own supply lists, and they vary quite a bit by grade level and campus. If you are shopping for your own child rather than for the drive, check your school's list first so you do not buy the wrong ruled paper twice.

Ryan's Take

I have written a lot about Clark County this month. Data center fights, water lawsuits, land selling for $1.75 million an acre. Most of it is about big money and big institutions moving pieces around. This one is a mom who decided her kids' birthdays could do two jobs at once.

What I keep coming back to is the first year. One backpack. That is a genuinely small result, and most people would have quietly dropped the idea and gone back to gift cards. She did not. She ran it again, and again, and now it is the biggest it has been.

That is the whole lesson, and it is not a small one. Community stuff in this valley almost never starts big. It starts with somebody doing a slightly awkward thing at a birthday party and being willing to look a little foolish the first time.

I sell houses here, so I will say the obvious thing once and then stop. The reason people put down roots in a specific neighborhood is rarely the square footage. It is whether the people around them show up. Clark County has a lot of people who show up, and stories like this one are the proof.

If you are reading this on July 20 or 21, you have time. Buy a backpack this week. Fill it with the cheap stuff. Drop it off Wednesday, Thursday or Friday. That is a 30 minute errand that puts a kid in a Clark County classroom on August 10 with the same gear as everyone else in the room.
