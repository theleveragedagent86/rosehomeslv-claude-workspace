Golden Knights Will Travel 50,668 Miles in 2026-27, the Most in Team History

The Vegas Golden Knights will travel 50,668 miles during the 2026-27 season. That is the most in team history and the first time the club has ever passed 50,000 miles in a single year. The same schedule also hands them the fewest back-to-back sets in the entire NHL, tied with Calgary at eight.

So it is a longer trip with better rest. Those two facts pull in opposite directions. That is what makes this worth a closer look.

SinBin.vegas published the breakdown on July 20, 2026. The mileage figure comes from Jason Gregor of Sports 1440, who tracks travel distance across the league. Last season the Golden Knights came in just short of 47,000 miles. The season before that was about 1,000 miles lower still.

The jump is real. Vegas will cover roughly 3,700 more miles than it did a year ago. That works out to an increase of about 8 percent. For a team that flies to every single road game, that is a meaningful add.

But the reason behind the increase is not what most fans would guess. It is not one brutal two-week swing through the East. It is the opposite. Vegas will not face a single road trip longer than four games all season, and that has never happened over a full season in franchise history.

Shorter trips mean more trips. More trips mean more flights home. More flights home mean more miles. The travel burden went up because the schedule got easier to live with, not harder.

What Happened

Here are the numbers, one at a time.

Total travel for the Golden Knights in 2026-27 is 50,668 miles. That is a franchise record and the first time the team has cleared the 50,000 mark. SinBin.vegas credits the figure to Sports 1440's Jason Gregor.

Part of the increase comes from a change to the league itself. For the first time since 1994, NHL teams will play 84 regular season games instead of 82. That is two extra games for every club, which naturally adds some travel.

But the extra games do not explain the whole jump. Last season Vegas covered close to 47,000 miles across 82 games. That averages out to roughly 573 miles per game. At that same rate, 84 games would come to about 48,100 miles.

The actual figure is 50,668. So somewhere around 2,500 miles of the increase comes from how the trips are arranged, not from the two added games. That is a rough estimate, since the prior-year total is reported as just under 47,000 rather than an exact number. Still, the gap is wide enough that the routing is clearly doing most of the work.

And the routing is the good part. Vegas will not play a five-game road trip at any point this season. SinBin.vegas notes this is the first time across a full season that the team will not face a stretch of five or more games away from T-Mobile Arena.

The back-to-back numbers are the other headline. A back-to-back means playing two games on consecutive nights, which is the hardest thing a hockey schedule can ask of a roster. Vegas has eight of them. That ties Calgary for the fewest in the NHL.

Compare that to the other end of the league. Pittsburgh leads the NHL with 15 back-to-back sets. More than half the league will face at least 12. Vegas plays seven fewer than Pittsburgh does, which is close to two full weeks of extra recovery spread across the year.

There is one more wrinkle worth knowing. The Golden Knights will face 10 opponents who are playing the second half of their own back-to-back. Tired teams tend to play worse. That is double the number Toronto and Winnipeg get. Division rival Anaheim leads the way with 18, which is about 21 percent of their schedule.

The calendar itself also shifts. The season opens September 29 with a home game against Chicago, which means Vegas will play games that count in September for the first time ever. The regular season wraps up April 10. There is no Olympic break and no 4 Nations break this year, so the games spread out more evenly than they did the last two seasons.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with the simplest piece. More games means more hockey in this city.

An 84-game season normally splits evenly between home and road. That would put 42 games at T-Mobile Arena instead of the 41 an 82-game season gives you. One extra home date is not a huge change, but it is a change in the right direction for anyone who buys tickets.

The bigger shift is the September start. Golden Knights hockey has always been a fall and winter thing that got going in October. Now the first home game lands on September 29. That puts opening night right in the middle of back-to-school season and football season.

For local families, that matters more than it sounds. The valley calendar in late September is already crowded. Clark County schools are in session, youth sports are underway, and the Strip is busy. Adding an NHL home opener to that week changes how a lot of households plan.

Game nights also shape how the middle of the valley moves. T-Mobile Arena sits right off the Strip, and an arena crowd changes traffic on Las Vegas Boulevard, Frank Sinatra Drive, and the surrounding side streets for a few hours. Anyone who commutes through that corridor already knows the pattern.

This is where the real estate side comes in, and I want to keep it honest. Home games at T-Mobile Arena are a genuine part of the Las Vegas calendar for a lot of local families, the same way Raiders Sundays are. When buyers ask me about neighborhoods near the resort corridor, arena and stadium traffic on event nights is a real thing to factor into a commute. I am not going to tell you a hockey schedule moves home values, because it does not.

What the schedule does affect is the product on the ice. Rest is one of the clearest drivers of performance in hockey. A team that never plays a five-game road trip and only plays eight back-to-backs is a team with fewer stretches where the legs are gone by the second period.

Fans who buy single-game tickets should care about this too. The Golden Knights get 10 games against opponents on the tail end of a back-to-back. Those are the nights where the visiting team is running on fumes. If you are picking a game to attend, those matchups tend to be the better bet.

And the mileage number, while it sounds ugly, is spread across a longer calendar. No Olympic break means no two-week gap followed by a compressed sprint. The travel is heavier but the pacing is calmer.

Background and History

This will be the 10th season of Golden Knights hockey. The team entered the NHL in 2017-18 and became the fastest expansion franchise in modern sports to build a real following here.

Travel has been part of the story from day one. Vegas has no divisional opponent close enough to reach by bus. The Pacific Division stretches from Southern California up through the Pacific Northwest and into Western Canada. Every single road game requires a flight, and every trip east is a long one.

That geography is why Golden Knights travel totals have always run high compared to Eastern Conference clubs. Western teams cover far more ground because the western half of North America is simply bigger and the cities are farther apart.

Long road trips have been a recurring problem. SinBin.vegas points out that in four of the team's first nine seasons, the Golden Knights had to go on the road for at least six straight games. Those trips did not go well. The 2017 version finished 1-4-1.

That is the context that makes the 2026-27 schedule interesting. The league gave Vegas more total miles but took away the thing that actually hurt them, which was the long stretch away from home with no reset in between.

The 84-game season is the other big piece of history here. The NHL has played an 82-game regular season since the mid-1990s. The move to 84 is the first change to the schedule length in more than 30 years, and it applies to every team, not just Vegas.

The last two seasons were unusual for a different reason. Both were interrupted by international breaks, the 4 Nations Face-Off and then the Olympics. Those breaks force the league to squeeze the same number of games into fewer available dates, which is how you end up with piles of back-to-backs. There is no break like that in 2026-27, so the schedule has room to breathe.

As for where the team stands, the Golden Knights are coming off a deep run. Their 2025-26 season ended in June 2026 with a Game 6 loss to Carolina in the Stanley Cup Final.

What Happens Next

The Golden Knights are in the off-season right now. Nothing on the ice changes until camp opens.

The next dates that matter are the bookends of the regular season. Vegas opens at home on September 29 against Chicago. The regular season ends April 10.

There is a small scheduling oddity in there worth mentioning. Vegas plays September 29, then does not play again until October 2 against Anaheim. That October 2 game is Anaheim's season opener. So the Golden Knights season is technically three days longer than the Ducks season, even though both teams finish on the same day.

On the roster side, the team has been busy. On July 15, 2026, the Golden Knights signed first-round pick Juho Piiparinen to a three-year entry-level contract. Piiparinen is 17 and was taken 29th overall in the 2026 NHL Draft.

He will not be part of this travel schedule. Piiparinen spends the 2026-27 season on loan to Tappara in Finland's Liiga, which is a normal development path for a defenseman that young.

The thing to actually watch this season is whether the shorter trips show up in the road record. That is the real test of the tradeoff. If Vegas travels 50,668 miles and still posts a better road record than in past years, the structure of the schedule was worth the extra mileage.

Keep an eye on the back half of the year too. Teams that pile up back-to-backs tend to fade in March and April. Vegas has only eight all season, which should leave them fresher than most of the league heading into the final stretch.

One note of caution on all of this. Schedules do get adjusted. Games move for arena conflicts, weather, and broadcast reasons. The mileage figure is a preseason projection based on the schedule as released, so the final number could shift slightly.

Ryan's Take

If you handed me this schedule and asked me to pick, I would take it every time.

The headline number sounds bad. Fifty thousand miles is a lot of flying, and it is fair for fans to look at that and wince. But mileage by itself is not what wears a team down. Compressed stretches are what wear a team down.

Eight back-to-backs is the fewest in the league. Pittsburgh has 15. More than half the NHL has at least 12. That is a genuine competitive edge spread across seven months, and it is the kind of edge that does not show up until March.

The no-five-game-road-trip thing is the piece I like most. Long trips are where seasons go sideways. You lose two in a row in the East, the mood turns, and suddenly you are limping home 1-4-1 like that 2017 trip. Capping every trip at four games gives this team a reset button it has never had.

I will be honest about the local angle here. A hockey schedule does not change what your house is worth. What it does is add another 42 nights to the Las Vegas calendar, and the arena district is a real part of how this city moves on those nights. That is worth knowing if you are house hunting near the resort corridor.

The September start is the change I think people will notice most. Hockey in this valley has always meant fall. Now it starts before fall really does.

What You Can Do

Mark September 29 on your calendar if you go to games. That is the home opener, and it is earlier than any opener in team history.

If you buy single-game tickets, look for the nights when the visiting team is on the second half of a back-to-back. There are 10 of those on the schedule. Those are usually the games where the other team is dragging.

Plan your commute around home game nights if you drive near the Strip in the evening. Traffic around Las Vegas Boulevard and the arena district gets heavy before puck drop and again when the building empties out. Leaving 20 minutes earlier usually solves it.

If the season gets your kids interested in hockey, the valley has real options. City National Arena in Summerlin and America First Center in Henderson both run youth programs, and Henderson is offering free public skating sessions through November. Those are the two rinks where most local hockey families end up.

For schedule details, go straight to the team. Game times and dates get updated there first, and third-party listings are not always current.

And if you are house hunting in the southwest valley, Summerlin, or Henderson and want to understand how commute patterns and event traffic actually work in each area, that is a normal part of the conversation I have with buyers. It is worth asking about before you pick a neighborhood.
