Vegas Jr. Golden Knights Fall House League Registration Is Open at Both Valley Rinks

Registration is open for the Vegas Jr. Golden Knights Fall 2026-27 House League. Families can sign up at City National Arena in Summerlin or at America First Center in Henderson. Both rinks are taking players for the coming season right now.

That matters because house league is the front door to hockey in Clark County. It is the recreational level. Kids play on local teams, practice at their home rink, and play most of their games right here in the valley. It costs less than travel hockey and asks less of your weekends.

For a lot of Las Vegas families, this is the first real decision point. Your kid liked skating. Now what? House league is usually the answer, and fall is when the season fills up.

Here is the honest part. The specific registration deadlines, age brackets, and fees are not published on the pages we could pull up. So this article will not quote you a price or a cutoff date. Instead it will explain what house league actually is, how a beginner gets there, and how the two rinks compare. Then it will point you to the sign-up pages so you can get the current numbers straight from the source.

If you are new to hockey, or new to Las Vegas, or both, this is the guide I wish somebody had handed my clients years ago.

Registration pages for the Vegas Jr. Golden Knights Fall House League are live at both of the valley's main ice facilities as of July 2026. America First Center in Henderson hosts its sign-up page at americafirstcenter.com/fallhouseleague. That page is labeled for the 2026-27 season.

City National Arena in Summerlin also runs a fall house league page at citynationalarena.com/fallhouseleague. One note of caution here. What is not published is the part every parent wants first. No fee schedule. No age group chart. No registration deadline. That information may sit behind a login or a registration portal, or it may be released closer to the season. Either way, we are not going to guess at it.

So do not budget off a number you read in a blog post, including this one. Open the registration page, or call the rink. Ask three questions. What does the season cost, what age groups are open, and when does registration close?

The other thing worth knowing is that the season label itself tells you the calendar. A 2026-27 house league season generally starts in the fall and runs into the following year. One more piece of context. Youth hockey in this valley is not a quiet, empty-rink activity. Keep that number in your head. It is going to be useful in a minute when we talk about what house league is actually for.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with the thing that confuses almost every new hockey parent. There is house league, and there is travel hockey, and they are not the same sport in practice.

House league is recreational. Teams are built from players who signed up at that rink. Most games are played against the other teams in the same league, at the same building or the sister building across town. Development and fun are the point. Playing time is generally more balanced. Costs are lower.

Travel hockey is the competitive track. Teams are picked through tryouts. They play other clubs, often in other states. Levels run from A and B up through AA and AAA. Families fly, drive, book hotels, and eat tournament weekends for months.

That is where the $2,000 to $2,450 Summer Showdown entry fee becomes useful. That number is just the team's entry into one tournament. It does not include travel, hotels, food, ice time, coaching, or gear. Travel families do several of those a year. House league families do not.

This is the real reason the distinction matters. A new parent hears "youth hockey" and pictures the expensive version. Then they never sign up. House league exists precisely so a Clark County kid can play organized hockey without the family committing to a national travel schedule.

The second reason this matters here is ice. Las Vegas is a desert with very limited sheets of ice. Two facilities carry most of the valley's organized youth hockey, and both are NHL practice buildings first. That means the ice is spoken for by pro teams, adult leagues, figure skating, public sessions, camps, and tournaments.

The practical result is scheduling. Youth practices in this valley often land early in the morning or late in the evening. That is not a complaint about the rinks. It is just arithmetic. When demand is high and sheets are few, the leftover slots are early and late.

Which brings us to the part that is genuinely a real estate question. If your kid plays, you are going to drive to a rink several times a week, often before sunrise or after dinner. Where you live changes that drive from fifteen minutes to fifty. Over a season, that is not a small thing.

City National Arena sits in Summerlin, on the west side. It is the Vegas Golden Knights practice facility. If you live in Summerlin, Centennial Hills, the northwest, or out toward Lone Mountain, that is your rink.

America First Center sits at 222 S. Water St. in Henderson, in the Water Street District. It is the practice home of the Henderson Silver Knights. If you live in Green Valley, Anthem, Inspirada, Seven Hills, or Boulder City, that is your rink.

A family in Anthem driving to Summerlin for a 5:30 a.m. practice is making a very different decision than a family in Summerlin making the same drive. Pick the rink that matches your zip code if you can.

Background and History

The Vegas Jr. Golden Knights program was created in 2017. According to the program's own site, the Vegas Golden Knights announced on April 4 that the team had formed a partnership with an existing local club, the Nevada Storm, to create a new youth hockey program under the Jr. Golden Knights name.

All existing Nevada Storm teams were rebranded. The program launched with teams from the mite level through midget. At the time, it used three city rinks, including the Golden Knights practice facility, the Las Vegas Ice Center, and the SoBe Ice Arena.

That is worth sitting with. Organized youth hockey existed in Las Vegas before the NHL arrived. The Nevada Storm was already here. What the Golden Knights did was attach a professional brand, a practice facility, and real visibility to a program that local families had already been building.

City National Arena opened as the Golden Knights practice facility in Summerlin. America First Center in Henderson came later and became the practice home of the Henderson Silver Knights, the Golden Knights American Hockey League affiliate. Two NHL-affiliated buildings in one desert metro is not normal, and it changed what is possible for kids here.

Now here is the pathway a Clark County kid usually follows, in order.

First comes Learn to Skate. Before anything else, a kid has to be able to stand up, glide, stop, and get back up after falling. No sticks. No pucks. Just skating.

Next comes Learn to Play. This is the introduction to hockey itself. Full gear, a stick, basic puck handling, and the rules. City National Arena lists Learn to Play programs on its current program menu.

One important note. Many Learn to Play programs around the country include a starter gear set as part of the fee. We could not confirm whether the City National Arena program includes gear, so do not assume it does. Ask the rink directly before you buy anything. [NOT VERIFIED]

After that comes cross-ice. The Lil' Knights Cross-Ice Program at City National Arena plays games sideways across the rink on a smaller surface. Small kids touch the puck more, skate more, and are not chasing play 200 feet down a sheet built for adults. It is how young players actually develop.

Then comes house league. Full teams, a real season, a schedule, a jersey. This is where most Las Vegas kids land and where many happily stay.

Travel hockey comes last, and only if the kid wants it. Plenty of good players never go. That is fine.

What Happens Next

The immediate next step for families is simple. Go to the registration pages and get the details we could not verify. That means the fee, the age groups, and the deadline.

The America First Center fall house league page is at americafirstcenter.com/fallhouseleague. The City National Arena programs live at citynationalarena.com. Those two links are the source of truth, not a news article and not a Facebook group.

While you are on the phone, ask about evaluations. Some house leagues hold skills sessions to balance teams. Whether the Vegas Jr. Golden Knights fall house league does that, and when, is not something we could confirm. [NOT VERIFIED]

Also ask what gear is required on day one. Hockey has a real equipment list. A beginner generally needs skates, a helmet with a cage, gloves, shin guards, elbow pads, shoulder pads, padded pants, a neck guard, a mouthguard, a protective cup or pelvic protector, socks, a jersey, and a stick. Goalies are a separate and more expensive conversation.

That list looks intimidating written out. It is less intimidating in practice, because used gear is common, kids outgrow everything, and rinks and local hockey families trade equipment constantly. Ask about used gear before you buy new.

If you are not sure your kid even likes ice yet, there is a genuinely free way to find out first. The City of Henderson is running free ice skating sessions at America First Center at 222 S. Water St. The program includes 10 sessions from June through November 2026. Afternoon and evening slots are available, and skate rentals are free on event dates.

Space is limited. Registration opens roughly three weeks before each session at HendersonHappenings.com. That is a zero-dollar test of whether your kid wants any of this before you spend a cent on a season or a helmet.

Use it in that order. Free skate first. Learn to Skate second. Then Learn to Play. Then decide about house league.

Ryan's Take

I sell real estate in this valley, so I will tell you the part most hockey articles skip. Drive time to the rink is a legitimate factor when a hockey family picks a Clark County neighborhood. Not the only factor. But a real one.

Here is why. Youth ice time in Las Vegas lands early and late because there are only so many sheets. A 6:00 a.m. practice and a 9:00 p.m. game are normal. If you are doing that two or three times a week for a season, a thirty-five minute drive each way stops being an inconvenience and starts shaping your family's life.

So when a client tells me their kid plays hockey, I ask which rink before I ask anything else. If the answer is City National Arena, we look hard at Summerlin, Summerlin West, the northwest, and Centennial Hills. If the answer is America First Center, we look at Green Valley, Anthem, Seven Hills, Inspirada, and the Henderson core.

The valley is big and the freeways are honest about it. Summerlin to Henderson at 5:30 in the morning is doable. Doing it every week for eight months is a different question.

I would also say this to families moving here from a hockey market. Do not assume Las Vegas is a hockey dead zone. There are two NHL-affiliated practice facilities here, an established youth program that predates the Golden Knights, and tournaments that fill both buildings in the middle of July. The ice is limited. The community is not.

And if you are a parent on the fence about the cost, look at that Summer Showdown entry fee again. That is the competitive end of the sport. House league exists so your kid does not have to start there.

What You Can Do

If you want your kid on the ice this fall, work the list in order.

First, pick your rink by geography. Open a map. Time the drive from your house to City National Arena in Summerlin and to America First Center at 222 S. Water St. in Henderson. Pick the shorter one unless you have a strong reason not to.

Second, register through the official pages. Go to americafirstcenter.com/fallhouseleague for the Henderson league and citynationalarena.com for the Summerlin programs. Get the fee, the age groups, and the deadline in writing before you commit.

Third, match the program to your kid's actual level. Never skated? Learn to Skate. Can skate but has never played? Learn to Play. Young and ready for games? The Lil' Knights cross-ice program. Ready for a full team and a season? House league.

Fourth, test it for free first if you have any doubt. Watch HendersonHappenings.com for the free skating sessions at America First Center, which run through November 2026. Registration opens about three weeks before each one and space is limited.

Fifth, ask about gear before you shop. Ask what the program provides, what you must supply, and whether there is a used gear swap. Do not buy a full new setup for a kid who has been on ice twice.
