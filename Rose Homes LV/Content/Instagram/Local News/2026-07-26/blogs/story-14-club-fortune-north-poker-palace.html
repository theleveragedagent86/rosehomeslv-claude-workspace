Former Poker Palace Reopens as Club Fortune North After a $2.8 Million Makeover

The old Poker Palace in North Las Vegas is back, and it goes by a brand new name. The longtime locals casino reopened on Wednesday, July 22, 2026, as Club Fortune North after a $2.8 million remodel by Truckee Gaming. The made-over property also added the 14th Nevada location of Boomer's Sportsbook, so people in the area can now bet on games and horse races just minutes from home.

This is about more than fresh paint and new carpet. A company that runs neighborhood casinos looked at North Las Vegas, saw a part of the valley that keeps growing, and decided to invest millions of dollars right in the heart of it. For homeowners and renters nearby, that is a real vote of confidence. When a business spends that kind of money fixing up an aging building, it usually believes the area around it is going to keep getting better.

The reopening also fits a bigger story in North Las Vegas. City leaders have been working to draw fresh investment into older parts of town, and this project got a boost from a city grant aimed at doing exactly that. Here is what happened, and why it matters for your neighborhood.

What Happened at the Old Poker Palace

Truckee Gaming bought the longtime Poker Palace, closed it down, and put $2.8 million into a full renovation. The company held a ribbon-cutting ceremony on Wednesday, July 22, 2026, to show off the finished product. The makeover was not small. Crews added new LED perimeter lighting, put up updated signage, refreshed the landscaping outside, and completely redesigned the casino floor inside.

Ferenc Szony, the CEO of Truckee Gaming, said his company saw North Las Vegas as an area of growth and redevelopment. That is what drew the company to the site when it went looking for a second Nevada market beyond its Henderson location.

Szony did not sugarcoat what the old building used to look like. "We took what was the old Poker Palace that kind of looked like Excalibur and Circus Circus maybe got together and had a baby," he said. "But we said, listen, we're going to take that and create something brand new. And we hope that the community feels that what we've done here at Club Fortune is kind of the new vision of where north Las Vegas is going in the future."

He admitted the project took longer and cost more than the company first expected. Even so, he said it was worth it. Truckee Gaming builds its whole business around locals, not tourists. "All of our operations are locals' casinos," Szony said. "We don't do hotels, we don't do tourists. We count on people that are going to be with us two or three times a week."

The big new feature is the sportsbook. Boomer's Sportsbook, described as Nevada's only independent sports betting operator, opened inside Club Fortune North on July 22. It is the company's 14th sportsbook in the state, and Boomer's also runs 18 deposit kiosk locations across Nevada.

Joe Asher, the president and CEO of Boomer's Sportsbook, said the new spot offers both sports wagering and pari-mutuel horse race betting. Customers can place bets at the retail counter, at kiosks on the property, or through the Boomer's mobile app. That app can be set up in the store in about two minutes and then used anywhere in Nevada. "Having a Boomer's Sportsbook inside this terrific new casino is something we are very excited about," Asher said.

Cory Uyesono, the general manager of Club Fortune North, said sports betting is a key part of the experience. "We know our players are going to enjoy having a nice, new sportsbook to enjoy betting on their favorite games and horse races," he said. City leaders showed up too. North Las Vegas officials said the project got support through the city's facade commercial program grant, which targets properties in the downtown redevelopment zone.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

So why should a casino remodel matter to people who may never place a bet? Because a locals casino is really a neighborhood business. It is where people grab a cheap meal, watch a game, meet friends, and celebrate a birthday. When one of these places sits empty or runs down, the blocks around it feel it. When one reopens fresh and busy, the whole area gets a lift.

Start with jobs. A renovated casino with a full floor and a new sportsbook needs dealers, cooks, servers, cashiers, cleaners, security, and managers. Those are steady paychecks that stay in North Las Vegas. Workers who live nearby can walk or make a short drive to a job, and they spend their money at other local shops. That kind of everyday spending keeps a neighborhood economy healthy.

Then there is the message a project like this sends. Truckee Gaming did not have to pick North Las Vegas. The company chose it on purpose because leadership believes the area is on the way up. When a business bets $2.8 million on a location, it is telling everyone else that the neighborhood has a future. Other owners, from restaurants to strip malls, watch those signals closely.

For homeowners, that confidence can matter over time. Property values are shaped by more than the house itself. Buyers care about what surrounds a home. Clean, active, well-run businesses nearby make an area more appealing. A boarded-up building does the opposite. Turning an aging casino into a bright, modern one removes an eyesore and replaces it with something people actually want to visit.

The new sportsbook adds real convenience too. Nevada residents love betting on sports and horse races, and now folks in this part of town have a spot close by. The Boomer's mobile app means a quick in-store signup lets someone bet from anywhere in the state. For fans of the Golden Knights, the Raiders, the Aces, or college teams, that is a handy perk that did not exist here before.

Renters benefit as well. A livelier commercial corridor often brings better lighting, more foot traffic, and a stronger sense that someone cares about the area. Those things make a place feel safer and more connected. People want to live where the neighborhood is clearly moving forward, not standing still. That feeling shows up in how quickly homes rent and sell.

There is also the community angle. Locals casinos become gathering spots, a kind of living room for the neighborhood. Szony said his company counts on regulars who come two or three times a week. That steady rhythm builds the familiar faces and routines that make a place feel like home. In a fast-growing area where many people are new, that sense of belonging matters more than you might think.

There is a money angle for the whole valley, too. Casinos in Nevada pay gaming taxes, and that revenue helps fund public services that residents count on every day. A property that sits closed collects dust and pays little. A property that reopens busy starts contributing again. It is a small piece of a very large budget, but every active business in North Las Vegas adds to the pool that supports roads, parks, and schools across the community. That is money working close to home instead of leaking out of the area.

Background and History of the Site

To understand the moment, it helps to know the backstory. The Poker Palace was a longtime fixture in North Las Vegas, one of the older neighborhood gaming spots in the area. For decades it served locals who wanted a low-key place to play, not a giant resort on the Strip. Over the years, though, the building showed its age. Szony's joke about it looking like Excalibur and Circus Circus "had a baby" says a lot about the dated design he inherited.

Truckee Gaming is not new to the locals casino business. The company already runs Club Fortune Casino in Henderson, and that Henderson property is where its Nevada story started. When leadership wanted to grow into a second market, the team studied the valley and landed on North Las Vegas. The new spot even carries the Club Fortune name, marking it as a sister property to the Henderson original.

The choice fits a larger trend. North Las Vegas has been one of the fastest-changing parts of the Las Vegas Valley for years. Master-planned communities, new schools, and fresh retail have spread across the northern edge of the region. City leaders have pushed hard to attract jobs and investment, and much of that energy has focused on the older core near the city's downtown.

That older core is exactly where this project sits. Club Fortune North is inside the city's downtown redevelopment zone. Cities set up zones like this to steer help toward areas that need a spark. One tool is a facade commercial program grant, which shares the cost of sprucing up the outside of a building. North Las Vegas partnered with Truckee Gaming on that kind of grant for this remodel. Mayor Pamela Goynes-Brown framed it as part of a broader plan. "The new, renovated, reimagined Club Fortune is in our downtown redevelopment zone," she said. "Partnering with them on our facade commercial program grant to revitalize this area, to bring more economic development, more economic growth speaks volumes for North Las Vegas."

What Happens Next

So what comes next? The first thing to watch is whether the locals model works here. Truckee Gaming is banking on regulars who show up two or three times a week. If those crowds appear, the company will have proof that North Las Vegas can support a modern neighborhood casino. If they do, expect the property to keep adding events, promotions, and reasons to visit.

Keep an eye on hiring, too. A busy casino and sportsbook create a steady need for workers. People in the area who want a job close to home may find openings as the property settles into its routine. Word of mouth from early hires often shapes how a place is seen in the community, so the first few months set the tone.

The bigger thing to watch is the ripple effect. City leaders clearly hope this project sparks more investment in the downtown redevelopment zone. If Club Fortune North draws steady traffic, nearby parcels could look more attractive to other owners and builders. A single success can make a lender or a developer take a second look at the blocks around it.

The facade grant program is worth following as well. If the partnership here is seen as a win, the city may point to it when courting other businesses. That could mean more storefronts getting facelifts and more older buildings finding new life. Watch the calendar too, because football season, hockey season, and the busy sports betting stretch are all coming. A new sportsbook that opens in the summer has time to build a following before the fall rush.

Food and entertainment are worth watching as well. Locals casinos often win regulars with strong dining deals, bar specials, and live events, not just the gaming floor. If Club Fortune North rolls out popular food options and a lively calendar, it can become a true neighborhood hangout rather than a place people simply pass by. Those little touches often decide whether a remodel turns into a lasting community anchor or fades once the opening buzz wears off. The next several months will show which way this one goes.
