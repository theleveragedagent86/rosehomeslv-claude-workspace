Golden Knights Games to Watch in the 2026-27 Season

The Vegas Golden Knights will play 84 games this season, and the hockey site SinBin.vegas just circled the five you should not miss. Writer Ken Boehlke picked the must-watch nights, and the list runs from Mitch Marner's return to Toronto on December 12 to a wild outdoor Stadium Series game in Dallas on February 20.

For the first time since the NHL arrived in Las Vegas, there are 84 games on the schedule instead of 82. That means two extra nights of hockey, some at T-Mobile Arena on the Strip and some on the road. Most of those games will blur together across a long winter. A handful carry real weight, though, because of a rivalry, a returning star, or a rematch with a team the Golden Knights knocked out last spring.

Boehlke's list is built for fans who want to plan ahead. It tells you which dates to request off work, which nights to grab tickets, and which games to build a watch party around. Two of the five land in a busy stretch in mid-December, so if you only pick a couple, that is where to look first.

Here is a closer look at the five games Vegas fans should mark on the calendar, why each one matters, and what all of this hockey means for daily life in the valley. Whether you live in Summerlin, Henderson, or right off the Strip, the Golden Knights have become part of the rhythm of the year in Southern Nevada.

The Five Games SinBin.vegas Says to Circle

The season opens with a home date against the Chicago Blackhawks, a game Boehlke expects Vegas to win handily. The real fun starts right after. Here are the five games he flagged as the most intriguing on the 84-game slate.

December 12 at the Toronto Maple Leafs. Every time Mitch Marner goes back to Toronto, it is an event. He spent years as a star for the Maple Leafs before joining Vegas, so the crowd there will be loud and emotional. This time Marner returns fresh off a Conn Smythe-worthy playoff run, the kind of performance that quiets doubters. Boehlke expects a looser, sharper Marner and a Golden Knights team that should be the stronger side. As a bonus, former Golden Knight Colton Sissons now plays for Toronto, so there is a second familiar face on the ice.

December 16 vs the San Jose Sharks. Four nights later, Vegas hosts San Jose at T-Mobile Arena. The Sharks are rebuilding, and their young roster keeps getting better. Boehlke makes an interesting point. San Jose's comeback will not feel real until they beat the Golden Knights. Vegas has spent years beating up on the Sharks, and if that flips, it will sting. If both teams sit near the top of the Pacific Division by then, this one could feel much bigger than a normal December game.

January 2 and January 4 vs the Colorado Avalanche. The schedule serves up a rare pair of games against Colorado just two nights apart. The first is January 2 at home in Las Vegas, a rematch of the Western Conference Final that Vegas won in convincing fashion. The second is January 4 in Colorado. Boehlke says the back end is the bigger one. If one team wins both, it sends a message to the entire conference.

October 2 vs the Anaheim Ducks. This is the second game of the season and, in Boehlke's words, the real beginning of it. Vegas knocked Anaheim out in the second round of the playoffs, and the Ducks have added talent, including Leo Carlsson, now the highest-paid player in the league. It is an early test against a team on the rise in the Pacific, and the kind of game that can set a tone.

February 20 at the Dallas Stars, Stadium Series. This is the showcase. The NHL will try to break its all-time single-game attendance record, with more than 100,000 fans packed into a football stadium for an outdoor game. Add tailgating, brand new jerseys, and two Cup contenders, and it is about as big as a regular season night gets.

Add them up and the heart of the schedule is that mid-December double. Toronto on December 12 and San Jose on December 16 sit just four days apart, so fans get a marquee road game and a marquee home game in the same week. The regular season then closes on home ice with a visit from the LA Kings in game 84, a fitting Pacific Division bookend to a long winter of hockey.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Golden Knights hockey is not just a sports story here. It shapes traffic, nightlife, and the calendar for tens of thousands of families across Clark County. When there is a marquee game at T-Mobile Arena, the whole south Strip feels it. Rideshare prices climb, restaurants fill up, and the pedestrian bridges around the arena get packed hours before puck drop.

The mid-December stretch is a good example. The San Jose home game on December 16 lands in the middle of the holiday season, when the Strip is already busy with visitors and locals out shopping and celebrating. If you live in Spring Valley, Enterprise, or the neighborhoods near Mandalay Bay, planning around that night can save you a long sit in traffic.

Parking and timing are the two things worth thinking about. T-Mobile Arena sits behind the Park district between New York-New York and Park MGM, so the garages and walkways there get crowded on game nights. Locals who know the drill park a little farther out and walk in, or they lean on the Strip's tram and monorail options. If you rent out a room or a condo near the Strip, marquee games and the holiday crowd can also mean stronger short-term demand.

The road games matter too, just in a different way. Marner's return to Toronto on December 12 will be a national broadcast, so it is a stay-home-and-watch night for a lot of fans. Sports bars from Henderson to Centennial Hills build their evening around games like that. The Stadium Series in Dallas on February 20 is the kind of event that has fans booking flights and hotels, and it pulls Vegas visitors the other direction for a change.

There is also a youth angle. Every big Golden Knights night sends more kids to the local rinks. City National Arena in Downtown Summerlin, the team's practice facility, has helped grow youth and adult leagues across the valley. Lee's Family Forum in Henderson gives fans a second pro team to follow in the Henderson Silver Knights, the Golden Knights' minor league affiliate. Games like these keep the pipeline of new young players full.

For anyone who moved here in the last few years, this is part of what makes Las Vegas feel like a real hometown and not just a tourist stop. A shared schedule, a home rink, and a few circled dates give neighbors something to talk about at the grocery store and the dog park.

Background and History

The Golden Knights arrived in 2017 as an expansion team and shocked the sport by reaching the Stanley Cup Final in their very first season. They built a loyal following fast. In 2023 they finished the job and won the franchise's first Stanley Cup, and the celebration at T-Mobile Arena and along the Strip is still fresh in a lot of memories.

That run to the Final in 2018 turned casual fans into diehards almost overnight, and the 2023 championship cemented the team as a fixture. Owner Bill Foley has kept the roster in win-now mode ever since, which is a big reason the Golden Knights keep landing stars like Marner. T-Mobile Arena, which opened on the Strip in 2016, has become the loud, sold-out backdrop for all of it.

Since then, Vegas has stayed a contender year after year. The reference to Marner's Conn Smythe-worthy run points to another deep spring for the team, and the Western Conference Final rematch with Colorado shows how far they went. That history is exactly why these circled games carry weight. When you have beaten a team in the playoffs, the next regular season meeting has an edge.

The San Jose rivalry goes back further. Boehlke points to 2019, when the two teams played one of the most heated playoff series in recent memory. It never fully cooled off. Even with the Sharks rebuilding, that history is why a December game against a young San Jose roster still gets attention.

The 84-game season is new. The league added two games to the schedule for the first time since Vegas joined, which is why the Golden Knights now have quirks like back-to-back meetings with Colorado. More games mean more chances for these storylines to build, and more nights of hockey for a city that has fallen hard for the sport in less than a decade.

All of that success has a side effect. Tickets are harder to get, and marquee games sell out well in advance. That is part of why a list like this one matters. Knowing which nights are special helps fans plan and budget before the good seats are gone.

What Happens Next

With the full 84-game schedule out, the next step for fans is simple. Pick your games and get your tickets. Single-game seats for the biggest nights, like the San Jose home game and any playoff rematch, tend to move fast. If you want to be in the building for one of the five, the earlier you plan, the better.

The season itself will answer the questions these games raise. Boehlke points to Thanksgiving as an early checkpoint. If the Golden Knights and Sharks are both near the top of the Pacific Division by late November, the December 16 meeting grows in size. The same goes for the Colorado back-to-back in early January. The standings will decide how much juice those nights really have.

The Stadium Series in Dallas on February 20 is its own project. Fans who want to make the trip will be watching for ticket releases, jersey reveals, and travel deals. An outdoor game with a shot at an attendance record is the sort of bucket-list event that people plan months ahead, and plenty of Vegas fans will be in that crowd.

Fans who cannot get enough hockey have a second option all winter. The Henderson Silver Knights play their American Hockey League season at Lee's Family Forum, often on nights when the Golden Knights are on the road. It is a cheaper ticket, a shorter drive for East Valley families, and a chance to watch the prospects who may end up on the big club.

Between now and opening night, expect the team to finalize its roster, settle any contract questions, and roll out its schedule of promotions and giveaways. For fans, the winter ahead is set. The dates are circled, and the countdown to that first home game against Chicago is on.

Ryan's Take

I have watched hockey change this city since 2017, and not just on game nights. When the Golden Knights show up on the schedule, they show up in the way people talk about their neighborhoods. Buyers ask me about the drive to T-Mobile Arena. Families in Summerlin mention that City National Arena is a few minutes away and that their kids skate there. Hockey has quietly become a selling point.

A packed sports calendar is good for Las Vegas in a broad way. It keeps hotels full, supports restaurants and bars in every corner of the valley, and gives people one more reason to plant roots here instead of just visiting. A marquee night like the December 16 game against San Jose, or a road event like the Dallas Stadium Series, pulls energy and money through the local economy. When national events put Vegas hockey on the map, it reminds the rest of the country that this is a real sports town and not just a place to visit.

My honest take on Boehlke's list is that he nailed the two December games. Marner's return to Toronto and the San Jose home date are the ones I would circle first. They are the games that turn a normal week in Las Vegas into an event, and that kind of energy is part of what makes living here fun.

What You Can Do

If you want to catch one of these five games in person, start with the Golden Knights ticket page and set an alert for the dates you care about. The San Jose home game on December 16 and the Anaheim game on October 2 are your two home shots from this list, so those are the ones to target if you would rather be at T-Mobile Arena than on your couch.

For the road games, find your spot early. Marner's Toronto return on December 12 and the Dallas Stadium Series on February 20 will draw big crowds at local sports bars, so call ahead or arrive early on those nights. If you are thinking about traveling to the Stadium Series, watch for ticket and hotel releases in the weeks ahead.

You can also get closer to the team for free. City National Arena in Downtown Summerlin often opens practices to the public, which is a great, low-cost outing for families and new fans. And if you want the sharpest coverage of the schedule and roster all season long, SinBin.vegas is a good place to follow.

If the games spark interest at home, this is a good time to get kids into the sport. Local rinks run learn-to-skate and youth hockey programs through the fall and winter, and the Golden Knights Foundation has long supported youth sports across the valley. For the marquee nights, pick a home base early. Neighborhood sports bars in Henderson, Summerlin, and the southwest fill up fast for a Marner return or a Stadium Series broadcast, so a quick call ahead goes a long way.
