Perez Hilton Lists His Las Vegas Home for $4.25M and Moves to Miami

Celebrity gossip columnist Perez Hilton has put his Las Vegas home up for sale at $4.25 million and moved back to Miami. The 6,569-square-foot house sits in the Blue Heron community of Prado, and it hit the market on June 24, 2026.

The listing gives locals a rare peek inside a custom celebrity estate in one of the valley's most design-forward neighborhoods. It also says something bigger about who is buying, selling, and moving around the Las Vegas luxury market right now.

What Happened

Perez Hilton, whose real name is Mario Armando Lavandeira Jr., listed his Las Vegas home for $4.25 million and decamped for Miami. The property is located at 7389 Olive Creek Court, on a private corner of the Blue Heron neighborhood called Prado. The listing is held by agent Kathryn Winterton of Las Vegas Sotheby's International Realty.

The home is 6,569 square feet. It sits on about half an acre. It has six bedrooms and seven bathrooms, and every bedroom is en suite, meaning each one has its own private bathroom. The primary suite is downstairs and comes with a large bathroom and closet areas. Hilton said his shower alone was a highlight. "I loved my bathroom," he told the Las Vegas Review-Journal. "It was the biggest shower I've ever owned, a two-person shower with an overhead spout. It was just magical."

Hilton was clear about why he is leaving. "I had a lot of health issues this year, and that was really hard for me and my mother," he told the Review-Journal. "I just felt this calling that we needed to be back where I was born and raised and be around family." He grew up in Miami, left at 18, and settled in Las Vegas back in January 2023. Now he is going home.

The house is not a resale flip. Hilton built it himself, so the finishes are all custom. "I built it, so it was brand new, so it's all done to my likings and customizations," he said. That kind of one-owner, built-to-order pedigree is a real selling point at this price level, and it is a big part of the story here.

The outdoor space is a headline feature. The property has a pool, a spa, and large outdoor living areas that Hilton said rank among the biggest in the whole development. That matters in the desert, where a private backyard oasis is a year-round luxury. The listing leans hard on this indoor-outdoor connection, with floor-to-ceiling windows that pull the desert light inside and blur the line between the living room and the yard. For a home built for entertaining, that flow is the whole point.

Hilton also praised the home's flexibility for hosting. "It's the perfect home for a family, especially one that loves to have guests, because there's also a casita, so your guests could have privacy and everybody could sleep in and not wake each other," he said. Between the casita, the NextGen suite, and seven bathrooms, the house was clearly designed to hold a crowd without anyone feeling on top of each other. That is a rare and valuable trait in any price range.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might wonder why a celebrity's home sale matters to a regular Las Vegas homeowner. It matters because the top of the market is a signal. When a well-known owner lists a custom home at $4.25 million, buyers, sellers, and appraisers all pay attention to what happens next. How long it sits, and what it finally sells for, helps set the tone for luxury pricing across the valley.

Blue Heron is a local builder, not a national one. The company is known for modern, indoor-outdoor architecture that feels different from the stucco tract homes most of the valley is built from. When one of their signature homes goes on the market at this level, it puts a spotlight on a style that has become a calling card for high-end Las Vegas living. That is good exposure for the whole region.

There is also a lesson in the home's layout for regular families. This house has one of Blue Heron's "signature" NextGen Suites, which is private living quarters with a separate entrance. Hilton's mother lived there. On top of that, the property has an attached casita for guests. Multigenerational living is a growing trend in Clark County, where families are pooling resources to afford a home and to care for older parents. This listing shows how that can look at the luxury end.

Finally, it is a reminder that people move for personal reasons, not just market reasons. Hilton is not leaving because of prices or taxes. He is leaving for family and health. Real estate is emotional, and even at the very top, life events drive the biggest decisions. Job changes, aging parents, health scares, and the pull of hometown roots move far more homes in Clark County than interest rate charts ever will.

There is a marketing angle here too that local sellers can learn from. This home has been on national television. It was the backdrop for interviews and documentaries, which gives it a story most listings will never have. Homes with a good story tend to attract more attention, more showings, and sometimes a higher price. You do not need to be a celebrity to use this idea. A well-told listing that highlights what makes your home special, the light, the layout, the way it lives, can set you apart in a crowded market.

Background and History

Hilton became famous in the mid-2000s for his celebrity gossip blog, which drew millions of readers and helped invent the modern online tabloid. Over the years he built a media brand that spans blogging, podcasts, and television. His Las Vegas home became part of that brand. It served as the backdrop for many of his TV interviews and documentary appearances, including coverage of the Blake Lively and Justin Baldoni legal drama.

He moved to Las Vegas in January 2023 and built this home from the ground up. The design reflects what buyers at the top of the market want today. The listing describes "striking contemporary architecture" with floor-to-ceiling windows and a true indoor-outdoor flow. "This home was designed for the way people want to live and entertain today," Winterton said. "The natural light, open spaces and seamless connection to the backyard create an atmosphere that feels both sophisticated and welcoming."

Blue Heron has spent years building its reputation in Southern Nevada. The company has designed some of the most talked-about custom homes in Henderson and Las Vegas, and it even built homes for HGTV's "Rock the Block" competition. Prado is one of its more secluded enclaves, which is exactly why it appealed to a public figure who wanted privacy. "It's a lovely Blue Heron home in a very secluded community," Hilton said.

Las Vegas has quietly become a magnet for celebrities and high earners over the past decade. No state income tax, big custom homes, and easy travel access all help. Hilton's arrival in 2023 fit that pattern, and his departure in 2026 shows the other side of it. People come and go, but the homes they build stay and reshape the local luxury inventory.

It is worth remembering how young the Las Vegas luxury market really is compared to places like Los Angeles or Miami. Much of the valley's high-end housing was built in the last twenty years, which means buyers here can find modern, move-in-ready custom homes that would be nearly impossible to build from scratch in older coastal cities. That is part of what draws wealth to Southern Nevada. Hilton's home is a textbook example, a from-the-studs custom build with current finishes and a layout designed around how people actually live now.

The listing agent, Kathryn Winterton of Las Vegas Sotheby's International Realty, works in exactly this corner of the market. Sotheby's is a global brand known for high-end and unique properties, and pairing a recognizable listing with a luxury brokerage is a smart match. It shows how the top tier of Las Vegas real estate has grown up. The valley now has the agents, the marketing reach, and the inventory to compete for buyers who could shop anywhere in the country.

What Happens Next

The next chapter belongs to the buyer. At $4.25 million, this home is aimed at a narrow slice of the market. Buyers at this level are often paying cash, relocating from higher-cost states, or looking for a custom home they do not have to wait two years to build. A ready-made Blue Heron home with a pool, spa, casita, and NextGen suite checks a lot of those boxes.

Watch how long the listing stays active and whether the price holds. Luxury homes tend to sit on the market longer than starter homes because the pool of qualified buyers is small. A quick sale near asking would signal strong demand at the high end. A price cut or a long wait would tell a different story about where luxury pricing sits in mid-2026. Either way, this listing will be a useful data point for anyone tracking the top of the Las Vegas market.

It is also worth watching the broader trend. If more high-profile owners list custom homes as inventory rises across the valley, buyers at every price point could gain a little more leverage. One celebrity move does not make a market, but it is part of a pattern worth following through the rest of the year.

For the neighborhood itself, a sale like this can set a fresh comp. When a well-known custom home closes, agents and appraisers use that number to help value nearby homes. That means Hilton's sale price, whatever it turns out to be, could ripple through the rest of Prado and the wider Blue Heron community. Neighbors who are thinking about selling in the next year should keep an eye on it, because it may quietly shape what their own home is worth.

Ryan's Take

Here is what stands out to me as a local agent. This is not a story about a struggling market. It is a story about a homeowner making a personal choice, and it happens to land on a beautiful custom property. Hilton built this home for himself, poured his taste into it, and is now handing it off to what he called "a very lucky next owner." That is how the luxury tier works. These homes are personal, and they trade based on lifestyle just as much as square footage.

The features he leaned on, the casita, the NextGen suite, the huge outdoor living space, are the same features I see regular Vegas buyers asking about more and more. Multigenerational space and real indoor-outdoor living are not just celebrity perks anymore. They are practical answers to affordability and family needs. A home that lets your parents or your adult kids live close but keep their own space is worth a premium in this market, and it always will be.

I would also tell anyone watching this listing not to read too much doom into a big home hitting the market. Luxury inventory moves on its own clock, and a $4.25 million custom home is a specialty product, not a market barometer. The rest of the valley, the homes most families actually buy and sell, follows its own path driven by rates, jobs, and everyday demand. Keep the celebrity headline in perspective. It is fun to snoop through, but your neighborhood's numbers matter far more to your own decision.

What You Can Do

If you are curious about the Las Vegas luxury market, this listing is a great place to start looking. You can search Blue Heron communities like Prado to see the style and pricing for yourself, and you can compare them to what more national builders offer. Even if you are not shopping at $4.25 million, studying high-end homes teaches you what design features hold value over time.

If you are thinking about a move for family or health reasons, like Hilton is, know that you have options and timing on your side. Inventory in the valley has been climbing, which gives sellers more competition but also gives move-up and move-across buyers more to choose from. The right plan depends on your price range, your timeline, and whether you are buying and selling at the same time. That is where a local agent earns their keep.

And if you love the idea of multigenerational living, ask about homes with casitas, NextGen suites, or separate guest quarters. These floor plans are more common in Las Vegas than in most metros, and they can help a family afford a home together. I am happy to point you to neighborhoods and builders that do this well.

You can also use this listing as a teaching tool even if you never plan to buy at the luxury level. Look at what a $4.25 million home offers, then look at what your budget buys, and notice which features carry value at every tier. Natural light, smart storage, a usable backyard, and flexible rooms are not just high-end perks. They are the same things that help a mid-range home sell faster and hold its price. Understanding what luxury buyers want helps you make smarter choices with your own home, from the upgrades you invest in to the way you present it when it is time to sell.
