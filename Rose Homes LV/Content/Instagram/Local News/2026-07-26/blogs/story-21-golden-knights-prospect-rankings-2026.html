Golden Knights Prospect Rankings Get a Big Shakeup After the Draft and the Dorofeyev Trade

SinBin.vegas just refreshed its Vegas Golden Knights prospect rankings, and the message is clear. Trevor Connelly is still the crown jewel of the farm system at No. 1, but the rest of the top five looks very different than it did a year ago. A 17-year-old defenseman named Juho Piiparinen debuted at No. 2, and late-round steal Ben Hemmerling rocketed all the way up to No. 4.

The update, published July 25, 2026 by SinBin.vegas writer Ken Boehlke, follows a busy stretch for the Golden Knights front office. The team added a fresh crop of draft picks and picked up a surprise first-round pick in the trade that sent forward Pavel Dorofeyev out of town. Add it all up and the prospect pool got a real makeover, with five players in the top 10 who were either brand new to the system or ranked outside the top 15 last time around.

What Happened With the New Rankings

SinBin.vegas ranks Golden Knights prospects a few times a year, and this was the first big reset after the 2026 NHL Entry Draft and the Dorofeyev trade. The site ranks players by their value to the organization. The biggest factor is a player's ceiling, or how good he can eventually become. It also weighs how close a player is to the NHL and how likely he is to actually get there. This is not a list of who is best right now. It is a list of who matters most for the future.

To even qualify, a player has to be younger than 23 for skaters, or younger than 25 for goalies. He cannot have played more than 10 NHL games. And he has to be either under contract or drafted by the Golden Knights. Those rules keep the list focused on true prospects and not established pros.

Here is how the top five skaters shook out. Trevor Connelly, a 20-year-old forward taken 19th overall in the 2024 draft, stayed at No. 1. Juho Piiparinen, a 17-year-old defenseman taken 29th overall in the 2026 draft, debuted at No. 2. Jakob Ihs-Wozniak, a 19-year-old forward, moved up to No. 3 from No. 4. Ben Hemmerling, a 21-year-old forward, jumped from No. 17 to No. 4. And Ben Wilmott, a 19-year-old forward taken 92nd overall in 2026, debuted at No. 5.

A lot of familiar names dropped off the list too. SinBin.vegas removed 10 players since the last update. Some were traded, like Christoffer Sedoff, Abram Wiebe, and Jesper Vikman. Some aged out of the rules, like Lukas Cormier, Jakub Demek, and Jackson Hallum. And one, Braeden Bowman, was removed because he now has NHL experience. That kind of turnover is normal for a farm system that keeps churning.

Piiparinen debuting at No. 2 is the eye-catcher. He is already playing professional hockey at 17 for Tappara in Finland, so his body is further along than most kids his age. SinBin.vegas says the next step for him is adjusting to the speed of the North American game, which is why he is expected to stay in Finland for one more season. Ihs-Wozniak, taken 55th overall in 2025, earned steady ice time in Sweden on a team that got promoted to the top league, and the scouting report says the non-scoring parts of his game are already fairly mature. Wilmott, meanwhile, had a coming-of-age postseason with the Barrie Colts, scoring 11 goals and 22 points in 20 games while helping the team reach the Ontario Hockey League final.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

You might wonder why a prospect list matters to anyone who is not a die-hard hockey fan. The answer is that the Golden Knights are one of the biggest reasons Las Vegas feels like a real sports town. Since the team arrived in 2017, it has given the city an identity beyond the Strip. A healthy prospect pool is how the Golden Knights stay good year after year, and staying good keeps T-Mobile Arena full and keeps the whole valley buzzing on game nights.

The pipeline runs right through our backyard. The Henderson Silver Knights, the team's American Hockey League affiliate, play at Lee's Family Forum in Henderson. Several of the top prospects on this list, including Connelly and Hemmerling, spent last season with the Silver Knights. That means Henderson families can watch tomorrow's Golden Knights stars up close and for a fraction of the cost of a Strip game.

This matters for neighborhoods too. When a team is winning and drawing crowds, it lifts the local economy. Restaurants near the arenas get busier. Hotels book up on game weekends. Youth hockey signups climb every time the Golden Knights make a deep playoff run. Rinks like City National Arena in Summerlin, the team's practice home, become community hubs where kids first fall in love with the game.

For anyone thinking about putting down roots here, the sports scene is part of the pitch. A city with a strong NHL team, a growing minor league club, and packed arenas feels alive. That energy is one more reason people keep moving to Southern Nevada, and steady demand is good news for anyone who owns a home in the valley.

There is also a very local, very human side to a prospect list. Every name on it is a young player who may soon be living, training, and shopping right here in the valley. Silver Knights players rent homes in Henderson and Green Valley. They eat at neighborhood spots, work out at local gyms, and become part of the community while they chase their NHL dreams. When you watch a prospect climb from the AHL to the big club, you are often watching one of your neighbors make it. That connection is part of what makes following the farm system so much fun for local families.

Background and History of the VGK Prospect Pool

The Golden Knights have never been a team that tanks for high draft picks. They made the Stanley Cup Final in their very first season and won the championship in 2023. That success means they rarely draft at the top of the first round, so they have had to build their prospect pool through smart scouting, trades, and late-round swings.

Trevor Connelly is the exception near the top. Vegas took him 19th overall in 2024, and he has been the clear best prospect in the system almost since the day he arrived. SinBin.vegas notes that when Connelly is healthy, in a Vegas, Providence College, or Team USA jersey, he is the undisputed top prospect and it is not even close. The trouble has been keeping him on the ice, since injuries cost him chunks of his college season and development camps.

Ben Hemmerling is the classic Vegas late-round gamble. The Golden Knights took him 177th overall back in 2021, deep in the sixth round. He was small, but he kept scoring in juniors, once posting 91 points in 65 games. A shoulder injury slowed his first pro season, but he bounced back as a rookie with the Silver Knights and was even named the AHL's Rookie of the Month in March. SinBin.vegas says his trajectory is on the upswing more than anyone else on the list.

The newest twist came from the Dorofeyev trade. Pavel Dorofeyev was a promising young scorer, and moving him brought back an unexpected first-round pick. Vegas used that draft capital to add players like Piiparinen and Wilmott, which is why the top of the list suddenly looks so fresh. It is a reminder that a farm system can change fast when a front office is active.

It also shows how the Golden Knights operate differently than most young teams. Rather than sitting on picks and waiting years for them to develop, Vegas is always willing to trade current pieces for future ones and future pieces for current ones. That constant motion is why the prospect rankings look so different from one update to the next. Longtime fans have learned to hold their favorites loosely, because a beloved prospect can become a trade chip overnight if the right NHL player becomes available.

What Happens Next

The next big test comes at training camp this fall. SinBin.vegas says Connelly will have a legitimate chance to make the Golden Knights roster right out of camp. Even if he does not crack the opening night lineup, he is expected to be one of the first players called up when Vegas needs a forward during the season. In other words, his NHL debut may be close.

The younger prospects are on a slower track. Piiparinen, the 17-year-old defenseman, is already playing pro hockey in Finland for Tappara. He is expected to stay overseas for at least one more season and should have a big year before he tries to adjust to the smaller, faster North American game. Wilmott, fresh off a strong playoff run with the Barrie Colts in the Ontario Hockey League, will keep developing in juniors.

Fans should also keep an eye on the Henderson Silver Knights. That is where players like Hemmerling and Ihs-Wozniak will keep proving themselves against grown men. A strong AHL season is often the last step before an NHL call-up, so Silver Knights games at Lee's Family Forum are the place to watch these rankings play out in real time.

Expect SinBin.vegas to update the rankings again as the season unfolds. Rankings like these are never final. An injury, a hot streak, or another trade can reshuffle the order in a hurry, just like the Dorofeyev deal did this summer. The one constant is that the Golden Knights front office stays active, and every move ripples down through the pool. For fans, that means there is always something new to argue about, which is half the fun of following a farm system in the first place.

One more thing to watch is how the front office handles this new wave of young talent. As one commenter on the SinBin.vegas post pointed out, Vegas has sometimes used prospects as trade chips to add proven NHL players. Others hope the team keeps these kids to fill out its bottom six forwards and third defensive pairing on cheap contracts. Which path the Golden Knights choose will tell us a lot about how they plan to stay competitive while managing the salary cap.
