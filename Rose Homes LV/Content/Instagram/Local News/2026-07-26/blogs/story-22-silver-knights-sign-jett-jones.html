Henderson Silver Knights Sign Forward Jett Jones to One-Year AHL Deal

The Henderson Silver Knights have signed forward Jett Jones to a one-year American Hockey League deal for the 2026-27 season, and the 23-year-old is a familiar face coming home. General Manager Tim Speltz announced the move on July 20, bringing back a player who already knows the room and the rink.

Jones is not a stranger here. He played parts of two seasons with the Silver Knights from 2023 to 2025 before spending last year in the ECHL, where he won a championship. Now he is back in Henderson, back in the Vegas Golden Knights pipeline, and back at Lee's Family Forum where local fans can watch him skate.

What Happened

On July 20, 2026, Henderson Silver Knights General Manager Tim Speltz announced that the team had signed forward Jett Jones to a one-year AHL contract for the 2026-27 season. The Silver Knights are the AHL affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights, so this is a developmental signing that feeds the bigger picture in Las Vegas hockey.

Jones, 23, is a returning player. He suited up for the Silver Knights across parts of two seasons from 2023 to 2025. The Olds, Alberta native then spent the 2025-26 season with the ECHL's Florida Everblades, where he helped the team win a Kelly Cup championship. That is a real accomplishment, and it shows he can contribute to a winning group.

Last season with Florida, Jones posted six goals and 24 points in 55 ECHL games. He added seven points in 19 playoff games during that championship run. He also got a taste of higher-level action with 11 AHL games for the Springfield Thunderbirds. In 43 career games with the Silver Knights, he has three goals, six points, and 10 penalty minutes.

Jones stands 6 feet 3 inches and weighs 216 pounds, and he shoots left. He has a deeper track record than his age suggests. Before turning pro, he played parts of five seasons with the Western Hockey League's Lethbridge Hurricanes from 2018 to 2023, serving as captain in his final two seasons. In 222 career WHL games, he registered 54 goals and 121 points. He has also played 93 career games for Silver Knights ECHL affiliates with the Savannah Ghost Pirates and Tahoe Knight Monsters, recording 24 goals and 44 points along the way.

Add it all up and you get a player who has paid his dues at every level of the sport. He wore a captain's letter as a junior, he has bounced between the AHL and ECHL as a pro, and he just finished a season that ended with a trophy. That is the profile of a reliable two-way forward who can play a role, kill penalties, and set an example for younger teammates. The Silver Knights clearly value what he brings, or they would not have brought him back for a third go-around in Henderson.

It is worth noting how the pieces of the organization connect. Jones has now touched almost every rung of the Golden Knights ladder, from ECHL affiliates in Savannah and Tahoe to the AHL in Henderson and Springfield. That kind of movement is normal for a young pro, and it is exactly how teams figure out who is ready to take the next step. This latest deal keeps him inside the family and gives the coaching staff a known quantity to build around.

Background and History

The Henderson Silver Knights joined the American Hockey League as the top affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights. The team is owned in part by Bill Foley and his family and is operated by the Foley Entertainment Group, the same ownership behind the Golden Knights. That connection is the whole point. Players who develop in Henderson can earn a call-up to the NHL club in Las Vegas.

The Silver Knights play their home games at Lee's Family Forum, a modern arena in Henderson. The building has become a gathering spot for local hockey fans, and it has helped grow the sport in Southern Nevada. When the Golden Knights came to town in 2017, hockey was new to a lot of desert residents. Now it is part of the culture, and the Silver Knights are a big reason why.

Jones fits the pattern the team likes. He is a bigger forward who plays a physical, honest game, and he has already shown he can win. His Kelly Cup title with Florida is a sign of a player who knows how to compete in the postseason. Bringing back a player who has worn the sweater before also helps team chemistry, because he already understands the systems and the staff.

The AHL is a proving ground. Very few players skip it on the way to the NHL. So every year, teams like the Silver Knights build rosters that mix young prospects with steady veterans and dependable role players. Jones is the kind of returning contributor who helps hold that mix together over a long season.

It also helps to remember how young this whole hockey story is in Southern Nevada. A decade ago, the idea of a pro hockey affiliate in Henderson would have surprised a lot of people. The desert is not the first place most fans picture ice hockey. But the Golden Knights changed that fast, and the Silver Knights extended it. Today there are youth programs, adult leagues, and a growing base of fans who grew up rooting for the Knights. Every roster move is another brick in that foundation.

What Happens Next

Jones will report to training camp and compete for a spot in the Silver Knights lineup for the 2026-27 season. His one-year deal means he has a clear window to prove himself and possibly earn more time at the AHL level or a longer look inside the organization. For a 23-year-old, this is a chance to keep climbing.

Fans can expect to see him at Lee's Family Forum once the season gets going. The Silver Knights schedule fills the fall and winter months, and home games are a fun, family-friendly option for a night out in Henderson. Keep an eye on the team's roster updates and preseason news as camp approaches.

The bigger thing to watch is how the whole pipeline shapes up. Signings like this one happen all summer as teams get ready for the year. Each move tells you a little about the plan. If you follow the Golden Knights, tracking who is in Henderson helps you understand who might get a shot in Las Vegas down the road.

For Jones specifically, the storyline is about production and opportunity. If he builds on the momentum from his championship year in Florida and carves out steady minutes with the Silver Knights, he puts himself in the conversation for more. A big, left-shot forward who can score and compete is always useful, and a strong camp could set the tone for his entire season. Local fans will get a front-row seat to that push at Lee's Family Forum.
