PWHL Las Vegas Names Kim Weiss First Head Coach and Completes Its Roster

Pro women's hockey now has a home in Las Vegas, and it has a leader. PWHL Las Vegas named Kim Weiss its first head coach and finished building its roster ahead of the team's debut season. This is a real professional team, playing in the Professional Women's Hockey League, and it is coming to a city that already loves the sport.

The team plays its home games at T-Mobile Arena and practices at America First Center. It wears green and gold. General Manager Dominique DiDia leads the front office. For a valley that turned into a hockey town almost overnight when the Vegas Golden Knights arrived, this is another big step. Las Vegas is now a two-team hockey city, and one of those teams is making history.

What Happened

PWHL Las Vegas named Kim Weiss as the first head coach in franchise history. Weiss now holds one of the most important jobs in Las Vegas sports. She will set the culture, pick the lineup, and guide a group of players who are helping launch a brand-new team from the ground up. Being the first coach of any franchise is a rare thing, and Weiss gets to write the opening chapter.

The team also completed its roster. That means the front office finished filling out the group of players who will pull on the green and gold. Building a roster from scratch is a huge job. The staff had to find talent, balance the lineup, and shape a team that can compete right away in a fast-growing league.

General Manager Dominique DiDia runs the front office and leads the hockey side of the operation. DiDia and Weiss will work as a team. The general manager brings in the players, and the head coach turns them into a unit. That partnership is the backbone of any successful franchise, and in Las Vegas it starts fresh with both leaders in place.

The home rink is T-Mobile Arena, the same building where the Vegas Golden Knights play on the Strip. The team practices at America First Center. Green and gold are the colors. The Professional Women's Hockey League, or PWHL, is the top pro women's hockey league in North America, and it has grown quickly since it first dropped the puck. Las Vegas is one of the newest markets to join that story.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

A new pro team changes a city. When the Golden Knights arrived, they did not just fill T-Mobile Arena. They filled bars, restaurants, and living rooms all across the valley. Neighborhoods from Summerlin to Green Valley found a shared thing to cheer for. PWHL Las Vegas gives families another team to rally behind, and this time it is history in the making.

This matters for families with daughters who play or want to play. For years, girls who loved hockey had almost no path to see women play the game at the highest level in their own city. Now they can buy a ticket, sit in the stands, and watch women skate as pros. That kind of role model close to home is powerful. It can push youth sign-ups higher and give young players a real dream to chase.

It also matters for the local economy, and that ties straight into real estate. Pro sports teams bring jobs, visitors, and spending. Game nights fill nearby hotels and eateries. Families plan weekends around the schedule. All of that activity supports the businesses that make a neighborhood feel alive, and lively neighborhoods hold their value better over time.

Las Vegas keeps proving it is a real sports town, not just a place people visit. The Golden Knights, the Raiders, the Aces, and now PWHL Las Vegas all call the valley home. Each new team adds to the reputation of the region as a place where big things happen. That reputation is one more reason people keep choosing to move here and put down roots.

Background and History

The Professional Women's Hockey League launched in the 2023 to 2024 season with six teams and dropped its first puck in January 2024. Those early clubs played in cities across the United States and Canada. The league drew strong crowds right out of the gate and showed there was real demand for pro women's hockey. Fans came, watched, and stayed.

That early success set the stage for growth. The league looked to add new markets, and Las Vegas landed on the map. It makes sense. The valley already had a proven hockey audience thanks to the Golden Knights, plus the arena space and the fan energy to support another team. Las Vegas had shown it would fill seats for hockey, and the league took notice.

Bringing a team to life takes months of quiet work behind the scenes. The front office has to hire staff, sign players, pick colors and branding, and lock in a place to play and practice. Naming Kim Weiss as head coach and completing the roster are two of the biggest milestones in that whole process. They turn an idea on paper into a team that will actually take the ice.

The choice of T-Mobile Arena as the home rink is a statement. That building is one of the best arenas in the country and sits right on the Las Vegas Strip. Practicing at America First Center gives the team a strong home base too. These are the kind of first-class facilities that tell players and fans this team is here to compete and here to stay.

What Happens Next

The next big moments are on the ice. With Kim Weiss in place and the roster complete, the team will move into training camp and preseason work. That is when Weiss and her staff mold a group of talented individuals into a real team. Fans will get their first look at how the group plays and which players stand out.

There is also a marquee crossover date already on the calendar. The Vegas Golden Knights announced a VGK x PWHL Las Vegas night set for December 5 as part of the Golden Knights Season X theme night lineup. That kind of shared promotion helps both teams. It introduces Golden Knights fans to the new club and shows how the two franchises can lift each other up.

Keep an eye out for the full home schedule, ticket details, and the team's first official game. Those are the moments to circle on the calendar. As the season nears, expect more news on player signings, promotions, and community events. This is a brand-new team, so almost everything it does will be a first, and each first is worth watching.
