Golden Knights Sign Eleven Free Agents Heading Into Their Anniversary Season

The Vegas Golden Knights announced eleven free agent signings on July 1, 2026, loading up their depth chart before their tenth anniversary season. The list runs six forwards and four defensemen, plus one more name, and it tells you exactly how the front office plans to build out the roster for Season X.

These are not the flashy top-line splashes that dominate the news cycle. They are the depth pieces, the tweener contracts, and the young players who fight for roster spots in Henderson and Las Vegas. That is the part most fans skip past, and it is also the part that decides how deep a team really is when injuries hit in January.

What Happened

On July 1, 2026, the first day of NHL free agency, the Vegas Golden Knights announced they had signed eleven free agents. The team laid out the group by position. The forwards are Demek, Gatcomb, Laczynski, Lavoie, Olofsson, and Rondbjerg. The defensemen are Coghlan, Ginning, Heinola, and Tuomisto. That is ten names by position, with the eleventh signing rounding out the announced group.

July 1 is the busiest day on the hockey calendar. Every team in the league can talk to and sign players whose contracts have expired. Big markets chase the headline stars, and the money moves fast in the first few hours. The Golden Knights used the day to add role players and organizational depth instead of one giant contract, which is a very different strategy than the one that grabs the front page.

Most of these deals are the kind that fill out a roster from top to bottom. Some players will compete for a spot on the big club at T-Mobile Arena. Others will start the year with the Henderson Silver Knights, the team's American Hockey League affiliate that plays at Lifeguard Arena in downtown Henderson. That two-team pipeline is how a franchise keeps fresh legs ready when the NHL club needs a call-up.

The signings landed right as the team prepares to celebrate ten years in Las Vegas. The Golden Knights arrived in 2017 and reached the Stanley Cup Final in their very first season. They won it all in 2023. Now, heading into the anniversary year, the front office is stacking the deck with competition for jobs. When you sign eleven players in one day, you are telling your current roster that nothing is guaranteed.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

The Golden Knights are more than a hockey team in this valley. They are a huge economic driver and a point of pride for a city that waited a long time for a major league franchise. Game nights fill hotels, restaurants, and bars across the Strip and beyond. When the team is deep and competitive, the whole downtown and Summerlin dining scene feels it on game days.

Roster depth matters to fans because it decides how the season goes. A team that signs eleven players has options. If a top forward gets hurt, there is someone ready to step in instead of a big drop-off in talent. That depth is the difference between a playoff run and an early exit, and playoff runs bring even more energy and money into Las Vegas.

There is also the Henderson connection. The Silver Knights play at Lifeguard Arena, and many of these signings will suit up there before they ever reach the NHL club. That gives Henderson families affordable pro hockey close to home. A night at Lifeguard Arena costs a fraction of a Strip game, and it is a real draw for neighborhoods like Green Valley and Inspirada.

For anyone who follows the team, these moves are also a reminder of how much the sport has grown here. Ten years ago, hockey was a curiosity in the desert. Today, kids across Clark County play the game, arenas run learn to play programs, and the pipeline of pro talent runs right through the valley. A deep, healthy franchise keeps that momentum going for the next ten years.

Background and History

The Vegas Golden Knights were born in 2017 as the NHL's first team in Nevada. They stunned the sports world by reaching the Stanley Cup Final in their expansion season, a feat no modern team had ever pulled off. That run turned casual fans into diehards almost overnight and put T-Mobile Arena on the map as one of the loudest buildings in the league.

The championship came in 2023, when the Golden Knights lifted the Stanley Cup and cemented their place as one of the most successful young franchises in pro sports history. Since then, the front office has built a reputation for aggressive, creative roster moves. They are not afraid to make trades, take on contracts, or reshape the lineup year after year.

Free agency depth signings like these are a normal part of that plan. Every summer, the team adds players who can push for jobs and provide insurance. The Henderson Silver Knights, founded in 2020, gave the organization its own in-house farm team, so the Golden Knights no longer had to share a minor league affiliate. That move made days like July 1 even more important, because now every signing has a clear place to develop.

The tenth anniversary season adds a layer of meaning to this offseason. The team has already announced a full slate of Season X theme nights to celebrate a decade of memories. Building a deep, competitive roster to match the celebration is the natural next step. The front office wants the anniversary year to be about winning, not just looking back.

What Happens Next

The next big step is training camp, which usually opens in September. That is when these eleven signings will battle for roster spots. Coaches will watch closely to see who can crack the NHL lineup and who starts the year in Henderson. Preseason games give fans an early look at the new faces before the regular season begins.

Expect some of these players to make the opening night roster at T-Mobile Arena and others to head to Lifeguard Arena with the Silver Knights. Injuries, waivers, and performance will shuffle names all season long. The forwards like Lavoie and Olofsson and the defensemen like Heinola and Coghlan will each have a chance to prove they belong.

Watch for the team to keep tweaking the roster right up to the trade deadline in the winter. The Golden Knights have never been shy about making a move when they see a chance to get better. These summer signings give the front office flexibility, and flexibility is exactly what a contender wants when the games start to matter most.
