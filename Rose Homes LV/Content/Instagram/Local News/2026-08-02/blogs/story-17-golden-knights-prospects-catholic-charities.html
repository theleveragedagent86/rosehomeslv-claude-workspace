Golden Knights Prospects Serve Meals at Catholic Charities to Kick Off Development Camp

Before the Vegas Golden Knights prospects laced up a single skate, they picked up serving spoons instead. To open the team's summer development camp, the young players spent a morning at Catholic Charities of Southern Nevada and served more than 600 meals to people in need. It happened on July 1, 2026, and it set the tone for the whole camp.

This is the kind of story that makes Las Vegas fall a little harder for its hockey team. The prospects are the future of the franchise, and the very first thing they did as a group was give back to the city they hope to play for. Names like Connelly, Ihs-Wozniak, and Piiparinen showed up early, put on gloves, and got to work in the kitchen and serving line.

What Happened at Catholic Charities

The Golden Knights hold a development camp every summer. It is where the team brings in its drafted prospects, recent free agent signings, and young players from its pipeline. Camp is usually about skating drills, scrimmages, fitness testing, and meetings with coaches. This year, the team chose to start with service instead of ice.

On the morning of July 1, the prospects reported to Catholic Charities of Southern Nevada. The nonprofit runs one of the largest meal programs in the valley. It feeds people who are homeless, seniors on fixed incomes, and families who are struggling to make ends meet. The prospects served more than 600 meals during their shift, working the line and handing plates to guests one by one.

Among the players on hand were forward prospects and defensemen from the organization's system, including Connelly, Ihs-Wozniak, and Piiparinen. These are young men, many of them still teenagers or in their early twenties, and many of them are new to Las Vegas. For some, this was one of their first real looks at the community off the Strip and away from the arena.

The event was not a photo op tacked onto the schedule. The team built it into the front end of camp on purpose. Coaches and staff have said for years that they want players who understand where they live and who lives around them. Starting camp with a service day is a clear way to send that message to the newest members of the organization.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Las Vegas is a big-league sports town now, and the Golden Knights are the team that started it all. When the players give back, it lands differently here than it might in an older market. This is still a young franchise in a young sports city, and every act of community service helps write what kind of team this is going to be for the next decade and beyond.

Food insecurity is a real problem across Clark County. Rising rents and grocery bills have stretched a lot of local households thin. Programs like the one at Catholic Charities help fill the gap for seniors, working families, and people who have fallen on hard times. When 600 meals go out the door in a single morning, that is 600 real neighbors who got fed.

There is also a ripple effect. When a beloved team shows up to volunteer, it shines a light on the nonprofit doing the daily work. Fans see the story, and some of them donate, sign up to volunteer, or drop off food. A morning of service by a handful of prospects can turn into weeks of extra support for the charity if the community follows the team's lead.

For families raising kids in Summerlin, Henderson, Green Valley, or anywhere in the valley, this is a good example to point to. The message is simple. You can chase your biggest goals and still make time to help the people around you. That is a lesson worth more than any highlight reel.

Background and History

The Golden Knights have leaned into community work since the very beginning. The team arrived in 2017 and won hearts almost overnight, in part because of how it showed up for Las Vegas during one of the city's hardest moments that first fall. That bond between the team and the city has never really faded, and community service is a big reason why.

The Vegas Golden Knights Foundation supports causes across Southern Nevada, from youth programs to health initiatives. Catholic Charities of Southern Nevada has been serving the valley for decades, long before the NHL ever came to town. Its meal program, shelter services, and family support fill needs that only grow as the region grows. A partnership between the two makes sense.

Development camp itself has become a summer tradition for fans. It is the first chance to see the players who could one day fill out the roster at T-Mobile Arena. Over the years, the camp has produced players who went on to real NHL minutes, so fans watch closely to see who stands out. Adding a service day gives the camp a heart to go with the hype.

This is also not a one-off. Teams across the league use camp to teach prospects about professionalism, and the best organizations treat community involvement as part of that education. The Golden Knights putting service first tells you how they want their young players to carry themselves once they wear the gold.

What Happens Next

After the service day, the prospects moved into the on-ice portion of development camp. That means skating sessions, skills work, fitness testing, and scrimmages in front of coaches and scouts. The players who impress build momentum heading into the fall, when the fight for roster spots and minor league assignments really begins.

Some of these names will show up again during the preseason. A few could push for a look with the big club, while others will develop in the minors with the Henderson Silver Knights or in junior and college programs. Fans who caught the service story now have a reason to follow these specific players through the season and root for the ones who gave back.

For Catholic Charities, the work does not stop when the players leave. The meal program runs every single day, and demand tends to climb in the hotter months and again around the holidays. The organization will keep serving, and the hope is that the attention from the team's visit brings in more volunteers and donations to keep the line moving.

Ryan's Take

As someone who helps people put down roots in this valley, I love seeing stories like this one. A home is not just a house. It is the neighborhood, the schools, the charities, and the shared sense that people here look out for each other. When the Golden Knights show up at Catholic Charities, they are investing in the same community fabric that makes Las Vegas a great place to live and buy a home.

Buyers ask me all the time what makes a neighborhood feel like home. A lot of it comes down to community pride, and this team feeds that pride in a real way. The Golden Knights have helped turn Las Vegas into a genuine sports city, and moments like this remind everyone that the team's roots go deeper than wins and losses. That civic energy is part of what keeps demand for Las Vegas homes so steady.

What You Can Do

You do not have to be a hockey prospect to make a difference at Catholic Charities of Southern Nevada. The organization takes volunteers year round, and there is almost always a need for extra hands in the meal program. You can visit catholiccharities.com to learn about volunteer shifts, donation drop-offs, and the many other services the group provides across the valley.

If you cannot give time, you can give food or funds. Even a small donation helps put meals on the table for seniors and families who are struggling. Gather a few neighbors, run a small food drive on your street, and drop it off together. It is an easy way to turn a feel-good story into real help for people who need it.

And if you want to support the team's charitable side, keep an eye on the Vegas Golden Knights Foundation and the community events the team hosts during the season. Following the prospects from this camp is a fun way to stay connected too. You never know which of these young players will be scoring goals at T-Mobile Arena in a few short years.
