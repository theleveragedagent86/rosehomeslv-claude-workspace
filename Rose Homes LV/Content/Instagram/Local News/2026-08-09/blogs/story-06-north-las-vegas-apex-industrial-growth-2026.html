North Las Vegas and Apex Are Leading the Valley's Industrial Boom

North Las Vegas and the nearby Apex Industrial Park are now the engine of Southern Nevada's warehouse and factory growth. The Q2 2026 RED Report shows the North Las Vegas submarket soaking up nearly three-fourths of all the new industrial space leased across the entire valley, and roughly 30 million square feet of new projects are moving forward in Apex alone.

That is a big deal for a part of Clark County that spent years being overlooked. All of this new space means jobs, trucks, tax dollars, and more people looking for homes close to work. If you live in North Las Vegas, Aliante, or the far northeast edge of the valley, this growth is happening right in your backyard.

What the Q2 2026 RED Report Actually Says

The RED Report is a quarterly look at commercial real estate in Southern Nevada, published through Nevada Business Magazine. It tracks how much industrial space is being built, how much is being leased, and which parts of the valley are growing fastest. The Q2 2026 edition puts North Las Vegas at the top of the list.

The report shows the North Las Vegas submarket accounting for nearly three-fourths of the region's net absorption. Net absorption is a simple idea. It measures how much more space companies moved into than moved out of during the quarter. A high number means businesses are hungry for space and are signing leases faster than they are leaving. North Las Vegas is doing most of the heavy lifting for the whole valley.

Then there is Apex. The report points to about 30 million square feet of industrial space under development in the Apex Industrial Park area. To put that in plain terms, 30 million square feet is enough for hundreds of football fields under roof. These are giant buildings, the kind used for warehouses, distribution hubs, manufacturing, and logistics. They do not go up unless companies believe the demand is real and lasting.

The takeaway is clear. While other parts of the valley are seeing slower commercial activity, North Las Vegas and Apex are pulling ahead. The land is available, the price is right, and the location works for companies that need to move goods across the West.

It helps to understand why industrial numbers matter more than they look at first. Warehouse and factory space is a leading sign of where an economy is headed. Companies do not sign long leases on giant buildings unless they expect to sell and ship more goods for years to come. So when one submarket grabs most of the region's net absorption, it is telling you where the business bets are going. Right now those bets are landing in North Las Vegas and Apex, quarter after quarter.

Compare that with the housing market, which has cooled a bit this summer. Home prices in the valley slipped slightly from their record high in July, and sellers have been cutting prices more often. Industrial is moving in the other direction. That split matters because a strong job base tied to industry can help steady the housing market over time, even when prices take a breather. Jobs are the foundation, and the RED Report shows that foundation getting stronger on the north end of the valley.

Why It Matters to North Las Vegas Residents

Jobs are the headline here. Big warehouses and factories need workers to run them. That means forklift drivers, packers, machine operators, truck drivers, managers, and office staff. When 30 million square feet of new space fills up, it can support thousands of steady paychecks. For families in North Las Vegas and Aliante, that can mean work close to home instead of a long drive to the Strip or the south end of the valley.

More jobs near home also change the housing story. When people can work a few miles from where they live, they want to buy or rent nearby. That steady demand helps hold home values in North Las Vegas, Aliante, and the newer communities off the 215 and Interstate 15. A neighborhood with jobs down the road is a neighborhood people want to move into.

There is a tax side too. Industrial projects pay property taxes and often bring business tax dollars into the city. That money can help pay for roads, parks, police, and other services North Las Vegas has needed for years. A stronger tax base gives the city more room to invest in the things residents actually use.

Local businesses win as well. Thousands of workers coming into an area every day need places to eat lunch, grab coffee, fill up on gas, and shop on the way home. That foot traffic can bring new restaurants, stores, and services to parts of North Las Vegas that have been light on options. When one big employer opens, a cluster of smaller businesses often grows around it. That ripple effect is part of why residents tend to root for this kind of growth even when the buildings themselves are plain warehouses.

Renters feel this too. A wave of new jobs usually brings new households looking for a place to live near their work. That demand can lift rents and push more apartment and build-to-rent projects into the northern part of the valley. For renters, it can mean more choices over time, but it can also mean landlords have less reason to cut deals when demand is strong. Watching how many new rentals get built alongside the warehouses will tell you which way the pressure goes.

It is not all upside, and honest neighbors will say so. More warehouses mean more trucks on local streets and freeways. Roads like the 215 Beltway and the northern stretch of I-15 could see heavier traffic. Some residents worry about noise, dust, and wear on the roads. These are fair concerns, and they are worth watching as the projects open.

There is also a quality-of-life balance to strike. Nobody wants a giant loading dock right up against their backyard fence. Smart planning puts buffers, walls, and landscaping between homes and heavy industry, and it keeps truck routes on the big roads instead of quiet residential streets. When it is done well, residents get the jobs and the tax dollars without living inside a truck yard. When it is done poorly, neighbors feel it every day. That is why local input during the planning stage carries so much weight.

Background and History

Apex has been a long time coming. The Apex Industrial Park sits northeast of North Las Vegas along Interstate 15, on a huge stretch of land set aside for heavy industry. For decades it was mostly empty desert with big plans and slow progress. The land was there, but the water lines, power, and roads needed to support large buildings were not fully in place.

That started to change over the past several years. Public and private money went into the pipes, power, and roads that make big industrial projects possible. Once that backbone was built, companies had a reason to come. Apex offers something the rest of the valley cannot easily match, which is very large blocks of land at a workable price, close to a major freeway.

North Las Vegas leaned into this shift. The city has spent years marketing itself as a business-friendly place to build. It approved projects, courted employers, and pushed for the infrastructure that lets warehouses and factories operate. The Q2 2026 numbers are the payoff from that long push. What looks like a sudden boom is really the result of years of groundwork.

The timing also helps. Las Vegas sits within a day's drive of huge Western markets like Southern California, Phoenix, and Salt Lake City. As companies look to move goods faster and store them closer to customers, a valley in the middle of the West becomes a smart place to plant a distribution hub. North Las Vegas and Apex are cashing in on that map.

Southern Nevada has also worked hard to move beyond just tourism. For a long time the local economy leaned heavily on the Strip, hotels, and casinos. That worked in good years, but it left the region exposed whenever travel slowed down. Building up industry, logistics, and manufacturing gives the valley a second and third leg to stand on. North Las Vegas and Apex are a big part of that plan to spread the economy across more than just visitors.

Cost is another piece of the story. Land and building costs in Southern California have climbed for years, and space there is tight. Companies looking to grow have been searching for nearby markets where they can build big for less. Apex offers exactly that, with room to expand and a lower cost to get in the door. That price gap is a quiet but powerful reason so much development is flowing to the north end of the valley right now.

What Happens Next

Watch the 30 million square feet in Apex fill in over the next few years. Not all of it will open at once. Projects like these come in waves, with some buildings finishing and leasing while others are still under construction. Each wave that fills up brings a fresh round of hiring and truck traffic.

Keep an eye on the roads. As more warehouses open, expect more attention on the 215 Beltway, I-15, and the local streets that feed Apex. New or widened roads, added freeway ramps, and traffic upgrades often follow big industrial growth. How the city and the state handle that will shape the daily commute for people who live nearby.

Also watch future RED Reports. If North Las Vegas keeps leading net absorption in the coming quarters, it confirms this is a lasting trend and not a one-time spike. Steady numbers over several quarters would tell homeowners and buyers that the jobs and demand are here to stay.

New home building is another thing to track. Builders follow jobs. If the industrial growth holds, expect more rooftops to go up in the northern communities to house the workers these projects attract. That new construction can bring fresh options for buyers, along with the builder incentives that have become common across the valley this year. The pace of new homes near Apex will be a good sign of how seriously builders take this growth.

When thousands of steady jobs land a few miles from neighborhoods like Aliante and the communities off the 215, those homes get more valuable and more stable over time. Buyers want to live near good work, and this growth puts good work right there.

My honest advice is to look north before everyone else does. North Las Vegas has offered some of the better value in the valley, and industrial growth like this tends to lift the whole area with it. If you are a first-time buyer or an investor, the mix of lower prices today and rising job demand nearby is worth a serious look. Just go in with clear eyes about the truck traffic that comes with a warehouse boom.

I also like what this does for stability. Markets that lean on one industry can swing hard when that industry stumbles. A valley that adds logistics, manufacturing, and distribution to its tourism base is a steadier place to own a home. Steady beats flashy when you are making the biggest purchase of your life. For families planning to stay put for years, that quiet strength on the north end of the valley is exactly the kind of thing I want my clients paying attention to, even when it does not make loud headlines.

What You Can Do

If jobs are what you care about, keep an eye on hiring at the new North Las Vegas and Apex projects. Large employers often post openings well before a building opens. Local workforce groups and the city's economic development pages are good places to check for training programs and job fairs tied to these projects.

If you are thinking about buying, get familiar with the northern communities now. Drive the neighborhoods off the 215 and northern I-15, look at the newer developments in and around North Las Vegas and Aliante, and pay attention to how close they sit to the growth. A short commute to steady work is a real selling point that will only matter more as Apex fills in.

Do your homework on the specific street and lot before you buy. Two homes a mile apart can have very different experiences with truck traffic, noise, and future construction. Ask about what is planned nearby, check the zoning around a neighborhood, and think about the drive to the jobs you care about. A little research up front can save you from surprises after you move in, and it can help you spot the pockets that will hold value best.

If you want to have a say in how the growth happens, show up. North Las Vegas holds public meetings where road plans, zoning, and new projects get discussed. That is where residents can speak up about traffic, buffers between homes and warehouses, and the improvements they want to see. The people who show up are the ones who help shape how this boom lands in their neighborhood.
