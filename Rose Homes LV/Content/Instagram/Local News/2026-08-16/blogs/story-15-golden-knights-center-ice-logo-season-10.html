Golden Knights Put a Roulette Wheel at Center Ice for Season X

The Vegas Golden Knights unveiled their new center ice logo at T-Mobile Arena on Friday, August 14, 2026, and it is a roulette wheel. The design wraps the team's 10th anniversary crest inside a spinning wheel of alternating red and black pockets, and the whole thing reads as a Roman numeral X.

Look closer and you will find two more numbers tucked into the design. The years 17 and 27 sit inside the wheel. The 17 marks the franchise's inaugural season in 2017. The 27 marks the end of Season 10 in the spring of 2027.

It is a small thing, a painted circle on a sheet of ice. But for a lot of people in Clark County, it is the first real sign that hockey is coming back. Training camp opens in about a month. The home opener is September 29, 2026 against the Chicago Blackhawks. And ten years after this town was told it would never support a hockey team, the logo at center ice is a casino game.

What Happened at T-Mobile Arena

The Golden Knights released photos of the finished center ice logo on Friday, August 14, 2026. The ice crew at T-Mobile Arena had already laid it in. That is how arena ice works. The logo goes down in layers under the playing surface weeks before the first puck drops, and once it is sealed in, it stays there all season.

The design itself is busier than what Vegas has used in past years. At the middle sits the 10th anniversary crest the team introduced earlier this summer. Around it runs a roulette wheel with alternating red and black pockets, the same look you would see on a table anywhere on the Las Vegas Strip. The wheel is not just decoration. Its shape and the gap in the ring combine to form a Roman numeral X, which is how the team is branding Season 10. They are calling it Season X.

The two small year markers are the detail most fans missed on first look. The 17 and the 27 bookend the decade. Vegas played its first NHL game in the fall of 2017. Season 10 wraps up in 2027. Ten years, two numbers, one wheel.

Reaction split almost immediately. Some fans loved it and called it the most Vegas thing the team has ever done. Others said the red and black pockets clash with the team's gold, steel gray, and black color scheme, and that a red ring on the ice will look strange next to gold sweaters. Vegas Hockey Knight published a piece walking through both sides of that argument the same weekend.

The logo arrives at an interesting moment. Vegas is coming off a 39-26-17 regular season and a run all the way to the Stanley Cup Final, where the Carolina Hurricanes beat them in six games. So the team is putting a celebration logo on the ice one season after a loss that still stings for a lot of season ticket holders.

Down in Henderson, the Henderson Silver Knights are running their own preseason preparations at Lee's Family Forum. And out in Summerlin, City National Arena is already busy. That building is the Golden Knights practice facility and also a public rink, so the pace picks up there every August as camp gets close.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

A center ice logo is not a policy vote or a tax change. It will not move your mortgage payment. But it is a marker of something that has changed a lot of neighborhoods in this valley, and that part is worth talking about.

Before 2017, Las Vegas had no team in any of the big four leagues. Locals drove to Los Angeles or Phoenix or Denver if they wanted to see pro sports. Visitors came here for shows and tables. Now T-Mobile Arena hosts 41 regular season home games plus playoffs, and roughly 18,000 people head to the south end of the Strip on those nights. That is a lot of dinner reservations, rideshare trips, and parking dollars moving through one corridor.

The ripple effects reached the suburbs. City National Arena opened in Summerlin as the team's practice rink, and it turned into a neighborhood hub. Learn to skate programs filled up. Youth hockey grew from almost nothing into travel teams and house leagues with real waiting lists. Ten years later, the city of Las Vegas is still working on a two sheet ice arena for the northwest valley because demand outran the rinks we have.

If you own a home near one of these facilities, you have watched this happen in real time. Buyers ask about the rink now. Families relocating from Michigan, Minnesota, or Alberta ask where their kid can play before they ask about the school rating. That is a genuine shift in what people want from a Las Vegas neighborhood, and it did not exist a decade ago.

There is a quieter effect too. The Golden Knights gave this town a shared thing to talk about. In a valley where a huge share of residents moved here from somewhere else, that matters more than people admit. The team became a common language between neighbors who grew up rooting for different clubs in different cities.

The jobs side is real as well. Arena nights mean work for ushers, security staff, concession workers, ice crews, camera operators, and the restaurant staff up and down the corridor. Add in the Henderson Silver Knights at Lee's Family Forum and the practice operation at City National Arena, and you have a year round hockey economy in a valley that had none of it before 2016. Those are not glamorous jobs, but they are steady shifts for Clark County households.

Renters feel the schedule too. If you live near the south end of the Strip, in the neighborhoods just west of the resort corridor, or anywhere along the routes people take to get there, you already know which nights the traffic turns. Forty one home dates a season, plus concerts and fights and playoff runs, changes how you plan a Tuesday evening. Some people love being that close to the action. Others move a few miles out to get away from it. Both of those are reasonable, and both show up in how buyers pick a neighborhood here.

Background and History

T-Mobile Arena opened in April 2016 on the west side of the Strip near New York-New York and the Park MGM. At the time, plenty of people questioned whether Las Vegas could fill an NHL building for a full season. The knock was always the same. Too many tourists, not enough locals, and no hockey culture.

The Golden Knights answered that fast. Their first season in 2017-18 ended with a trip to the Stanley Cup Final against the Washington Capitals, which almost never happens for an expansion team. That season also came right after the October 1, 2017 shooting, and the team's role in the city's recovery is a big part of why the fan base bonded so hard, so early.

Vegas won the Stanley Cup in 2023, beating the Florida Panthers. That gave the franchise its banner and made the 10th anniversary a celebration of a decade that actually delivered, not just a decade that happened. The team has been a consistent playoff club for most of its existence, which is rare for a young franchise.

Center ice designs have their own small history here. For most of the past decade Vegas kept the middle of the ice fairly simple, usually the primary gold and black crest inside a clean ring. That restraint made the logo easy to read on television and easy to skate over without visual noise. The Season X wheel is a deliberate break from that habit, and it is the boldest center ice look the franchise has tried.

The 10th anniversary branding rolled out in stages this summer. Mystery gold billboards went up across the valley first, and locals spent days guessing what they were for before the team revealed the anniversary crest behind them. The center ice logo at T-Mobile Arena is the next piece of that same campaign. Expect more of it, on jerseys, on merchandise, and inside the arena, over the next few months.

What Happens Next

Training camp opens in about a month. That is when City National Arena in Summerlin turns into the busiest hockey building in the state. Camp practices are usually open to the public and free, which makes them one of the better cheap outings in the valley for families. Parking fills early, so plan for that.

Preseason games follow camp, then the home opener on September 29, 2026 against the Chicago Blackhawks at T-Mobile Arena. That is the night most fans will see the roulette wheel logo in person for the first time, under full arena lighting and on television. A design can look very different on a laptop screen than it does on live ice, and that is where the current debate will get settled one way or the other.

Watch how the logo reads on broadcast. Red and black pockets against white ice, with gold jerseys skating over the top, is a real test. If it works, it becomes an instant classic and the merchandise sells out. If the red muddies up on camera, expect the complaints to get louder by October.

The bigger story of Season 10 is what the team does after last year's Final loss. Vegas went 39-26-17 and got within two wins of a second Cup before Carolina closed it out in six. A roster that close usually gets one more serious run. The anniversary logo will sit under every shift of it.

Expect the anniversary campaign to keep unrolling through the fall. Anniversary merchandise, in arena tributes, and alumni moments are standard for a milestone season, and the team has already shown it will build the reveals up slowly. If you collect team gear, the Season X items tied to a specific milestone tend to be the ones people hold onto ten years later.

One more thing to watch. Because the logo is sealed under the playing surface, it cannot be swapped out mid season. Whatever the reaction is in October, that wheel is staying at center ice through the spring of 2027. That is part of what makes a center ice decision a bigger commitment than a jersey patch or a billboard.

Ryan's Take

I like it. A roulette wheel at center ice is the kind of thing that would look ridiculous in almost any other city and looks exactly right here. Vegas has spent ten years proving it is a real hockey market, and the team has earned the confidence to lean all the way into where it plays instead of hiding it.

From a real estate angle, the interesting part is not the logo. It is what the last ten years did to how people shop for homes here. When I started showing houses, nobody asked me about ice time. Now I get questions about which neighborhoods sit close to a rink, whether the youth program has openings, and how bad the drive is to City National Arena on a weeknight. Summerlin, Centennial Hills, and Skye Canyon all benefit from that in ways the 2016 version of this valley would not have predicted.

Pro sports do not single handedly move home values. Schools, jobs, commutes, and inventory do most of that work. But amenities shape demand at the margins, and hockey is now a real amenity in Clark County. That is a fair thing to factor in if you are choosing between two neighborhoods with hockey families in the house.

What You Can Do

If you want to see the new logo in person without paying opener prices, watch for the training camp schedule at City National Arena in Summerlin. Camp sessions are usually the cheapest and most relaxed way to see the team, and kids often get closer to the players there than they ever will at T-Mobile Arena. The team posts camp details on its official channels as September gets closer.

For a full game night, the September 29 home opener against the Chicago Blackhawks will be one of the priciest tickets of the year. Midweek games later in the fall cost far less. If your budget is tight, the Henderson Silver Knights at Lee's Family Forum run group tickets starting at $35 for the 2026-27 season, which is a strong option for families and youth teams.

If your kid wants to play, start with the learn to skate and youth programs at City National Arena and the other valley rinks. Sign up early. Ice time in Clark County is limited, and the city of Las Vegas is still working through the process on a new two sheet arena in the northwest valley, so the shortage is not solved yet.

Season ticket holders should look at their September and October schedule now. Ten years in, the routine part of being a fan is planning around 41 home dates plus a possible playoff run. Figure out which games you actually want to attend, which ones you would rather resell, and which ones make sense to hand off to family or coworkers. That planning saves money and keeps seats filled.

If you are house hunting and hockey is part of the decision, be specific about it with your agent. Tell them which rink your family will use and how far you are willing to drive on a weeknight in traffic. That one detail narrows a search fast and keeps you from touring homes that look great on paper and do not work once practice starts at six in the morning.

And if you have an opinion on the logo, you are not alone. Fans have been arguing about it since Friday. That argument is part of the fun of a 10th anniversary season, and it beats the alternative, which is a town that does not care enough to argue.
