The Vegas Golden Knights Are Getting a Brand New Jersey for Season X

The Vegas Golden Knights will add at least one brand new sweater to the rotation for the 2026-27 season, and the gray jersey that fans have worn for years is moving to throwback status. The Las Vegas Review-Journal reported on August 19, 2026 that the new look comes out of a league-wide apparel line the NHL is doing with Fanatics called Hometown Remix, which builds uniforms and headwear around each team's city and local culture.

If you own a gray VGK sweater, you did not waste your money. It just changed jobs. And if you have been waiting for a third look that feels like Las Vegas rather than a generic alternate, this is the announcement you have been waiting on since the franchise started playing at T-Mobile Arena.

Season X is a big deal here. The tenth season of Golden Knights hockey is a milestone for a city that had no major professional sports team a decade ago, and the team has leaned into that with center ice art, anniversary branding, and now a fresh uniform. For thousands of local families, a new jersey is a real purchase decision, so people want details.

What Happened

On August 19, 2026, Danny Webster of the Las Vegas Review-Journal reported that the Vegas Golden Knights will get a new jersey for the 2026-27 season. The report tied the new sweater to a national apparel program the NHL teased alongside Fanatics, the league's uniform and merchandise partner. The program is called Hometown Remix.

The idea behind Hometown Remix is that each participating team gets on-ice uniforms and matching headwear built around its city and its local culture. That is a different concept from the usual alternate jersey, which often just flips the color scheme or brings back an old crest. Hometown Remix is meant to say something specific about the place the team plays.

For Vegas, the new sweater becomes a third look. The metallic gold home kit stays. The white road uniform stays. The new Hometown Remix jersey joins them in the rotation. The gray sweaters, which have been part of the Golden Knights identity for years, move into throwback status. That single detail is the one lighting up comment sections across the valley right now, because plenty of fans consider the gray their favorite.

The team and the league have not released the full design details yet. According to the Review-Journal report, more information is expected after the season starts in September. That means fans will likely get the reveal, the pricing, and the specific game dates the jersey will be worn sometime in the fall rather than before the puck drops.

What we do know for certain is the framework. At least one new sweater. A league-wide Fanatics program. A local culture theme. Three active looks instead of two. And the gray moving to throwback. Anything beyond that, including the colors, the crest treatment, or the exact reveal date, is not confirmed yet.

It is also worth being clear about what has not been said. The Review-Journal report does not say the gray sweater is being retired. Throwback status is a different thing. A throwback stays in the closet, gets worn on specific nights, and often comes back into the retail rotation for anniversary games. Fans who read the headline and assumed the gray is gone forever are reading more into it than the reporting supports.

The timing lines up with a busy stretch for the franchise. Training camp, preseason games, and the regular season opener all land in September, and the team has been building out Season X branding since the summer. A uniform announcement in the middle of that run gives the club another news beat to work with during the part of the calendar when local attention is highest.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with the simple version. A new authentic NHL jersey is not a small purchase. Fans in Summerlin, Henderson, Centennial Hills, and Mountains Edge who buy team gear for the whole family plan around announcements like this. Knowing a new sweater is coming changes whether you buy now or wait, and it changes what you put on a holiday wish list.

Then there is the emotional part, which is bigger than people admit. The gray sweater has been in this city's closets since the early years. It shows up at watch parties in Green Valley, at youth games at City National Arena, and in family photos. Telling fans it is now a throwback closes out a chapter. That is why the reaction has been loud.

There is also a community angle. Golden Knights game nights drive real activity across Clark County. People park downtown, eat on the Strip, ride in from Henderson and North Las Vegas, and fill bars and restaurants in neighborhoods nowhere near T-Mobile Arena. A fresh jersey and a tenth anniversary season give the team another reason to draw a crowd, and that crowd spends money in local businesses.

For families deciding where to live, this stuff matters more than outsiders think. When people ask me what it is like to live in Las Vegas, hockey comes up constantly. The Golden Knights gave this valley a shared identity that did not exist in 2016. A jersey built around the city itself is the league recognizing that Las Vegas has a culture worth putting on a sweater.

Retail is the other piece. Golden Knights merchandise is sold all over the valley, from the team shop at T-Mobile Arena to sporting goods stores in Henderson and the northwest. A new sweater changes what those shelves look like for the next year. Local shops that carry team gear plan their fall inventory around exactly this kind of announcement, and so do the people who buy from them.

Background and History

The Golden Knights started play in the 2017-18 season and immediately became one of the strangest and best stories in modern sports. An expansion team reached the Stanley Cup Final in its first year. The city, still processing the October 1 tragedy that happened weeks before the opener, adopted the team fast and completely. That bond is why jersey news gets treated like real news here.

The uniform set has been stable since the beginning. Steel gray, gold, red, and black have carried the brand. The gold home sweater, introduced as a metallic gold look, gave the team one of the most recognizable kits in the league. The white road uniform handled away games. The gray filled the third slot in the rotation and became a fan favorite for its clean, understated look.

Across the NHL, alternate and special-edition jerseys have become a regular business. Teams have run Reverse Retro programs, Winter Classic sweaters, Stadium Series looks, and city-themed uniforms borrowed from the NBA playbook. Fanatics took over NHL uniform production, and league-wide programs like Hometown Remix are part of that partnership.

Vegas has already been marking its tenth season in other ways, including new center ice artwork. Season X branding has been showing up all offseason. Adding a new sweater to that mix fits the pattern of a franchise that understands how to build a moment. This is a team that has never been shy about spectacle, which is fitting for the city it plays in.

It helps to remember how young this all is. City National Arena in Summerlin opened as the team's practice facility and immediately became one of the busiest public rinks in the Southwest. Lifeguard Arena opened in downtown Henderson a few years later. Lee's Family Forum in Henderson gave the American Hockey League affiliate a permanent home. In under a decade, a desert valley built a real hockey infrastructure from nothing, and the uniforms are the most visible part of that identity.

What Happens Next

The next milestone is the start of the season in September. The Review-Journal reported that more details on the new jersey are expected after that point. Practically, that means fans should watch for an official team reveal, a Fanatics product drop, and a list of the specific home dates the Hometown Remix sweater will be worn.

Expect the rollout to follow the usual pattern. Teams typically tease the design first, then hold a reveal event, then put the jersey on sale through the team shop and Fanatics at the same time. Authentic versions usually sell out fastest in popular sizes and popular player numbers, so if you want one on day one, be ready to move quickly.

Keep an eye on the gray sweater too. When a jersey moves to throwback status, retailers often discount remaining inventory, and the team will sometimes schedule specific throwback nights so fans have a reason to keep wearing it. If you love the gray, this is likely the last stretch where it is easy to find at full retail.

Nothing about the design itself is confirmed yet, so treat any color rumor or leaked mockup you see online with real skepticism until the team or the league posts it. The only sourced facts right now are the ones in the Review-Journal report.

One more thing to watch is whether the team pairs the jersey with a promotional giveaway night. Clubs often hand out a hat, a rally towel, or a replica item tied to a new uniform, and those dates sell out early. When the promotional schedule posts, that is the moment to grab tickets if you want to be in the building for the debut.

A Few More Things Worth Knowing

Jersey rotations matter for resale value in the fan collectibles world. Sweaters that were only worn for a season or two often become the hardest to find later. If you are the kind of fan who keeps jerseys long term, a first-year Hometown Remix sweater from the tenth season is the type of item that holds interest.

Customization is another thing to think about. Authentic jerseys with player names and numbers cost more and take longer to ship, and if a player is traded, that name stays on your sweater. Plenty of local fans buy blank jerseys for exactly that reason, then add a patch or a number later.

Finally, remember that this is a league-wide program. Other NHL clubs are getting Hometown Remix looks too, which means the reveal calendar will be crowded. Vegas fans should watch specifically for the Golden Knights announcement rather than assuming a league-wide teaser applies to this team's design.

Sizing is worth a mention as well. Authentic on-ice jerseys run differently than the replica versions most fans buy, and returns on customized gear are usually limited. If you have never bought an authentic before, trying one on at the arena team shop before ordering online will save you a headache. Local hockey shops in the southwest valley and Henderson can help with sizing too.

The short version is simple. Three looks this season instead of two, the gold and white stay, the gray becomes a throwback, and a brand new city-themed sweater joins the rotation for Season X. Details land after the season opens in September.
