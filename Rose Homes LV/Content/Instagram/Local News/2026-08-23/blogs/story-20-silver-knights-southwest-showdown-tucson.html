Silver Knights Will Play Two Preseason Games at the New Southwest Showdown in Tucson

The Henderson Silver Knights will play two preseason exhibition games at the first Southwest Showdown, a new four-team event hosted by the Tucson Roadrunners at Tucson Arena from September 25 to 27. The team announced it on August 20, 2026, which means Henderson hockey families finally have a firm date on the calendar for the return of live Silver Knights hockey.

The Silver Knights will share the weekend with the Roadrunners, the Colorado Eagles, and the Texas Stars. There is also a fan event on Friday, September 25, where several Silver Knights players will meet fans. For anyone in Green Valley, Anthem, Inspirada, or Cadence who counts down to the first drop of the puck every fall, this is the first real marker of the new season.

What Happened

On August 20, 2026, the Henderson Silver Knights announced that the team will take part in the first Southwest Showdown. The event is hosted by the Tucson Roadrunners and runs from September 25 to 27 at Tucson Arena in downtown Tucson, Arizona. Four American Hockey League clubs are involved: the host Roadrunners, the Henderson Silver Knights, the Colorado Eagles, and the Texas Stars.

Henderson will play two preseason exhibition games across that weekend. Preseason games in the AHL are not the same as regular season games. They do not count in the standings, and rosters are usually much deeper than what you see once the season starts. Coaches use these games to look at young players, camp invites, and prospects who are trying to earn a contract or a roster spot. That is part of what makes preseason fun to watch. You get to see names you have never heard before, and a few of them turn into players you cheer for all year.

The weekend is built as a fan event as well as a hockey event. On Friday, September 25, several Silver Knights players will be on hand to meet fans. Team-hosted meet and greets like this are usually far more relaxed than anything you can get during the regular season, when players are locked into a game-day routine. If you have a kid who plays youth hockey in Henderson or Las Vegas, that kind of access matters. It is the sort of thing they remember.

This is the first year for the Southwest Showdown, so there is no history to compare it to. What we do know is the format on paper. Four clubs, one building, one long weekend in late September. Tucson Arena sits inside the Tucson Convention Center complex downtown, which puts hotels, food, and parking within a short walk of the rink. That layout matters if you are traveling with a family and do not want to spend the weekend in a car.

The four clubs involved are also worth a moment. The Roadrunners are the host and the closest AHL club to Southern Nevada. The Colorado Eagles and the Texas Stars are both established programs with their own long-running fan bases. Putting those four organizations in one building for a weekend is a different kind of event than a single exhibition game against whoever happens to be available. It gives the weekend a shape, and it gives fans a reason to stay for more than one night.

The Silver Knights have not released ticket details, exact game times, or opponent matchups for their two games in this announcement. Fans should watch the team's official site and the Tucson Roadrunners for the full schedule as the date gets closer. What a Preseason Weekend Actually Looks Like

If you have never been to a professional preseason game, it helps to know what you are walking into. The building is quieter. The crowd is smaller. The pace of the game can swing from sloppy to genuinely fast depending on which line is out there. None of that is a knock. It is simply what happens when a coaching staff is running an evaluation instead of chasing two points in the standings.

What you get in exchange is access and honesty. Preseason lineups tend to be long, which means more players get shifts than you would ever see in January. Young players who are fighting for a job play like it. There is an intensity to that kind of hockey that regular season games do not always have, because for some of the guys on the ice, the next three days decide the next three years.

It also changes the feel of the building. Smaller crowds mean better sightlines, shorter concession lines, and a real chance of standing near the glass during warmups. If your kid has never seen a professional player up close, a preseason weekend is often a better first experience than a sold-out regular season night. The Friday fan event on September 25 stacks on top of that.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Hockey is not a side note in Henderson anymore. It is part of the identity of the city. The Silver Knights are the American Hockey League affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights, and they play their home games at Lee's Family Forum in Henderson. For thousands of families in Green Valley, Anthem, Seven Hills, Inspirada, and Cadence, the Silver Knights season is the fall and winter calendar. Knowing that the team starts playing again on September 25 gives people something to plan around.

There is a practical side to this too. Tucson is a manageable trip from Southern Nevada. It is roughly a seven hour drive from Henderson, and there are short flights out of Harry Reid International. That makes this a realistic weekend getaway rather than a fantasy road trip. Families who would never fly across the country to see an away game can absolutely make a Friday to Sunday run to Tucson work, especially with a fan event built into the Friday.

For parents of kids in the local hockey pipeline, the value goes past the games themselves. Southern Nevada has grown a real youth hockey community over the past decade, and the kids in it now have professional players in their own backyard to look up to. A weekend where those players are meeting fans face to face is the kind of thing that keeps a 10 year old excited about a sport that asks a lot from a family in time and money.

And if you are new to the valley, this is a quick lesson in what makes Henderson feel like its own city rather than a suburb of Las Vegas. Henderson has its own arena, its own pro team, its own theme nights, and its own crowd. People who move here from hockey markets in the Midwest, Canada, or the Northeast are often surprised at how strong the scene is in the desert. It is one of the small things that makes Henderson an easier sell to buyers than the raw numbers suggest.

Background and History

The Silver Knights arrived in Southern Nevada as the Golden Knights built out their own development system. Having the AHL club in the same metro area as the NHL club is a real advantage. Call-ups can happen in hours instead of days, and prospects live and train in the same region as the big club. Very few NHL teams have that setup. It is a competitive edge and it is also a gift to local fans, because you can watch tomorrow's Golden Knights before anyone outside Nevada knows their names.

Lee's Family Forum in Henderson became the team's home and gave the franchise a permanent identity in the city. Before that, the team played in downtown Las Vegas while the Henderson building was being finished. Having a dedicated arena changed everything. It created a real season ticket base, a real game-day atmosphere, and a reason for families in the southeast valley to build a Friday night around hockey instead of driving to the Strip.

Preseason tournaments are not new to the AHL, but a branded, multi-team event in the Southwest is a newer idea. The southwestern corner of the league has grown, and the travel map for AHL teams in this region is very different from the traditional Northeast and Midwest footprint. Tucson, Henderson, Colorado, and Texas are all long hauls from one another during the regular season. Packing four of those clubs into one building for one weekend in September is an efficient way to get preseason work done and to build regional rivalries at the same time.

It also reflects something bigger about hockey in the desert. Twenty years ago, the idea of Las Vegas, Henderson, and Tucson forming a hockey corridor would have sounded like a joke. Now it is a schedule. The Golden Knights arriving in 2017 changed the temperature of the entire region for the sport, and the growth in youth programs, adult leagues, and rink time across Clark County followed.

How the Silver Knights Fit the Golden Knights Pipeline

The Silver Knights exist to develop players for the Vegas Golden Knights. That relationship shapes everything about a weekend like the Southwest Showdown. The players who suit up in Tucson in late September are, in most cases, players the organization is still making decisions about. Some are under contract. Some are trying to earn one. Some are coming off a season in juniors or college and getting their first look at the professional pace.

That is why preseason results matter so little and preseason performances matter so much. Nobody in the organization will remember who won a September exhibition game in Tucson. Plenty of people will remember the young defenseman who did not look out of place against a lineup of older, stronger players. The Silver Knights are where those judgments get made, and a four-team event compresses several of those looks into one weekend.

For fans in Henderson, that creates a genuinely fun long game. You watch a player in September, follow him through the winter at Lee's Family Forum, and then one night the Golden Knights announce a call-up and you already know the name. That experience is not available in most NHL markets, because the affiliate is usually hundreds or thousands of miles away. Here it is a short drive.

What Happens Next

The next thing to watch for is the full schedule. The team announcement confirmed the dates, the venue, the four clubs, and the Friday fan event. It did not confirm exact puck drop times for Henderson's two games or which opponents the Silver Knights will face. Those details usually follow within a few weeks of an announcement like this, and they will come from either the Silver Knights or the Tucson Roadrunners.

Ticket information is the other open question. For a first-year event in a mid-sized downtown arena, tickets tend to be far more affordable than an NHL preseason game, and single-game and weekend options are both possible. If you are planning a trip, watch for on-sale dates before you book hotels, because a downtown Tucson weekend in late September can fill up quickly once the schedule is public.

Details on the Friday fan event are also still thin. The announcement confirmed that several Silver Knights players will meet fans, but not the time, the location within the venue, or whether there is a separate ticket or registration involved. If meeting players is the reason your family wants to make the trip, that is the piece of information to wait on before you book anything nonrefundable.

After Tucson, attention shifts back to Henderson and the start of the regular season at Lee's Family Forum. The Silver Knights have already released their theme night and giveaway schedule for 2026-27, so families can line up the Tucson trip with the home dates they care about most. Training camp roster news out of the Golden Knights organization will also shape which prospects show up in Tucson, so the two things move together. If a young forward has a strong camp with Vegas, you may see him in a Silver Knights sweater in September and in a Golden Knights sweater by midseason.

Ryan's Take

I sell homes in this valley for a living, and I will tell you that the things people actually love about a neighborhood almost never show up in a listing. Nobody writes "20 minutes from a pro hockey arena where your kid can meet the players" in the remarks. But that is exactly the kind of detail that makes a family fall in love with Henderson after they move here.

Henderson has quietly built a full lifestyle package. Good schools, real parks, master-planned communities like Inspirada and Cadence, and a downtown-adjacent arena that fills up on winter nights. When buyers ask me why Henderson holds value the way it does, the honest answer is that it is a place people want to stay. Amenities like the Silver Knights are part of that stickiness. A first-year regional tournament in Tucson is a small story on its own, but it is one more sign that hockey here is not a novelty. It is infrastructure now, and infrastructure is what holds neighborhoods together.

I also think about this from the relocation side. A lot of the buyers I work with are moving here from places where a winter sport was part of the family routine. They worry about losing that. Then they find out there is an AHL team a few minutes from the house they are considering, with theme nights, giveaways, and ticket prices that do not require a second mortgage. That conversation changes how a neighborhood feels to them, and it happens more often than you would think.

What You Can Do

If you want to go, start by following the Henderson Silver Knights official site and social channels for the Southwest Showdown schedule and ticket release. The Tucson Roadrunners will post the same information from the host side, and sometimes the host announces schedule details first. Set a reminder for early to mid September so you are not scrambling the week of the event.

If you are planning the trip itself, hold off on locking in hotels until the game times are public. A Friday to Sunday window is the natural shape of the weekend given the September 25 fan event, but knowing whether Henderson plays Friday night or Sunday afternoon will change whether you need two nights or three. Downtown Tucson keeps the arena, food, and lodging close together, which is worth paying attention to if you are traveling with kids.

If a road trip is not realistic, you can still plan the home season now. The Silver Knights have released 14 theme nights and 24 giveaway dates for 2026-27 at Lee's Family Forum, which is the cheapest quality family night out in Henderson. Pick two or three dates, put them on the calendar, and buy early. Theme nights with popular giveaways tend to move fastest.

And if hockey is something your family is getting into for the first time, look into local youth and learn to skate programs around the valley. Ice time is one of the real perks of living in Clark County that newcomers do not expect. Between Henderson, Summerlin, and the northwest valley, there are more options than most people realize when they first arrive. Start with a learn to skate session before you invest in gear. Most families figure out pretty quickly whether the sport is going to stick.
