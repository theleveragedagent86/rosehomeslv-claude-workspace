### 1. Feds Finalize Colorado River Plan That Cuts Nevada's Water Allocation by 17 Percent
- **Category:** Government and Development
- **Story Type:** Local
- **County/Area:** Clark County (valley wide)
- **Viral Score:** Red
- **Extremely Important:** Yes
- **Viral Reasoning:** Every household in Clark County drinks Colorado River water, and a signed federal cut with a hard start date turns a long-running worry into a dated, personal deadline that people will argue about in the comments.
- **Scoring Reasoning:** Major policy change that directly affects every homeowner and renter in the valley, with a concrete number and a January 1 effective date. Red tier, and it is the one story this week worth leading with, so it carries the Extremely Important flag.
- **Summary:** The U.S. Department of the Interior finalized a two-year operating blueprint and a 10-year adaptive framework for the Colorado River on August 21, 2026, cutting Nevada's annual allocation by roughly 17 percent, about 50,000 acre-feet. Interior Secretary Doug Burgum signed the 2027-2028 Operating Guidelines and the Record of Decision for the Post-2026 Colorado River Operations Environmental Impact Statement. The cut takes effect January 1, 2027, and the three Lower Basin states must collectively reduce consumption by 1.25 million acre-feet per year. Lake Mead sits at about 1,039 feet and roughly 27 percent full.
- **Source:** Las Vegas Sun; Nevada Current; 8 News Now; Las Vegas Review-Journal
- **URL:** https://lasvegassun.com/news/2026/aug/22/feds-finalize-10-year-colorado-river-management-fr/

- **Date:** August 22, 2026 (Nevada Current, August 21, 2026)
- **Why It Matters Locally:** Clark County draws about 90 percent of its drinking water from the Colorado River, so this decision shapes water rules, landscaping policy, and growth limits for every household here.
