CCSD Doubles Ticket Taker Pay to $20 an Hour in Revised Teachers Union Contract

Clark County School District trustees voted 7-0 on August 27, 2026 to approve revisions to the 2025-2027 negotiated agreement with the Clark County Education Association, and the headline change is simple. Ticket taker pay goes from $10 per hour to $20 per hour. If you have ever sat in a folding chair at the gate of a Friday night football game and scanned tickets for four hours, your pay for that work just doubled.

The vote covered more than one line item. The revised agreement also requires that employees working extended day extracurricular activities be told in advance what they will be paid, spells out how pay gets prorated, and restores an athletic stipend computation table that had been deleted by accident. The district reported no immediate fiscal impact from the changes.

That last detail matters. This is not a new pot of money. It is a cleanup of an existing agreement, plus one rate that had fallen far behind what anyone in Southern Nevada would accept for an evening of work.

What Happened at the August 27 Board Meeting

The Clark County School District Board of Trustees took up revisions to the negotiated agreement between the district and the Clark County Education Association, the union that represents licensed CCSD employees. The agreement in question covers the 2025-2027 term. Trustees approved the revisions unanimously, 7-0.

Four changes are named in the district's agenda documents. First, employees who work extended day extracurricular activities must now be informed in advance of the rate of pay for that assignment. Second, the agreement spells out proration rules, meaning how pay is calculated when an assignment does not run a full term or a full season. Third, an athletic stipend computation table that had been removed from the agreement in error gets restored. Fourth, the hourly rate for ticket takers rises from $10 to $20.

The district reported no immediate fiscal impact. That phrase shows up often in board documents and it usually means the change is either already covered inside an existing budget line or it is small enough that it does not require a new appropriation. It does not mean the change is free. It means the district does not expect it to move the budget in this cycle.

None of this was a contested fight at the podium. There was no split vote, no long debate reported, no dramatic public comment. It was a housekeeping item with one real number attached to it. Which is exactly why it is worth pulling apart, because housekeeping items are where the actual working conditions of thousands of school employees get set.

Coverage of the meeting came from Opportunity 180's Nevada Ed-Watch series, which tracks CCSD Board of Trustees agendas and summarizes the votes. Their write-up of the August 27, 2026 meeting is where these details are documented.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with the people directly affected. Somebody sits at the gate of every high school football game, every basketball game, every volleyball match, every band concert with an admission charge, and every wrestling meet in Clark County. That person is often a teacher, a support professional, or a school employee picking up extra hours after a full workday. At $10 an hour, that work was barely worth the gas to drive back to campus. At $20 an hour, it becomes a real supplemental shift.

Clark County School District is one of the largest school districts in the country, and it runs a huge number of after-school events across the valley. Every one of those events needs staff at the door. When the rate is too low, schools struggle to fill the assignment, and when schools struggle to fill the assignment, the burden falls on whoever is already there. Athletic directors and coaches end up covering the gate themselves. Doubling the rate is a direct attempt to make that job worth taking.

The advance notice requirement may end up mattering just as much. Under the revised agreement, an employee working an extended day extracurricular activity has to be told what the pay is before the assignment starts. That sounds obvious. In practice, school staff have long taken on extra-duty work without a clear number attached, then waited to see what showed up on the check. Writing the disclosure into the agreement moves that conversation to the front end.

For families across Las Vegas, Henderson, North Las Vegas and the unincorporated valley, the connection is less direct but still real. Extracurricular activities are staffed by adults who have to be paid. When staffing gets easier, games and clubs and after-school programs run more smoothly. When staffing gets hard, events get scaled back, start times get moved, and some programs quietly stop happening. The gate worker rate is a small piece of a much larger question about whether a school can keep its activity calendar full.

There is also a fairness angle that is easy to miss. Extra-duty work in a school district is not distributed evenly. Some campuses have deep benches of staff willing to pick up evening assignments. Others, often the schools with the highest need, rely on the same handful of people over and over. A rate that nobody wants to work at makes that imbalance worse, because the schools with the most volunteers absorb it and the schools without them do not. Raising the rate at least gives every campus the same tool to recruit with.

And for renters and buyers weighing one part of the valley against another, school activity programs are part of what makes a zoned high school feel like a real community anchor. A packed gym on a Tuesday night, a marching band competition, a wrestling meet with a full parking lot, those are the things people remember about a neighborhood. They do not happen without staff at the door.

How Extra Duty Pay Works in a School District

To understand why a table and a proration rule matter, it helps to know how school employees actually get paid for work outside the school day. Regular salary comes from a salary schedule based on education and years of service. Anything beyond the contracted day falls into a separate bucket, and that bucket has two main shapes.

The first shape is the stipend. A stipend is a flat amount paid for taking on a defined role over a defined period, like coaching a sport or advising a club. It is not tied to hours worked. A coach who spends sixty hours on a season and a coach who spends a hundred and twenty hours on the same season can receive the same stipend, which is why the computation table matters so much. The table is what determines the amount, and it typically accounts for factors like the activity and the level of the assignment.

The second shape is the hourly extra-duty rate. This is what a ticket taker gets. You work the event, you log the hours, you get paid the rate. It is simpler than a stipend, and it is the category where an outdated number is most obvious, because an hourly figure sits right next to every other hourly wage in the local economy. When $10 an hour is printed in the agreement and the grocery store down the street is advertising more than that, the comparison makes itself.

Proration bridges the two. When a stipend assignment does not run the full period, somebody has to decide what fraction is owed. Written proration language removes that decision from the individual school and puts it in the agreement, where it applies the same way everywhere. That is not a glamorous change, but it is the kind of clarity that prevents disputes from turning into grievances.

Background and History

A negotiated agreement between a school district and a union is a long, technical document. It sets salary schedules, working hours, transfer rules, grievance procedures, and dozens of stipends for extra duties that fall outside the regular workday. The Clark County Education Association represents licensed CCSD employees, and the 2025-2027 agreement is the current contract term.

Documents that long get amended. Language gets moved, tables get renumbered, and sometimes something gets dropped that nobody meant to drop. That is what happened with the athletic stipend computation table. It was deleted by accident, and the August 27 vote puts it back. A stipend computation table is the reference chart that determines how a coaching or activity stipend is calculated. Without it in the document, the calculation has no written anchor, which creates room for disputes that nobody wants.

Proration rules cover a similar gap. If a coach takes over a team halfway through a season, or an advisor picks up a club in the spring instead of the fall, how much of the stipend do they earn? Spelling that out in the agreement removes guesswork. It also removes the possibility of two schools handling the same situation two different ways.

As for the $10 ticket taker rate, that number had clearly been sitting in the agreement for a long time. Nevada's minimum wage has climbed over recent years, and $10 an hour for an evening assignment stopped being competitive with almost any other part-time work in the valley. Raising it to $20 brings the rate closer to what the local labor market actually pays for a few hours of evening work.

What Happens Next

The revisions are approved, so the next step is implementation at the school level. Athletic directors, activity coordinators and school office staff have to apply the new rate and the new notice requirement to assignments going forward. The fall sports season is already underway across Clark County, so the practical test comes quickly.

Watch for whether the advance notice requirement actually changes how extra-duty assignments get offered. The language is in the agreement now. Whether every campus follows it consistently in the first semester is a different question, and it is the kind of thing that surfaces through grievances rather than board meetings.

Also watch the fiscal side. The district reported no immediate fiscal impact, but a rate that doubles will show up in event budgets somewhere. Individual schools fund gate staffing out of athletic and activity accounts, and those accounts are built on gate receipts, fundraising and school allocations. If the cost of staffing the gate goes up and the revenue does not, schools will have to adjust. That adjustment could show up in future budget discussions rather than this one.

The 2025-2027 agreement runs through its term, and further revisions can come back to the board the same way these did. Anyone who wants to track it can follow CCSD Board of Trustees agendas, which are posted ahead of each meeting.

One more thing worth watching is whether other extra-duty rates in the agreement get the same treatment. If a $10 ticket taker rate had drifted this far out of line, it is fair to ask what else in the document has been sitting untouched. The board handled this one as a revision rather than waiting for a full contract cycle, which suggests the same path is available for other outdated line items.

Ryan's Take

I follow school district votes closely because buyers ask me about schools constantly, and the things that shape a school's reputation are rarely the things that make headlines. A stipend table and a gate worker rate will never lead the evening news. But the health of a school's activity program is one of the first things a family notices when they move into a neighborhood. Does the school have a full sports calendar? Are the clubs actually running? Is there somebody at the door on game night, or did they cancel the event?

Those answers come from staffing, and staffing comes from whether the extra-duty work is worth taking. Doubling the ticket taker rate is a small fix, but it is the kind of small fix that keeps the Friday night lights on. In neighborhoods where the high school is the center of community life, and there are plenty of those across Clark County, that matters more than a test score chart.

The advance notice piece is the one I would highlight to any CCSD employee reading this. Knowing the rate before you commit to the shift is basic, and now it is written down. If you work extra-duty assignments, that is worth knowing.

What You Can Do

If you are a CCSD employee who works extended day extracurricular activities, ask your school for the pay rate in writing before you accept the assignment. The revised agreement requires advance notice of the rate. If you are not getting it, that is now a documented expectation rather than an informal courtesy, and your union representative is the right person to raise it with.

If you coach or advise an activity, look at the restored athletic stipend computation table and the proration language in the current agreement. Those two pieces determine what you earn when an assignment starts late, ends early, or gets split. Knowing how the calculation works before the season is easier than sorting it out afterward.

If you are a parent, the practical version of this story is that gate staffing just got easier to fill. Show up to the games. Buy the ticket. School activity accounts run on gate receipts, and the events that draw a crowd are the ones that survive the budget conversation.

Anyone who wants to follow future votes can watch CCSD Board of Trustees agendas, which are published before each meeting, and can sign up for public comment. The items that shape day-to-day school operations almost always move through consent and routine agenda items, not through the ones that draw a crowd.
