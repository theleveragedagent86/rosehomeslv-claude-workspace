Silver Knights Home Opener Set for October 3 at Lee's Family Forum

Henderson hockey is back on October 3, when the Henderson Silver Knights open the 2026-27 season at home at Lee's Family Forum against the Bakersfield Condors. Before that, the team plays only two preseason games, both on the road in Tucson, which means the October 3 opener is the first chance anyone in Southern Nevada gets to see this roster in person.

That is a tight runway. The Silver Knights announced on August 20, 2026 that they will take part in the first Southwest Showdown, hosted by the Tucson Roadrunners at Tucson Arena from September 25 to 27. Henderson plays Tucson on Saturday, September 26 at 7 p.m. and the Colorado Eagles on Sunday, September 27 at 3 p.m. Then the team comes home. We covered the tournament announcement itself in Silver Knights Will Play Two Preseason Games at the New Southwest Showdown in Tucson. This piece is about the six days between the last preseason game and the home opener, and what Henderson families should actually plan for.

The Calendar Between Now and Opening Night

Here is the whole runway in one place. The Silver Knights play two preseason games, both away. Saturday, September 26 against the host Tucson Roadrunners at 7 p.m. Sunday, September 27 against the Colorado Eagles at 3 p.m. Both are at Tucson Arena in downtown Tucson, Arizona, as part of the first Southwest Showdown. That event runs September 25 to 27 and is hosted by the Roadrunners.

Six days later, on Saturday, October 3, the Silver Knights host the Bakersfield Condors at Lee's Family Forum in Henderson. That is the regular season opener and the first home game of the year. There is nothing between the Sunday afternoon game in Tucson and the Saturday home opener. No home exhibition, no tune-up game in the valley, nothing local for fans to attend first.

That matters more than it sounds. In a lot of American Hockey League markets, fans get a home preseason game to shake off the summer. Henderson does not get one this year. The first time the doors open at Lee's Family Forum for a Silver Knights game in 2026-27 is a game that counts in the standings. If you have been waiting all summer, October 3 is the date, and there is no soft opening before it.

The compressed schedule also puts real pressure on the coaching staff. Two exhibition games is a short evaluation window. Rosters in a preseason weekend are usually deep, with camp invites and prospects mixed in, so the staff has to sort a large group across roughly four periods of meaningful ice time per player group. Whoever is in the lineup on October 3 will have been chosen off a very small sample.

One more thing worth flagging. The Silver Knights are the American Hockey League affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights, and roster decisions at the NHL level ripple down. Players who do not stick with Vegas out of training camp often land in Henderson. That means the group you see in Tucson in late September may not be the group that skates on October 3. Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

For a lot of families in Green Valley, Anthem, Seven Hills, Inspirada and Cadence, the Silver Knights schedule is the fall and winter calendar. Once the home dates start, Friday and Saturday nights have a default plan. October 3 is the day that plan switches on, and it is worth putting on the family calendar now rather than in late September.

The practical value is real. A night at Lee's Family Forum is one of the more affordable ways to take a family out in Henderson. You are not driving to the Strip, you are not paying resort parking, and you are usually home before the kids fall apart. For households in the southeast valley, the arena is a short drive rather than a trip across the entire metro area. That convenience is the whole reason the building works.

There is also the timing to think about. Early October in Southern Nevada is when the weather finally turns pleasant and everyone wants to be out of the house. School is well underway, youth sports are in full swing, and family schedules are already tight. A single Saturday night home opener sitting right at the front of that window is easy to build around, but only if you claim the date before something else takes it.

And for anyone new to the valley, the home opener is a decent introduction to how Henderson actually works. Henderson is not a suburb that borrows its identity from Las Vegas. It has its own arena, its own professional team, its own crowd and its own traditions. People who move here from hockey markets in the Midwest, Canada or the Northeast are usually surprised at how established the scene is in the desert. Opening night is the clearest single look at that.

Planning the Trip to Lee's Family Forum

If October 3 is going to be your night, a little planning goes a long way. Lee's Family Forum sits in Henderson, which for most of the southeast valley means a drive measured in minutes rather than freeway miles. Coming from Green Valley, Anthem, Seven Hills or Inspirada, the trip is short enough that you can eat dinner at home first if you want to. Coming from the northwest valley or Summerlin, plan for a real drive across the metro and give yourself the extra half hour.

Opening night is different from a random Tuesday in January. Crowds are bigger, arrival is more bunched up, and the parking lot fills earlier than usual. If you are bringing kids, getting there before doors rather than right at puck drop is the difference between a relaxed night and a stressful one. Warmups are also the best free part of the evening. Kids who get to the glass early tend to remember that more than the game itself.

Concessions, seating tiers, parking cost and any opening night promotion are things the team publishes closer to the date, and none of that has been announced for October 3 yet. Treat those specifics as [NOT VERIFIED]. What is confirmed is the date, the opponent and the building: Saturday, October 3, Bakersfield Condors, Lee's Family Forum in Henderson.

One planning note for parents. American Hockey League games run three 20 minute periods with two intermissions, so a full night at the rink is roughly two and a half hours from puck drop to final horn. For a Saturday, that is very manageable even with younger kids. It is one reason AHL hockey works so well as a family outing compared to sports that stretch past bedtime.

Background and History

The Silver Knights came to Southern Nevada as part of the Golden Knights building out their own development system. Keeping the AHL club inside the same metro area as the NHL club is unusual and valuable. Call-ups take hours instead of days, prospects train in the same region as the big club, and local fans get to watch players before anyone outside Nevada knows the names.

Lee's Family Forum gave the franchise a permanent home in Henderson and changed what the team could be. Before the Henderson building was ready, the team played in downtown Las Vegas. A dedicated arena created a real season ticket base, a real game night atmosphere, and a reason for southeast valley families to make hockey a weekly habit instead of an occasional outing.

Short preseason schedules are normal in the American Hockey League. Unlike the NHL, where clubs often play six or more exhibition games, AHL teams typically get a handful at most. Two is on the light end but not unusual, especially for a club in the southwestern corner of the league where every opponent is a long haul. That travel math is exactly why a four-team event in one building makes sense for Henderson.

The bigger picture is that a hockey corridor now exists in the desert. Henderson, Tucson, Colorado and Texas are all AHL stops, and the region has enough clubs to organize a preseason weekend around. Twenty years ago that would have sounded like a joke. The Golden Knights arriving in 2017 changed the temperature for the sport across the entire region, and youth programs, adult leagues and rink demand across Clark County followed.

What Happens Next

The next thing on the calendar is Vegas Golden Knights training camp news. Those decisions shape the Henderson roster. Watch for which prospects and camp invites get assigned down, because that group is what walks into Lee's Family Forum on October 3. If a young forward has a strong camp with Vegas, you may see him in Tucson in September and in a Golden Knights sweater later in the winter.

After that, watch for the Silver Knights to release opening night details. Doors-open time, promotional giveaways, any pregame ceremony, parking guidance and single-game ticket availability all typically come out in the two to three weeks before a home opener. None of that has been announced yet, so anything you read about opening night promotions right now should be treated as unconfirmed.

Then there is the Tucson weekend itself. The two preseason games on September 26 and September 27 are the only public look at the team before the home opener. Neither is in Henderson, so unless you are making the trip, you will be following along through team channels and box scores. Tucson is a manageable drive from Southern Nevada and there are short flights out of Harry Reid International, so a Friday to Sunday run is realistic for families who want the early look.

The opponent is worth a note too. The Bakersfield Condors are the American Hockey League affiliate of the Edmonton Oilers and play in the same Pacific Division as Henderson. Division games carry more weight in a league where the standings are tight and travel is brutal, so an opening night against a divisional opponent is a real two points rather than a warm-up. That is a better first game than a nonconference visitor would have been.

Beyond opening night, the thing to watch is how the roster settles over October. Early season AHL lineups move constantly. Injuries at the NHL level, call-ups, reassignments and professional tryout contracts all reshuffle the group in the first few weeks. The team you see on October 3 will not be exactly the team you see at Thanksgiving, and that churn is a normal part of following a development club.

Ryan's Take

I sell homes in this valley for a living, and the things people fall in love with about a neighborhood almost never show up in the listing remarks. Nobody writes "fifteen minutes from a pro hockey arena where opening night is a family tradition." But that is the kind of detail that turns a house into a decision. Buyers who move to Henderson from hockey markets often assume they are giving that part of their life up, and then they find out the arena is a short drive from the street they are already considering.

What I keep noticing is that the amenity is not the arena by itself. It is the routine. A team with a home schedule gives a city a rhythm, and a rhythm gives people a reason to stay put. Henderson has quietly assembled a full lifestyle package over the last decade: good schools, real parks, master-planned communities like Inspirada and Cadence, and a building that fills up on winter nights. When people ask why Henderson holds value the way it does, that stickiness is a bigger part of the answer than any single statistic.

An October 3 home opener is a small story on its own. But it is the first date of a season that will fill four or five months of Saturday nights for thousands of Henderson families, and that is not a small thing at all.

What You Can Do

Start by putting October 3 on the family calendar right now, before youth sports, birthday parties and fall travel eat the date. Opening night at Lee's Family Forum is a Saturday, and Saturdays in early October go fast. If you want seats together, the earlier you claim the date the better.

Next, follow the Henderson Silver Knights official site and social channels for opening night details. Doors, promotions, giveaways and any ticket on-sale information will come from the team. If you want the road trip instead, the Tucson Roadrunners will carry the Southwest Showdown information from the host side, and the two Henderson games are September 26 at 7 p.m. and September 27 at 3 p.m.

If you are planning to make Silver Knights games a regular part of the season instead of a one-off, look at the full home schedule and the theme and giveaway nights together and pick your dates in a single sitting. Buying two or three games at once is easier than deciding week to week, and the most popular giveaway dates tend to move first. It is one of the better value family outings in Henderson, and planning ahead is the whole trick.

And if hockey is new to your household, use the fall to try a learn to skate session before you spend money on gear. Ice time is one of the genuine perks of living in Clark County that newcomers do not expect. Between Henderson, Summerlin and the northwest valley, there are more options than most families realize when they first arrive.
