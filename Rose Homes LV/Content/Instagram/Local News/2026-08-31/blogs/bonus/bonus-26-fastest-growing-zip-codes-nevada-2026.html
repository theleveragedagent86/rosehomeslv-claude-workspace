Three Clark County ZIP Codes Rank Among Nevada's Fastest Growing

Three of the five fastest growing ZIP codes in Nevada this year sit inside Clark County, and the fastest of all is not in Las Vegas at all. A mid-year report from the moving company MovingPlace, which looked at about 5 million moves, named Mesquite's 89034 the top growth ZIP code in the state, with roughly 73.3 new residents arriving per 1,000 people from January through May 2026.

Henderson's 89011, the ZIP code that covers Lake Las Vegas, came in second at about 50 moves per 1,000. Summerlin's 89138 landed third at 40.5. Southwest Las Vegas ZIP 89113 also made the state's top five, alongside Reno's 89501. The Las Vegas Review-Journal reported the findings on August 26, 2026.

If you own a home in one of those ZIP codes, this is worth reading closely. Growth data is not the same thing as price data, but it tells you something price data cannot. It tells you where the demand is walking in the door.

What the Report Actually Found

MovingPlace built its mid-year list by analyzing roughly 5 million moves recorded from January through May 2026. Instead of ranking places by raw headcount, it measured arrivals against existing population. That is why the list is expressed as movers per 1,000 residents. A small town with a few thousand new neighbors can beat a big city that added far more people in total.

Mesquite's 89034 topped Nevada at about 73.3 new residents per 1,000. That is a striking number for any ZIP code. It means that for every 1,000 people already living there, roughly 73 more showed up over a five month stretch. Mesquite is in Clark County, but it is not a Las Vegas neighborhood. It sits about 80 miles northeast of the Las Vegas Valley, right up against the Arizona line along Interstate 15.

Second place went to Henderson's 89011. That ZIP code covers Lake Las Vegas and the surrounding stretch of east Henderson. It logged about 50 moves per 1,000. Third was Summerlin's 89138 on the far west side of the valley at 40.5 moves per 1,000.

Southwest Las Vegas ZIP 89113 rounded out the state's top five along with Reno's 89501 in downtown Reno. The exact fourth and fifth place order between those two, and their individual per 1,000 rates, are [NOT VERIFIED] in the reporting available. What is clear is that four of Nevada's five fastest growing ZIP codes were in Clark County, and only one was in the north.

It also helps to understand what these ZIP codes are on the ground. 89011 runs through east Henderson and takes in Lake Las Vegas along with the neighborhoods around Lake Mead Parkway. 89138 sits on the far west edge of Summerlin near the 215 and the foothills below Red Rock. 89113 covers a chunk of southwest Las Vegas roughly around the Durango and Blue Diamond corridor. Three different price points, three different buyer profiles, and all three on the same statewide list.

One more thing worth naming up front. This is a moving company's dataset, not a Census release. It captures people who hired a mover and filed a move through that network. It does not capture every family that rented a truck and did it themselves, and it does not subtract everyone who moved out. So treat it as a strong directional signal about where interest is concentrated, not as an official population count.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with what this means if you are thinking about selling. Buyer demand is not spread evenly across Clark County. It clusters. When a report like this puts Lake Las Vegas, Summerlin and southwest Las Vegas near the top of a statewide list, it is telling sellers in those areas that the pool of people actively looking at their part of the valley is deeper than average right now.

That matters in a market that has been softening on price. Southern Nevada's median resale price has pulled back from its spring record, and inventory has been climbing across the valley. In that kind of market, location does a lot of heavy lifting. Two homes with the same square footage and the same condition can sit for very different lengths of time depending on how many buyers are shopping that specific pocket. If you are in 89011, 89138 or 89113, you have more traffic working in your favor than a seller across town does.

For buyers, the read is the opposite side of the same coin. High in-migration areas tend to have more competition, and they hold value better when the broader market cools. But you will usually pay for that. If your budget is tight, the smarter move can be to look one ZIP code over from the hot one, where you get similar commutes and similar schools without the premium that comes from being the address everyone is searching.

Renters feel this too. Places absorbing a lot of new arrivals tend to see rents firm up first, because new households need somewhere to live while they figure out where they want to buy. If you rent in east Henderson or the far southwest and your renewal is coming, it is worth shopping the market early rather than assuming the number will hold.

And then there is the day to day. Growth is not just a real estate story. More people in 89138 and 89113 means more cars on Sahara, Blue Diamond, the 215 and Fort Apache. It means schools filling up, grocery stores getting busier and longer waits at the urgent care. Anyone who has lived in Las Vegas through a growth cycle knows the infrastructure conversation always arrives a few years after the moving trucks.

There is a tax and insurance angle too. New arrivals to Nevada are often surprised by how the property tax abatement works here, and by how much a home's tax bill can differ from the one next door depending on when it last changed hands and whether it is owner occupied. If you are one of the people who just moved into 89011 or 89138, check that your primary residence tax cap is applied correctly. It is a small piece of paperwork that people miss in the chaos of a move, and it costs real money over time.

Background and History

None of this came out of nowhere. Nevada has been near the front of the national in-migration story for most of the last decade, and Clark County has carried most of that. The drivers are familiar to anyone who has sold a home here: no state income tax, housing that is still cheaper than coastal California, and a job base that has widened well past the Strip into logistics, health care, sports and professional services.

Summerlin's 89138 is the least surprising name on the list. The far west side has been the valley's most consistent growth engine for years, with new villages, new schools and a steady pipeline of homes coming out of the ground. When a master planned community keeps delivering inventory, it keeps posting migration numbers, because new construction gives arriving households somewhere to actually land.

Henderson's 89011 is a more interesting story. Lake Las Vegas spent years as one of the valley's cautionary tales after the last downturn, with stalled projects and a reputation for being far from everything. That has flipped. The area has filled in, homes have been built, and the combination of water views, golf and a quieter setting has found its buyer. Landing second in the state is a real turn for a place that used to be shorthand for overbuilding.

Mesquite deserves its own paragraph, because people misread it constantly. It is a Clark County city, so it shows up in Clark County datasets, but it is a separate community about 80 miles from the Las Vegas Valley. It has long drawn retirees and part-time residents who want golf, warm weather and lower prices, plus a steady flow of people crossing over from the Utah and Arizona side. Because its population base is small, a modest number of arrivals produces a very large per 1,000 rate. That is the math behind the number one ranking, and it is why a Mesquite headline does not tell you much about what is happening in Henderson.

It is also worth remembering how much of Southern Nevada's growth story is tied to land. Most of the developable ground in the valley is federal, and it only becomes housing when it is released and auctioned. That is a big reason growth keeps landing in the same handful of ZIP codes year after year. The far west and the far southwest are simply where the buildable land and the entitlements have been. Migration follows the shovels.

What Happens Next

The first thing to watch is whether the second half of 2026 holds this pattern. This report covers January through May. Summer is normally the heaviest moving stretch of the year in Southern Nevada, so the full year picture may look different from the mid-year snapshot. If MovingPlace or a similar dataset publishes a year-end version, the useful question will be whether Lake Las Vegas and Summerlin held their positions or whether the ranking reshuffled.

The second thing to watch is supply. In-migration only converts into recorded moves if there are homes to move into. Southwest Las Vegas and Summerlin both still have new construction coming, which is a big part of why they rank where they do. Builders in Southern Nevada have been more cautious this year, and if that caution deepens, growth can shift toward whichever ZIP codes still have product on the shelf. Watch permit activity in the far southwest and the northwest for early signals.

The third thing is what local government does with the information. Growth concentrated in specific ZIP codes puts pressure on specific school zones, specific road corridors and specific fire and police response areas. Henderson, Clark County and the Clark County School District all plan around enrollment and traffic projections. If the arrivals in 89011 and 89138 keep coming at this pace, expect those conversations to show up on public meeting agendas over the next year or two.

Finally, keep an eye on where these arrivals are coming from. Southern California has been the largest single feeder into Clark County for years, and reports on Nevada's growth have consistently pointed the same direction. If California outbound migration slows, the ZIP codes at the top of this list would feel it first, because they are the ones absorbing the most of it. If it holds steady, expect these same names to keep showing up.

Ryan's Take

I like this report because it measures something most market reports miss. Median price tells you what closed last month. Days on market tells you how fast. Neither one tells you where people are pointing themselves. Migration data does, and right now it is pointing at east Henderson, west Summerlin and the far southwest.

Here is how I would use it if you own in one of those ZIP codes. Do not read "fastest growing" as "you can name your price." The valley median has been drifting down from its spring high, and buyers still have choices. What the growth number really buys you is traffic, and traffic is what turns a good listing into a sold listing. Price it right, present it well, and you should see more showings than a comparable home in a slower pocket. That is a meaningful edge, but only if you use it.

The Mesquite number is the one I would be careful with. A 73.3 per 1,000 rate on a small population base is a real signal for Mesquite and almost no signal for the Las Vegas Valley 80 miles away. If you own in Summerlin and you see a headline about Nevada's fastest growing ZIP code, that headline is about a different market than yours. Use the 89138 number instead. It is the one that describes your street.

What You Can Do

If you are thinking about selling in the next six to twelve months, start by finding out what has actually been happening in your ZIP code, not the valley as a whole. Countywide medians blend Mesquite, Boulder City, North Las Vegas and Summerlin into one number that describes almost no one. Ask for closed sales and current active listings inside your ZIP, ideally inside your subdivision, and compare those to where your home sits on condition and finishes.

If you are buying, use the growth list as a shopping map rather than a shopping list. Look at 89011, 89138 and 89113, then look at the ZIP codes immediately next to them. You will often find homes with the same access to work, schools and shopping at a lower number, simply because they are not the address in the headline. That gap is where the value usually sits.

If you are staying put, this is still your business. Growth changes school boundaries, traffic patterns and what gets built near you. Clark County and the City of Henderson both post planning commission and council agendas online, and the Clark County School District publishes enrollment and rezoning information. Reading those a few times a year is the cheapest way to see what is coming to your neighborhood before it arrives.

One practical habit: set a saved search for your own ZIP code, not just the neighborhoods you like. Watching what lists, what goes pending and what sits in 89011 or 89138 for a couple of months teaches you more about your local market than any headline will. It takes five minutes to set up and it makes you a much sharper negotiator when it is your turn to buy or sell.

And if you simply want to know what the growth in your ZIP code has meant for your own home value, that is a short conversation and it does not cost anything.
