PWHL Las Vegas Is Preparing for Its Debut Season at T-Mobile Arena

Las Vegas is getting a professional women's hockey team, and it will play its home games at T-Mobile Arena. The Professional Women's Hockey League announced on May 13, 2026 that it is expanding to Las Vegas for the 2026-27 season, making our city one of the newest homes for the fastest growing pro league in North America.

The team is owned by the Mark Walter Group. Dominique DiDia is the general manager. The colors are green and gold. And for now, the club is simply called PWHL Las Vegas while a permanent name and logo get finalized.

If you have a daughter who plays hockey in this valley, or if you have ever wondered whether Las Vegas could support more than one pro hockey team, this is a big moment. Here is everything we know so far, and what it means for families and neighborhoods across Clark County.

What Happened

The PWHL made its Las Vegas announcement official on May 13, 2026. The league said Las Vegas would be an expansion market for the 2026-27 season, with home games played at T-Mobile Arena on the Strip. That is the same building the Vegas Golden Knights call home, which means PWHL Las Vegas is starting out in one of the best hockey venues in the country rather than a temporary practice facility.

Ownership belongs to the Mark Walter Group. That name carries weight in professional sports, and it signals that this is not a small experiment. The league also named Dominique DiDia as the team's general manager, giving the front office a leader to build the roster, the staff, and the culture from the ground up.

The team colors are green and gold. That palette gives Las Vegas a look that stands apart from the Golden Knights' steel gray and gold, and it gives merchandise designers something fresh to work with once the branding is locked in.

One thing that is still open is the name. The club is operating as PWHL Las Vegas while the permanent team name and logo are finalized. That is normal for a new franchise. Naming takes time because trademarks, fan input, and merchandise production all have to line up before anything gets announced. Until then, PWHL Las Vegas is what you will see on tickets and league materials.

Fans who want in early can put down a $50 season ticket membership deposit. That deposit is the standard way pro teams gauge demand in a new market and set up priority for seat selection later. As for the schedule, PWHL schedules typically come out in late September or early October, so the calendar for opening night and the rest of the home slate should land soon.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with the simplest reason. This is another reason to be downtown on the Strip on a weeknight that is not a Golden Knights game. T-Mobile Arena is already one of the busiest event buildings in the country. Adding a full PWHL home schedule means more dates, more foot traffic, and more people spending money at restaurants, bars, hotels, and parking garages around the arena district.

For families, the impact is more personal. Las Vegas has a growing youth hockey community. Kids skate at rinks across the valley, from City National Arena in Summerlin to the ice sheets on the north and east sides of town. Until now, a girl in this valley who wanted to see the very best women's players in the world had to watch them on a screen. Starting this season, she can watch them live, twenty minutes from her house.

That matters for participation numbers. Every market that has added a pro women's team has seen more girls sign up to play. More sign-ups mean more ice time demand, more learn-to-skate programs, and more pressure on the valley to build and maintain rinks. That is a real infrastructure conversation for Clark County, and it connects directly to the recent news about the ice arena rebuild in North Las Vegas.

There is also a plain quality-of-life angle. Las Vegas spent decades as a city that hosted other people's teams. Now we have the Golden Knights, the Raiders, the Aces, the Athletics on the way, and a PWHL club. Every one of those teams gives residents a reason to stay in town on a weekend instead of driving to Southern California. That is the kind of thing people quietly factor in when they decide where to buy a house.

Renters and homeowners near the arena corridor should expect the same thing they already deal with on Golden Knights nights, just on more evenings. Traffic on Frank Sinatra Drive and along the Tropicana and Las Vegas Boulevard corridors picks up before puck drop. That is a tradeoff residents in that area already understand, and most of them will tell you the energy is worth it.

And there is the workforce piece. More event dates at T-Mobile Arena means more shifts for the people who staff that building. Ushers, concessions workers, security, cleaning crews, and the restaurant staff in the surrounding blocks all pick up hours when the arena calendar fills. In a city where so many households depend on hospitality and event work, a full season of extra home dates is a paycheck for real people.

Background and History

The PWHL is young. It launched with a small group of teams and quickly proved that there was a real audience for top-level women's hockey in North America. Attendance records fell. Broadcast deals followed. What started as a league trying to survive turned into a league that markets were competing to join.

Las Vegas was an obvious candidate for a few reasons. The Golden Knights showed that this city would show up for hockey, and show up loudly. The Vegas Golden Knights arrived in 2017 as an expansion team that most national observers expected to struggle, and instead the fan base packed T-Mobile Arena from night one. That erased any doubt about whether a desert city could love a winter sport.

The valley also built out its hockey infrastructure in the years that followed. City National Arena in Summerlin became a community hub. Lifeguard Arena opened in downtown Henderson as the home of the Henderson Silver Knights. Youth programs expanded. Adult leagues filled up. A city that had almost no ice in the 1990s now has a real skating culture.

Ownership matters too. The Mark Walter Group's involvement gives PWHL Las Vegas financial backing and professional sports experience from day one. New franchises often struggle in year one because they are learning how to run a business at the same time they are learning how to run a hockey team. Starting with an experienced ownership group and an established arena removes a lot of that risk.

It also helps that the PWHL built its early reputation on quality of play rather than novelty. The rosters are stacked with Olympians and national team players from the United States, Canada, and across Europe. Fans who show up expecting a developmental product leave surprised at the speed and the physicality. That word of mouth is what has driven attendance growth in market after market, and it is what will drive it here once the first few home games are in the books.

And the timing lines up with a broader shift. Women's pro sports in the United States are drawing bigger crowds and bigger media deals than at any point in history. Las Vegas already saw this with the Las Vegas Aces, whose success made the case that this market supports women's professional teams enthusiastically. The PWHL is walking through a door the Aces helped open.

It is worth pausing on what T-Mobile Arena itself brings to this. The building opened in 2016 and seats roughly 17,500 for hockey. That is a large room for a league that has often played in smaller venues, and it means the team will not be capped by capacity if interest takes off. It also means the club can scale, curtaining off upper sections early on and opening them as demand grows. Sharing a building with an established NHL club gives PWHL Las Vegas access to professional ice crews, locker facilities, and event operations that a startup franchise would normally spend years building.

What Happens Next

The nearest milestone is the schedule. PWHL schedules typically come out in late September or early October, so fans should watch for the 2026-27 calendar in that window. That release will tell you when opening night at T-Mobile Arena happens, how many home dates the team has, and which weekends to keep clear.

The team name and logo are the other big reveal. The club is still operating as PWHL Las Vegas while that work is finished. When it drops, expect a full brand launch with jerseys, merchandise, and a marketing push across the valley. Green and gold are already confirmed as the colors, so you can at least guess at the look.

Roster construction is happening in the background right now. General manager Dominique DiDia has to assemble a coaching staff, work the league's player allocation process, and build a competitive group for a debut season. Expansion rosters in the PWHL come together quickly, and the names on that first sheet will shape how the team is received here.

On the ticket side, the $50 season ticket membership deposit is the entry point. Deposits usually convert into priority for seat selection, presale access, and sometimes founding-member perks. If you have a hockey family and you know you want to be there for opening night, the deposit is the low-cost way to hold your place in line.

Keep an eye on the broadcast side as well. How you watch the team at home matters almost as much as how you watch it in person, especially for families in Henderson, Centennial Hills, or the far southwest who are not driving to the Strip on a Tuesday. Golden Knights fans in this valley just got good news about free over-the-air games, and local hockey households have gotten used to easy access. Whatever the PWHL announces for local viewing will shape how quickly this team becomes part of the everyday conversation here.

Also watch for community events tied to the launch. New franchises typically run open practices, meet-the-team nights, and school visits in their first season. Those events tend to be free or cheap, and they are often the best way for a kid to see a professional athlete up close without buying a ticket.

Ryan's Take

I have watched this valley change a lot in the last decade, and sports has been one of the biggest drivers. When the Golden Knights arrived, people started organizing their social lives around a hockey schedule. Neighborhoods near the arena and near practice facilities got busier. Youth programs exploded. A PWHL team is a smaller version of that same effect, and it lands in a market that already knows how to be a hockey town.

From a real estate perspective, this does not move home prices by itself. No single team does. What it does is add one more line to the list of reasons somebody chooses Las Vegas over Phoenix or Sacramento or Salt Lake. When I show homes to families relocating here, the questions are almost always about schools, commutes, and what there is to do. Being able to say the city has pro football, pro hockey, two pro women's teams, and a major league ballpark under construction changes the answer to that last question in a real way.

I also think the youth angle is underrated. Rink access is a genuine amenity in this valley, the same way a good park or a short freeway on-ramp is. When a family with skaters calls me, the drive time to ice is one of the first filters we apply to the search. If a PWHL team pushes girls' participation up the way it has elsewhere, that filter is going to matter for more households in Clark County, not fewer.

The neighborhoods I would watch are the ones with easy access to the arena corridor and to the valley's ice facilities. Summerlin, the southwest, and the areas near the Strip already benefit from event traffic. Families who skate tend to buy within a reasonable drive of their home rink, and that is a pattern I have seen play out over and over.

What You Can Do

If you want to be part of the debut season, start with the $50 season ticket membership deposit through the PWHL's Las Vegas team page. It is a small commitment and it gets you in line ahead of the general public when seats go on sale. For a family of four, buying together as a group usually gets you better seat locations than buying individually later.

Watch for the schedule release in late September or early October. Once it lands, mark the home dates and plan around them. T-Mobile Arena sits on the Strip, so parking and traffic take planning, especially on weekend nights when the arena district is already busy. Rideshare drop-off zones and the pedestrian bridges from the Park are usually the smoothest way in.

If you have kids who might want to try the sport, this is the season to look into learn-to-skate and youth programs at the valley's rinks. New teams almost always bring community clinics and school outreach with them, and those programs fill fast. Getting your child on a waitlist now is easier than getting them on one after opening night.

Season ticket demand also tells the league something. If Las Vegas puts up strong deposit numbers, it strengthens the case for more investment here, better game times, and better broadcast placement. Markets that show up early get treated well. That is true in every league, and it was true for the Golden Knights when this city sold out a building for a team that did not exist yet.

Finally, follow the team's official channels for the name and logo reveal. That announcement will come with the first real merchandise drop, and founding-season gear tends to sell out quickly in a market like ours.
