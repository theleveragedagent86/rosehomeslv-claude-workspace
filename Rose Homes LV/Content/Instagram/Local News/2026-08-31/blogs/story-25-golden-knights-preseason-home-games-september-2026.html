Golden Knights Preseason Brings Two Home Games to T-Mobile Arena in September

The Vegas Golden Knights will play two preseason home games at T-Mobile Arena this September. They host the Utah Mammoth on Thursday, September 24 at 7 p.m. and the San Jose Sharks on Saturday, September 26 at 7 p.m.

Those two nights are the first chance for local fans to see the 2026-27 team on home ice. Preseason games usually cost less than regular season games, and they land right before the regular season opener. If you have been waiting for a reason to take the family to a hockey game without paying playoff prices, this is the window.

The Golden Knights also scheduled road preseason games against the Los Angeles Kings and the San Jose Sharks. Fans who want ticket information for the home games can call 702-645-4259.

What Happened

The Vegas Golden Knights released their 2026-27 preseason schedule, and it puts two games in front of a home crowd at T-Mobile Arena. The first is Thursday, September 24 against the Utah Mammoth. Puck drop is set for 7 p.m. The second is Saturday, September 26 against the San Jose Sharks, also at 7 p.m.

The Utah matchup is worth a closer look. Utah is one of the newest franchises in the Western Conference, and the drive from Salt Lake City to Las Vegas is short enough that visiting fans travel for these games. That has already started to build a rivalry feel between the two clubs. A preseason game does not carry standings points, but the building still gets loud when a division opponent is on the ice.

The San Jose game on Saturday is the more family friendly of the two. Saturday nights mean no school the next morning, no work alarm, and a much easier decision for parents with young kids. The Sharks are a long-running Pacific Division opponent, so the matchup is familiar to anyone who has followed the team since it arrived in 2017.

Beyond the two home dates, the Golden Knights scheduled road preseason games against the Los Angeles Kings and the San Jose Sharks. That gives the club a short and regional preseason slate, which is common. Teams try to limit travel in September so players can settle into a routine before games start counting.

The team listed 702-645-4259 as the phone number for ticketing information. That is the practical detail most people miss in schedule announcements. If you would rather talk to a person than click through a resale marketplace, that number is the direct route.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Preseason hockey is the cheapest reliable way into T-Mobile Arena. Regular season and playoff tickets in this market have climbed a lot since the team's first season, and a family of four can spend real money on a single night out. Preseason games are the exception. Demand is lower, the stakes are lower, and the ticket prices usually follow.

That matters more than it sounds. A lot of families in Summerlin, Henderson, and the southwest valley want to introduce their kids to live hockey but have never pulled the trigger because of cost. September gives them a low risk way to try it. If the kid loves it, you have a new family tradition. If the kid falls asleep in the third period, you did not spend a mortgage payment finding out.

There is also a scheduling angle. Late September in Clark County is when the calendar gets busy again. School is in full swing, youth sports seasons are running, and the weather finally drops out of triple digits. A Thursday and a Saturday game give people two very different options. Thursday works for adults and older kids. Saturday works for everyone else.

For anyone who works on or near the Strip, these games also mean more traffic and more foot traffic on those nights. T-Mobile Arena sits at the south end of the Strip near Park MGM and New York-New York. Event nights change how long it takes to get through that corridor. If you commute through Tropicana Avenue or Las Vegas Boulevard, September 24 and September 26 are worth putting on your mental map.

And for people who live in the neighborhoods that feed the arena crowd, the preseason is the first signal that hockey season is back. The Golden Knights have become one of the strongest year-round community anchors in the valley. When the team starts playing, the bars fill up, the jerseys come out, and the city feels a little different.

Local businesses feel it too. Restaurants, bars, and shops around the south Strip build their fall staffing around the event calendar, and hockey nights are a meaningful piece of that. Two extra sold nights in September are two extra nights of shifts for people who live and work in Clark County. That is a quiet economic detail that never makes the headline but shows up in a lot of household budgets.

Preseason is also useful for people who are new to town. Las Vegas gains thousands of new residents every year, and plenty of them have never been to an NHL game. Walking into a building for a game that does not decide anything is a much lower pressure introduction than showing up in the middle of a playoff run. You learn the entrances, the concourse, the parking, and the pace of the night before it actually matters.

Background and History

The Golden Knights arrived in Las Vegas as an expansion team in 2017 and immediately changed how this city thinks about pro sports. Before them, Las Vegas had no team in any of the four major North American leagues. Now the valley has the Golden Knights, the Raiders, the Aces, an Athletics ballpark under construction, and a PWHL team preparing for its first season.

T-Mobile Arena opened in 2016 on the Strip and has been the team's home from the beginning. The building holds roughly 17,500 for hockey and has hosted everything from Stanley Cup Final games to concerts and fights. Preseason games use the same building, the same ice, and the same concessions. The only thing that changes is the intensity and the price.

Preseason exists for a reason that most casual fans overlook. Coaches use these games to evaluate young players, test line combinations, and get veterans back into game shape. Rosters in September are much larger than they are in October, so preseason games often feature prospects who will start the year in the minors. That is part of the appeal for die-hard fans. You get an early look at the players who might matter in two or three years.

The regional preseason slate against Utah, San Jose, and Los Angeles also fits a pattern. NHL teams generally schedule preseason opponents that are close by, which keeps travel costs and player fatigue down. For Vegas, the Pacific Division neighbors are the obvious fit, and Utah's arrival in the Western Conference gave the league another short trip to work with.

Hockey in the desert was treated as a punchline before the team arrived. Nine years later the argument is over. Youth programs have grown, rink projects have moved forward, and a professional women's league picked Las Vegas for expansion. The preseason schedule is a small piece of that story, but it is the piece that opens the door every fall.

What Happens Next

After the two home preseason games, the regular season starts fast. The Golden Knights open the 2026-27 season on September 29 against the Chicago Blackhawks, and that game airs on ESPN. That is three days after the San Jose preseason game, which means the roster has to be set almost immediately after the preseason wraps.

Expect roster cuts in the days between the last preseason game and the opener. Every NHL team trims down to its final roster before opening night, and the preseason games are the last live evaluation the coaching staff gets. If you go to the September 24 or September 26 game, some of the players you watch will not be on the ice for the opener.

On the broadcast side, the season ahead is easier to follow than it has been in years. The Golden Knights have 15 nationally televised games across ESPN, ESPN+, TNT, and ABC, with 11 of those at home. Separately, the Season X broadcast plan puts 69 regular season games on free over-the-air television locally, which means fans without cable can watch most of the season for nothing more than an antenna.

There is one more thing on the horizon for hockey fans in this valley. PWHL Las Vegas is preparing for its debut season with home games at T-Mobile Arena. Schedules for that league typically come out in late September or early October, so the same stretch of the calendar that brings Golden Knights preseason hockey should also bring the first look at pro women's hockey in Las Vegas.

Watch the start times too. The league already announced updated start times for six Vegas games this season, which is a reminder that a schedule printed in September is not always the schedule you get in January. If you buy tickets for a specific date, check the time again a week out. Broadcast partners move games, and the change is easy to miss.

The other thing to watch is the roster itself. Preseason is when you find out which young players are ready and which veterans look a step slow. Those September answers shape how the team plays in October and whether the front office goes shopping before the trade deadline. Fans who show up on September 24 and September 26 get to form their own opinion before anyone tells them what to think.

Ryan's Take

I pay attention to hockey scheduling for a reason that has nothing to do with the standings. Live sports are one of the clearest signals of a city maturing, and Las Vegas has spent the last decade turning from a place people visit into a place people build a life around. The Golden Knights were the first proof of that shift, and every September the preseason reminds me of it.

When I show homes to people relocating here, the questions have changed. It used to be all about price per square foot and how far the commute was. Now people ask what there is to do on a Tuesday night in October. They ask if their kids can play hockey here. They ask how far they will be from T-Mobile Arena. Buyers looking at Southern Highlands, Mountains Edge, and the southwest valley in general like being a short drive from the Strip venues without living in the middle of the tourist corridor.

That is not a small thing for property values. Neighborhoods with easy access to real entertainment options, good roads to get there, and a growing youth sports scene tend to hold their value better over time. Hockey did not build the Las Vegas housing market. But the culture that grew around this team is part of why families stay here instead of treating Vegas as a stop on the way somewhere else.

My honest advice on the preseason is simple. Go if you have never been. The seats you can actually afford in September are the seats you would never get in April, and the building is the same building. You will learn whether live hockey is your thing for a fraction of what it costs to find out during the regular season.

What You Can Do

Start with the dates. Put Thursday, September 24 and Saturday, September 26 on your calendar now, both at 7 p.m. at T-Mobile Arena. If you want ticket information straight from the source, call 702-645-4259. Prices and availability change, so calling early is better than waiting until game week.

Plan the drive before you go. T-Mobile Arena sits at the south end of the Strip, and the surrounding streets get slow on event nights. Give yourself extra time, decide on parking before you leave the house, and consider arriving early enough to walk the plaza area instead of sprinting to your seat. Warmups start well before puck drop and are often the best part of the night for younger kids.

If tickets are not in the budget this year, you still have options. Between the 15 nationally televised games and the 69 games on free over-the-air local television, most of the season is watchable at home without a cable bill. Check that your antenna picks up the local channel carrying the games before opening night so you are not troubleshooting during the first period.

And if you have a kid who catches the hockey bug at one of these games, look into local ice time. The valley has more rink options than it did a few years ago, including the North Las Vegas ice arena that is getting a rebuild. Youth hockey in Clark County has grown right alongside the Golden Knights, and September is when most programs are forming teams for the season.

Bring a little cash for the small stuff and set expectations before you walk in. Concessions at any arena cost more than they do at home, and a first game is a lot for a young kid. Eat before you go, plan on leaving early if you need to, and let the night be fun instead of an endurance test. Nobody remembers the score of a preseason game. They remember the first time they saw the ice.

Finally, tell the people who would actually enjoy this. Preseason games are the kind of thing that sells out quietly among friends and coworkers rather than through big advertising. If you know a family in Henderson or the southwest valley that has been putting off a Golden Knights game because of price, September 24 and September 26 are the two nights to point them toward.
