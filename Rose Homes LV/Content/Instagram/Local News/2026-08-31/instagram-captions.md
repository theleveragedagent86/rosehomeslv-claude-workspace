## Story 1: Nevada Is Suing the Federal Government Over the Water Under Your House

Nevada Is Suing the Federal Government Over the Water Under Your House

On August 24, the State of Nevada walked into federal court and sued the Interior Department over the Colorado River. That is how serious this got.

The lawsuit was filed by the State of Nevada, the Colorado River Commission of Nevada, and the Southern Nevada Water Authority.

It challenges the Record of Decision on Colorado River operations that Interior released on August 21.

Here is the part that stops people cold. Nevada's allocation is 300,000 acre-feet. Under that decision, the cut could be as large as 213,556 acre-feet.

That would leave Las Vegas and the cities around it with under 86,500 acre-feet per year.

Now look at what we actually used. In 2024, this community used slightly under 212,500 acre-feet.

Read those two numbers next to each other. The proposed cut is larger than our entire annual use.

This is not a story about conservation tips. This is a story about whether the math works at all.

Southern Nevada has already done more than almost anywhere else in the West. We ripped out grass. We recycle nearly every drop of indoor water. We built a straw at the bottom of Lake Mead so we could keep pulling when others could not.

And the reward for being the most efficient user on the river is a cut this size.

That is what makes people angry, and honestly, they have a point. Efficiency should not be punished.

If you own a home here, this is not abstract. Water policy shows up in your utility bill, in your HOA landscaping rules, and eventually in what a builder is allowed to build.

It also shows up in whether new housing gets approved at all. Every future subdivision in Clark County runs through a water conversation.

The suit asks the court to set the decision aside. That will take time, and nobody should expect a quick answer.

But the filing itself tells you where the state thinks this is headed.

Should Southern Nevada be cut harder than states that never bothered to conserve in the first place?
