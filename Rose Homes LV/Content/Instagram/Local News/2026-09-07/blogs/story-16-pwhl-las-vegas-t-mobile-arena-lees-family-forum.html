PWHL Las Vegas Will Split Home Games Between T-Mobile Arena and Lee's Family Forum

PWHL Las Vegas announced it will play its first season of home games in two buildings instead of one. Some games will be at T-Mobile Arena on the Strip, and the rest will be at Lee's Family Forum in Henderson.

That single decision changes the drive for a lot of families in the south and east valley. For years, watching top level pro hockey in Southern Nevada meant a trip to the Strip, parking garages, and traffic on Tropicana. Now part of a professional women's hockey season lands about fifteen minutes from Green Valley, Anthem, and Seven Hills. The team also said Henderson will serve as its home base, with practices at America First Center, the same facility the Henderson Silver Knights use.

What Happened

The Las Vegas Review-Journal reported on August 27, 2026 that PWHL Las Vegas will split its inaugural season home schedule between T-Mobile Arena and Lee's Family Forum in Henderson. The report came from Danny Webster, who covers hockey for the paper. The team confirmed the two venue plan rather than picking one permanent home for year one.

The reasoning the club gave is simple and, honestly, pretty smart. Splitting venues lets the team reach residential communities across the valley as well as visitors on the Strip. T-Mobile Arena sits in the middle of the tourist corridor, which means out of town hockey fans and convention crowds can walk to a game. Lee's Family Forum sits in Henderson, which means the people who actually live here can get to a game on a school night without a freeway.

Henderson is the club's home base. That part matters more than the venue split. The team will practice at America First Center, the Henderson Silver Knights facility. A home base is not just a practice sheet. It is where staff work, where players spend their days, where community appearances get scheduled, and where local youth programs tend to attach themselves.

Season ticket members can now select their seats. The full league schedule is expected in the fall, which means the exact split between the two buildings has not been published yet. Here is the math worth keeping in your head. If the PWHL keeps a 30 game schedule, that works out to roughly 15 home dates. Split those across two arenas and you are looking at somewhere in the neighborhood of seven or eight games per building. The team has not confirmed an exact division, so treat that as an estimate and not a promise.

A few things are still unanswered, and it is worth being clear about what we do not know yet. We do not know which specific opponents land in which building. We do not know whether the Henderson dates skew toward weekends. We do not know how ticket pricing will differ between the two venues, and there is a real chance it will, since the two buildings are very different sizes with very different operating costs. None of that has been announced, and anyone telling you otherwise is guessing.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with the drive, because that is what most families actually weigh. If you live in Inspirada, Cadence, Anthem, Seven Hills, or Green Valley Ranch, a trip to T-Mobile Arena on a Tuesday night is a project. You are budgeting an hour each way once you factor in parking, walking, and getting back out of the garage. Lee's Family Forum is a different story. For much of Henderson it is a fifteen to twenty minute drive, and the parking situation is dramatically friendlier.

That difference decides whether a family goes once a season or goes four times a season. Pro sports in a city like ours live and die on repeat attendance from locals, not on one time tourist tickets. When you cut the round trip in half, you turn a special occasion into a normal weeknight outing. Parents with kids who have school the next morning start saying yes.

There is a second group this matters to, and it is bigger than people think. Southern Nevada has a real youth hockey scene now. It grew fast after the Vegas Golden Knights arrived, and it kept growing when the Henderson Silver Knights set up shop. A lot of those young players are girls. Until now, if a girl in Henderson wanted to watch women play hockey at the highest professional level, she watched it on a screen. This season she can watch it from the lower bowl, in her own city, and then go skate at her own rink the next morning.

Then there is the neighborhood economics angle. Arena nights move money. Restaurants near Lee's Family Forum, bars along Water Street and in the Green Valley area, and the businesses around America First Center all see traffic on event nights. Seven or eight extra pro hockey dates is not a windfall by itself, but it stacks on top of the Silver Knights schedule, concerts, and other events. Henderson has been building an identity as a place where you do not have to drive to the Strip to have a night out. This adds another brick to that wall.

Do not overlook the T-Mobile Arena half of this either. Those games put a Las Vegas team in front of tourists, conventioneers, and visiting fans who fly in from hockey markets all over the country. That is free marketing for the city and for the league at the same time. It also means locals who work on the Strip, and there are a lot of them, can catch a game before or after a shift without crossing the valley.

And there is a quieter effect that takes a few years to show up. Every professional team that lands here gives Southern Nevada another reason to be taken seriously as a place people move to and stay. We spent decades being described as a place people pass through. Teams, arenas, and season schedules are the opposite of passing through. They are commitments with leases attached, and buyers notice that even when they cannot quite articulate why the city feels more permanent than it used to.

Background and History

Las Vegas was not a hockey town twenty years ago. That changed in a hurry. The Golden Knights arrived and turned T-Mobile Arena into one of the loudest buildings in the sport. The Henderson Silver Knights followed and gave the south valley its own team, first at Orleans Arena and then in a purpose built Henderson arena. Along the way, ice sheets went in, youth leagues filled up, and a generation of Clark County kids grew up thinking hockey was normal here.

Lee's Family Forum, the Henderson arena, has carried several names in its short life. Locals may still call it by an older name out of habit. What has not changed is what it does well. It is a right sized building for hockey. You are close to the glass almost anywhere you sit, the concourse does not swallow you, and getting in and out is quick. For a league still building its audience city by city, that is arguably a better first impression than a cavernous room that looks half empty on television.

America First Center is the practice and community side of the operation. It is the facility the Silver Knights use, and it has been a hub for local skating and youth programs. Having PWHL Las Vegas base itself there puts professional women players in the same building where local kids take lessons. That kind of proximity is how leagues build lifelong fans, and it is cheaper and more effective than any billboard.

The Professional Women's Hockey League itself is young. It launched in 2024 as the top professional women's hockey league in North America and has been adding markets since. Las Vegas landing a club fits a pattern the city knows well. We are no longer the market that gets skipped. We are the market that gets tested first, and then copied.

The two venue idea also has roots here. The Silver Knights played their early seasons in one building and then moved into a new one. Local fans have already been through a venue transition once, and the team survived it. That history probably made a split season feel less risky to everyone involved than it would in a city that has only ever known one arena.

What Happens Next

The next real milestone is the league schedule, which the team says is expected in the fall. That release will answer the questions people are actually asking. How many games land in Henderson? Are the Henderson dates weeknights or weekends? Do the marquee opponents go to T-Mobile Arena? Until that drops, everything about the split is an educated guess.

Season ticket members are already selecting seats, which is the other thing to watch. Seat selection is when a franchise finds out where its real support lives. If Henderson zip codes buy heavily and the Strip games lean on tourists and walk up sales, the team will notice, and year two planning will reflect it. If the T-Mobile dates outdraw the Henderson dates by a wide margin, the split may not survive past the first season.

Longer term, the honest question is whether this becomes a permanent two building arrangement or a trial run. Splitting a season across two arenas is unusual. It creates logistical work, complicates season ticket pricing, and makes it harder to build one consistent game night atmosphere. Teams usually do it to gather data. Watch attendance numbers by venue this season, because those numbers will decide whether PWHL Las Vegas eventually plants itself in Henderson full time or moves everything to the Strip.

There is also a scheduling puzzle nobody talks about until it bites. Both buildings have other tenants and other events. T-Mobile Arena hosts the Golden Knights plus concerts and fights. Lee's Family Forum hosts the Silver Knights and its own event calendar. Fitting a third team's home dates around all of that is genuinely hard, and it is one reason the league schedule takes time to finalize. Expect some odd dates and a few weeknight games that would not be your first choice.

One more thing to watch is community programming. Teams in a first season usually roll out school visits, learn to play clinics, and youth partnerships in the first few months. With the club based at America First Center, those programs will likely be centered in Henderson. If you have a kid in a local hockey or skating program, that is where the announcements will land first.

Ryan's Take

I have watched Henderson change a lot over the last decade, and the pattern is always the same. First a piece of infrastructure shows up, then the amenities follow, then people start telling their friends they do not need to leave the area anymore. Lee's Family Forum was one of those pieces. The Silver Knights were another. Adding professional women's hockey dates to that same building is a small headline with a real effect on how Henderson feels to live in.

When buyers ask me what Henderson has going for it, I do not lead with hockey. I lead with schools, commute times, and the fact that neighborhoods like Inspirada, Cadence, Anthem, and Green Valley have their own centers of gravity. But amenities like this are what turn a list of practical reasons into an actual lifestyle pitch. A family relocating from Minnesota or Michigan who hears there is pro hockey fifteen minutes from their front door pays attention. That is not a price driver. It is a preference driver, and preference is what fills neighborhoods.

My honest read is that the Henderson games will do better than people expect and the Strip games will look great on TV. Both of those things can be true. And if you are a parent of a kid who plays out here, this is a genuinely good year to buy tickets.

One caution, since I try to be straight with people. Do not buy a house because of an arena schedule. Venues change, teams relocate, and a season split across two buildings is by definition temporary. Buy for the school, the commute, the floor plan, and the payment. Treat the hockey as a bonus that makes a good decision feel better, not as the reason you stretched your budget.

What You Can Do

If you want in on the first season, the practical step right now is season tickets. Season ticket members are the group selecting seats, which means they get first pick at both buildings before single game inventory opens. Go to the PWHL Las Vegas team site for membership details and pricing. If a full season is more than you want, wait for the schedule release in the fall and target the Henderson dates, which are likely to be the easier tickets logistically for local families.

Watch for the league schedule announcement. That is the moment to circle dates on your calendar, especially if you are trying to line up a game night with a kid's practice schedule at America First Center or another local rink. If you have a young skater, ask your program whether it is coordinating group nights. Teams in their first season are usually very willing to work with local youth organizations, and group rates are typically the cheapest way in the door.

If you live in Henderson and care about how event nights affect your street, parking, or traffic, keep an eye on City of Henderson council agendas. Arena district activity, parking policy, and surrounding development all get discussed publicly, and residents who show up get heard. And if you are thinking about buying near the arena district or anywhere in the Green Valley and Anthem corridor, factor in that this area keeps adding reasons for people to want to live here.
