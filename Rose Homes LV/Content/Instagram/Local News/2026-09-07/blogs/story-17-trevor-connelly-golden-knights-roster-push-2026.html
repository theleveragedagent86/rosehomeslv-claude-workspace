Trevor Connelly Pushes for a Full-Time Golden Knights Job After a Record Season in Henderson

Trevor Connelly, the 20-year-old forward the Vegas Golden Knights took in the first round of the 2024 draft, is in real position to compete for a full-time NHL job this fall. The Las Vegas Review-Journal reported on September 5, 2026 that Connelly could battle for the last spot on the roster, or at minimum earn regular call-ups during the season.

The reason is simple. Connelly put up 49 points in 46 games for the Henderson Silver Knights last season and set a franchise record with a 15-game point streak. He also helped Henderson reach the second round of the Calder Cup Playoffs for the first time in team history.

For anyone who lives in Henderson and has watched Silver Knights games at Lee's Family Forum, this is the fun part of following a farm team. You get to see a player before the rest of the league does. Now the question is whether he takes the next step up the road to T-Mobile Arena.

What Happened

On September 5, 2026, Review-Journal reporter Danny Webster published a piece laying out the five biggest questions facing the Vegas Golden Knights with training camp about to open. Trevor Connelly was one of them. The framing was direct. Connelly is knocking on the door, and the team has to decide what to do with him.

Connelly is 20 years old. Vegas picked him in the first round of the 2024 NHL Draft, which put him in a small group of players the organization has invested a top pick in. First-round picks come with expectations, and in a market where the Golden Knights have been a contender almost every year, those expectations arrive fast.

His 2025-26 season with the Henderson Silver Knights is the reason the conversation is happening at all. He scored 49 points in 46 games. That is better than a point per game in the American Hockey League, which is a hard place for a teenager to produce. Older, stronger, more experienced players fill AHL rosters. Plenty of first-round picks spend a full year there just learning how to survive.

Then there is the streak. Connelly recorded points in 15 straight games, a Henderson Silver Knights franchise record. Streaks like that are not luck. A player has to keep producing while opponents adjust, coaches game-plan against him, and the schedule grinds on. Henderson has been around since 2020, so the record belongs to a full run of team history.

To put the production in context, better than a point per game at that age is the kind of number that forces a decision. Teams can leave a prospect in the minors for a full extra year when the numbers are merely fine. Numbers like Connelly's remove that option, because there is not much left for him to learn at that level.

Connelly's season also lined up with the best playoff run Henderson has had. The Silver Knights reached the second round of the Calder Cup Playoffs for the first time. That is a real milestone for a franchise that plays in front of Henderson families at Lee's Family Forum, and Connelly was part of the group that got them there.

The Review-Journal also named the main concern, and it is not talent. It is health. Connelly missed training camp last year. He then missed roughly a month and a half in December with lower-body injuries. That is a lot of missed development time for a young player, and it is the kind of thing coaches think about when they decide whether someone is ready for an 82-game NHL season.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Hockey in Southern Nevada is not just a Strip attraction anymore. It is a Henderson thing, a Summerlin thing, and a North Las Vegas thing. The Silver Knights play at Lee's Family Forum in Henderson. The Golden Knights practice at City National Arena in Summerlin. Youth programs and rec leagues have grown around both. A player like Connelly connects those pieces for local families.

If you bought Silver Knights tickets last season, you paid AHL prices to watch a first-round pick have one of the best years in franchise history. That is the whole pitch of a minor league team in your own city. Henderson families do not have to fly anywhere to see future NHL players. They drive 15 minutes and park in a normal lot.

There is a practical side too. When a farm team is good, the building stays busy. Playoff runs mean extra home dates, extra concession nights, extra people eating dinner nearby before the game. Henderson's second-round run brought more of those nights to the area around Lee's Family Forum than the franchise had ever had before.

For parents with kids in local hockey programs, this story has another layer. Connelly is 20. He is barely older than the high school players skating at valley rinks right now. Watching someone that young move from Henderson toward T-Mobile Arena makes the path feel real instead of theoretical. That matters when you are the one paying for ice time at 6 a.m.

And if Connelly does make the Golden Knights, the trip does not get much longer for local fans. Henderson to the Strip is a short drive. The player Henderson watched develop would be playing a few exits away. That kind of continuity is rare in pro sports, where prospects usually develop three time zones from the parent club.

It also changes how a season feels for the people who follow both teams. Most cities get one or the other. Here you can watch a player struggle through a rough November in Henderson, then see him get a shot at T-Mobile Arena a few months later. You are not reading about it. You are in the building for both parts.

Renters and homeowners near these venues feel it in smaller ways. Traffic patterns on game nights, restaurant wait times, and weekend activity all shift when a building has a busy schedule. Most people who live near Lee's Family Forum or T-Mobile Arena already know which nights to plan around, and a longer playoff run adds more of them.

Background and History

The Golden Knights have been in Las Vegas since 2017 and have spent most of that time competing at the top of the league. That success created a specific problem for young players. When a roster is built to win now, there are fewer open spots for a 20-year-old still learning the pro game. Veterans get the minutes.

Bringing the AHL team to Henderson in 2020 was the organization's answer. Instead of shipping prospects across the country, Vegas put its development team about 20 miles from the NHL rink. Coaches can watch players in person. Call-ups take an afternoon, not a flight. Players stay inside the same system, with the same expectations and the same staff checking in.

Connelly is the current example of what that setup is supposed to produce. Drafted in the first round in 2024, sent to Henderson, and given a full season of real minutes against grown men. He responded with 49 points in 46 games and a 15-game point streak that no Silver Knight had matched before him.

The injury history is part of the background as well. Missing training camp last year meant Connelly started the season behind. Missing about six weeks in December with lower-body injuries meant he lost momentum in the middle of the year. That he still finished with 49 points in 46 games says something about how productive he was when healthy. It also explains why the organization is careful about how much it asks of him next.

Henderson's playoff breakthrough closes out the picture. The Silver Knights had never won a Calder Cup Playoff round before. Reaching the second round gave the franchise its first taste of meaningful postseason hockey and gave its young players something a regular season cannot teach.

There is also a coaching change sitting behind all of this. Ryan Craig is the fifth head coach in Golden Knights history and takes over a team that lost in the Stanley Cup Final. A new coach usually means a fresh evaluation of everyone in camp, which can help a young player who was buried on last year's depth chart. Nobody's spot is fully locked in during a first camp under new leadership.

Put those pieces together and you get the situation the Review-Journal described. A first-round pick with a record-setting AHL season, a real injury question, a compressed camp, and a new coach making the calls. That is a lot of variables for one roster decision.

What Happens Next

The calendar answers this one quickly. The Golden Knights announced a 23-player rookie camp roster on September 1, and Connelly is on it. That group holds its first practice on Friday, September 11 at 12:45 p.m. at City National Arena in Summerlin, then travels to San Jose for the Rookie Faceoff from September 12 to 15. Vegas faces Utah on September 12 and Colorado on September 13.

Main training camp opens September 16. New head coach Ryan Craig said the team will run 12 days of camp instead of the usual 19 and play four exhibition games instead of seven. The condensed 2026-27 schedule and the Olympic break are the reasons. That compression matters for a player like Connelly, because he has fewer days and fewer games to prove he belongs.

From there, the outcomes are pretty clear. Connelly either wins a spot out of camp, or he starts the year back in Henderson with the expectation of call-ups when injuries or slumps open a door. Neither result would be a surprise. What people will watch is whether he can string together a healthy camp, since he missed camp entirely last year.

The regular season opener is September 29. That gives everyone about two weeks from the start of main camp to see how the roster shakes out. If you want to follow the story, the exhibition games are where the answers usually show up.

One more thing worth watching is how the team uses him if he does start in Henderson. There is a difference between a prospect sitting in the minors and one who gets called up the first time a forward goes down. The Review-Journal's framing pointed at the second version, which is a much better sign for a 20-year-old than it sounds.

Ryan's Take

I have said for years that the Golden Knights changed how people talk about living in this valley, and the Silver Knights quietly did the same thing for Henderson. When I show homes in Green Valley, Inspirada, or Cadence, sports and rinks come up more than people expect. Families want to know where the kids can skate and what there is to do on a weeknight. Lee's Family Forum is a real answer to that question.

A prospect like Trevor Connelly is a small piece of a bigger pattern. Henderson has spent the last several years turning into a city with its own draw instead of a suburb people describe by how far it is from the Strip. Pro hockey, new entertainment venues, and a growing youth sports scene all feed that. Buyers notice. It is rarely the reason someone picks a house, but it shows up on the list of reasons they like the area.

The other thing I notice is how much a healthy farm team helps a city build its own identity. Henderson does not need to borrow anything from the Strip to sell itself anymore. It has its own arena, its own team, its own record book, and now its own homegrown story about a kid who might make the NHL. That is the sort of thing residents point to when they talk about where they live.

My honest read on the hockey side is that the health question decides this. The talent is not in doubt after 49 points in 46 games and a record point streak. If he gets through camp clean, he is going to be in the mix. Either way, Henderson fans got to watch the best individual season in Silver Knights history, and that alone was worth the ticket.

What You Can Do

If you want to see Connelly and the rest of the young group in person, the rookie camp practice on September 11 at City National Arena in Summerlin is the closest and cheapest look you will get. Rookie camp sessions are one of the few times valley families can watch future Golden Knights up close without buying an NHL ticket. Check the team's official site for public access details before you go, since practice schedules can shift.

After that, the four preseason games and the September 29 opener at T-Mobile Arena are the next chances. With a shorter camp and fewer exhibitions this year, those dates fill up faster than usual, so plan ahead if you want seats.

Following the Silver Knights during the season is the other move. Henderson's roster is where most of these decisions play out over the long run, and the schedule runs well past the NHL roster cuts. If Connelly starts the year in the AHL, that is where you will see him.

For Henderson families, Silver Knights games at Lee's Family Forum remain the best value in local hockey. AHL tickets cost a fraction of NHL prices, the building is easy to get in and out of, and you are watching the players who may be on the NHL roster in a year or two. If your kids are interested in playing, local youth programs and the valley rinks are a reasonable next step.
