Golden Knights Open Training Camp September 16 With a Shorter Camp and Only Four Preseason Games

The Vegas Golden Knights open training camp on September 16, and this year the whole thing is compressed. New head coach Ryan Craig said camp will run 12 days instead of the usual 19, and the team will play four exhibition games instead of seven. That is a real change for anyone in Clark County who plans their fall around hockey.

Craig laid it out in a question and answer session with Gary Lawless on VegasGoldenKnights.com. The reason is the calendar. The 2026-27 NHL season is condensed because of the Olympic break, so every team is squeezing the same preparation into fewer days. Craig said he does not mind the added sense of urgency.

For local fans, the practical takeaway is simple. There are fewer chances to see this team live before the season opener on September 29. If you were planning to catch a cheap preseason game with the kids, or take out of town family to a low pressure night of hockey, the window just got a lot smaller.

What Happened

Ryan Craig is the fifth head coach in Vegas Golden Knights franchise history. He takes over a team that reached the Stanley Cup Final and lost. That is a heavy starting point for any new coach, and now he gets less time than his predecessors had to install his systems and sort out his roster.

The numbers Craig gave are clear. Training camp opens September 16 and runs 12 days. A normal camp is about 19 days. That is a full week of practice time gone. On the exhibition side, the team goes from seven preseason games down to four. Nearly half the warmup schedule disappeared.

Craig explained the reason to Gary Lawless of VegasGoldenKnights.com. The 2026-27 NHL schedule is condensed. The league is pausing during the season for the Olympic break, and that pause has to come out of somewhere. It comes out of the front end and the back end. Camps get shorter, preseason gets shorter, and the regular season gets tighter.

Craig's response to all of it was not complaint. He said the compressed calendar creates a sense of urgency and that he does not mind it. Coming off a Stanley Cup Final loss, urgency is probably not the worst thing to have in a locker room. Players who might have used a long camp to ease into shape do not get that option this year.

The Golden Knights regular season opener is September 29. Count backward from there. Camp opens the 16th. Four exhibition games fit into the days between. Then the games start counting for real. Rookie camp already came first, with the 23-player rookie group holding its opening practice September 11 at City National Arena in Summerlin.

It is worth being clear about what a training camp actually does, because the word gets thrown around loosely. Camp is where a coaching staff teaches its systems, meaning how the team breaks the puck out of its own zone, how it forechecks, how it kills penalties, and how it defends in front of its own net. It is also where players who spent the summer training get their legs back under game conditions. Both of those things normally take weeks.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Golden Knights hockey is not just a sport in this valley. It is a calendar. Since 2017, families in Summerlin, Henderson, Centennial Hills, and the southwest have organized fall weekends around it. When the preseason shrinks from seven games to four, that calendar changes for tens of thousands of households.

Preseason games matter to regular families for one big reason. They are usually the most affordable and most relaxed way to get into an NHL building. Parents take young kids to preseason games because the crowds are smaller and the stakes are lower. Grandparents visiting from out of state get taken to preseason games. With three fewer of them on the schedule, competition for those seats goes up.

There is also a local business angle. Preseason nights bring people to the Strip corridor and to the neighborhood bars and restaurants that fill up on game days. Every home date is a night when servers, bartenders, rideshare drivers, and parking attendants pick up hours. Fewer preseason dates means fewer of those shifts in a September that already competes with football for attention.

Then there is the youth hockey community, which is bigger in Clark County than people outside the valley expect. City National Arena in Summerlin is the team's practice home and a busy community rink. Kids in Vegas grow up skating there. A shorter camp means fewer open practice days for those families to watch NHL players work up close, which for a lot of kids is the whole reason they fell in love with the sport.

Scheduling matters for households too. September in Clark County is already crowded. School is back in session, youth sports seasons are starting, football is on, and the weather is finally good enough to be outside again. A tighter hockey calendar means the few preseason dates land in a busier stretch, so families have to choose earlier and commit earlier than they normally would.

Finally, there is the simple emotional piece. This is a team that just lost in the Stanley Cup Final. The valley wants to see how the group looks under a new coach. A shorter runway means fans get fewer live looks before the answers start to matter. Most people will end up watching camp clips on their phones instead of standing rinkside.

Background and History

The Golden Knights have been unusual since day one. The 2017 expansion team went to the Stanley Cup Final in its first season, which had never happened in the modern era. The franchise won the Cup in 2023. Then it reached the Final again and lost, which is the season Ryan Craig inherits.

Coaching turnover has been part of the story from the beginning. Craig is the fifth head coach in franchise history. For a team that is not even a decade old, that is a fast pace of change. The organization has never been shy about making a move when it believed a move would help, and the front office under General Manager Kelly McCrimmon has a track record of aggressive decisions.

The Olympic break is the other piece of background here, and it is a league-wide issue rather than a Vegas one. When the NHL sends players to the Winter Olympics, the regular season has to stop for a stretch of weeks. Those weeks do not vanish. The league compresses the rest of the calendar around them. Training camps get trimmed, preseason schedules get cut, and regular season games get packed tighter.

This is not the first time NHL teams have dealt with a squeezed camp. Shortened offseasons after long playoff runs, lockout-shortened years, and pandemic-era schedules all forced coaches to compress preparation. What is different here is that it is planned rather than an emergency. Every team knows in advance, so the advantage goes to whichever staff prepares the best.

It also helps to remember how young this market is. Plenty of people in the valley became hockey fans in 2017 and later, which means the routines around the season are still being formed. The preseason, the open practices, and the rookie games are how a lot of casual fans first got hooked. Trimming those entry points, even for one year, touches the newest part of the fan base more than the season ticket holders who were there from the start.

For Vegas specifically, there is a wrinkle. Teams that go deep into June start their next season already tired. A Stanley Cup Final run means a short summer. Now that short summer is followed by a short camp. Craig's job is to get a group that played into the summer ready for real games in less time than normal.

What Happens Next

The immediate schedule is tight and easy to follow. Rookie camp came first, with the rookie group practicing September 11 at City National Arena in Summerlin. Main training camp opens September 16 and runs about 12 days. Four preseason games fill the space after that. The regular season opener is September 29.

Watch for roster decisions to come faster than usual. In a normal camp, a coaching staff has 19 days and seven exhibition games to evaluate borderline players. With 12 days and four games, some of those calls get made on fewer minutes of live hockey. Young players trying to earn a job have less time to make an impression, and veterans on the bubble have less time to prove they still belong.

Also watch how Craig handles workload. A coach with a short camp has to choose between installing systems and building conditioning. Most coaches try to do both and end up prioritizing one. Craig's public comments suggest he is leaning into intensity rather than easing the group in, which fits what he said about not minding the urgency.

For fans, the thing to track is the preseason ticket release and the exact dates and venues of those four exhibition games. Because there are fewer of them, they are likely to move faster than in past years. If seeing a preseason game in person matters to your family, treat it as a smaller window than you are used to. One more thing to keep an eye on is the Henderson Silver Knights connection. The organization's American Hockey League affiliate plays at Lee's Family Forum in Henderson, and a compressed NHL camp usually means faster movement between the two rosters. Players who might have hung around the NHL camp for an extra week get sent down sooner, which can make the Henderson roster more interesting earlier in the fall.

Ryan's Take

I have lived and worked in this valley long enough to watch the Golden Knights change how people think about Las Vegas. Before 2017, this was a city with no major league team. Now it is a city where people in Summerlin, Green Valley, Skye Canyon, and Inspirada plan their fall around a hockey schedule. That shift shows up in how buyers talk about neighborhoods.

I hear it in showings. Buyers ask how far a house is from City National Arena because their kid skates. They ask about the drive to T-Mobile Arena on a weeknight. Families relocating from colder states often mention the team as one of the reasons Las Vegas felt livable to them, not just affordable. Pro sports gave this city a shared calendar, and a shared calendar is part of what makes a place feel like home rather than a stopover.

A shorter camp is a small story on its own. But it is a reminder that the sports economy here is now a real part of daily life in Clark County. Allegiant Stadium, T-Mobile Arena, Lee's Family Forum, and City National Arena all pull people and money into specific parts of the valley. When schedules change, restaurants, rideshare drivers, and neighborhood businesses feel it. That is worth paying attention to if you own property near any of those corridors.

The other thing I would say is do not read too much into a short camp. Every team in the league is dealing with the same compressed calendar, so nobody is at a disadvantage relative to the field. What it does change is the margin for error. A slow start in a condensed season is harder to fix, because there are fewer soft spots in the schedule to make up ground. That is the part I will be watching in October.

What You Can Do

If you want to see the team before the season starts, plan early. With only four preseason games, seats will move faster than in a normal year. Check the official Vegas Golden Knights site for the exhibition schedule and ticket release dates, and set a reminder rather than assuming you can grab tickets the week of the game.

If you have young kids who skate, keep an eye on City National Arena in Summerlin. The practice facility is where rookie camp opened on September 11, and it is generally the most accessible place in the valley to see NHL level hockey up close without a full ticket price. Practice access rules and times can change, so confirm before you drive out.

If you cannot get to a game, the compressed camp actually makes the team's own coverage more useful than usual. VegasGoldenKnights.com and NHL.com publish camp updates, roster moves, and coach interviews throughout the 12 days. With fewer live viewing chances, that reporting is how most Clark County fans will follow the buildup to the September 29 opener.

It is also a good year to look at the rest of the local hockey calendar instead of only the Golden Knights. The Henderson Silver Knights play at Lee's Family Forum, and youth and adult leagues run all season at valley rinks. If the four preseason games sell out before you get to them, there is still plenty of live hockey within a short drive of most Clark County neighborhoods, usually at a fraction of the price.

And if you are new to the valley and trying to figure out which neighborhoods put you closest to the arenas, the rinks, and the youth hockey programs, that is a question worth asking before you buy. Commute times in Las Vegas change a lot depending on which side of the 215 you land on.
