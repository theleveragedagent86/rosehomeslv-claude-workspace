Golden Knights Name 23-Player Rookie Camp Roster, First Practice September 11 at City National Arena

The Vegas Golden Knights have named 23 players to their 2026 Rookie Camp roster, and the group hits the ice for its first practice on Friday, September 11 at 12:45 p.m. at City National Arena in Summerlin. General Manager Kelly McCrimmon announced the list on September 1, 2026.

The roster breaks down to 13 forwards, seven defensemen, and three goaltenders. It includes some of the most talked about young players in the organization, including forward Trevor Connelly, forward Mathieu Cataford, forward Tuomas Uronen, and goaltender Cameron Whitehead.

If you live anywhere near Summerlin, this is one of the few times each year that the future of the franchise skates a few minutes from your house. Rookie camp is short, it is local, and it happens before the noise of the regular season takes over the valley.

What Happened

On September 1, 2026, Golden Knights General Manager Kelly McCrimmon released the roster and the schedule for the team's 2026 Rookie Camp. Twenty three players made the list. That number is fairly standard for an NHL rookie camp, which is built to give the organization a close look at drafted prospects, recent signings, and invited players all in one place.

The breakdown is 13 forwards, seven defensemen, and three goaltenders. Three goalies for a short camp makes sense once you understand the schedule. Rookie tournaments are played on back to back days, and teams do not want to burn out one young goaltender in a four day stretch.

Among the names on the list, Trevor Connelly is the one most Vegas fans already know. Connelly is a 2024 first round pick who put up 49 points in 46 games with the Henderson Silver Knights last season. He also put together a franchise record 15 game point streak and helped Henderson reach the second round of the Calder Cup Playoffs for the first time in team history. Mathieu Cataford and Tuomas Uronen are also on the roster, along with goaltender Cameron Whitehead.

The first practice is Friday, September 11 at 12:45 p.m. at City National Arena, the Golden Knights practice facility in Summerlin. From there, the group travels to San Jose for the Rookie Faceoff, which runs September 12 through 15. Vegas plays Utah on September 12 and Colorado on September 13.

That timing is worth noting. Rookie camp lands almost a week before the main training camp opens on September 16. So the young players get their reps in, they get evaluated, and then the ones who earned a longer look walk into main camp with the coaching staff already having fresh notes on them.

It is also worth understanding what a rookie camp roster actually is. It is not a depth chart and it is not a promise. Some of these players are drafted prospects the team already controls. Some are recent signings. Some are invited players trying to earn a contract. The camp exists to compress a lot of evaluation into a very small number of days, and for the players, it is often the only structured look they get all year with the NHL staff watching.

The seven defensemen on the list is the number that quietly tells you the most. Rookie tournaments chew through blue liners because the games are fast, the systems are new, and nobody has played together before. Seven gives the staff three full pairs plus a spare, which is exactly enough to survive back to back games without anyone playing 30 minutes a night.

Kelly McCrimmon has run this process the same way for years. He was the general manager who helped build the roster that won the Stanley Cup in 2023, and the organization has leaned hard on drafting and developing since then. Announcing the rookie group in the first days of September, roughly two weeks before main camp, has become a routine marker on the Vegas hockey calendar.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Most people think of Golden Knights hockey as a T-Mobile Arena thing. You buy a ticket, you drive to the Strip, you deal with parking, and you pay for the night out. Rookie camp at City National Arena is the opposite of that. It happens in the middle of a Friday, in a neighborhood rink, in Summerlin.

For families in Summerlin, Centennial Hills, Skye Canyon, and the northwest valley, that matters. City National Arena sits at Downtown Summerlin, which means the trip is short for a huge chunk of the west side. Parking is not a nightmare. You are not fighting Strip traffic. And if practices are open to fans, you get to stand a few feet from players who may be on the NHL roster within a year or two.

Whether this specific September 11 practice is open to the public has not been confirmed in the announcement [NOT VERIFIED]. City National Arena has historically hosted open practices and public skating, and the team posts details on its own channels. If you plan to go, check the Golden Knights website and City National Arena's schedule before you drive over.

There is also a bigger picture here for anyone who cares about the valley. Hockey in Las Vegas is not just an entertainment product anymore. It is a youth sports pipeline, a set of jobs, and a reason people put down roots in specific neighborhoods. Rookie camp is a small event, but it is a sign of how deep the sport has planted itself in Clark County in less than a decade.

Think about the cost side for a minute. A Golden Knights game at T-Mobile Arena is a real expense once you add up tickets, parking, and food for a family of four. A weekday practice at a neighborhood rink is a completely different budget. For a lot of Clark County families, that gap is the difference between following the team on TV and actually experiencing it in person.

Renters and new arrivals should pay attention too. Southern Nevada takes in a steady stream of people from California, Arizona, and the Midwest, and a lot of them land here without a social circle. Local sports events at community level venues are one of the easiest ways to plug in. You do not need a season ticket or an invitation. You just need to show up.

Background and History

City National Arena opened as the Golden Knights practice facility before the franchise ever played a regular season game. It was built in Summerlin, not near the Strip, and that decision shaped how the team connects with the valley. The building holds two sheets of ice, a team practice rink and a community rink, plus a restaurant and a team store.

That second sheet is the part most people underestimate. It gave Las Vegas real youth hockey capacity in a place where, for decades, ice time was extremely limited. Kids in Summerlin and the northwest valley now grow up with a rink they can actually get into. Adult leagues, learn to skate programs, and travel teams all run out of that building.

The player development side grew right alongside it. The Henderson Silver Knights, the team's American Hockey League affiliate, gave Vegas a full development chain inside Clark County. A prospect can be drafted, play in Henderson, practice in Summerlin, and get called up to the Strip without leaving the county. Very few NHL markets have that geography.

Rookie camp is the entry point of that chain. The Rookie Faceoff tournament in San Jose brings together prospect groups from several western teams so the young players get real games against real competition instead of just intrasquad scrimmages. Facing Utah on September 12 and Colorado on September 13 gives the coaching staff two useful data points before main camp.

Connelly's season in Henderson is a good example of why this pipeline matters locally. Fans who bought Silver Knights tickets last year watched a 15 game point streak happen in their own building, in real time, before most of the hockey world was paying attention. That is the payoff of having an affiliate 20 minutes down the 215 instead of in another state.

What Happens Next

The immediate calendar is tight. Practice at City National Arena on Friday, September 11. Travel to San Jose. Rookie Faceoff games September 12 through 15, with Utah on the 12th and Colorado on the 13th. Then the group comes home and main training camp opens on September 16.

Main camp this year is shorter than usual. The Golden Knights are running 12 days of camp instead of the usual 19, with four exhibition games instead of seven, because of the condensed 2026-27 schedule and the Olympic break. That compression puts more weight on rookie camp than normal. There is simply less runway in main camp for a young player to change minds.

Watch Trevor Connelly closely. He is the prospect with the clearest path to real NHL minutes this season, and health is the main open question after he missed training camp last year and about a month and a half in December with lower body injuries. A strong, healthy rookie camp is the cleanest way for him to answer that question early.

Goaltending is the other thread to follow. Carrying three goalies through a short tournament tells you the organization wants live looks at each of them, and Cameron Whitehead is the name that jumps off the list for most fans who track the prospect pool. Goalie development takes longer than skater development, so nothing gets decided in four days, but this is the stage where the club starts sorting its order.

After the Rookie Faceoff, most of this group will head back to junior teams, college programs, or the Henderson Silver Knights. A few will stick around for main camp. That sorting usually becomes clear in the days right after September 15.

The other date to circle is the regular season opener on September 29. Everything between rookie camp and that night is evaluation. If you want to understand the roster you will be watching all winter, the next three weeks are when it gets built.

Ryan's Take

I sell homes across this valley, and I have watched what a practice rink does to a neighborhood. When City National Arena opened in Summerlin, it did not just add a building. It added a reason for families with kids in hockey to look at that part of town first. I have had buyers tell me straight out that they wanted to be within 15 minutes of that rink because of early morning ice times.

That is the quiet story behind rookie camp. Amenities like this are part of why the northwest valley held its value through a rough national stretch. A neighborhood with a rink, a park system, and real retail nearby gives people more reasons to stay put. And homes in areas people do not want to leave tend to sit on the market for fewer days. That is not a hockey opinion, that is just what shows up in the numbers.

There is a second piece agents do not talk about enough. Sports infrastructure tends to arrive with everything else. City National Arena did not go up alone, it went up as part of a larger Downtown Summerlin build out that brought retail, restaurants, and office space with it. When buyers ask me why homes on the west side hold their price, the honest answer is that the daily conveniences are dense there. The rink is one visible piece of a much larger pattern.

I also think events like this are underrated for families who are new to Las Vegas. If you moved here in the last two years and you are still figuring out where you fit, a free or low cost hockey practice on a Friday afternoon is a very easy way to meet people who live near you. That is how neighborhoods actually form.

What You Can Do

If you want to see the practice, check the Golden Knights official site and City National Arena's public schedule before Friday, September 11. Public access to team practices can change day to day, so confirm before you drive out. The arena is at Downtown Summerlin, and the listed practice time is 12:45 p.m.

If you cannot make the Summerlin practice, the Rookie Faceoff games from San Jose are usually covered by the team's own media channels and by local Las Vegas sports reporters. Following the Golden Knights and the Henderson Silver Knights accounts is the simplest way to see which prospects stood out.

Parents with kids interested in hockey should treat this week as a good on ramp. City National Arena's community rink runs learn to skate and youth programs, and the Silver Knights play in Henderson at Lee's Family Forum, which is a much cheaper night out than a Golden Knights game at T-Mobile Arena. Between the two, a family can watch a lot of hockey in Clark County without spending Strip money.

If you are shopping for a home in the northwest valley, do the drive at the hour you would actually be driving it. A house that looks 12 minutes from City National Arena on a map can be a very different trip at 6 a.m. on a Saturday or at 5 p.m. on a weekday. The same goes for families splitting time between Summerlin ice and Henderson games. Test the route before you commit to the address.

And if you are house hunting and proximity to ice time actually matters to your family schedule, say so out loud when you talk to your agent. It changes which neighborhoods make sense and it is a much more useful filter than a generic search radius.
