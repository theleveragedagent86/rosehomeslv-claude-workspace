Golden Knights Single-Game Tickets Go on Sale With 42 Home Games

Vegas Golden Knights single-game tickets for the 2026-27 regular season went on sale September 1, and this year there are 42 home games at T-Mobile Arena instead of the usual 41. The 2026-27 season is the first in NHL history to run 84 games, which means one extra night of hockey on the Las Vegas Strip.

The schedule also includes two five-game homestands, one in November and one in March, and a rematch of the 2026 Stanley Cup Final against the Carolina Hurricanes on Monday, December 21. The team is also rolling out new Flex Plans that let fans pick six or more home games at a discount off single-game pricing.

If you live in Clark County and you have been priced out of a full season ticket package, this is the news you have been waiting for. More home dates and a smaller minimum commitment means more realistic ways to get into the building.

What Happened

The Vegas Golden Knights put single-game tickets for the 2026-27 regular season on sale on September 1, 2026. The announcement came through NHL.com and the team's own channels.

The headline structural change is the schedule itself. The 2026-27 season is the first time NHL clubs play an 84-game regular season. The league previously played 82 games, a number that had been in place for decades. Two additional games per club means one more home date and one more road date for every team, and for Vegas that puts 42 games inside T-Mobile Arena.

Vegas has two five-game homestands built into the calendar, one in November and one in March. Long homestands matter more than casual fans realize. They give the team an extended stretch without travel, and they give local fans a concentrated block of nights where hockey is in town.

The marquee date on the schedule is Monday, December 21, when the Carolina Hurricanes come to T-Mobile Arena in a rematch of the 2026 Stanley Cup Final. That is a Monday night in the middle of the holiday stretch, and it will be one of the toughest tickets of the year.

The team is also introducing Flex Plans for 2026-27. The concept is straightforward. Instead of committing to a full or half season package, a fan picks six or more home games and gets a discount compared to buying those same games individually. It sits between a single ticket and a season plan, which is a gap that a lot of Las Vegas households fall into.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

T-Mobile Arena is a Las Vegas Strip venue, and for the first several years of this franchise, that fact worked against local families as much as for them. Strip venues price for tourists. Golden Knights demand has been strong since the expansion season, and secondary market prices for good matchups have often been well out of range for a family of four from Henderson or the northwest.

The Flex Plan changes that math a little. A six game commitment is a very different financial decision than a 41 or 42 game one. It is a plausible thing for a household to budget for, and it locks in a per game price rather than leaving a family at the mercy of resale pricing on the night they happen to be free.

The extra home date helps too, in a small way. Supply is supply. One more game in the building is one more chance for a local fan to be in the room, and across a full season, marginal supply increases tend to soften the middle of the pricing curve more than the top of it. The Carolina game in December is going to be expensive no matter what. A Tuesday in January against a non rival is where local fans find value.

There is a broader Clark County angle here as well. The Golden Knights are one of the reasons this stopped feeling like a city that only hosted other people's events. Between the Golden Knights, the Raiders, the Aces, the Henderson Silver Knights, and the incoming baseball franchise, Las Vegas built a real professional sports calendar in under a decade. That has changed how residents relate to the Strip. A lot of locals who used to avoid the corridor entirely now drive down it a dozen nights a year.

For anyone who tracks the housing market, that shift matters. Neighborhoods with reasonable access to the resort corridor, places like the southwest valley, parts of Spring Valley, Paradise, and the areas feeding onto the 215 and the 15, get a small quality of life bump from every event night that a local can realistically attend. It is not something that shows up in a comp report. It is absolutely something buyers talk about on a tour.

Season ticket holders feel the change differently. An 84-game schedule means their existing package now covers 42 dates instead of 41, which is one more night to use, sell, or hand to a friend. For anyone who has been carrying seats for years, that is a small bonus. For anyone thinking about splitting a package with neighbors, an even 42 divides more cleanly than an odd 41, which sounds trivial until you have tried to split a season with three other families.

And there is the everyday economics of a game night. Parking, food, and a couple of jerseys add up fast on the Strip, and those costs do not change whether you paid resale or bought a plan. Locals who go often figure out where to eat before, where to park, and which nights the corridor is manageable. That knowledge is worth as much as the ticket discount.

Background and History

The NHL's move to an 84-game schedule ends a very long run at 82 games. Adding games is not a simple decision for a league. It touches the collective bargaining agreement, player workload, travel, arena availability, and broadcast inventory. That the league did it says something about the confidence in demand across the sport right now.

For Vegas specifically, the timing lands well. The Golden Knights have been one of the NHL's most consistently successful franchises since entering the league, and reaching the 2026 Stanley Cup Final kept the team at the front of the national conversation. A December rematch against Carolina is the kind of scheduled event that a league builds a broadcast window around.

T-Mobile Arena opened in 2016 on the Strip near New York New York, and it landed a franchise the following year. The building has become one of the loudest home environments in the league, and the nickname that stuck around it reflects how hard it has been for visiting teams to win there. That home ice reputation is part of what makes 42 dates instead of 41 a genuine talking point rather than a footnote.

The Henderson Silver Knights, the organization's American Hockey League affiliate, play at Lee's Family Forum in Henderson and give Southern Nevada a second and considerably cheaper professional hockey option. Between the two clubs, plus City National Arena in Summerlin serving as the practice facility and youth hockey hub, the valley now has a real hockey infrastructure that did not exist ten years ago.

Ticketing itself has evolved along with all of that. Full season packages, half season plans, and now flexible multi game plans reflect a league wide understanding that the modern fan base is not one thing. Some people want 42 nights. Most people want six good ones.

It is worth remembering how unlikely all of this looked in 2016. Las Vegas had no major professional franchise at all. The prevailing argument against the city was that it was a transient market full of transplants who would keep rooting for the teams they grew up with, and that a desert town would never care about ice hockey. Ten seasons later the Golden Knights have a Stanley Cup banner, sold out buildings, and a youth hockey pipeline that keeps growing. That argument aged badly, and it is one of the more satisfying things about living here.

The 2026 run to the Stanley Cup Final against Carolina is the most recent chapter in that. Deep playoff runs do things to a market that a regular season cannot. They create a shared memory across an entire valley, and they pull in casual fans who then become December ticket buyers. The December 21 rematch is the league handing this city a chance to relive that, on a Monday night, in the middle of the holidays.

What Happens Next

Single-game tickets are already available, so the practical next step for fans is choosing dates. The two five-game homestands in November and March are worth circling if you want to build a Flex Plan around games that cluster together. Homestands are also generally the easiest stretch of the season to find a mid week seat at a reasonable price.

The December 21 game against Carolina will move first and it will move fast. If that is the game you want, waiting is not a strategy. Monday night games are usually softer sells, but a Stanley Cup Final rematch is an exception to every normal pattern.

Between now and the regular season, the club has preseason home games at T-Mobile Arena in late September and training camp opening in mid September. Preseason games are the cheapest way into the building all year, and they are a reasonable option for families with young kids who want the arena experience without the regular season price.

There is a longer term question hanging over the 84-game schedule as well. More games means more wear on players across a season, and coaches and general managers around the league will be watching how rosters hold up through a longer grind. For a team like Vegas that expects to play deep into the spring, load management and depth become even more important. That is one reason the fourth line and the bottom of the roster get so much attention at camp every fall.

Fans will feel the longer season in their own way. Two more games spread across roughly the same calendar window means slightly tighter scheduling, more back to back sets, and a few more mid week home dates. Mid week games are historically the softest tickets in any market, so for locals hunting value, the expanded schedule is quietly good news.

Ryan's Take

I have lived through the entire arc of this franchise, from a city that was told it would never support hockey to a valley where kids in Summerlin and Henderson grow up playing it. What strikes me about this announcement is not the extra game. It is the Flex Plan. That is a team paying attention to the fact that its local fan base is made up of households with mortgages and car payments, not just conventioneers with expense accounts. Six games is a number a normal Las Vegas family can say yes to.

From a real estate seat, the sports calendar has quietly become part of the Las Vegas pitch. When I show homes to a family relocating here, the conversation almost always turns to what there is to do. Ten years ago I talked about restaurants and the outdoors. Now I can list a hockey team, a football team, a championship basketball team, and a baseball franchise on the way. That is a different city, and it makes the case for living here a lot easier to make. Forty two home nights on the Strip is a piece of that, and it is worth appreciating.

What You Can Do

Start by pulling up the full 2026-27 home schedule and picking your realistic nights. Look at weeknights against non rivals if you are budget conscious, and look at the November and March homestands if you want options. Then compare what those specific games cost individually against a six game Flex Plan. If you were going to attend four or five games anyway, the plan usually pencils out.

Buy through official channels. Verified resale and the team's own platform protect you in a way that a random listing does not, and hockey tickets are a common target for fraud around high demand games. For the Carolina game in particular, be careful.

If cost is the barrier, look at the Henderson Silver Knights at Lee's Family Forum. It is a genuinely good product at a fraction of the price, the building is easier to get in and out of, and you will see future Golden Knights before anyone else does. For families in Henderson, Anthem, Inspirada, and Green Valley, it is also a much shorter drive than the Strip.

And plan your transportation before game night, not during it. T-Mobile Arena sits in the middle of the resort corridor, and parking, rideshare pickup, and Strip traffic all take longer than newcomers expect. Locals who go regularly have a system. Build yours on a low stakes preseason night rather than on December 21.
