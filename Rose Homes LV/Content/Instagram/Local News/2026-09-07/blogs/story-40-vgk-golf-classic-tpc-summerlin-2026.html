Golden Knights Hold Annual Charity Golf Classic at TPC Summerlin on September 8

The Vegas Golden Knights host the 2026 VGK Golf Classic at TPC Summerlin on Tuesday, September 8, and the money raised goes straight to Las Vegas nonprofits. It is the one day each year when the entire team gets back together in the same place, in this case a golf course in Summerlin instead of an ice rink.

Money from the tournament flows through the Vegas Golden Knights Foundation. The Foundation supports three main areas: education and youth sports, military and first responders, and health and wellness. All of it stays in Southern Nevada.

This is not a public event. Fans cannot buy a general admission ticket and walk the course. Foursomes and partnership packages were made available for purchase, which is how businesses and individuals get involved. But the results of the day show up all over Clark County in the months that follow.

What Happened

The Golden Knights announced the 2026 VGK Golf Classic on July 14, 2026. The date was set for Tuesday, September 8, 2026, at TPC Summerlin. The Las Vegas Review-Journal reconfirmed the event as upcoming in a September 5, 2026 story about the questions facing the team heading into training camp.

The tournament is an annual fundraiser. Proceeds go to the Vegas Golden Knights Foundation, which then distributes support to Las Vegas nonprofit organizations. The Foundation's stated focus areas are education and youth sports, military and first responders, and health and wellness.

The Review-Journal noted something that makes the timing meaningful. The golf classic marks the first time the whole team is together since the end of last season. Hockey players scatter in the summer. Some go home to Canada, some go to Europe, some train in Michigan or Minnesota or Florida. The golf outing is the reunion.

That matters more than it sounds. A hockey season is long, and it got longer this year. The 2026-27 season is the first with an 84-game schedule. Chemistry gets built in small moments, and a full day on a golf course with new teammates is one of them. Players who signed in July meet the guys they will spend eight months with.

The event is not open to the general public. There is no gate, no grandstand, and no walk-up ticket. Instead the team sold foursomes and partnership packages. A foursome buys a spot in the field. Partnership packages give local businesses visibility and a way to write a meaningful check to a local cause at the same time.

TPC Summerlin is the venue, and that choice is not accidental. It is one of the best known private courses in the Las Vegas Valley, and it is the longtime home of the PGA Tour event held in Las Vegas each fall. The course sits inside Summerlin, tucked against the western edge of the valley with Red Rock views. For a fundraiser that wants to attract serious money, it is the right address.

Format details for these tournaments are usually simple by design. Charity scrambles pair sponsor groups with players and coaches so that everyone in the field gets some time with the team. The golf is secondary. The point is access, and access is what convinces a business to write the check again next year.

It is also worth noting what the event is not. It is not a season ticket holder perk, not a fan festival, and not a media day. Fans who want to see the team in person in September should look at rookie camp, main camp practices, and the preseason home games instead. Those are the public-facing dates.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

A charity golf tournament is easy to scroll past. This one should not be, because the money lands in programs that Clark County families actually use.

Youth sports funding is the piece with the widest reach. Equipment costs are the single biggest barrier to kids playing hockey. A set of gear runs into the hundreds of dollars, and kids outgrow it every year. Programs backed by the Foundation help cover those gaps for families who would otherwise never try the sport. That is how a desert city builds a hockey pipeline.

Education support is the second piece. Clark County School District is one of the largest districts in the country and one of the most stretched. Outside funding for classroom programs, reading initiatives, and school recognition events fills holes that a district budget cannot. When a professional sports team writes a check into that system, it moves real dollars into real schools.

The military and first responder focus hits close to home in a city with Nellis Air Force Base, a large veteran population, and police, fire, and EMS crews who serve one of the busiest tourist corridors in the world. Support for those groups is not symbolic here. Las Vegas learned that in the hardest possible way, and the Golden Knights have carried that responsibility since the franchise started.

There is an economic angle too. A tournament at TPC Summerlin brings sponsors, vendors, caterers, and course staff a full day of work. Local businesses that buy partnership packages get exposure to a well connected crowd. That is not charity, that is commerce, and it is part of why these events keep getting supported year after year.

For Summerlin residents specifically, this is one more entry on a long list of reasons the community stays desirable. Master planned neighborhoods hold value partly because of what happens inside them. Golf, events, parks, and community identity are part of the product, and TPC Summerlin is one of the anchors.

Health and wellness, the third Foundation focus, tends to be the least visible of the three and one of the most needed. Southern Nevada has ranked poorly for years on measures like access to mental health care and the number of physicians per resident. Private dollars do not fix that on their own, but they fund the programs that reach people while the bigger problems get worked on.

The quiet benefit of all of this is trust. A lot of cities have a complicated relationship with their pro sports teams, usually over stadium money and public subsidies. In Las Vegas, the Golden Knights have largely avoided that friction, and the community work is a big reason why. When people feel like a team gives back, they show up.

Background and History

The Vegas Golden Knights Foundation has been part of the franchise almost from the start. The team arrived in 2017 and immediately became more than a sports property in this city. The events of October 2017 cemented a relationship between the club and the community that most expansion teams take decades to build, if they build it at all.

Since then, the organization has kept community work near the center of its identity. Foundation dollars have gone to youth hockey growth, school programs, first responder support, and health causes. The annual golf classic is one of the largest single fundraising days on that calendar.

TPC Summerlin opened in 1991 as part of the Summerlin master plan developed by Howard Hughes. The course was designed by Bobby Weed with consultation from Fuzzy Zoeller, and it has hosted professional golf in Las Vegas for decades. It sits on the northwest side of the valley, framed by the Spring Mountains and Red Rock Canyon.

Summerlin itself has grown from a plan on paper into one of the largest master planned communities in the United States. It now includes Downtown Summerlin, the Las Vegas Ballpark, Lee's Family Forum in nearby Henderson for the AHL club, and City National Arena, the Golden Knights practice facility that sits right in Summerlin. Hockey and this neighborhood have been linked since the franchise's first season.

City National Arena is worth mentioning because it explains why so many Golden Knights players live on the west side. The practice rink is in Summerlin, which means players and their families cluster in Summerlin, The Ridges, Red Rock Country Club, and the surrounding neighborhoods. A golf tournament at TPC Summerlin is essentially a home game for a lot of the roster.

City National Arena also changed youth hockey in this valley. It opened with public ice, learn to skate programs, and adult leagues alongside the NHL practice sheet. Kids in Summerlin, Centennial Hills, and Skye Canyon suddenly had a rink within a reasonable drive. Demand for ice time in Clark County has been climbing ever since.

Charity golf as a fundraising model is common across pro sports, but the scale varies wildly. What separates a good one from a token one is whether the money stays local. In this case the Foundation's grants go to Southern Nevada organizations, which means a Summerlin tee time in September turns into a program in a Las Vegas school or a first responder initiative later in the year.

What Happens Next

The tournament takes place Tuesday, September 8, 2026. After that, attention shifts almost immediately to hockey. Rookie camp and main training camp follow within days, with the full squad reporting for camp on September 16.

Preseason games start Saturday, September 19, and the Golden Knights play two home exhibition dates at T-Mobile Arena, on September 24 against the Utah Mammoth and September 26 against the San Jose Sharks. The regular season opens Tuesday, September 29 against the Chicago Blackhawks.

So the golf classic is effectively the last quiet day of the offseason. Once camp opens, the schedule does not let up until spring. If you follow the team, September 8 is the informal starting gun.

On the charity side, the Foundation typically announces grants and community partnerships throughout the season rather than all at once. Watch for school visits, equipment donations, and first responder nights at T-Mobile Arena during the year. Those are where the golf money shows up in practice.

Businesses that missed the window this year should watch for the 2027 announcement, which based on this year's timeline would come in the middle of summer. Partnership packages and foursomes are limited, and a course like TPC Summerlin fills its field.

Ryan's Take

I sell homes in Summerlin, so I will admit my bias up front. But the reason Summerlin commands the prices it does is not just the houses. It is the density of good things in a small radius. TPC Summerlin, City National Arena, Downtown Summerlin, Red Rock, the trail system, and the schools all sit within a few minutes of each other.

An event like this is a small piece of that picture, and small pieces add up. Buyers who tour Summerlin are not just looking at square footage. They are asking what happens here on a Tuesday in September. The answer being "the NHL team is playing a charity golf tournament down the street" is a better answer than most cities can give.

The part I actually care most about is the youth sports funding. Hockey in the desert is not cheap, and it is not automatic. Every dollar that helps a kid in North Las Vegas or Henderson get on the ice for the first time is a dollar that builds the next generation of this fan base. That is a long game, and this organization has been playing it since day one.

One more thing I would tell anyone shopping Summerlin right now. Golf course adjacency carries a premium here, and it always has. But the value is not only in the view. It is in the fact that a course like TPC Summerlin keeps drawing events, sponsors, and attention to the neighborhood. Amenities that stay busy hold their value. Amenities that go quiet do not.

What You Can Do

Since the tournament is not open to the public, the best way to participate is on the business side. Foursomes and partnership packages are sold through the Golden Knights organization. If your company wants in for next year, the team's community and partnership pages are where those opportunities get posted.

If you want to support the same causes without buying a foursome, the Vegas Golden Knights Foundation accepts direct donations and runs 50/50 raffles at home games throughout the season. Those raffles are the easiest entry point. You are buying a ticket at a game you were already attending, and the proceeds go to the same local organizations.

You can also support local youth hockey directly. Programs across the valley take equipment donations, and gear that your kid outgrew is worth more to a starting family than it is in your garage. Check with the rinks at City National Arena in Summerlin and Lee's Family Forum in Henderson for current drives and clinic schedules.

Finally, keep an eye on the team's community calendar during the season. First responder nights, military appreciation games, and school program announcements all happen at T-Mobile Arena and around the valley. Those are the visible results of days like September 8.

And if you run a small business in the valley, this is a good reminder that sponsorship money does not have to go to the biggest possible platform to work. A local charity event with the right crowd often does more for a small company than a broad ad buy. The Golden Knights are simply the most visible example of a model that plenty of Clark County nonprofits use every year.
