SinBin.vegas Breaks Down Every Pacific Division Rival Before Golden Knights Camp

The Vegas Golden Knights won the Pacific Division last season with 95 points, and the division just got harder. Independent Golden Knights site SinBin.vegas ran a full offseason review of every Pacific rival from September 1 through September 7, 2026, and the roster movement across the division is significant.

Writer Ken Boehlke covered the Anaheim Ducks, Calgary Flames, Edmonton Oilers, Los Angeles Kings, San Jose Sharks, and Seattle Kraken. Each entry lists what each team added, what each team lost, how they did head to head against Vegas last season, and a projected lineup for the coming year.

Two moves stand out. The Los Angeles Kings added Mats Zuccarello and Corey Perry while losing Anze Kopitar. The San Jose Sharks, long the easiest night on the schedule, added defensemen Darnell Nurse and Jacob Trouba. For Golden Knights fans in Clark County, that means the 2026-27 division race is not going to look like last year's.

What Happened

SinBin.vegas is an independent site that has covered the Golden Knights since before the franchise played a game. Ken Boehlke runs it, and the site is one of the few outlets that does deep, unglamorous roster work on this team all summer long.

From September 1 through September 7, 2026, the site published a Pacific Division offseason review series. Each installment focused on one division opponent. The series covered the Anaheim Ducks, the Calgary Flames, the Edmonton Oilers, the Los Angeles Kings, the San Jose Sharks, and the Seattle Kraken.

The format for each entry was consistent. Boehlke listed the roster additions and the roster subtractions for that club. He included last season's head to head results against Vegas. And he laid out a projected lineup for the coming season so readers could see how the pieces fit.

The baseline for all of it is that the Golden Knights won the Pacific Division last season with 95 points. That is the number every rival is trying to beat. Winning a division and then watching everyone else get better is a familiar problem for good teams, and it is where Vegas sits right now.

The Los Angeles Kings made the loudest moves. They added Mats Zuccarello, a skilled veteran winger, and Corey Perry, a player who has been an irritant to Vegas for years and has a long playoff resume. On the other side of the ledger, the Kings lost Anze Kopitar. Kopitar was the face of that franchise for two decades and one of the best two-way centers of his generation.

That is a strange combination for Los Angeles. They got deeper on the wings and older, and they lost the player who defined their identity. How that nets out is one of the most interesting questions in the division.

San Jose made the other headline move, and it is a defensive one. The Sharks added Darnell Nurse and Jacob Trouba, two big, physical, experienced NHL defensemen. San Jose has spent several years at the bottom of the standings while stockpiling young forwards. Adding real defensemen is the sign of a rebuild moving into its next phase.

For Vegas, the San Jose change might matter more than the Los Angeles one. Divisional teams play each other repeatedly. Games against the Sharks have been reliable points in recent seasons. If San Jose becomes a hard team to play against, that is several standings points that no longer come easy.

The rest of the division did not stand still either. Boehlke's entries on the Anaheim Ducks, Calgary Flames, Edmonton Oilers, and Seattle Kraken each ran through the same additions and subtractions format. Anaheim has been collecting young talent for years and is closer to being dangerous than the standings suggest. Edmonton remains the team most people expect to challenge Vegas at the top.

The head to head results in each entry are worth reading closely. Season series matter in the standings and in tiebreakers, and they also tell you which opponents give a team trouble regardless of talent. Some matchups are just bad fits, and those patterns often repeat year over year.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Division games are the games you can actually get to. Pacific opponents play at T-Mobile Arena multiple times each season, and the West Coast start times mean you can watch a road game on a weeknight without staying up past midnight.

A tougher division changes the value of a ticket. Los Angeles, Edmonton, and Calgary already draw well here because those fan bases travel. Add a Sharks team that is no longer a pushover and a Kings team with new names, and the schedule gets more compelling from October through April.

It also changes the emotional temperature of the season. Winning a division with 95 points is comfortable. Fighting for a playoff spot in March is not comfortable, but it is more fun to follow. Local sports bars, jersey sales, and watch parties all move with tension in the standings.

There is a schedule wrinkle this year that makes divisional strength matter even more. The 2026-27 season is the first with an 84-game schedule, up from 82. That brings 42 games to T-Mobile Arena. More games means more divisional matchups and more chances for a rival to steal points.

For families budgeting a season of entertainment, this is useful planning information. The Golden Knights are one of the primary live sports options in Clark County alongside the Raiders, the Aces, the Athletics, and the Henderson Silver Knights. Knowing which games will be the hardest tickets helps you decide when to buy.

And for anyone who has moved here from a Pacific Division city, and there are a lot of you, these are the games where the transplant crowd shows up. An Edmonton or Calgary game at The Fortress has a different feel than a Tuesday against an Eastern Conference club. That is part of what makes hockey work in a city built on people from somewhere else.

Hockey has also become a real driver of weekend travel into Las Vegas. Visiting fans book hotel rooms, eat on the Strip, and fill seats that would otherwise go unsold on a slow midweek night. A stronger division means more visiting fan bases with something to play for, which is good for the local economy and good for anyone who works in hospitality here.

None of this decides anything about home values, and I am not going to pretend it does. But quality of life factors matter to buyers, and a healthy pro sports calendar is one of the things people list when they explain why they stayed in Clark County instead of moving on after a few years.

Background and History

The Golden Knights have been a Pacific Division team since they entered the league in 2017. The division has included Anaheim, Calgary, Edmonton, Los Angeles, San Jose, Seattle, and Vancouver in recent alignments, with Utah's arrival reshaping the Western Conference map.

Vegas has been a consistent contender in that group. Winning the Pacific with 95 points last season continued a run of success that few expansion teams in any sport have matched. That success has raised expectations locally in a way that changes how a 95-point season gets received.

The Edmonton Oilers have been the measuring stick in this division for several years, built around elite top-end talent. Calgary and Vancouver have cycled through retools. Anaheim has been rebuilding for a long stretch and has accumulated young talent that is starting to arrive. Seattle, as the league's most recent expansion team before Utah, is still finding its identity.

San Jose's story is the most dramatic. The Sharks were a perennial contender through the 2010s, then fell apart and spent several seasons near the bottom of the standings. Those years produced high draft picks. Adding veteran defensemen like Darnell Nurse and Jacob Trouba is what a team does when it thinks the young core is ready to compete.

Anze Kopitar leaving Los Angeles is a genuine era marker. He was drafted by the Kings in 2005 and played his entire NHL career there, winning Stanley Cups in 2012 and 2014. Franchise players who spend twenty years in one city are rare. His departure closes a chapter for one of the Golden Knights' most frequent opponents.

Independent coverage like SinBin.vegas fills a real gap in this market. Las Vegas has a strong daily paper in the Review-Journal, but a single beat writer cannot produce a six-part division breakdown in the first week of September. Sites run by dedicated fans often do the deepest offseason work in any market.

SinBin.vegas has been doing this since 2016, before the team had players or a name most people could remember. The site covered the expansion draft process, the early Silver Knights years, and every roster transaction since. That kind of continuity is why its offseason series carries weight with local fans.

It is also a reminder of how fast hockey culture formed in Southern Nevada. In most markets, independent team blogs grow over decades alongside generations of fans. Here the whole ecosystem, the team, the AHL affiliate, the practice rink, the youth programs, and the coverage, got built in under ten years.

What Happens Next

Training camp opens September 16, and every team in the division is doing the same thing Vegas is: sorting out lines, finding a defensive pairing that works, and figuring out which prospects are ready. Projected lineups from early September rarely survive first contact with the preseason.

Preseason games run through late September. The Golden Knights play a four-game exhibition schedule with two home dates at T-Mobile Arena, September 24 against the Utah Mammoth and September 26 against the San Jose Sharks. That Sharks game is an early look at the new San Jose blue line, though preseason lineups are never full strength.

The regular season opens Tuesday, September 29 against the Chicago Blackhawks. Divisional matchups will be spread across the full 84-game schedule, and the first meetings with the Kings and Sharks will tell you a lot about whether the offseason moves worked.

The real verdict does not arrive until December or January. Teams that add veterans in the summer often need months to sort out roles. Watch the standings around the holidays. If San Jose is still hanging around and Los Angeles has found a way to replace Kopitar's minutes, the Pacific race will be genuinely close.

SinBin.vegas will keep covering the division through the season, and the projected lineups from this series make a useful reference point. Save the Kings and Sharks entries and check them again in November to see how much changed.

Ryan's Take

I like this story because it is the kind of work that does not get done in most cities. Somebody sat down and wrote six detailed breakdowns of teams that are not even the local club, in the first week of September, for a Vegas audience. That is a mature sports market, and Las Vegas did not have one of those a decade ago.

On the hockey side, I think the San Jose news is being underrated. Everybody is talking about the Kings because Kopitar leaving is a big name story. But divisional standings are decided by the games you are supposed to win. When a bottom-feeder adds two legitimate top-four defensemen, those free points disappear, and a 95-point season becomes an 88-point season without anything actually going wrong.

What that means for fans here is simple. Do not assume the Golden Knights coast to another division title. Buy tickets to the games you actually want to see, because the second half of this season could get tight. A tight season is better for the city anyway. It fills the building, it fills the bars, and it keeps this town talking about hockey into April.

The other thing I take from this series is a reminder about how quickly a good situation can be taken for granted. Vegas has been a playoff team for almost its entire existence. That is not normal. Most expansion franchises spend a decade losing. Enjoy the years when the biggest worry is that the division got a little tougher.

What You Can Do

Read the series. SinBin.vegas published all six entries between September 1 and September 7, 2026, and they are free. If you only read one, make it the San Jose Sharks entry, because that is the team whose change affects the Vegas standings the most.

If you plan to attend games, look at the divisional dates first when single game tickets are available. Kings, Oilers, and Flames games at T-Mobile Arena tend to sell fastest because those fan bases travel and because a lot of transplants in Summerlin, Henderson, and Centennial Hills grew up rooting for them. Buying early usually beats buying at the door.

The two preseason home games on September 24 and September 26 are the cheapest way to get in the building this month, and the September 26 game is against San Jose. It will not be a full-strength look at the new Sharks, but it is a look.

For ongoing coverage, follow both the Las Vegas Review-Journal Golden Knights beat and the independent sites. The paper gives you daily news and quotes from the room. The independent sites give you the roster math and the long-form breakdowns. Together you get a much clearer picture than either one alone.
