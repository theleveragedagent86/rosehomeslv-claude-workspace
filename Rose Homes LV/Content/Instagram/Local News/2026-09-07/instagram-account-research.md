## 8. General guidance (NOT Ryan's data, published best practice as of 2026)

Kept as a fallback layer. Where it conflicts with Sections 3 through 6, Ryan's own data wins.

### Trial reels
- Trial reels are shown only to non-followers. They do not appear on the profile grid, in follower feeds, or on the reels tab. They are a pure cold-audience test.
- Volume is the point. More trials means more data. Use them to A/B test hooks, thumbnails, and content angles before committing a piece to the main feed.
- Recommended split: trials for hypotheses you are unsure about, regular reels for anything that already has evidence behind it.
- Do not lean on the auto-share-to-followers conversion. Reporting suggests Instagram treats a converted trial as if it were posted at the original trial time, so it does not get treated as fresh content. Re-uploading the winner as a new reel generally performs better.
- Low trial numbers are normal. Many trials land at 100 to 300 views. That is expected and not a signal of account health.

Important caveat for this account: Ryan is already pulling 89.5% non-follower reach on regular reels. Trial reels are designed to solve a cold-reach problem he does not currently have. Their value here would be hook testing, not distribution.

### Posting time
- Broad 2026 benchmarks: weekday mid-morning 9 to 11 AM, lunch 12 to 1 PM, and evening 6 to 9 PM in the audience's local time zone. Evening tends to be strongest for reels specifically.
- Benchmarks claim Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday are strongest and Friday and Saturday are weakest. **Ryan's own data contradicts the Friday and Saturday part.** Ignore that piece.
- For a local account, the better framing is real-life scroll moments for that specific market. Las Vegas has heavy shift-work and hospitality employment, which flattens the usual commuter peaks. Ryan's active-times curve confirms exactly this flattening.

## 9. Bottom line for content decisions

1. Local news reels are the entire business. 99.5% of views and 99.9% of interactions. Everything else on the account is decoration.
2. Household-cost stories beat everything. Water cuts and STR fines at 19K to 21K, versus CCSD administrative stories at 5K to 7K. Lead with the bill, the tax, the rate, the fine.
3. Shares are the metric to optimize. The top reel had 540 shares against 606 likes and reached 93% non-followers. Write for forwardability, meaning the story should make someone think "my neighbor needs to see this."
4. Baseline for judging a new reel over the last 90 days: median in the 5K to 6K range, top quartile above roughly 13K, exceptional above 19K. Anything under 4K is bottom-tier.
5. The follow conversion is real and worth chasing. 89 follows off a single reel. Ask for the follow in reels about direct household costs, since those are the ones that travel.
6. Followers barely see this content (10.5% of views) but drive 45.3% of interactions. Early follower engagement is likely what triggers the wider push, which is an argument for posting when followers are actually awake rather than when generic benchmarks say to post.
