## 2. What Actually Wins on This Account

This section is new and it should drive which stories get the premium evening slots.

Reels are the entire account. 99.5 percent of views and 99.9 percent of interactions come from reels. Stories and static posts contribute under one percent of each. Nothing in this schedule should be planned around any other format.

Every single one of the top 12 reels by views over 90 days is a hard local-news story. Not one home tour, market update, hockey post, or personality reel appears anywhere in the top 20. That is the clearest signal in the entire dataset.

Inside the winners there is a strict hierarchy. Direct household cost beats institutional scandal, and institutional scandal beats district administration. The two biggest reels are a federal water cut and short-term rental fines, both above 19K, and both are "this hits your bill" stories. Regulatory and accountability stories sit at 15K to 17K. CCSD administrative stories sit at 5K to 7K, roughly a third of the pocketbook stories.

Shares are the engine. The top reel had 540 shares against 606 likes and reached 93 percent non-followers off that ratio. A reel that gets shares at 80 percent or more of its like count travels. A reel with shares far below likes is being consumed by existing followers and will cap out.

The working test for a premium evening slot is three-part. Does the story have a direct household impact, does it carry a single shareable stat, and does it have a civic or money angle. A story that hits all three goes to Monday, Tuesday, or Friday at 6:30 PM. A story that hits two goes to a weekend evening. A story that hits one goes to a midday or a Wednesday and Thursday slot.

Applied to this week, the stories that hit all three are S05 (what a Fed hike does to a Vegas payment), S04 (a household now needs $116,563), S01 (940 acres and up to 6,000 homes), S03 (Henderson ends a retiree health subsidy), and S12 ($75 million in public money). Those five hold every premium evening slot on the board.
