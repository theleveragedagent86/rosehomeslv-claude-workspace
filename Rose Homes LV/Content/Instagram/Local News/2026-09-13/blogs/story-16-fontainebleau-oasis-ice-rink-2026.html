The Strip Has Only One Real Outdoor Ice Rink Left and It Reopens November 19

Fontainebleau Las Vegas confirmed that its rooftop Oasis Ice Rink reopens on November 19 and runs through January 17, 2027. That makes it the only real outdoor ice on the Las Vegas Strip this holiday season, because the Cosmopolitan discontinued its Boulevard Pool rink in late 2025.

The rink sits on the resort's third-floor pool deck and covers nearly 8,000 square feet. Skate fees start at $15 for Nevada locals on select dates, which is the detail most valley families care about. If you have been putting off planning your December, this is the date to circle.

What Happened

Fontainebleau Las Vegas announced that the Oasis Ice Rink is coming back for another winter run. The opening date is November 19, 2026. The closing date is January 17, 2027. That gives the valley a two-month window that covers Thanksgiving weekend, the entire month of December, and the first half of January.

The rink is built on the resort's third-floor pool deck. That is an unusual place to put ice in the desert, and it is part of why the attraction gets attention every year. The surface is close to 8,000 square feet, which is large enough to handle a real crowd of casual skaters without feeling like a crowded loop around a tiny oval.

Pricing starts at $15 for Nevada locals on select dates. That locals rate is the piece worth reading twice. Strip attractions are often priced for visitors first, and a family of four can burn through a hundred dollars fast on the Boulevard. A $15 entry point on select dates puts this in reach for a lot of Clark County households that would otherwise skip it.

The bigger news inside the news is what happened to the competition. The Cosmopolitan of Las Vegas ran a seasonal rink at its Boulevard Pool for years. It became a holiday tradition for a lot of locals and a photo stop for visitors. That rink was discontinued in late 2025. With it gone, Fontainebleau's Oasis Ice Rink is now the Strip's only real outdoor ice. Other resorts and shopping centers around town run holiday displays and synthetic skating surfaces, but real outdoor ice on the Strip is down to one.

Two months is also a longer run than a lot of people expect. November 19 to January 17 is roughly 60 days of operation. That is a real commitment of staff, equipment, and deck space for a resort that could otherwise be using that third-floor area for something else. It tells you the attraction performed well enough last season to earn another full winter.

One thing worth saying plainly: the rink is real ice, not a synthetic panel surface. That distinction gets glossed over in a lot of holiday coverage around the valley. Several shopping centers and neighborhood events in Clark County set up plastic skating surfaces every December. Those are fun, and they are cheap to run, but they do not feel the same under a blade. Fontainebleau's is the genuine article, outdoors, on the Strip.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Living in Las Vegas means you are surrounded by attractions built for people who do not live here. Room rates, show tickets, and resort fees all assume a visitor budget. So when a Strip property puts out a locals rate, it matters. It is one of the small things that makes the trade-off of living in a tourist town feel fair.

For families, this is a real December option. Clark County does not get snow days. Kids here grow up without the winter traditions that people in other states take for granted. An outdoor rink on a cool desert night is about as close as the valley gets, and it gives parents something to plan that is not another mall trip or another movie.

There is also a practical angle for anyone who lives near the resort corridor. The stretch of Las Vegas Boulevard around Fontainebleau, near the Sahara Avenue end of the Strip and the convention corridor, has been changing fast. New attractions pull traffic, parking demand, and rideshare volume into specific blocks on specific nights. If you own a condo in that area or you commute through it, a two-month seasonal draw is part of your winter reality.

And for people thinking about moving here, questions like this come up constantly. Buyers relocating from Chicago, Denver, Minneapolis, or the Northeast ask what their kids will do in winter. They ask whether there is anything seasonal at all. Having a specific answer, with a date and a price, is more useful than saying the weather is nice.

Hockey families feel this one differently. Las Vegas has turned into a real hockey town over the last decade, between the Vegas Golden Knights at T-Mobile Arena, the Henderson Silver Knights at Lifeguard Arena, and now PWHL Las Vegas bringing pro women's hockey to America First Center and T-Mobile Arena. A lot of kids in Summerlin, Henderson, and the northwest valley got their first pair of skates because of that. Casual open ice is how most of them start, and losing rinks shrinks that on-ramp.

There is a renter and condo angle too. The north Strip and the area around the convention corridor has a heavy mix of high-rise condos, mid-rise rentals, and short-term stays. Seasonal attractions change the rhythm of those buildings. More foot traffic, more rideshare pickups, busier elevators and garages on weekend nights. If you are shopping a condo near Las Vegas Boulevard and Sahara Avenue, it is worth visiting the block on a December Saturday, not just on a quiet Tuesday afternoon.

There is a jobs piece here as well, even if it is a small one. Seasonal attractions mean seasonal hiring: rink attendants, skate rental staff, food and beverage, and security. Those are not career positions, but in a valley where hospitality drives so much of the payroll, a two-month operation on the Strip is a couple of months of shifts for people who live here. A steady, busy resort corridor is still the engine behind a lot of Clark County household income, and household income is what supports home prices.

Background and History

Seasonal ice on the Strip is not new, but it has always been fragile. Running a real outdoor rink in the Mojave Desert takes serious equipment and serious power, because the chiller system has to fight ambient heat even in December. Resorts build these rinks as marketing amenities, not as profit centers, so they come and go with the priorities of whoever is running the property.

The Cosmopolitan's Boulevard Pool rink was the best-known version of this in recent years. It sat on a pool deck overlooking Las Vegas Boulevard, it was wrapped in the property's holiday programming, and it drew both locals and tourists. Its discontinuation in late 2025 removed the valley's most recognizable Strip skating tradition, and it left a gap that Fontainebleau's rink now fills by default.

Fontainebleau Las Vegas itself is a relatively new arrival at the north end of the Strip after one of the longest and most complicated development stories in the city's history. The property opened in December 2023 following years of stalled construction and ownership changes. Since then it has been building out the kind of programming that gives a resort a reason to be part of a local's year, not just a visitor's weekend.

The Oasis Ice Rink fits that strategy. A seasonal attraction with a locals rate is a way to get valley residents to walk the property, and to build a habit. That is the same playbook the valley has seen work in other places, from holiday light displays in Henderson to seasonal events at Downtown Summerlin.

It also lands at a moment when the valley's ice footprint is growing in some places and shrinking in others. Youth and adult hockey demand has climbed steadily since the Golden Knights arrived in 2017, and Lifeguard Arena in downtown Henderson was built in part to serve that demand. At the same time, casual seasonal rinks on the Strip have thinned out. The two trends are pulling in opposite directions, and that is exactly why losing the Cosmopolitan rink registered with so many locals.

What Happens Next

Mark November 19, 2026 as the opening. The run continues through January 17, 2027. Expect the first two weekends and the stretch between Christmas and New Year's to be the busiest windows, because that is when both visitor volume and local family schedules peak at the same time.

Watch for the resort to publish its full session schedule, locals-rate eligible dates, and skate rental details closer to the opening. The $15 locals starting price applies to select dates, so the calendar matters. If you want the low rate, plan around the dates rather than showing up and hoping. Nevada ID is the standard requirement for locals pricing at Strip properties, so bring it.

Also watch how this overlaps with everything else happening on the Boulevard this winter. The Formula 1 Heineken Las Vegas Grand Prix runs November 19 through 21, 2026, which is the exact weekend the rink opens. Track prep has already started closing lanes on Harmon Avenue overnight. If you are heading to the north Strip that opening weekend, give yourself extra time and check closures before you go.

The longer question is whether any other Strip property decides to bring outdoor ice back. Resorts watch each other closely, and an attraction that runs two full months without competition tends to attract imitators. If Fontainebleau has a strong season, it would not be a surprise to see another property test a seasonal rink in 2027. If the season is quiet, the Strip may simply stay at one.

Locals should also keep an eye on how the pricing holds up. A $15 starting rate for Nevada residents on select dates is a headline number. What matters over a two-month run is how many dates qualify, what skate rental adds, and whether the good sessions sell out before working families can get to them. Those details usually become clear within the first two weekends.

Ryan's Take

I get asked about winter in Las Vegas more than almost anything else by buyers coming from colder states. The honest answer is that our winter is mild, our December evenings are genuinely pleasant, and the trade-off is that we do not get the seasonal rituals a lot of people grew up with. So when a real outdoor rink survives on the Strip, I pay attention, because it is one of the few things that answers that question directly.

From a housing standpoint, nobody buys a home because of an ice rink. But quality-of-life inventory is real, and it adds up. Buyers compare Las Vegas to Phoenix, to Boise, to Salt Lake City, and to Texas. What tips those decisions is usually a stack of small things: what a family can do on a Saturday in December, whether locals get treated like locals, and whether the entertainment corridor keeps investing. A north Strip property spending money on a two-month seasonal draw with a $15 locals rate is a small signal that the corridor is still building for residents, not only for the convention calendar.

I will also say this as somebody who shows homes all over the valley: the north Strip is one of the more misunderstood pockets in Clark County. People write it off based on what it looked like ten years ago, when Fontainebleau was an unfinished shell and that stretch of Las Vegas Boulevard felt like a dead zone. That is not the block anymore. Steady programming, a working resort, and a reason for locals to show up in December all change how a corridor reads to a buyer. Keep watching it.

What You Can Do

If you want to skate this season, start with the calendar. The rink opens November 19 and closes January 17. Check the Fontainebleau Las Vegas website for the published session times and for the specific dates that qualify for the $15 Nevada locals rate before you buy. Weekday evenings early in the run are usually the easiest tickets and the shortest lines.

Bring your Nevada ID if you are claiming the locals rate. Plan parking or rideshare ahead of time, especially on the opening weekend, because F1 race weekend lands on the same dates and the resort corridor will be busy. If you live near the Strip, sign up for the F1 text alerts and check the closure map so you are not surprised by an overnight lane restriction on your street.

If you are new to the valley or you are helping family relocate here, keep a short list of seasonal local options. Beyond this rink, Clark County has year-round indoor ice at City National Arena in Summerlin and Lifeguard Arena in Henderson, both of which run public skate sessions and youth hockey programs. Those are the places locals actually use all year, and they are worth knowing about if your kids get hooked after one night on the Strip.

Dress for a desert winter night, not for a Vegas afternoon. December evenings in the valley regularly drop into the 40s, and standing on ice in the open air on a third-floor deck is colder than the forecast suggests. Gloves matter more than a heavy coat, especially for kids who are going to fall. Go early in the evening if you have younger children, because the later sessions tend to draw a heavier visitor crowd.

Finally, if holiday traditions are part of how you are deciding where to live in Clark County, make a list and go see them in person this season. Compare what is walkable from a Henderson neighborhood against what is easy from Summerlin, Centennial Hills, or a condo near the Boulevard. Those small quality-of-life details are easy to overlook in a listing photo and very hard to change after you close.
