PWHL Las Vegas Opens Training Camp November 18 With Two Preseason Games in Henderson

PWHL Las Vegas will open training camp on November 18, and the team's first two games in front of a home crowd are both in Henderson. The Professional Women's Hockey League announced the schedule on September 10, confirming that PWHL Las Vegas hosts the Minnesota Frost on November 22 and the Vancouver Goldeneyes on November 24. Both preseason games are at America First Center in Henderson.

Regular season home games will be played at T-Mobile Arena on the Las Vegas Strip. That split matters more than it sounds. It means the valley now has a professional women's hockey team with a real presence in two very different parts of Clark County, and hockey families in Henderson finally have a top-level team playing games a short drive from home.

If you have been waiting for a concrete date to put on the calendar, this is it. November 18 for camp. November 22 and November 24 for the games. Everything else about this franchise has been announcements and anticipation. Now there are actual dates.

The announcement is short on its face, but it answers the two questions valley hockey fans have been asking all year. When does this team actually start, and where can I see it in person without buying a Strip ticket? November 18, and Henderson.

What Happened

On September 10, 2026, the Professional Women's Hockey League published its training camp and preseason plans. The league set November 18 as the date training camps open across the league, and PWHL Las Vegas is part of that group.

The two preseason games on the Las Vegas schedule are both home dates. The first is against the Minnesota Frost on November 22. The second is against the Vancouver Goldeneyes on November 24. Two games, two nights apart, both at America First Center in Henderson.

America First Center is the same building that hosts the Henderson Silver Knights, the American Hockey League affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights. It is a modern arena built for hockey, with good sightlines and a size that makes a game feel close instead of cavernous. For a preseason game, that is exactly the right room. A big empty NHL bowl can make a strong crowd look small. A right-sized arena makes the same crowd feel loud.

The regular season is a different story. PWHL Las Vegas plays its regular season home games at T-Mobile Arena, the 20,000-seat building on the Strip that the Vegas Golden Knights call home. That is a serious commitment of a premium venue to a women's professional team, and it says something about how the league and the market view the ceiling here.

So the pattern for fans is simple. Preseason in Henderson. Regular season on the Strip. Two venues, two different experiences, one team.

It is worth pausing on the opponents. The Minnesota Frost are one of the league's established franchises and have been near the top of the standings since the PWHL launched. The Vancouver Goldeneyes are a newer western club, which makes them a likely regular opponent for Las Vegas given the travel map. Getting both on the preseason slate gives valley fans a look at an old guard team and a fellow west-side club in the same week.

The timing is deliberate too. November 18 for camp and November 22 for the first game is a short runway. That is normal for professional hockey, where camps are compressed and rosters come together quickly, but it means the first game in Henderson will be raw. Lines will be experimental. Goalies will split time. That is part of the appeal of a preseason game. You are watching decisions get made in real time rather than watching a finished product.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with the obvious. If you have a kid who plays hockey in this valley, you now have a professional women's team playing real games 20 minutes from most Henderson neighborhoods. That is not a small thing. Youth hockey has grown fast in Clark County since the Golden Knights arrived, and a lot of those players are girls who have never had a local pro team that looks like them.

Seeing it in person changes what a kid believes is possible. A poster on a wall is one thing. Watching a professional player take a faceoff 40 feet away is another. Parents in Green Valley, Anthem, Inspirada, and Cadence now have that option on two November nights without booking a flight.

Then there is the everyday practical side. Henderson has spent years building out its entertainment and events footprint, and America First Center is the anchor. Every event that fills that building brings people to nearby restaurants, bars, and hotels. Those dollars stay in Henderson. Over time, that kind of activity is part of what makes a submarket feel alive rather than just residential.

For homeowners near the arena district, this is another data point in a long trend. Henderson keeps adding reasons for people to drive into the city rather than out of it. That is good for local businesses and good for the long-term story you tell when you sell your house. "Walking distance to a pro hockey venue" is a line that did not exist here 10 years ago.

There is a Strip angle too. T-Mobile Arena hosting regular season women's pro hockey means more event nights on the Strip in the winter months. More event nights means more visitors, more hotel bookings, and more shift work for the tens of thousands of Clark County residents whose paychecks depend on that calendar staying full. A steady events calendar is a steady jobs calendar, and a steady jobs calendar is what keeps housing demand from swinging wildly.

Commuters should note the geography. America First Center sits in Henderson, so the November 22 and November 24 crowds are coming off the 215 and local Henderson arterials rather than the Strip corridor. If you live near the arena, expect the usual event-night traffic bump on those two evenings. If you live closer to the Strip, the regular season dates at T-Mobile Arena are the ones that will touch your drive.

Renters feel this differently than owners. A lot of people who work Strip events rent in the central and southeast valley, and a fuller winter event calendar means more available shifts. That is real money in a household budget. It also tends to firm up rental demand in the neighborhoods closest to the venues, which cuts both ways depending on whether you are signing a lease or collecting one.

Background and History

Las Vegas was not a hockey town before 2017. That is worth remembering, because it is easy to forget how fast this happened. The Vegas Golden Knights arrived, sold out T-Mobile Arena, and turned a desert city into a place where kids ask for sticks at Christmas.

The Henderson Silver Knights followed, giving the valley a second level of pro hockey and a reason to build out America First Center. That building has since become a regular stop for hockey, basketball, concerts, and community events. It gave Henderson an anchor venue it did not previously have.

The Professional Women's Hockey League is the newest layer. The league consolidated the top of women's professional hockey into a single well-funded structure, which was the piece that had been missing for decades. Instead of competing leagues splitting the talent pool, the best players in the world ended up in one place with real salaries, real arenas, and real broadcast coverage.

Las Vegas joining that league puts the valley in a specific category. This is not a minor league experiment or a barnstorming tour. It is a franchise in the top women's professional hockey league in the world, playing regular season games in an NHL arena. For a market that had zero hockey nine years ago, that is a remarkable run.

The choice of Henderson for the preseason games also fits a pattern. Teams here have consistently used the Henderson arena for the events where atmosphere matters more than capacity. It is a smart way to build a fan base. Fill a mid-size building and let the noise do the marketing.

Henderson's own growth is part of this story. The city has pushed hard to build an identity separate from Las Vegas, with a redeveloped Water Street district, expanded parks and trails, and an events calendar that keeps residents inside city limits on weekends. An arena that hosts professional hockey multiple nights a month is the kind of asset that anchors all of that. It gives the rest of the plan something to orbit.

The youth hockey side has grown alongside it. Rinks in the valley that once sat half empty now run full schedules, and travel teams out of Clark County are a normal part of the regional landscape. That growth happened because kids had somewhere to watch and somewhere to play. A women's professional team in the valley extends the same logic to a group of players who previously had to look out of state for role models.

What Happens Next

The calendar from here is straightforward. Training camp opens November 18. Players report, coaches sort out lines, and the roster starts taking shape. Camp is usually when you learn who is actually going to play and who is on the bubble.

Four days later, on November 22, the Minnesota Frost come to Henderson. Two days after that, on November 24, the Vancouver Goldeneyes visit. Those two games are the first real look valley fans get at this team on home ice.

After the preseason, attention shifts to T-Mobile Arena for the regular season. Specific regular season home dates and ticket details were not part of the September 10 announcement, so watch the league and team channels for that release. If it follows the usual pattern, single-game tickets and season packages get announced well before the first regular season home game.

For families planning around this, the practical move is to treat late November as the window. Camp opening on November 18 typically means open practices or fan events are possible in that stretch, though nothing specific has been announced. The two Henderson games are the confirmed dates. Build around those.

There is also a longer game to watch. How these two preseason nights draw will shape how aggressively the team markets the regular season and how much programming the league schedules in this market going forward. Strong crowds in Henderson in late November become the argument for more Las Vegas dates later. Empty seats become the opposite argument. Preseason attendance rarely gets talked about publicly, but it gets watched closely inside the building.

Ryan's Take

I have watched this valley change since the Golden Knights showed up, and the sports story keeps turning into a housing story. Every time Las Vegas or Henderson adds a franchise, the relocation conversation shifts a little. People move here for jobs and weather and no state income tax. They stay because there is something to do on a Tuesday night in November.

Henderson in particular benefits from this. Buyers who look at Green Valley, Anthem, Inspirada, and Cadence are usually families, and families care about what their kids can do here. A pro women's hockey team playing in their city is a real answer to that question, not a marketing line. When I show homes in those areas, the arena district comes up more than people expect.

I will not tell you a preseason hockey game changes property values. It does not. What it does is add one more piece to a picture that has been getting stronger for a decade, and pictures like that are what keep demand steady when the market gets choppy.

The thing I keep coming back to is how normal all of this feels now. Nine years ago a pro hockey franchise in the desert was a punchline. Today we have an NHL team, an AHL team, and a top-level women's professional team sharing two arenas in the same county. Markets that add institutions like that tend to hold their value better over long stretches, because people put down roots when a place feels finished rather than temporary.

What You Can Do

First, put the dates on your calendar now. November 18 for camp opening, November 22 for the Minnesota Frost game, and November 24 for the Vancouver Goldeneyes game. Both games are at America First Center in Henderson. Late November fills up fast with holiday plans, so claim those nights early if you want them.

Second, follow the team and league channels directly rather than waiting for news coverage. Ticket on-sale dates, open practice announcements, and any schedule adjustments go out through official channels first. The PWHL site is where the September 10 announcement landed, and that is where the next round of details will land too.

Third, if you have a young player in the house, look at this as more than a night out. Introductory hockey programs in the valley tend to see a bump after big local hockey moments, and spots fill quickly. If watching a game sparks something, be ready to act on it while the interest is fresh.

Fourth, plan the logistics like you would for any event night. Arrive early, expect parking near the arena to fill, and check whether the team is running any locals pricing or family packages when tickets go on sale. Two games two nights apart also means you can pick the one that fits your week instead of scrambling for a single date.

Finally, if you are thinking about Henderson as a place to live, go to one of these games and pay attention to the area around the arena on a game night. You learn more about a community from an hour in it on a busy evening than from an hour of reading about it online.
