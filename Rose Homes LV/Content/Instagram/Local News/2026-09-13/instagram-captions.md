## Story 1: CCSD Is Cutting $51.6 Million And Every School Feels It

CCSD Is Cutting $51.6 Million And Every School Feels It

Clark County School District just announced it is cutting $51.6 million from its budget this fall. Not next year. This fall.

Here is how the money breaks down. $29.1 million of those cuts land directly on individual schools.

The other $22.5 million comes out of the central office through a 2.5% across-the-board reduction.

The reason the district gave is enrollment. CCSD lost more than 10,400 students from last year to this year.

Fewer students means less funding. Less funding means fewer people in the building.

This is not a spreadsheet problem. It is a classroom problem.

When a school loses money, it does not lose the principal. It loses the reading specialist, the elective teacher, the aide who works with the kids who need the most help.

Class sizes creep up. Programs get folded into other programs. The music teacher starts splitting time between three campuses.

And parents notice. They always notice.

Here is the part that matters if you own a home in this valley. School quality is one of the biggest drivers of what buyers will pay for a house.

People shop schools before they shop floor plans. I have watched buyers cross an entire zip code off their list over a school rating.

So when a neighborhood school loses staff and programs, that does not stay inside the fence. It eventually shows up in what your house is worth.

The flip side is worth saying too. A 10,400 student drop is a big signal about who is moving in and who is moving out of Clark County.

That is a demographic story, not just a budget story. Families are the backbone of the housing market here.

I am not saying the district had a choice. When the students leave, the money leaves with them.

But somebody should be asking the harder question. Why are 10,400 families gone?

Is CCSD losing students because of the schools, or because families cannot afford to stay in Clark County anymore?

Not discouraged. Barred.

The part that catches people is the word assigned. Your three days are not whatever three days you feel like. They are set for your address.

If your sprinkler timer is still running a summer schedule, it is very possible you are out of compliance right now and do not know it.

Grass in this valley is on borrowed time. We have been moving toward conversions and turf removal for years, and every season the rules tighten a little more.

So if you are buying a home with a big lawn, you are not just buying a lawn. You are buying a future decision about whether to rip it out.

And if you own a home you plan to sell, a smart desert landscape reads as a lower water bill to a buyer. That is a real selling point in a valley where everybody watches the water news.
