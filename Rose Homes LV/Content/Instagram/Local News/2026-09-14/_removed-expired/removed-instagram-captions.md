Three Road Projects Are About To Wreck Your Commute This Week

If you drive Buffalo and Charleston in the morning, give yourself extra time starting today. That intersection is down to one lane in each direction on every approach.

The RTC's Seeing Orange traffic page lists the current restrictions, and it updates every Thursday.

Here is what is actually happening. Intersection improvements at Buffalo Drive and Charleston Boulevard hold one lane open in each direction on all approaches, Monday through Friday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m.

The Charleston storm drain project from Essex to Lindell is in final construction cleanup. Target completion date is September 19, 2026.

And Lake Mead Boulevard from Losee Road to Simmons Street stays under road improvements through fall 2026. A minimum of one lane stays open each direction, and Martin Luther King Boulevard southbound is reduced to two lanes.

That last one is the one nobody is talking about.

The Buffalo and Charleston work and the storm drain cleanup both have end dates you can count on your fingers. Lake Mead Boulevard does not. That corridor is torn up into the fall, and it feeds a huge chunk of North Las Vegas traffic every single day.

Here is my honest take. These are not tourist closures. Nobody flying in for a weekend is sitting at Buffalo and Charleston at 7:45 in the morning. This is a northwest Las Vegas commuter intersection, and the people paying for it are the ones who live here.

The storm drain work is genuinely worth it. Anyone who has watched Charleston turn into a river after a monsoon knows exactly why that project exists.

But the timing of three overlapping projects on major corridors is the part that gets frustrating. If you are running school drop-off and then heading east, you are hitting at least one of these.

Plan an extra ten minutes this week and check Seeing Orange before you leave. It is free and it is updated weekly.

Two Golden Knights Prospects Came Home From San Jose Hurt

The Vegas Golden Knights rookies split their two games at the Rookie Faceoff in San Jose. That is not the headline. The headline is who came back banged up.

Ben Hemmerling left the Colorado game in the first period after an open-ice hit from Gustav Stjernberg and did not return.

Mathieu Cataford fought Stjernberg right after it happened.

The rookies lost a shutout to the Utah Mammoth and then beat the Colorado Avalanche in a shootout. Sixth-round pick Trent Swick, a 6-foot-7 forward, posted two goals and an assist on Sunday.

So there is good news in there. It is just sitting next to two injury questions.

Here is why this matters more than a normal rookie tournament. Training camp opens this week at City National Arena in Summerlin, and Connelly is the prospect most likely to make the NHL roster out of camp.

A player fighting for a job cannot afford to miss reps. Camp is already compressed this year, and every practice a prospect sits out is a practice somebody else uses to pass him.

Hemmerling is in a similar spot. He is a depth forward trying to prove he belongs, and depth forwards get evaluated on availability as much as talent.

My take is that rookie tournaments are a strange risk. You are putting your most valuable young assets in a low-stakes game against other teams' most aggressive young players who are all trying to make an impression with physicality. The hit on Connelly is exactly the outcome the team was hoping to avoid.

Swick's three-point Sunday is the story that would have led if everybody stayed healthy. A 6-foot-7 sixth-round forward producing like that is a real find.

The Golden Knights Just Pulled Their First-Round Pick Out Of Finland

The Vegas Golden Knights announced on Monday, September 14 that defenseman Juho Piiparinen has been recalled from Tappara of Finland's Liiga. He will attend training camp later this week.

Piiparinen is 18 years old. He was the 29th overall pick in the 2026 NHL Draft.

He signed his entry-level contract with Vegas back in July.

He is 6-foot-3 and 203 pounds, which is already NHL size for a teenager.

Last season with Tappara he put up a goal and 12 assists in 15 U20 games, plus three assists in 29 games with the senior club.

That senior club line is the one worth paying attention to. Playing 29 games against grown men in Finland's top league at that age is not normal.

This is the newest first-round pick showing up in Las Vegas for the first time, and City National Arena is where fans get their first real look at him.

Here is the bigger picture. Vegas has built a decade of success partly by trading picks and prospects for players who could win right now. That works until your blue line gets older and you need somebody in the pipeline.

Piiparinen is that somebody. He is the player the organization is counting on to eventually replace aging defensemen, and every organization needs that guy coming up behind the veterans.

He is almost certainly not making the roster out of camp. An 18-year-old defenseman rarely does, and the smart play is more development time in Finland.

But camp is where the coaching staff finds out what they actually have. Two weeks of practice against NHL forwards tells you more than a full season of junior highlights.

My take is that this is a quietly important week for the franchise's next five years, even though almost nobody will be talking about it.

Carter Hart Is The Starter. Adin Hill Is The Backup. Here Is The Math.

The argument Vegas fans have been having all summer is settled. Carter Hart enters the Golden Knights' 10th season as the clear starting goaltender, with Adin Hill as the backup.

Hart started all 22 playoff games last spring. That is not a timeshare. That is a starter.

Here is the part that makes it interesting.

Director of Goaltending Sean Burke has run roughly a 65/35 starter-to-backup split each of the last four seasons.

Burke also swaps goalies on every back-to-back.

And Vegas has only two back-to-backs in its first 20 games. Both on the road.

Run that forward. Fewer back-to-backs means fewer automatic Hill starts, which pushes Hart's workload up.

The projection lands at possibly more than 50 starts for Hart.

Only two goalies in franchise history have hit 50 starts. Hill did it in 2024-25, and Marc-Andre Fleury did it in 2018-19.

So the schedule itself is quietly making this a bigger Hart season than a 65/35 split would suggest.

Here is my concern, and it is not about talent. It is about mileage.

This team went to the Stanley Cup Final and lost to Carolina. Hart played all 22 of those playoff games. Now he is looking at a front-loaded schedule with almost no built-in rest through the first quarter of the season.

Goalies who carry that kind of volume in October and November are the ones you worry about in April.

The counterargument is real too. Hill is a legitimate NHL starter sitting in the backup chair, which is a luxury most teams do not have. If Hart needs games off, the drop-off is small.

That is a good problem. Most teams in this league would take it.

Opening night at T-Mobile Arena is the answer everybody wanted, and now we have it.
