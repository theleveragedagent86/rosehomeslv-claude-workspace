### Story 1: CCSD Trustees Approve 8 New Dual Enrollment Courses That Count for College Credit
- **Category:** School Board and Education
- **County/Area:** Clark County
- **Summary:** At the September 10, 2026 meeting, the CCSD Board of School Trustees approved adding eight new dual enrollment courses to the district course catalog. ### Story 3: CCSD Has Added About 2,200 Free Pre-K Seats Since Last August, Topping 14,000 Districtwide
- **Category:** School Board and Education
- **County/Area:** Clark County, with a featured campus at Don Hayden Elementary School in North Las Vegas
- **Summary:** Since August 2025 the Clark County School District has added roughly 2,200 prekindergarten seats, its largest single-year expansion, pushing districtwide capacity past 14,000 seats. About a week into the school year, 90 percent of pre-K seats were filled for children not in special education. At Don Hayden Elementary in North Las Vegas, pre-K capacity nearly doubled to 88 seats, 40 of them new this year. All of this year's growth came from the state-run, full-day Nevada Ready! program.
- **Source:** Las Vegas Sun
- **Article Title:** Southern Nevada families get relief as CCSD expands free prekindergarten access

- **Date:** September 8, 2026
- **Why It Matters:** A free full-day pre-K seat can replace a daycare bill or let a parent go back to work, so waitlist relief is real money for households.
- **Flag:** none

### Story 4: Clark County Teachers Union Wants Data Centers to Pay a School Tax Worth an Estimated $2 Billion
- **Category:** School Board and Education
- **County/Area:** Clark County and statewide Nevada
- **Summary:** The Clark County Education Association unveiled a plan on September 9 that would end the abatement of the Local School Support Tax for data centers and revisit the 75 percent personal property tax abatement. CCEA estimates the change could send $2 billion into the state's pupil-centered funding formula over four years. Data centers now pay a reduced 2 percent sales and use tax rate, and the state abated $240 million in sales tax for data centers in 2023 and 2024. The Nevada State Education Association and the Sierra Club Toiyabe Chapter both criticized the proposal.

- **Article Title:** ‘This industry is the future:’ Clark County teachers union wants state to bargain with data centers

- **Why It Matters:** Nevada sits below the national average on per-pupil spending, and this is the first concrete plan to tie the data center boom to classroom money.

### Story 8: State Board Hears CCSD Started the Year With 167 Teacher Vacancies, Down From 348
- **Category:** School Board and Education
- **County/Area:** Clark County
- **Summary:** CCSD presented a staffing update to the Nevada State Board of Education on September 9. Day one vacancies stood at 167, down from 348 the prior year, with 90 licensed special education vacancies, down from 163, and a 99.3 percent fill rate for substitutes and temporary coverage. The presentation also detailed the spring "surplus" process driven by falling enrollment: 707 positions were identified for surplus in the 2026 Spring Surplus Cycle, 337 of those employees could not find a new position in the spring transfer process, 103 later selected qualifying positions, 14 resigned and 154 licensed professionals were placed into attrition vacancies.
- **Source:** Opportunity 180
- **Article Title:** Nevada Ed-Watch: State Board of Education 9/09/26

- **Date:** September 10, 2026 (meeting held September 9, 2026)
- **Why It Matters:** Fewer empty classrooms is good news, but the surplus numbers show enrollment losses are still moving hundreds of teachers around the valley.
- **Flag:** none
- **Note:** Dedup check. Prior weeks covered "CCSD Declares Reduction in Force, About 60 Licensed Staff Affected" and "CCSD Reports Near-Full Staffing and Zero Layoffs, but Not Everyone Buys It." ### Story 9: Nevada Logged 933 Testing Irregularities Out of 1.5 Million Test Sessions Last Year
- **Category:** School Board and Education
- **County/Area:** Clark County and statewide Nevada
- **Summary:** The Nevada State Board of Education received the annual test security summary for 2025-26 on September 9. Out of roughly 1,500,000 test sessions statewide, there were 933 reported testing irregularities, about 0.06 percent. Improper test administration accounted for 38 percent of the irregularities, student cheating or misconduct 24 percent, and testing interruptions 20 percent. Recommended fixes include better web-based tracking software, revised training materials for testing staff, more frequent webinars for district staff and on-site monitoring by state staff.
- **Source:** Opportunity 180
- **Article Title:** Nevada Ed-Watch: State Board of Education 9/09/26
- **URL:** https://www.opportunity180.org/nevada-ed-watch-state-board-of-education-9-09-26/
- **Date:** September 10, 2026 (meeting held September 9, 2026)
- **Why It Matters:** Test scores drive school star ratings and school choice decisions, so parents have a stake in whether the tests are administered cleanly.
- **Flag:** none
- **Primary source:** The full report PDF is posted by the Nevada Department of Education at https://webapp-strapi-paas-prod-nde-001.azurewebsites.net/uploads/item_6b_2026_annual_testsecuritysummary_nevadapublicschools_9debd14585.pdf
- **Related same-meeting item:** The board also heard an update on the new Nevada District Performance Framework. District Supports and School Outcomes are being measured this year for a baseline. Beginning in 2026-27, Student Outcomes get measured, and districts that miss expectations may be designated low-performing, with two consecutive years triggering an underperforming label. The board separately set a goal of raising the share of high school students graduating with a College and Career Readiness or advanced diploma from 36 percent to 50 percent by the end of the 2030-31 school year.
