Henderson Silver Knights Re-Sign Goaltender Jordan Papirny for a Fifth Season

The Henderson Silver Knights are bringing back goaltender Jordan Papirny for a fifth season in the organization. General Manager Tim Speltz announced on September 10 that the team signed Papirny to a one-year American Hockey League contract for 2026-27.

It is a small move on paper and a meaningful one in practice. Papirny, 30, has been in this system longer than almost anyone else who will pull on a Silver Knights sweater this winter. For a Henderson team that turns over a big chunk of its roster every summer, that kind of continuity is worth something.

If you live in Henderson and you take your family to games at Lee's Family Forum, this is the type of signing that quietly decides how your season goes. Goalies win minor league hockey games. Depth at the position is the difference between a fun winter and a long one.

What Happened

On September 10, 2026, Henderson Silver Knights General Manager Tim Speltz announced that the club re-signed goaltender Jordan Papirny. The deal is a one-year AHL contract covering the 2026-27 season.

Papirny is 30 years old. This will be his fifth season inside the organization. That is a long run by minor league standards, where a player can change teams two or three times in the span of a single winter.

He spent all of the 2025-26 season with the Tahoe Knight Monsters of the ECHL. In 37 games there he went 16-17-4 with a 3.66 goals-against average and a .901 save percentage. That is a heavy workload, and it came on a team that asked him to play a lot of hockey.

At the AHL level, Papirny has appeared in 14 career games and holds a record of 4-7-1. Every one of those 14 appearances came with Henderson. He has not played AHL games anywhere else.

Put those two lines together and you get the shape of the signing. Papirny is a goaltender the organization knows well, who has played in Henderson before, and who just handled a starter's workload one level down. He gives the Silver Knights a known option in net without forcing the team to gamble on somebody new.

The announcement came from the team itself, through the Henderson Silver Knights website, under the headline "SILVER KNIGHTS RE-SIGN GOALTENDER JORDAN PAPIRNY." It landed in the middle of a busy stretch of September roster news across the Vegas Golden Knights organization, which is often when depth signings get finalized and quietly posted.

A one-year AHL contract is the standard structure for this kind of move. It commits the club for a single season and it commits the player for a single season. Both sides get to see how the year goes before anybody talks about what comes next. In a league where lineups change week to week, that is the normal arrangement rather than the exception.

It is also worth being clear about what the deal is not. This is an American Hockey League contract, not an NHL contract. The distinction matters because it tells you which club holds the paper and where the player is expected to spend his season. Papirny is signed to Henderson, not to Las Vegas.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Henderson is a hockey town now. That happened faster than a lot of people expected. Families who moved here from California, Arizona, or the Midwest brought the sport with them, and the Silver Knights gave those families a place to go on a weeknight without driving to the Strip.

Lee's Family Forum sits in Henderson, and it draws from Green Valley, Anthem, Inspirada, Cadence, and the neighborhoods around Water Street. When the team is competitive, the building feels like a community gathering spot. When the team struggles, attendance and energy both dip. Goaltending is a big part of which version of the season you get.

Here is why goalie depth matters more in the AHL than it does at the NHL level. The Silver Knights are the top affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights. When a goaltender gets hurt in Las Vegas, the Golden Knights often call one up from Henderson. That leaves Henderson short, and Henderson then has to pull a goalie up from the ECHL. The chain reaction runs downhill all season long.

Having a goaltender like Papirny in the mix, one who has already played AHL games for this exact team and just started a heavy ECHL season, gives the organization a piece it can move around without panic. That stability shows up on the ice in Henderson on a Friday night in January.

For local families, that is the practical read. A Silver Knights ticket is one of the more affordable live sports options in the valley, and a competitive team is what keeps it fun. Roster moves in September decide what February looks like.

There is a second layer that matters to residents. Pro sports venues anchor the areas around them. A busy arena means restaurants, bars, and shops nearby get traffic on nights they would otherwise be quiet. Henderson built its arena in a part of town that has been steadily adding housing and retail, and the team is part of what keeps that corner active during the week.

Homeowners in nearby neighborhoods feel that in small ways. More foot traffic, more reasons for a new business to open within a short drive, more nights where the area feels alive. None of it comes from one goalie signing. All of it depends on the franchise staying healthy over the long run, and franchises stay healthy by putting a watchable team on the ice year after year.

Renters and buyers looking at Henderson often ask the same question in different words: what is there to do here. A team that plays a full winter of home dates in Henderson is a real answer, and it is one that does not require a drive down the 215 to the resort corridor.

Background and History

The Silver Knights arrived in Southern Nevada as the AHL affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights, and they have become a real part of Henderson's identity. The franchise gave the city a professional team of its own instead of asking Henderson residents to think of themselves as spectators of a Las Vegas team.

Jordan Papirny has been part of that story for four seasons already, and 2026-27 will make five. In a league built on one-year deals and constant movement, a player sticking with one organization that long is unusual. It usually means the organization values what he brings in the room and on the practice ice, not only what shows up in a box score.

His 2025-26 season in Tahoe is the most recent data point. A 16-17-4 record on a 37-game workload means he was in net for roughly half his team's schedule or more, depending on how the season broke. A 3.66 goals-against average and a .901 save percentage are numbers that come from a goalie facing steady traffic rather than one protected by a dominant defense.

The ECHL sits one level below the AHL. Goaltenders move between the two constantly, often multiple times in one season. A goalie who plays 37 games in the ECHL and then gets an AHL contract for the following year is following a normal and healthy path in professional hockey development. It is not a demotion story. It is a depth story.

The 14 career AHL appearances, all in Henderson, tell you the rest. Papirny has been the goalie the Silver Knights turn to when they need somebody who already knows the building, the coaches, and the systems. That is a role, and it has real value over a long winter schedule.

Think about what four seasons inside one organization actually looks like from the inside. It means knowing the training staff, the travel routines, the way the coaches want the defense to box out in front of the net. It means younger goalies coming through camp have somebody to ask questions. Teams do not put that on a stat sheet, but front offices pay attention to it, and general managers talk about it every time they explain a depth signing.

It also says something about Henderson as a place to play. Players who do not like a city find reasons to move on. A goaltender coming back for a fifth year in the same organization, in a market that did not have pro hockey at all a decade ago, is a quiet endorsement of how the franchise runs.

What Happens Next

The next step is training camp. Papirny will compete for playing time like everybody else, and the way goaltending shakes out across the Golden Knights organization will determine where he spends most of his season. Contracts get signed in September. Depth charts get settled in October.

Watch how the organization stacks its goalies. The Golden Knights carry goaltenders at the NHL level, the Silver Knights carry them in the AHL, and the affiliated ECHL club carries more. Injuries anywhere in that chain move everyone. A signing like this one gives the front office a lever to pull when that happens.

For fans, the practical next step is the schedule. The Silver Knights play at Lee's Family Forum in Henderson, and home dates are the ones worth circling on a family calendar. If you have never been, an AHL game is a good introduction to the sport. The building is smaller than an NHL arena, the sightlines are close, and kids can actually see the puck.

Expect more roster news through the rest of September and into October. Minor league rosters are not finished until they have to be. Every year a handful of names get added late, and a few players who looked like locks end up somewhere else. That is normal. Papirny being locked in now removes one question mark from the list.

The number worth watching once games start is workload. A goaltender who gets a run of starts usually tells you the coaching staff trusts him. A goaltender who sits usually means somebody else won the job or a call-up changed the picture. Neither is a verdict in September. Both become clear by December.

Keep an eye on the wider organization too. Goaltending decisions in Las Vegas ripple into Henderson within days. If the Vegas Golden Knights need a body in net, the Silver Knights are the first place they look, and whoever is left in Henderson gets more work than anybody planned. Depth signings exist for exactly that week.

Ryan's Take

I pay attention to Silver Knights roster news for a reason that has nothing to do with hockey analytics. When I talk with buyers who are moving to Henderson, especially families relocating from colder states, the question of what there is to do here on a Tuesday night in January comes up constantly. People want to know the place has a rhythm outside of the Strip.

A professional hockey team playing in a Henderson arena is part of that answer. So are the youth programs and the rinks that grow up around a pro team. Buyers notice. It is the kind of amenity that does not show up in a comparative market analysis and still affects how somebody feels about a neighborhood when they are deciding where to raise kids.

Henderson has spent the last several years building out exactly this kind of local infrastructure, and it shows in how the southeast valley has held up. Continuity matters in a franchise the same way it matters in a neighborhood. A team that keeps familiar names around builds the kind of loyalty that fills a building, and a building that fills up keeps supporting the restaurants and small businesses around it.

I will be honest that a single goaltender signing does not move home values. Nothing about one one-year contract shows up in a Henderson price per square foot. What it does is add one more small piece to a bigger picture, and the bigger picture is what people are actually buying when they pick a city inside the valley.

The buyers I work with almost never lead with the arena. They lead with schools, commute, and price. But somewhere in the third or fourth conversation, when we are talking about what a normal Saturday looks like, the amenities come up. Being able to say that Henderson has its own pro team, its own arena, and a growing youth hockey scene fills in a picture that a spreadsheet cannot.

What You Can Do

If you want to follow the Silver Knights roster this fall, the team's own website is the primary source. Signings, releases, and call-ups get posted there first, usually before they show up anywhere else. That is where this Papirny announcement came from.

If you have kids who are curious about hockey, an AHL game is a good place to start. Tickets to a Silver Knights game are generally an easier lift than an NHL night at T-Mobile Arena, and the atmosphere at Lee's Family Forum is built around families rather than tourists. Check the team schedule for home dates and pick a weekend game for a first visit.

You can also follow the wider organization. The Vegas Golden Knights hold public practices and rookie sessions at City National Arena in Summerlin at points during the preseason, and those are usually free to watch. Many of the prospects skating there end up playing in Henderson, so it is a way to see the pipeline before it reaches the AHL.

And if a growing hockey scene is part of why you are looking at Henderson, say so when you start a home search. Proximity to the arena, to the rinks, and to the practice facilities is a real factor for some families, and it changes which neighborhoods make sense.

One more practical note for parents. Youth hockey runs on ice time, and ice time runs on how close you live to a rink. A family in Anthem and a family in Summerlin can end up with very different weeknight schedules for the same program. If that is going to be part of your life here, it is worth mapping before you pick a neighborhood rather than after.

If you want to stay current on Henderson news in general, from the arena to new development along the Water Street corridor, the local outlets covering Clark County are the ones to follow. Team announcements, city council agendas, and new construction all shape what living here looks like, and they move faster than most people expect.
