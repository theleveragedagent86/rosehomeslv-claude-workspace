Rookie Camp at City National Arena: What Local Fans Actually Got to See

The Vegas Golden Knights held exactly one rookie camp practice in Las Vegas this year, on Friday, September 11, at City National Arena in Summerlin, and then the whole group flew to San Jose. If you were planning to swing by "sometime during camp week," that was the window. It opened and closed in a single morning.

That is the part worth understanding for anyone who lives here. City National Arena is the one place in the valley where locals can watch these young players skate before the season starts, and it does not cost anything to stand there and watch. The catch is that the calendar moves fast. Rookie camp is not a week-long open house. This year it was one session, and then the team was gone.

We covered the roster announcement and the practice date in Golden Knights Name 23-Player Rookie Camp Roster. This piece is the other half of that story: what the single session actually told us, and how a Las Vegas family should plan around this stretch of the hockey calendar next time.

What Happened at the Single Session

The Golden Knights opened rookie camp with one practice at City National Arena on Friday, September 11. Twenty-three players were on the camp roster. That group stretched all the way from players the team drafted in 2026, who are still teenagers, to prospects who are genuinely close to playing NHL games.

That range is what makes a rookie practice fun to watch in person. On a normal NHL night everyone on the ice is already a finished product. At rookie camp you get a teenager who just got drafted in June skating next to somebody who put up big numbers in the American Hockey League last season. The gap between them is visible. You can see it in how fast they move and how quickly they make decisions with the puck.

The name most locals came to see was Trevor Connelly. Connelly scored 49 points in 46 games for the Henderson Silver Knights last season, which is a strong number for a young player at that level. He told reporters he spent most of the summer in Las Vegas working on strength, after a season that was slowed by injuries. That is a specific and useful detail. It is a player saying, out loud, that he decided the fix was a heavier offseason in the gym rather than a change of scenery.

There was also news for one of the youngest players in the group. Noel Pakarinen, a seventh-round pick, is headed to the Penticton Vees for his first season in North America. KTNV 13 listed Penticton as a WHL club. Either way, the meaning is the same: Pakarinen is not staying in the valley this winter. He skated at City National Arena, then went off to play junior hockey and get used to a smaller rink and a longer bus schedule.

After the practice, the group flew to San Jose for the rookie showcase. That is the normal pattern. Rookie camp in Las Vegas is short on purpose, because the real evaluation happens in the games against other teams' prospects, and those games are somewhere else.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Free things to do with your kids in this valley are not exactly everywhere, especially in September when it is still hot and the pools are getting old. A morning at City National Arena is one of the good ones. It is indoors, it is cool, it is real professional hockey, and the barrier to entry is that you showed up.

It is also different from a game. At a game you are up in a seat, far from the ice, paying for parking and paying for a soda. At a practice you are close. Kids can actually see faces and hear sticks. For a family that is curious about hockey but not ready to spend real money on tickets yet, a rookie practice is the cheapest possible test drive.

There is a Henderson angle too. Many of these prospects are the same players who will be skating for the Henderson Silver Knights this winter. Trevor Connelly already did. So the people you watch in Summerlin in September may be the people you watch in Henderson in January, and following one player through that path is how a casual fan turns into an actual fan.

And the timing is a real constraint for working parents. One Friday morning practice is hard to catch if you are at a job site or in an office. That is not a complaint about the team, it is just the reality of how a compressed camp works. Knowing that in advance is the whole point. If you want to see rookie camp, you have to treat it like a one-day event, not a week of drop-in opportunities.

Background: Why Rookie Camp Is So Short

Rookie camp exists to get young players on the ice together before the main training camp opens. It is a warmup for a warmup. The team gets a look at its draft picks, the coaches get a sense of who is ready, and the players get a few days of structure before the real competition starts.

Because it feeds directly into the rookie games against other clubs, the local portion has to be brief. A team cannot spend a week practicing at home and still make its travel schedule. So the Las Vegas piece gets compressed down to whatever fits, and this year that was a single session on September 11.

City National Arena has been the Golden Knights' practice home since the franchise arrived, and over the years it has become something more than a practice rink for people who live nearby. It is a place where Summerlin families go to watch, and it has helped build a hockey culture in a desert city that did not have one a decade ago. A lot of Las Vegas kids first saw professional hockey players in that building rather than at T-Mobile Arena.

Connelly's own history is part of this background. A player who scores at nearly a point-plus pace in the AHL is knocking on the door. A season interrupted by injuries can stall that. Choosing to stay in town all summer and work on strength is the kind of decision that does not show up in a box score but often shows up the following spring.

What Happens Next

The next thing on the calendar is the main Golden Knights training camp, which opens September 16. That is a bigger operation with the full roster, and it is the next natural chance for locals to see NHL players skate in Summerlin. Schedules and public access for any given camp session can change day to day, so check the team's official schedule before you drive out. For the prospects themselves, the paths split quickly after the showcase. Some head to Henderson to play for the Silver Knights. Some go back to junior hockey, like Noel Pakarinen heading to Penticton. A few will get a long look at the main camp and push to stick around. Trevor Connelly is the one local fans will be tracking hardest, for the simple reason that he has already produced here and has now put in a full summer of work behind it.

If you missed the September 11 session, the honest answer is that you missed rookie camp for this year. The better plan is to put the whole stretch on your calendar for next fall, because the pattern is consistent: draft picks report, the team practices at City National Arena, and then the group travels. When you know that shape in advance, one morning is enough notice.

Ryan's Take

I sell homes all over this valley, and I have watched the way an anchor like City National Arena changes how people talk about a part of town. Summerlin already had plenty going for it. The practice facility added something different, which is a free, repeatable, family-friendly reason to be in the neighborhood on a random weekday morning. Buyers notice things like that, even when they cannot name them.

The Henderson side of this works the same way. When prospects from a Summerlin practice end up playing in Henderson a few months later, it ties two parts of the valley together in a way a map does not show. Families follow the players. That is a small thing on its own and a real thing when it repeats every season for a decade.

My honest advice on rookie camp is to treat it like any other short-window local event. Do not assume it will be there all week, because this year it was not. One practice, one morning, and then a flight to San Jose.

What You Can Do

Start by following the Golden Knights' official schedule rather than word of mouth. Camp dates move, and a single-session rookie camp punishes anyone relying on a rumor from a neighbor. The team posts what is open and when, and that is the only source worth planning around.

If you have kids who are curious about hockey, use the fall calendar in order. Watch a practice when one is open, then look at a Henderson Silver Knights game at Lee's Family Forum, which is generally an easier and cheaper first live game than a Vegas Golden Knights night at T-Mobile Arena. By the time they are ready for a VGK game, they will already know a few names.

And keep an eye on Trevor Connelly this season. He is the clearest local story in this group: a young player who scored 49 points in 46 games for Henderson, got slowed by injuries, and spent his summer in Las Vegas trying to fix it. Whether that work pays off is the sort of thing you can follow all winter without spending a dollar.
