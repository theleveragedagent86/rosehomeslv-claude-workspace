Former Raider Andre James Cuts the Price on His Henderson House to $6.75 Million

Former Las Vegas Raider Andre James has cut the price on his Henderson house, and it is now listed at $6.75 million. The home sits on Teramo Street in the Seven Hills area and measures 8,480 square feet, with five bedrooms and eight bathrooms.

The Las Vegas Review-Journal reported the price change on September 10, 2026. It is a small item in the grand scheme of the Clark County housing market. Most people reading this will never shop in that price range. But it is still worth paying attention to, because price cuts do not stay in one lane. When a home at the very top of the Henderson market comes down in price, it usually means the same thing that a cut on a $480,000 house in Inspirada means. The first number did not find a buyer.

That is the real story here, and it is a useful one for anyone who is thinking about listing a home in Henderson this fall. Below is what we know, what we do not know, and what a price reduction at this level actually tells you about the market you are selling into.

What Happened

Andre James, a former Las Vegas Raider, has his Henderson house on the market. The current asking price is $6.75 million. That figure follows a price cut, meaning the home was listed higher before and the price has since been reduced.

The property is on Teramo Street, which is in the Seven Hills area of Henderson. According to the listing details reported by the Review-Journal, the house measures 8,480 square feet. It has five bedrooms and eight bathrooms. Eight bathrooms against five bedrooms is common at this size, because homes this large usually carry powder rooms for guests, a pool bath, and bathrooms attached to secondary spaces like a casita, a gym, or a game room.

To put 8,480 square feet in perspective, that is roughly four times the size of a typical newer single family home in Clark County. It is a house with real scale to it. Everything about carrying a property like that is bigger too, including the utility bills, the landscaping, the pool service, and the property tax bill.

The Review-Journal article on the price cut is partially paywalled, so some of the listing detail sits behind a subscription. What we can confirm from the reporting is the price, the street, the square footage, the bedroom count, and the bathroom count. We are not going to guess at anything past that. The original list price, the lot size, the year built, and the amount of the reduction are not things we can state without the full listing in front of us. [NOT VERIFIED]

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Here is the honest version. A $6.75 million house in Seven Hills is not going to move the median price in Clark County. It is one house. The reason to care is what the price cut signals about pricing power, and pricing power is something every seller in Henderson shares, no matter the price point.

When a market is hot, sellers set the price and buyers react. Homes go under contract fast, often above asking, and price cuts are rare enough that they stand out. When a market cools, that flips. Buyers set the pace, they take their time, and the seller who priced ambitiously ends up making the first move. A price cut is the market telling a seller something, in the only language it speaks.

The luxury tier usually feels this first and feels it hardest. There are fewer buyers at $6.75 million than at $675,000, so it takes longer to find the one person whose taste, timing, and budget all line up. When buyers at that level start hesitating, homes sit. When homes sit, prices come down. That pattern tends to show up at the top of the market before it works its way down to the middle.

So if you own a home in Henderson and you are planning to list this fall, the useful takeaway is not the celebrity name. It is this. Even a property with a great address, a big footprint, and a recognizable owner has to be priced to what buyers will actually pay today. Nobody gets a pass on that. Not a Raider, not a builder, not a neighbor down the street who swears their house is special.

The flip side is good news if you are buying. A market where price cuts are common is a market where you can negotiate. You can ask for repairs. You can ask for a rate buydown. You can walk away from a house that does not work and expect to find another one. Buyers have not had that kind of room in Clark County for much of the past several years.

Background and History

Seven Hills is a master planned community in the southern part of Henderson, up against the hills that give it its name. It has been one of the area's established high end addresses for a long time, with larger lots, custom and semi custom homes, golf frontage on some streets, and elevation that produces valley views. Several streets inside it sit behind gates.

Henderson in general has become the home of choice for a lot of Las Vegas professional athletes, and that is not an accident. The city is close enough to the Strip and to Allegiant Stadium for a manageable commute, it has strong schools, and the top end of the housing stock is genuinely large by national standards for the money. Anthem, MacDonald Highlands, Seven Hills, Ascaya, and The Ridges over in Summerlin have all drawn that buyer.

The Raiders arriving in Las Vegas in 2020 added a steady stream of high income households looking for exactly this kind of house. Rosters turn over constantly in professional football, so those homes also come back on the market on a regular cycle. A player signs elsewhere, retires, or simply moves, and a big Henderson house lists. That churn is a normal part of the local luxury market now.

What Happens Next

The house is on the market at $6.75 million. From here, one of three things happens, and all three are worth watching.

It sells at or near the new number. That would say the reduction did its job and found the buyer pool. It is the cleanest outcome, and the sale price, once it is recorded, becomes a real data point for every other high end Henderson seller and their agent.

It sits and gets cut again. A second reduction would be a stronger signal that the top of the Henderson market has more softening left in it. Multiple cuts on one property usually mean the gap between the asking price and the market price was wider than the first cut acknowledged.

Or it comes off the market entirely. Sellers at this level often have the option to simply wait. If carrying the home is not a financial strain, pulling the listing and relisting in a different season is a real strategy. A withdrawn listing is not a failure, but it is also not a sale, and it leaves the pricing question unanswered.

For the rest of us, the thing to watch is not this one house. It is whether price cuts keep showing up across Henderson listings in the coming weeks, at every price band. One cut is an anecdote. A pattern of cuts is a market condition. We track that in our weekly local news roundups, and a broader look at seller reductions across the valley is in Las Vegas Seller Price Cuts.

Ryan's Take

I do not get excited about celebrity listings. I get interested in what they reveal, and this one reveals something simple that I end up saying out loud in almost every listing appointment. The market does not care what you paid, what you put into it, or whose name is on the deed. It cares what a ready buyer will pay this month.

A price cut is not an embarrassment. It is a correction, and the sellers who make it early do far better than the sellers who make it late. The homes that sit on the market for months while the price drifts down in small steps almost always end up selling for less than the homes that were priced right on day one. Buyers watch days on market. A long history of reductions tells them to come in low, because they can see the seller moving toward them.

If you are a Henderson homeowner reading this and wondering whether your own number is right, the test is not what your neighbor listed at. It is what actually closed nearby in the last sixty to ninety days, adjusted honestly for condition, size, and location. That is a conversation I am happy to have with you, and it costs you nothing.

What You Can Do

If you are thinking about selling in Henderson, start by looking at closed sales rather than active listings. Active listings tell you what sellers hope for. Closed sales tell you what buyers did. Those two numbers can sit a long way apart in a shifting market, and the gap is exactly where price cuts come from.

Next, be realistic about time. A larger, more expensive, or more unusual home has a smaller buyer pool by definition, and a smaller pool means a longer search. Build that into your plan before you list, not after. If you need to be out by a certain date, price for that reality up front instead of chasing the market down later.

If you are buying in Henderson right now, use the moment. Ask about how long a home has been listed and whether the price has already moved. Ask for a seller credit toward closing costs or a rate buydown. Get a full inspection and actually negotiate on what it finds. In a market where sellers are reducing prices, those requests land very differently than they did a few years ago.

And if you just want to keep an eye on your own neighborhood, watch the listings on your street. You do not need a subscription or a data service. Noticing which homes sell quickly and which ones sit through two price changes will teach you most of what you need to know about where your local market actually stands.
