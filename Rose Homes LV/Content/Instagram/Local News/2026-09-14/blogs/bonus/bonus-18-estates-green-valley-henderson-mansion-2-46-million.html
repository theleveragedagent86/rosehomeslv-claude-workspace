A Guard-Gated Green Valley Mansion Hits the Market at $2.46 Million

A mansion inside the guard-gated Estates at Green Valley in Henderson just came on the market for $2.46 million. The home sits at 2276 Candlestick Ave., and the listing leans hard on one feature above all the others: views of the Las Vegas Strip.

That price tag is the reason this listing is worth a few minutes of your time. Green Valley is not a brand new luxury enclave carved out of raw desert. It is a mature, settled part of Henderson that most locals still picture as move-up territory, the place you go when you outgrow your starter house. Seeing a $2.46 million asking price inside that neighborhood says something about how far the top end of Henderson has climbed.

The Las Vegas Review-Journal reported the listing on September 9, 2026. Here is what it tells us, and what it does not.

What Happened

The property at 2276 Candlestick Ave. in Henderson was listed for sale at $2.46 million. It sits inside the Estates at Green Valley, a guard-gated community. The listing is marketed on its Las Vegas Strip views.

That is the core of the news, and it is worth being clear about the parts we are not going to guess at. We are not going to tell you the square footage, the bedroom count, the lot size, or the year it was built, because those details are not in the reporting we are working from. If you want them, the listing itself is the place to get them, and you can always ask an agent to pull the full record for you. What we can talk about honestly is the price, the place, and what both of those mean.

One small note on the source, because accuracy matters here. The Review-Journal web address for the story reads 2-6m, but the published headline and the body of the article both say $2.46 million. The headline figure is the correct one. Little mismatches like that are common in web publishing, and they are exactly the kind of thing that turns into a bad number when it gets copied around. If you see $2.6 million attached to this house somewhere, it came from the URL, not from the reporting.

We are also not naming the listing agent or the brokerage handling the sale. That is our standing practice on this blog. We report the property and the market, not the competition.

What makes the listing interesting is the address, not just the number. The Estates at Green Valley is behind a guard gate. That means a staffed entry, not just a keypad and a rolling gate. Guard-gated communities in Clark County are a small subset of the market, and they carry their own set of rules, dues, and expectations. A home inside one is priced for the gate as much as for the walls.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Most people in Henderson are never going to write a $2.46 million check for a house. So why should you care what one listing asks?

Because the top of a neighborhood pulls on everything under it. When a home inside an established community lists at a number that would have sounded absurd there fifteen years ago, it resets what sellers three streets over think their house is worth. It changes what appraisers see when they look for comparable sales. It shifts how builders price the next project nearby. High-end listings are the loudest signal a neighborhood sends, and everybody within a mile hears it, whether they are shopping or not.

It also matters because of what Green Valley is. This is not the far edge of town. Green Valley has grocery stores, parks, schools, restaurants, and traffic patterns that have been settled for decades. Families live there because it works, not because it is new. When a mature, functional, everyday neighborhood produces a multimillion dollar listing, that is a different story than a brand new luxury tract selling a brand new luxury price. It tells you the value is in the location and the established setting, not in the shine of new construction.

For homeowners already in Green Valley, the practical takeaway is simple. Your neighborhood has a wider price range than you may realize. The gap between the ordinary house on a normal street and the guard-gated property with a Strip view is enormous, and both of them share a zip code. That range is worth understanding before you assume you know what your own house is worth. Neighborhood averages hide a lot.

For buyers, this listing is a reminder that Henderson's high end is real and is not confined to the newest hillside communities. If you have been shopping only in the newest areas because you assumed that is where the upper-tier inventory lives, you are skipping over established neighborhoods that quietly carry their own luxury pockets. Guard-gated sections tucked inside older master plans are easy to miss from the outside. That is sort of the point of them.

And for renters and younger buyers watching all of this from the sidelines, there is an honest observation worth making. Price growth at the top of a market does not automatically drag the bottom up with it, and it does not automatically price everyone out either. These are somewhat separate markets that happen to share a map. A $2.46 million listing and a $450,000 listing in the same city are competing for completely different buyers.

Background and History

Green Valley is one of the reasons Henderson became Henderson. It was one of the first large master-planned efforts in the area, and it turned a stretch of desert into an actual community with a street grid, parks, schools, and commercial corners that people use every day. Decades later, the trees are grown in, the schools have graduated generations, and the neighborhood has the kind of settled feel that new construction cannot manufacture at any price.

That maturity is exactly what creates the pattern we are looking at. In a young master plan, the price ceiling is set by whatever the builder is selling this quarter. In a mature one, the ceiling is set by whatever a buyer is willing to pay for a specific, irreplaceable spot. There is no lot left to build another one. A guard-gated section with a line of sight to the Strip is a finite thing, and finite things get priced accordingly.

The Strip view piece deserves its own paragraph, because in this valley it functions almost like waterfront does in other markets. You cannot add it. You cannot renovate your way into it. Elevation and sight lines are either there or they are not, and the homes that have them tend to hold a premium through both good markets and slow ones. That is why a listing description leads with the view instead of the finishes. The finishes can be replaced by the next owner. The view cannot be replicated by the neighbor.

Henderson's upper tier has broadened over the past several years. What used to be a short list of addresses is now a longer one, spread across several communities and several price bands. Green Valley showing up in that conversation is not new exactly, but a $2.46 million ask inside it is still a marker worth writing down. It is the kind of number that becomes a reference point in later conversations, including the ones sellers have with their agents.

What Happens Next

The listing is now on the open market, which means the next chapter writes itself over the coming weeks. Homes in this price range in Clark County generally take longer to sell than homes in the middle of the market. The buyer pool is smaller. The showings are fewer. The negotiation is usually slower and more detailed. None of that is a problem, it is just how the upper tier works.

Watch for three things. First, whether the price holds. A price reduction on a high-end listing is not a scandal, it is information. It tells you the market answered back. Second, how long it sits. Days on market is the cleanest, least spinnable number in real estate, and at the top of the market it is the one that tells the truest story. Third, what it actually closes for. The asking price is an opinion. The sale price is a fact. Those two numbers can end up quite far apart on a property like this, in either direction.

If it sells near ask, that validates the number for every other guard-gated, view-oriented property in Henderson, and you will see similar asks follow. If it sits and gets cut, that is equally useful information, and it will cool some ambitious pricing conversations elsewhere in the city. Either outcome teaches us something, which is why single listings at the top end are worth tracking even when they have nothing to do with your own house.

We will keep an eye on it. When there is a closing price or a meaningful change, we will report it the same way we reported the listing, with the number and the source attached.

Ryan's Take

What stands out to me here is the address more than the price. I have been in enough Green Valley houses to know that most people still think of it as the reliable middle of Henderson, the neighborhood where you land when you need more space and better schools and you do not want a forty minute commute. That reputation is earned and it is accurate for most of the community. It just is not the whole community anymore.

Established neighborhoods develop layers over time. The original product gets absorbed, the custom and semi-custom pockets fill in, the gated sections mature, and twenty years later you have a place where a modest house and a multimillion dollar house sit inside the same master plan. That is not a distortion. That is what a healthy, aging neighborhood looks like. Summerlin does it, Green Valley does it, and the older parts of the northwest are starting to do it too.

My practical advice to homeowners is to stop pricing your house off neighborhood gossip. If your neighbor's cousin sold for a big number, find out whether that house was in a gated section with a view or on an interior lot like yours. Those are different products. The only pricing that works is pricing against genuinely comparable homes, and in a layered neighborhood like this one, the comparable set can be a lot narrower than you expect.

What You Can Do

If you are curious about this specific listing, it is publicly on the market, and you can look it up by address at 2276 Candlestick Ave. in Henderson. The Review-Journal story includes photos if you want the visual tour without scheduling anything.

If you own a home in Green Valley and this story made you wonder about your own value, do the honest version of that homework. Pull the actual sales on your street and in your specific section over the last six months. Compare like to like: same style, similar lot, similar condition, gated versus not gated, view versus no view. If you want help assembling that, that is exactly the kind of thing we do, and there is no obligation attached to asking.

If you are shopping in the upper tier, widen your search beyond the newest communities. Guard-gated sections inside established master plans in Henderson are easy to overlook on a map-based home search, and they often deliver mature landscaping, settled streets, and shorter drives than the newer hillside options. Ask your agent to filter for gated subdivisions specifically rather than searching by community name, because the gated pockets frequently do not show up the way you would expect.

And whatever your price range, be skeptical of any number you see repeated without a source. The small URL mismatch in this very story is a good example of how a wrong figure gets loose. Always trace a number back to the reporting or the record.
