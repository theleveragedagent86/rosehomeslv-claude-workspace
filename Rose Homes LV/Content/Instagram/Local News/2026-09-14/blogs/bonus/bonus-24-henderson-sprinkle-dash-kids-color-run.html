Henderson Sprinkle Dash Kids Color Run Returns September 26 at O'Callaghan Park

The City of Henderson holds its third annual Sprinkle Dash Kids Color Run on Saturday, September 26, 2026, from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. at O'Callaghan Park, 601 Skyline Road. It is built for kids ages 2 to 12, and it is not free. Pre-registration is $15 through Friday, September 25. If you wait and sign up the morning of the event, it costs $20.

That is the whole decision in one paragraph. Fifteen dollars, four hours on a Saturday morning, one park in Henderson, and a kid who comes home covered in color and ready for a nap. If you have a toddler or an elementary schooler in the house, this is one of the easier yes answers on the September calendar.

Here is what the morning looks like. Kids run a course with four color stations along the way. At each station they get dusted with colored powder. By the end of the run they are wearing most of the rainbow. Then there is a finish-line celebration with a dance party, bounce houses and treats.

What Is Happening and When

Let's lay out the details in plain order so nothing gets lost.

What: The third annual Sprinkle Dash Kids Color Run, put on by the City of Henderson.

When: Saturday, September 26, 2026. The event window runs from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m.

Where: O'Callaghan Park, 601 Skyline Road, Henderson, Nevada.

Who: Children ages 2 to 12.

Cost: $15 per child if you pre-register. Pre-registration is open through Friday, September 25. Same-day registration is $20.

The word "third annual" is doing some quiet work in that description. This is not a first-year experiment that might get canceled or moved. Henderson has run it twice before and brought it back again. Events that survive to year three usually have their logistics figured out, and parents who went the first two years tend to spread the word.

The four color stations are the core of the thing. A color run is not a race in any serious sense. Nobody is timing a four year old. Kids move through the course, they hit a station, they get covered in colored powder, and they keep going. The powder is the point. The running is just how you get from one cloud of color to the next.

The finish line is where the event turns into a party. A dance party, bounce houses and treats are all part of the celebration once kids come through. For a lot of families that back half is the real draw. The run takes a few minutes. The bounce houses take as long as your kid has legs.

One practical note on the price. The five dollar gap between pre-registration and same-day registration is small per child, but it adds up if you have three kids. Three kids pre-registered is $45. Three kids at the gate on Saturday morning is $60. The deadline to lock in the lower price is Friday, September 25, which is the day before the event. Put a reminder on your phone now.

Why It Matters to Henderson Families

Family events in Clark County fall into two buckets. There is the free bucket, which is great but usually crowded and sometimes thin on actual activities. Then there is the paid bucket, which often means a theme park price, a long drive, or a membership. A fifteen dollar city-run event with bounce houses and a dance party sits in a gap that does not get filled very often.

Think about what fifteen dollars buys a family on a Saturday in this valley. It is roughly a couple of kids meals. It is less than a movie ticket for one person at most local theaters. For that same money, your child gets a morning outside, a course to run, four color stations, a finish-line party and a story they will tell at school on Monday. That is a good trade.

There is also the September weather factor, which every Las Vegas and Henderson parent understands in their bones. Late September is when outdoor mornings become possible again. From June through most of September, a 9 a.m. outdoor event with running children is a heat question first and a fun question second. By the last Saturday in September, mornings in the valley are usually manageable. This event lands right at the start of the season when families can be outside again without planning around a heat advisory.

That timing is not an accident for city recreation calendars across Clark County. Fall is when park programming wakes back up. If you have been cooped up indoors all summer trading screen time for sanity, this is the weekend the calendar starts giving you options again.

The age range matters too. Two to twelve is unusually wide. Most kids events pick a lane, either toddler-friendly or big-kid-friendly, and families with a five year old and a ten year old end up splitting up or skipping it. A course with color stations works for both. The two year old walks it with a parent. The ten year old sprints it and then goes back for another lap through the bounce houses. One event, one registration window, one trip in the car.

For families newer to Henderson, events like this are also how you meet your neighbors. You will see kids from your street, teachers from the local elementary school, and other parents who are also trying to fill a Saturday. Community does not happen at a house. It happens at the park.

Background on O'Callaghan Park and Henderson's Event Calendar

O'Callaghan Park sits at 601 Skyline Road in Henderson. That address puts it in the older, established side of the city, the part that grew up long before Inspirada and Cadence existed. This is the Henderson that people mean when they talk about the city having actual roots rather than just master-planned subdivisions.

The neighborhoods around Skyline Road are mature. Trees are grown in. Lots tend to be a little larger than what new construction gives you. Schools have been in place for decades. It is a part of town where the parks were built when the neighborhood was built, not added later as an amenity to sell homes.

That history is part of why a city event like this works there. The park is not a new pad of grass. It is a place families already use. Putting an annual event in a park that neighbors already treat as theirs is how you get year three instead of year one and done.

Henderson as a city has leaned into family programming for a long time. Parks and recreation events, seasonal festivals and low-cost youth activities are a regular feature of the city's calendar. That reputation is one of the most common reasons families give me for choosing Henderson over other parts of the valley. It is not just the schools and the lower crime numbers. It is the sense that the city actually plans things for kids to do.

The Sprinkle Dash is a small example of that pattern. It is not a giant festival. It is a four hour Saturday morning event for elementary-age kids at a neighborhood park, priced so that a family can say yes without a budget conversation. Do that a few dozen times a year across a city, and you build the thing people are actually paying for when they buy a house in Henderson.

What Happens Next and How to Prepare

The next date that matters is Friday, September 25. That is the last day for $15 pre-registration. After that the price is $20 and you are registering in person on Saturday morning with a kid who is already asking when the running starts.

The event itself is Saturday, September 26, from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. The specific start time for the run within that four hour window, the check-in process, the exact registration link, parking guidance, and whether adults need to register to walk the course with a young child are all details the City of Henderson controls, and they are not confirmed here. A few practical things parents usually want to know about any color run. Dress your kid in clothes you do not care about, because colored powder and white shirts have a permanent relationship. Old sneakers, not the new school shoes. Bring a towel or an old sheet for the car seat on the ride home. Sunglasses or a hat help keep powder out of eyes. A change of clothes in a bag is never a bad call. Water, always, even in late September.

If your child has asthma or reactive airways, ask the city ahead of time what the color powder is made of and how it is applied. That is a fair question and organizers of these events are used to answering it. Do not guess. [NOT VERIFIED]

One more scheduling note. Four hours is the event window, not the length of the run. If your two year old is done after twenty minutes of bounce house, you can leave. Nobody is checking. Plan the rest of your Saturday like a normal Saturday.

Ryan's Take

I sell houses in Henderson, and I will tell you what actually closes deals with families. It is almost never the countertops. It is the drive from the house to the park, the school, and the Saturday morning stuff. When a buyer with two young kids walks a neighborhood and sees a city event flyer on a light pole, that does more work than a staged living room.

Events like the Sprinkle Dash are part of the case for the older side of Henderson. The Skyline Road area does not get the marketing budget that the newer master-planned communities get. There is no sales office, no model home park, no glossy brochure. What it has is grown trees, established schools, a park that families have used for years, and a city that keeps putting things on the calendar there.

If you are shopping in Henderson right now and you keep getting pushed toward the newest inventory, go spend a Saturday in one of the older neighborhoods first. Walk O'Callaghan Park. Look at the lot sizes. Notice how many people are out. Then decide what you are actually buying. Some families want new construction and they should get new construction. Other families want a street where the neighbors have known each other for fifteen years. Both exist in Henderson, and they are priced differently.

The honest version is this. A fifteen dollar kids run is not a housing market indicator. But the fact that a city keeps funding small family events, year after year, in an older park, tells you something real about how that city treats its established neighborhoods. That is worth paying attention to when you are choosing where to put down roots.

What You Can Do

First, handle the registration. Get it done before Friday, September 25, and pay $15 per child instead of $20. If you are on the fence, the five dollar difference is small enough that pre-registering and skipping is still cheaper than deciding at the gate. Look up the City of Henderson parks and recreation registration page for the Sprinkle Dash Kids Color Run, or call the city to register by phone. [NOT VERIFIED on the exact registration link and phone line.]

Second, plan the logistics the night before. Lay out the throwaway clothes. Put a towel and a trash bag in the car. Charge your phone, because you will take more pictures than you expect. Decide whether you are doing breakfast before or treats at the finish line, and tell the kids the plan so there is no negotiation at 8:30 a.m.

Third, bring someone. Text a neighbor, a cousin, or another family from your kid's class. Color runs are better with a group, and splitting the driving or the parking search makes the morning easier. This is also the low-effort way to build the kind of neighborhood friendships that make a house feel like a home.

Fourth, keep an eye on the rest of Henderson's fall calendar. The city runs family programming through the season, and the Sprinkle Dash is one item on a longer list. If this one works for your family, the next one probably will too.
