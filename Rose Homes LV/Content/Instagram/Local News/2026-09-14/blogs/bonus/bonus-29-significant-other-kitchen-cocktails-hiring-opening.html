New Airport-View Restaurant Significant Other Kitchen and Cocktails Is Hiring Now for a Mid-October Opening

A new restaurant called Significant Other Kitchen and Cocktails is set to open in mid-October on Sunset Road, directly across from Harry Reid International Airport, and it is hiring its opening team right now. That means two things for people who live here. There is a new place to eat and drink coming to southeast Las Vegas, and there is a fresh stack of hospitality jobs open to local applicants this month.

The space is planned with a main dining room, a bar, a private event room, and a pet-friendly outdoor patio that looks out at active airport runways. That patio is the detail people are going to talk about. Watching planes take off and land while you sit outside with your dog is not something you can do at many places in the valley.

FOX5 Vegas reported the hiring push and the mid-October target on September 14, 2026. Below is what is known, what it means for the neighborhoods nearby, and how to approach it if you are thinking about applying.

What Happened

Significant Other Kitchen and Cocktails is a new restaurant coming to Sunset Road in southeast Las Vegas. The site sits directly across from Harry Reid International Airport. The target for opening is mid-October. The exact street address and the exact opening date have not been published. [NOT VERIFIED]

Ahead of that opening, the restaurant is recruiting its leadership team. The roles posted so far are Assistant General Manager, Floor Manager, Banquet Manager, Beverage Manager, Executive Sous Chef, Sous Chef, and Kitchen Manager. That is seven management and senior kitchen positions in a single round of hiring.

More positions are coming. The restaurant says additional front-of-house and back-of-house jobs will roll out over the next few weeks. Front of house generally covers servers, hosts, bartenders, bussers, and food runners. Back of house covers line cooks, prep cooks, dishwashers, and expediters. If you do not see your role listed yet, the listings are not finished.

The hiring order here is normal for a new restaurant, and it tells you something about the timeline. Kitchens hire leadership first because those people write the prep lists, order the equipment, set the stations, and train everyone who comes after them. A Banquet Manager on the early list is also a signal. It means the private event side of the business is meant to run from day one, not get added later once the dining room settles down.

As for the building itself, the plan calls for a main dining room, a bar, a private event space, and an outdoor patio. The patio is pet friendly and faces the runways. No menu has been released publicly, and no pricing has been announced. Anything you read describing the food at this point is a guess, so treat it that way.

It is also worth being clear about what has not been announced, because that is where rumors usually start. There is no published wage range for any of the posted roles. There is no stated number of total hires. There is no confirmed hours-of-operation schedule, so we do not know yet whether the restaurant will serve lunch, dinner only, weekend brunch, or late night. Every one of those answers has to come from the restaurant.

What the posted roles do tell you is that this is a full-service operation rather than a counter-service concept. A Floor Manager means table service with a server team. A Beverage Manager means a real bar program with someone accountable for it. An Executive Sous Chef sitting above a Sous Chef and a Kitchen Manager means a kitchen with layers, which usually means scratch cooking and a menu with some range to it. None of that confirms a single dish, but it does describe the shape of the place.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with the jobs, because that is the part with a deadline on it. A restaurant opening in mid-October is hiring leadership in September and hourly staff not long after. If you work in hospitality in this valley, or you used to and you want back in, this is a real window and it is open now.

New openings are one of the few moments when a restaurant hires a large group at once instead of filling a single hole in a schedule. Everybody starts together. Nobody walks into a room where the culture is already locked and the good sections are already claimed. For a lot of people, being part of an opening crew is the easiest way to move up a level, because the whole staff chart is being drawn from scratch.

Then there is the location. Sunset Road across from the airport puts this restaurant within an easy drive of a lot of homes. Think southeast Las Vegas, the neighborhoods around Sunset and Eastern, the Green Valley side of Henderson, Paradise, and the areas near the Sunset corridor going toward Boulder Highway. Plenty of people in those neighborhoods also work at or near the airport, which makes this a candidate for a shift-change spot, not just a dinner-out spot.

The pet-friendly patio matters more than it sounds like it should. Las Vegas has a lot of dog owners and a fairly short list of places where a dog can sit comfortably outside with you. Add runway views on top of that and you have a reason for people to drive across the valley on a nice night in October, November, or March. That kind of draw is what turns a restaurant into a regular stop instead of a one-time visit.

Private event space is worth noting too. Southeast Las Vegas does not have an endless supply of rooms you can book for a retirement party, a work dinner, a quinceañera planning meeting, or a memorial lunch. When a new venue adds a private room, families in the surrounding neighborhoods get one more option that does not require booking somewhere on the Strip and paying Strip prices.

There is a commuting angle here as well. A restaurant this close to the airport is convenient for someone picking a family member up off a delayed flight, and it is convenient for the airport workforce leaving a shift. If the hours end up running late, it becomes one of the few sit-down options in that pocket of the valley for people who clock out at ten or eleven. If the hours skew earlier, it becomes a dinner spot for the surrounding neighborhoods instead. Either way, residents get a choice they did not have before.

For renters and homeowners weighing where to land next, amenities like this are part of the math. People rarely choose a neighborhood because of one restaurant. They do choose neighborhoods where there is somewhere decent to go without getting on the 215 or fighting Strip traffic. Adding a full-service dining room, a bar, and a patio to the Sunset corridor makes that corner of the valley a little more self-sufficient than it was last year.

Background and History

The Sunset Road corridor near the airport has been quietly building for years. It is a working part of the valley. Warehouses, offices, hotels near the airport, car rental operations, and a steady stream of people going to and from Harry Reid International Airport all share the same roads. Restaurants in that area serve a mix of airport workers, nearby office staff, and residents from the neighborhoods just north and east.

Harry Reid International Airport is one of the busiest airports in the country by passenger count, and the traffic it generates has shaped everything built near it. That is also why a runway-view patio is an actual amenity here rather than a gimmick. The planes are constant. On a clear evening, you get a show that does not stop.

Restaurant openings and closings have been a regular part of the local news cycle in Clark County this year. We have covered both sides of it. Some rooms open with a line out the door and settle into a neighborhood staple. Others do not make it past the first slow summer. The valley's restaurant scene rewards places that build a base of locals who come back on a Tuesday, not just places that get a busy opening weekend.

It is also easy to forget how much of Clark County's dining scene lives off the Strip. Most of the meals people here eat out are in strip centers, standalone buildings, and neighborhood rooms like this one, not in resort corridors. Those places survive on repeat customers who live within about fifteen minutes, which is exactly the crowd a Sunset Road address is aiming at.

Hospitality is still the backbone of employment in southern Nevada. When a restaurant posts seven leadership roles at once and says more are coming, that is not a small event for the people who work in this industry. A single opening can put dozens of people back on a schedule, and those paychecks land in the neighborhoods around the restaurant.

What Happens Next

The next few weeks are the hiring window. Leadership roles are posted now. Front-of-house and back-of-house postings are expected to follow. If you want a server, bartender, host, line cook, or dish position, watch for those listings to appear and apply early, because opening crews fill fast and the first strong applicants usually get first pick of shifts.

After hiring comes the part guests never see. Training, menu tastings, equipment testing, health permits, and usually a few friends-and-family service runs before the doors open to the public. That stretch is why a mid-October target can slide. Construction inspections and permit timing move restaurant opening dates all the time, in every city. Plan for mid-October and stay flexible.

Watch for an official announcement of the exact opening date and the address. Those will come from the restaurant directly and from local news outlets covering the opening. When a menu is released, that will tell you what kind of place this is meant to be and what a typical check might look like. Until then, nobody outside the company knows.

There is a second wave of hiring to watch for as well. Restaurants almost always hire more people than they need for opening week, because the first month sorts out who stays. If you miss the first round of postings, keep checking through late October and into November. Openings routinely add staff once real service volume shows them where the schedule is thin.

One more thing to watch: whether the patio has any restrictions on the pet-friendly policy. Most pet-friendly patios in the valley have rules about leashes, which sections dogs can use, and whether dogs can pass through the interior of the building to reach the patio. Those details usually get posted right around opening.

Ryan's Take

I pay attention to restaurant openings because of what they say about a part of town. A company does not sign a lease, build out a dining room, a bar, a private event space, and a patio unless somebody ran the numbers on who lives nearby and what they spend. Choosing Sunset Road across from the airport is a bet on southeast Las Vegas having enough people with money to spend on a night out within a short drive.

That matters for homeowners in the surrounding neighborhoods. Buyers ask me what is nearby. They ask where they would go on a Friday. A dining and drinks option with a patio and a private room is exactly the kind of answer that makes a neighborhood feel finished rather than in progress. One restaurant does not move a home value by itself. A pattern of new openings in the same corridor, over a few years, absolutely shows up in how people talk about an area.

If you live near Sunset and are watching your home value, the thing to track is not this one opening. It is whether other operators follow. Restaurants tend to cluster, because the second one benefits from the traffic the first one created. When that starts happening in a corridor, the area usually gets more attention from buyers too.

What You Can Do

If you want one of these jobs, go straight to the source. Look for the restaurant's own hiring listings rather than a third-party repost, because the official posting is where the current openings and application instructions live. Apply for the role you actually want, and if your role has not posted yet, check back weekly over the next few weeks as the front-of-house and back-of-house listings roll out.

A practical tip for opening-crew hiring. Managers reading a stack of applications in a three-week window are looking for people who can start immediately, who have open availability, and who have worked an opening before. If any of that is true for you, say so in the first line of your application. Bring a short, current resume with real dates and real references, and be ready for a fast interview process, because opening timelines do not leave room for slow hiring.

It also helps to know what an opening crew is walking into. The first few weeks are busy and a little chaotic in every new restaurant, no matter how well it is run. Systems get rewritten on the fly. Menus get adjusted. Shifts change. If you thrive on that, say it out loud in the interview, because managers building an opening team are specifically looking for people who stay calm when the plan changes twice in one night.

If you are not job hunting and you just want to eat there, mark mid-October on your calendar and watch for the official opening announcement. If you have a dog and you want to try the patio, wait until the pet policy is posted so you know the rules before you show up. And if you are planning a party for the holidays, the private event space is worth a phone call once the restaurant is open and taking bookings.
