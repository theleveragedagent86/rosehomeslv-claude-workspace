Nevada DMV Sets Closing Dates for the Henderson and North Las Vegas Offices

The Nevada DMV is closing two Southern Nevada offices next month. The Commercial Driver License office on Donovan Way in North Las Vegas closes at the end of business on Friday, October 9, 2026. The Henderson office on American Pacific Drive closes on Wednesday, October 14, 2026.

Both operations move into the newly built Silverado facility this fall. The DMV announced the dates on Tuesday, September 8, 2026.

If you live in Henderson or North Las Vegas, this is a real change to your errand list. The office you have been driving to is about to shut its doors. The replacement is a bigger building with more counters, but for a lot of families it also means a longer drive. Mark the dates now so you are not standing in a parking lot in front of a locked door in late October.

What Happened

On Tuesday, September 8, 2026, the Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles announced closing dates for two of its Southern Nevada locations. The two offices are not being replaced in place. They are being folded into one new building.

The first to go is the Commercial Driver License office on Donovan Way in North Las Vegas. That office closes at the end of business on Friday, October 9, 2026. This is the office that handles commercial driver license work, which matters to anyone in Clark County who drives a truck, a bus, or heavy equipment for a living.

The second is the Henderson office on American Pacific Drive. That one closes on Wednesday, October 14, 2026. It has been the go-to spot for a large slice of the southeast valley, including drivers coming from Green Valley, Anthem, Inspirada, and the neighborhoods off Lake Mead Parkway.

Both offices consolidate into the newly built Silverado facility this fall. DMV Director Tonya Laney said both of the closing buildings are aging and no longer meet the needs of a growing customer population. That is the core reason the agency gave. The buildings are old, the valley is bigger than it used to be, and the old footprints do not stretch far enough anymore.

The new Silverado facility is built to a different scale. It adds more service counters than the offices it replaces. It has upgraded ADA access, which matters for older drivers and for anyone using a wheelchair or a walker. It has more than 350 parking spaces. And it has six self-service kiosks, which is where a lot of routine business, like a registration renewal, is meant to get handled without waiting for a counter.

Those numbers tell you what the DMV is trying to fix. Parking has been a well-known headache at older offices around the valley. So has capacity on a busy Monday. More counters and more than 350 stalls is the agency's answer to both.

What the announcement does not do is change the deadlines that apply to you. Your registration expiration does not move because an office closed. Your commercial license renewal does not pause. The building changes. The rules do not.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

For most people, the DMV is not something you think about until you need it. Then it becomes the most important building in the county for about two hours. That is why a closure like this lands harder than it sounds on paper.

Start with Henderson. The American Pacific Drive office has been the closest DMV for a huge number of southeast valley households. When it closes on October 14, those trips shift to Silverado. For some families that is a modest change. For others, especially people coming from the far side of Henderson or from Boulder City, it is a longer drive and a bigger chunk of a workday.

Now think about who actually has to make that trip. New residents have a limited window to register a vehicle in Nevada after they move here, so a newcomer does not get to wait for a convenient month. Teenagers getting a first license have to show up in person. Anyone who needs a REAL ID has to bring documents to a counter. None of that can be finished from a couch.

The North Las Vegas closure hits a smaller group, but it hits them harder. Commercial driver license work is job work. A truck driver who cannot get a license issue resolved is a truck driver who is not earning. When the Donovan Way office closes on October 9, that work moves across the valley to Silverado. For a North Las Vegas driver, that is not a quick swing by the office anymore. It is a planned trip.

There is also a timing problem worth naming. Two offices close within five days of each other in the middle of October. Anytime service points consolidate, the first few weeks tend to be the busiest, because everyone who was putting off an errand shows up at the same new building. If you have something you can finish before October 9, finishing it early is the smartest move you can make.

Think about the households that feel this most. A two-income family with kids does not have a spare weekday. A trip to the DMV usually means somebody takes time off, arranges a ride for a teenager, or squeezes the errand into a lunch break. Add twenty or thirty minutes of drive time to each leg of that trip and a manageable errand turns into a half day.

Seniors are in the same boat for a different reason. Plenty of older Henderson residents drive short distances on purpose. They know their route, they know the parking, and they do not want a long unfamiliar drive. The good news is that the new building has upgraded ADA access and more than 350 parking spaces, so the destination should be easier to use once you get there. The drive itself is the part that got longer.

And for homeowners, this is one more piece of the picture of what your address actually gets you. Buyers ask me about schools, commute times, grocery stores, and parks. Government services belong on that list too. Where you renew a registration, where you take a teenager for a license test, and how far that drive is are small things until the day you need them.

Background and History

The short version of the background is the one the DMV gave: the buildings got old and the valley got big. Clark County has added people and vehicles for years. The offices that served the valley a couple of decades ago were sized for a smaller population.

You can see the strain in the agency's own recent news. Nevada's Registration Spotter tool, which lets drivers report expired tags and missing plates, has topped 50,000 reports. That is a lot of Nevadans telling the state about vehicles that are out of compliance. Some of that is people ignoring the rules. Some of it is people who ran into a wall trying to get an appointment or a counter and let it slide.

That is the backdrop for a consolidation. When an agency is dealing with volume, it has two options. It can add more small offices, or it can build fewer, larger ones that can move more people per hour. Nevada picked the second path with the Silverado facility.

The design choices point in that direction. More service counters means more people processed at once. Six self-service kiosks means simple business does not have to occupy a counter at all. More than 350 parking spaces means the lot itself stops being the bottleneck. Upgraded ADA access means the building works for people the older buildings served poorly.

It also helps to remember what the two closing offices were built to do. The Donovan Way location in North Las Vegas is a specialty office. Commercial driver license work is its own lane, with its own paperwork and its own customers. A specialty office is efficient when demand is steady and it is expensive when demand is uneven. Folding it into a larger general facility is a way to share staff and counter space across both kinds of business.

The Henderson office on American Pacific Drive is the opposite case. It is a general office serving a part of the valley that kept adding homes year after year. Henderson today is not the Henderson those walls were sized for. The same is true of North Las Vegas, where Apex and the industrial corridor keep pulling in workers and trucks.

Consolidation always comes with a trade. You gain capacity in one place and you lose convenience in another. Henderson and North Las Vegas residents are the ones paying the convenience side of that trade this fall. Whether the new building actually delivers shorter waits is the question everyone will be answering with their own experience in November and December.

What Happens Next

The calendar is the simplest part of this story. The North Las Vegas Commercial Driver License office on Donovan Way closes at the end of business on Friday, October 9, 2026. The Henderson office on American Pacific Drive closes on Wednesday, October 14, 2026. Both sets of services move to the Silverado facility this fall.

Between now and October 9, both offices are still open and still doing business. That window is the opportunity. If your registration comes due in the fall, if your license is close to expiring, or if you have a commercial license matter you have been sitting on, handling it before those dates removes the whole question from your life.

After the closures, expect the new building to be busy. That is normal for any consolidation. The first several weeks at Silverado will carry traffic from two closed offices plus its own regular customers. If your errand is not urgent, waiting until the rush settles is a reasonable strategy. If it is urgent, plan for an early arrival and bring every document you might need so you do not have to make the drive twice.

It is also worth watching what happens to demand at the other Southern Nevada offices. When two locations close, some customers will not drive to Silverado at all. They will go to whichever office is closest to work or to a relative's house instead. That ripple usually shows up as longer waits at nearby locations for a few weeks before things level out.

The other thing to watch is the kiosks. Six self-service kiosks at one building is a meaningful number, but only if people use them. If a large share of the customers walking in are there for a simple renewal that a kiosk can handle, the counters move faster for everyone who genuinely needs a person. That is the quiet lever that decides whether the new building feels better than the old ones.

A few details are worth confirming yourself rather than taking from a summary. Ryan's Take

I do not think this changes anyone's home value. Nobody has ever backed out of a Henderson purchase because of a DMV. But I do think it belongs in the honest conversation about what daily life looks like at a given address, and that conversation is one I have with clients constantly.

Here is what I actually notice. The southeast valley has been growing fast for years, and the services have been catching up the whole time. New rooftops arrive before new counters do. The Silverado consolidation is the county living through that gap in real time. Two familiar buildings close, one bigger building opens, and the people in between adjust.

I will also say the thing agents rarely say out loud. Convenience is a real part of what people pay for in a neighborhood, and it is almost never on the listing sheet. A shorter drive to the grocery store, the urgent care, the school, and yes the DMV is part of why some pockets of the valley feel easy to live in and others feel like a constant commute. Those small minutes add up over the years you own a home.

My advice to Henderson and North Las Vegas homeowners is practical, not dramatic. Handle your DMV business before October 9 if you can. Put the two dates in your phone. And if you are moving to the valley this fall, get your Nevada registration sorted early, because a move plus a closed office plus a compliance deadline is exactly the kind of pileup that turns into a fine.

What You Can Do

First, check your own dates. Look at the sticker on your plate and at the expiration on your driver license. If either one lands in October, November, or December, deal with it before October 9 while both offices are still open and the new building is not absorbing two closures at once.

Second, use the tools that do not require a building. Plenty of routine DMV business, including many registration renewals, can be handled online or at a self-service kiosk. The Silverado facility has six kiosks. If your errand is a simple renewal, a kiosk visit is a very different experience from a counter visit.

Third, if you hold a commercial driver license, treat the October 9 date as a hard deadline for anything you have been putting off. Commercial work often involves documents, medical certificates, and endorsements that take longer than a normal renewal. Doing that at your familiar office before it closes is worth the afternoon.

Fourth, spread the word. Not everyone reads local news. An older neighbor in Henderson who always drives to American Pacific Drive may show up on October 20 with no idea the office is gone. A quick text saves someone a wasted trip.

Finally, verify before you drive. Confirm the Silverado facility's address, opening date, and hours directly with the Nevada DMV before making the trip, and check whether an appointment is available for the service you need.
