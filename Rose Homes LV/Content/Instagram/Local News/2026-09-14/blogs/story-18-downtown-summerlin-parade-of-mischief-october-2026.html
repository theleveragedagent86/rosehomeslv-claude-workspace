Downtown Summerlin Brings Back the Free Parade of Mischief for October

The Parade of Mischief is coming back to Downtown Summerlin, and it is free. The annual Halloween parade runs every Friday and Saturday at 6 p.m. from October 2 through October 24, 2026, along Park Centre Drive at 1980 Festival Plaza Drive.

Downtown Summerlin announced the return on September 10, 2026. More than 60 local youth performers take part, and the floats and characters are inspired by Hocus Pocus, Harry Potter, Wicked, The Addams Family, Cruella, and Dia de los Muertos.

Here is the detail people miss every single year, so write it down now. There are no performances on October 30 or October 31. The parade wraps up on October 24. If you show up on Halloween night expecting a parade, you will be standing on an empty street.

What Happened

On September 10, 2026, Downtown Summerlin announced that its annual Halloween parade, the Parade of Mischief, is returning for another October run. FOX5 Vegas carried the announcement. The event is free to attend, which is the part that matters most to families who are watching every dollar right now.

The schedule is simple and easy to plan around. The parade runs on Fridays and Saturdays only, at 6 p.m. each night. The first performance is Friday, October 2, 2026. The last performance is Saturday, October 24, 2026. That gives you eight chances to see it across four weekends.

The location is Park Centre Drive at 1980 Festival Plaza Drive in Downtown Summerlin. If you have been to a concert or a tree lighting at Downtown Summerlin, you already know the area. Festival Plaza Drive is the open stretch in the middle of the shopping district, and Park Centre Drive runs alongside it. This is the same general part of the property Downtown Summerlin uses for its big seasonal events.

More than 60 local youth performers are part of the show. These are kids and teens from the valley, not a traveling troupe. That is a big reason this parade feels different from a theme park production. When you are standing on the curb, there is a real chance you are watching a neighbor's kid, a classmate, or somebody from your child's dance studio go by.

The creative theme leans on characters families already know. The announcement names floats and characters inspired by Hocus Pocus, Harry Potter, Wicked, The Addams Family, Cruella, and Dia de los Muertos. That mix is wide on purpose. A six year old gets the pumpkin and witch energy of Hocus Pocus. A middle schooler gets Wicked. Parents get The Addams Family and Cruella. And the Dia de los Muertos section honors a tradition that a lot of Clark County families celebrate at home in early November.

One more time, because it is the single most common mistake with this event: there are no performances on October 30 or October 31. Halloween falls on a Saturday in 2026, and that is exactly why people assume the parade will be running. It will not be. Plan your Halloween night separately.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Free family entertainment is getting harder to find in this valley. A movie night for a family of four runs real money once you add tickets and snacks. A weekend at a theme park is a plane ticket away. The Parade of Mischief costs nothing to watch, and it runs eight separate nights. That is a lot of value for a family calendar in October.

It also solves a problem a lot of Summerlin parents recognize. Kids get excited about Halloween in early October and then have to wait almost a full month for the actual holiday. The parade gives them something real to do on the front end of the season. You get four weekends of costumes, music, and floats instead of one night of trick or treating.

The 6 p.m. start time is friendly to younger kids. It is early enough that a preschooler can watch the whole thing and still get to bed at a reasonable hour. It is also early enough that you can eat dinner in Downtown Summerlin afterward without the restaurants being slammed at 9 p.m. Families with toddlers should still plan on an early arrival, because the front row spots fill in before the start time.

There is a neighborhood angle here too. Downtown Summerlin has spent years building itself into the community gathering spot for the west side of the valley. When people who live in Spring Valley, Centennial Hills, or the northwest ask where to take the kids on a Friday night in October, this is the answer. Events like this are part of what makes a master planned community feel like an actual town rather than a collection of subdivisions.

The cost matters more than people want to admit. October is an expensive month for families. Costumes, school parties, pumpkins, and decorations add up fast, and that is before you get to the paid haunted attractions that charge per person. An event that costs nothing at the gate lets a family say yes without doing math first. You can bring the grandparents, bring a cousin, bring your kid's friend from down the street, and the price does not change.

Repeat visits are the other quiet benefit. Because the parade runs eight nights instead of one, you are not stuck picking between it and a soccer tournament or a birthday party. If a Friday does not work, the next Saturday probably does. Families with split custody schedules, shift workers, and parents who travel for work all get a real chance to see it.

And if you are new to the valley, this is a good first look at how Las Vegas families actually spend their fall. Locals do not do Halloween on the Strip. They do it at neighborhood parks, at community centers, at trunk or treat events in church parking lots, and at things like this. The Parade of Mischief is a resident tradition, not a tourist attraction.

Background and History

The Parade of Mischief is described as an annual event, which means Downtown Summerlin has run it in previous Octobers. The 2026 announcement is a return, not a debut. That track record is part of why it draws a crowd. Families who went last year already have it on the calendar.

Downtown Summerlin opened as a shopping, dining, and entertainment district on the west side of the valley and has grown into the event hub for Summerlin. Festival Plaza Drive was built with exactly this kind of programming in mind. It is a wide, open stretch that can be closed off and turned into a stage, a parade route, or a market depending on the season.

The use of local youth performers is worth pausing on. More than 60 kids and teens from the valley perform in this parade. That takes rehearsal time, parent volunteers, and coordination with local performing arts programs. It is a community production dressed up as a shopping center event, and the kids on the floats are the reason the crowd is as loud as it is.

The character lineup also tells you something about how these events evolve. Hocus Pocus and The Addams Family are the classics that parents grew up with. Harry Potter and Wicked pull in the older kids and teenagers. Cruella is newer. And the inclusion of Dia de los Muertos reflects the valley we actually live in, where a large share of families mark the holiday with their own traditions at the start of November.

There is also a practical reason the parade works at this location. Downtown Summerlin already has the parking, the restrooms, the restaurants, and the sidewalks to handle a crowd. A neighborhood park cannot absorb a few thousand people on a Friday night. A built environment designed around foot traffic can. That is why the parade can run eight separate nights without wearing out the people who live nearby.

Halloween programming in Clark County has grown a lot over the last decade. Between community center events, HOA block parties, pumpkin patches, and haunted attractions, October is now one of the busiest months on the local family calendar. A free, repeatable, eight night parade at a central location is a rare thing in that mix, and it is why the Parade of Mischief tends to be the first October event families lock in.

What Happens Next

The first parade is Friday, October 2, 2026 at 6 p.m. From there it repeats every Friday and Saturday: October 2, October 3, October 9, October 10, October 16, October 17, October 23, and October 24. Eight nights total. Then it stops.

Expect the crowds to build as the month goes. The opening weekend is usually lighter than the middle of the month, and the last weekend before Halloween is typically the busiest. If you want room to move and a clear view for a small child, the earlier dates are the smarter pick. If you want maximum energy and a packed street, go on October 23 or October 24.

Downtown Summerlin has not announced any additional Halloween programming tied to the parade in this release, so treat any extras you hear about as unconfirmed until the property posts them. It is also worth thinking through the two Saturdays that are not parade nights. October 31 is a Saturday in 2026, and it is not on the schedule. Neither is October 30. Clark County families usually have plenty of options on Halloween itself, from neighborhood trick or treating to community center parties to trunk or treat events, but those fill up and some require registration. Book whatever you plan to do for Halloween weekend now rather than assuming the parade will cover it.

After October 24, Downtown Summerlin typically shifts into its holiday season programming. If you miss the parade, you will be waiting until October 2027 for the next one. That is the whole reason to put these dates in your phone today rather than in three weeks.

What You Can Do

Start by putting the dates in your calendar right now. Fridays and Saturdays, 6 p.m., October 2 through October 24, 2026, at Park Centre Drive near 1980 Festival Plaza Drive in Downtown Summerlin. Set a reminder for the afternoon of whichever night you pick so it does not sneak up on you.

Plan to arrive early. Downtown Summerlin parking fills up on event nights, and a 6 p.m. start means you are competing with the dinner crowd. Giving yourself 30 to 45 minutes of cushion means you get a parking spot, a decent viewing position, and a chance to let the kids use the restroom before the floats start moving. Bring a light jacket, because October evenings in the valley cool off faster than people expect.

Let the kids wear costumes. This is a Halloween parade, and the crowd leans into it. If you have a child who has been begging to wear their costume since the middle of September, this is eight legitimate opportunities to do it. Just keep masks off small kids in a crowd so they can see where they are going.

Think about the sightline before you settle in. A parade is a moving show, so a spot with a little open space in front of it beats a spot right at the curb behind a row of strollers. If you have a small child, standing near a corner or a wider section of the walkway usually gives you more room to lift them up when a float goes by. Phones down for at least part of it, too. The kids on those floats worked for months, and they notice who is watching.

Tell your neighbors. This is exactly the kind of event that spreads by word of mouth in a Summerlin HOA group or a school parent chat, and a lot of families still do not know the parade skips Halloween weekend. Passing along the dates, the 6 p.m. start, and the October 30 and 31 blackout saves somebody a wasted drive across the valley.

And once more, because it is the thing people get wrong: do not plan your October 30 or October 31 around this parade. There are no performances on those dates. Line up your trick or treating, your neighborhood block party, or your community center event for Halloween night, and treat the Parade of Mischief as the warm up that runs the three weekends before it.
