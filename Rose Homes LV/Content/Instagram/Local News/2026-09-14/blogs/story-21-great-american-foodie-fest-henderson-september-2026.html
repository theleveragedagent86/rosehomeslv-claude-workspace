Great American Foodie Fest Returns to Galleria at Sunset September 25 to 27

The Great American Foodie Fest comes back to Henderson from Friday, September 25 through Sunday, September 27, 2026, at the Galleria Mall at Sunset, 1300 W. Sunset Road. Single-day admission is $7 early bird or $10 at the gate, so a family can get through the gate for the price of one fast food run.

This is a food truck festival with carnival rides, shopping, and live entertainment, set up in a mall parking lot near Sunset Road and Stephanie Street. It is not a Strip event. It is a Henderson event that valley families have been going to for years at the same spot.

Here is everything you need to plan the weekend, plus a little context on why an event like this keeps coming back to this exact corner of the valley.

What Is Happening and When

The dates are Friday, September 25, Saturday, September 26, and Sunday, September 27, 2026. Three days, one location, no travel required for most of the east and south valley.

The hours are different each day, so write these down. Friday runs from 5 p.m. to 11 p.m. Saturday runs from noon to 11 p.m. Sunday runs from noon to 10 p.m. Friday is the short day because it starts in the evening. Saturday is the long one at eleven hours.

The location is the Galleria Mall at Sunset, 1300 W. Sunset Road in Henderson. That is the mall near Sunset Road and Stephanie Street, right off the 515. If you have picked up a phone case or grabbed a pretzel at that mall in the last twenty years, you already know how to get there.

Tickets come in three tiers. A single-day pass is $7 if you buy it early bird, or $10 if you buy it at the gate. A full weekend general admission pass is $10 early bird or $15 at the gate, and that weekend pass includes three-day re-entry. VIP tasting passes start at $45 early bird.

That re-entry detail on the weekend pass is the one most people miss. If you buy the weekend pass, you can leave and come back, all three days. For families with young kids who melt down around 4 p.m., that is the difference between a stressful afternoon and an easy one. Go home, nap, come back at seven when it cools off.

The event features food trucks that have been seen on national TV, carnival rides, shopping, and live entertainment. The specific truck lineup and the specific ride list are not something I am going to guess at here. Vendor rosters at food truck festivals change right up to the week of the event. It helps to picture the setup. This is an outdoor event on mall property, not inside the stores. That means asphalt, open sky, generators, and the smell of fryer oil drifting across a parking lot. Dress for that. It also means the event is weather dependent in a way an indoor event is not, so the forecast for that last weekend of September is worth a look before you commit.

One more honest note. This event is not free. Some of the fall events in the valley are, and I always say so when they are. This one has a gate price. It is a low gate price, but it is a gate price, and rides and food are separate on top of admission.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Henderson and the east valley do not get a huge number of weekend events that are built for locals. A lot of what happens in this town is aimed at people who flew in. This one is not. It is in a suburban mall parking lot, on a residential side of the valley, at prices normal families can absorb.

Think about the actual math for a family of four. Four single-day early bird tickets at $7 each is $28. Four weekend passes at $10 each is $40 for three days with re-entry. Compare that to almost any other ticketed weekend activity in Clark County and the value is obvious. You are paying for admission, then choosing how much you want to spend inside.

Late September is also when the valley finally becomes livable outside again. We spend June through early September indoors. By the last weekend of the month, an evening outdoors is genuinely pleasant. Events like this one exist in that window for a reason. Friday's 5 p.m. start and Saturday and Sunday evening hours are built around the heat.

For anyone living in Green Valley, Whitney Ranch, Anthem, Paradise, or the neighborhoods that ring Sunset Road, this is a short drive. That matters more than people admit. A ten-minute drive means you actually go. A forty-minute drive across the valley means you talk about going and then do not.

The VIP tasting pass is worth a separate mention, because $45 early bird is a real jump from a $7 general admission ticket. That is not a family-of-four purchase for most households. It is a date night purchase, or a purchase for the person in your group who genuinely wants to taste their way across the whole lot. If you are just taking the kids for rides and a burger, general admission is the right ticket and you are not missing the point of the event.

I also think the price point does something useful for the neighborhood. A $10 gate means a lot of different kinds of households can show up on the same weekend. Retirees from Sun City Anthem, families from Whitney Ranch, hospital workers coming off a shift at St. Rose. You do not get that mix at a $90 event. Cheap admission is what makes an event feel like a community event instead of a private one.

There is also a quieter reason this matters. The Galleria at Sunset is a Henderson landmark that has been through a lot of retail change over the years, like nearly every enclosed mall in America. Events that bring thousands of people to the property keep it relevant. Foot traffic is what keeps a mall alive, and a live mall is worth something to the neighborhoods around it.

Background and History

The Great American Foodie Fest has been a recurring Southern Nevada event for years, and this Henderson location has been one of its regular homes. That repetition is what separates it from the pop-up events that appear once and vanish. Locals know the name. Locals know the parking lot.

Food truck festivals took off across the country in the 2010s, and Las Vegas took to the format quickly. Our valley has a deep restaurant workforce and a lot of people with commercial kitchen experience. When food trucks became a viable path for independent operators, plenty of Vegas cooks took it. A festival that gathers them in one lot is a natural fit here.

The carnival ride element is what pushes this from a food event to a family event. A food-only festival is an adult outing. Add rides and a kid has a reason to want to go, which changes who shows up and how long they stay. That combination is a big part of why the format keeps working in a suburb like Henderson.

Henderson has also changed a lot around this mall. The city has grown south and east for decades, and the population that ringed the Galleria at Sunset in 2005 is not the population that rings it now. There are more young families, more first-time buyers, and more people who moved here from California and are still learning what this valley offers outside the resort corridor. An event with a $7 gate is a low-risk way for those households to try something local.

The Galleria at Sunset itself opened in the 1990s and has anchored that part of Henderson ever since. The stretch of Sunset Road between Stephanie Street and Marks Street is one of the busiest retail corridors on the east side. There is an enormous amount of parking, easy freeway access, and enough surrounding commercial space that a weekend event does not choke the neighborhood streets. Those are the boring logistics that make an event site work year after year.

What Happens Next

The immediate next step is the early bird window. The price difference between early bird and gate pricing is $3 on a single day and $5 on a weekend pass. For a family of four buying weekend passes, that is a $20 swing. If you already know you are going, buying ahead is the obvious call. After that, watch for the lineup announcement. Food truck festivals usually publish the participating trucks in the week or two before the gates open, and that is when you find out whether the specific truck you have been chasing on Instagram is going to be there. If there is a truck you love, that announcement is the thing to watch for.

Then comes the weather. Late September in Clark County can still deliver a 95-degree afternoon. Check the forecast the week of, and plan your hours around it. Friday evening and the later hours Saturday and Sunday are the comfortable windows. Noon on Saturday in a parking lot is a different experience than 8 p.m. on Saturday in the same parking lot.

It is also worth thinking about what the rest of the fall calendar looks like once this weekend passes. Late September into October is the busiest stretch of the year for outdoor events in Clark County. If this weekend goes well for your family, it is usually the first of several, and the same planning habits apply every time: check the hours, buy ahead when there is a discount, and go at the edges of the day rather than the middle.

Longer term, the thing to watch is whether events like this keep multiplying on the Henderson side of the valley. Every year there seem to be a few more. That trend is worth paying attention to if you live out here, because a neighborhood with things to do on a weekend is a neighborhood people want to move to.

Ryan's Take

I talk to buyers every week who are deciding between Henderson and the west side, and the question underneath the question is almost always the same. What is there to actually do out here? Not on the Strip. Near my house.

A weekend like this one is part of the answer. Three days of food trucks and rides in a mall lot off Sunset Road is not a headline, but it is exactly the kind of thing that makes a suburb feel like a real place instead of a subdivision with a grocery store. When I show homes in Green Valley or Whitney Ranch or Anthem, the events calendar is a selling point, and it is an honest one.

The other thing I notice is how often the "things to do" question gets answered with Strip attractions that locals visit once every five years. That is not a real answer for someone deciding where to raise kids. A recurring, affordable, family-friendly weekend at a mall three miles from your house is a real answer, and Henderson has been quietly stacking up more of those.

I will also say this as someone who watches commercial corridors closely. Retail centers that host events tend to hold their value and their tenants better than the ones that just sit there. The Galleria at Sunset keeps putting things in its parking lot, and that is a healthy sign for the properties and neighborhoods around it. If you own a home within a couple miles of that mall, you want it busy.

What You Can Do

Start by picking your day and your ticket. If you are only going once, a single-day pass at $7 early bird is the cheapest way in. If there is any chance you go twice, the weekend pass at $10 early bird with three-day re-entry is the better buy, because it costs $3 more than one day and covers all three. That is the simplest money decision at this event.

Then bring cash and set a budget before you walk in. Admission gets you through the gate. Food, drinks, rides, and shopping are separate. The easiest way to blow $200 at a $10 event is to walk in with no plan and three hungry kids. Decide what each person gets, say it out loud in the car, and the day goes better.

Go early or go late, not in the middle. Friday from 5 p.m. to 11 p.m. is the calmest window of the weekend. Saturday from noon to 11 p.m. is the biggest day and the most crowded. If you want short lines and cooler air, Friday night or Sunday evening before the 10 p.m. close are your best bets.

Bring water, sunscreen, and a hat if you are going during daylight. Wear real shoes; you will be standing on asphalt. And if you have a stroller, pick a meeting spot with your group the second you arrive, because parking lot events all look the same once the sun goes down.

If you go and you love it, tell a neighbor. Local events survive on word of mouth more than on advertising, and the ones that fill the lot are the ones that come back next year. That is true for this festival and it is true for the smaller community events all over Henderson and the east valley that never make the news at all.
