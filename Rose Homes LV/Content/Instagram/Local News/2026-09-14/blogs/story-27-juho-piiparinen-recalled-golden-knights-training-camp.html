Golden Knights Recall First-Round Pick Juho Piiparinen From Finland for Training Camp

The Vegas Golden Knights announced on Monday, September 14, that defenseman Juho Piiparinen has been recalled from Tappara of Finland's Liiga and will attend training camp later this week. Piiparinen is the team's newest first-round pick, and this is the first time Las Vegas fans will get to see him on local ice.

Piiparinen is 18 years old. Vegas took him 29th overall in the 2026 NHL Draft, then signed him to his entry-level contract in July. He is 6-foot-3 and 203 pounds, which is a real NHL frame already, and he plays defense at a time when the VGK blue line is one of the most talked about parts of the roster.

For local hockey families, this is not just a transaction line on a website. Training camp happens at City National Arena, the team's practice facility in Summerlin, and camp is one of the few times of year when regular people can watch a future NHL defenseman work without buying a ticket to T-Mobile Arena.

What Happened

On Monday, September 14, 2026, the Golden Knights confirmed that Juho Piiparinen had been recalled from Tappara. Tappara is a club in Liiga, the top professional hockey league in Finland. A recall in this situation simply means Vegas asked for its prospect back so he could take part in the team's own camp instead of starting the season overseas.

Piiparinen will report to camp later this week. That timing matters. It puts him in the building at the same moment the full veteran roster arrives, rather than showing up after the lines are already set. He will be around NHL coaches, NHL trainers, and NHL veterans from day one of the on-ice work.

The numbers behind the name are worth knowing. Last season with Tappara's U20 team, Piiparinen put up a goal and 12 assists in 15 games. That is 13 points in 15 games from a defenseman, which is a strong rate at that level. He also appeared in 29 games with Tappara's senior club, the professional side, and recorded three assists.

Those two lines tell slightly different stories, and both are useful. The U20 production says he can drive offense against players his own age. The 29 senior games say the club already trusted him enough to put an 18-year-old on the ice against grown professional men. Very few teenagers get that many professional games in a season in Finland.

The draft slot adds context too. Vegas selected him 29th overall in 2026. Late first round is a spot where teams often take a player they believe has size and a long runway, rather than a finished product. At 6-foot-3 and 203 pounds, Piiparinen already has the size part handled.

He signed his entry-level contract with Vegas in July. That is the standard first contract for a drafted player. Signing it in July, rather than waiting, is usually a sign that both sides wanted the relationship formalized early. The specific contract terms beyond the fact that it is an entry-level deal are not detailed in the reporting on this story. [NOT VERIFIED]

It also helps to understand what Liiga is. It is not a junior league. It is Finland's top professional circuit, with full rosters of grown men playing for paychecks. When a team there hands 29 games to an 18-year-old defenseman, it is making a statement about trust, because a mistake by a teenage defenseman costs that club points in the standings.

The U20 level sits underneath that. Tappara, like most Finnish clubs, runs a full development ladder under one organization. A player can move up and down between the junior side and the senior side in the same season, which is exactly what Piiparinen's split numbers reflect. He was not stuck at one level all year.

One thing to keep clear. A recall for training camp is not the same as making the team. Plenty of young prospects attend an NHL camp, get evaluated, and go back to their club or to junior hockey to keep developing. What camp does guarantee is exposure, coaching, and a real measurement against NHL pace.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Start with the simplest reason. Golden Knights training camp is free entertainment for a family in Clark County. City National Arena sits in Summerlin, and camp practices are one of the rare chances to watch professional hockey up close without the cost of a game night on the Strip.

If you have kids who play, this matters even more. Youth hockey has grown a lot in Southern Nevada since the Golden Knights arrived, and a lot of that growth runs through rinks in Summerlin and Henderson. Watching an 18-year-old prospect go through the same drills your kid does at practice is a different kind of teaching moment than watching a highlight reel.

There is also a plain civic point here. The Golden Knights are one of the few things in this valley that locals and visitors both care about, and locals care more. Camp week is the start of the hockey calendar that runs from now through spring, and it shapes what people around town talk about for months.

For homeowners, the connection is less direct but it is real. The area around City National Arena in Summerlin has built a genuine hockey culture over the last several years. Practice facility, restaurants, retail, youth programs, and a steady flow of people coming in and out on weekends all sit in the same corridor. That kind of anchor is part of why buyers ask about Summerlin by name.

The same thing happens in Henderson around the Henderson Silver Knights and Lifeguard Arena. Vegas is a young sports market compared to most NHL cities, and the facilities are still new. When a first-round pick shows up for camp, it reinforces that the pipeline is active and that the buildings here are being used the way they were meant to be used.

Camp week also changes the rhythm of the neighborhood around the practice facility. Traffic picks up. The parking lot fills. Kids in team jerseys show up on a Tuesday morning. If you live nearby, you feel it before you read about it, and most people who live there consider that a feature rather than a nuisance.

And there is the competitive angle that fans actually care about. Vegas is coming off a Stanley Cup Final loss to the Carolina Hurricanes. A team that just came that close does not rebuild. It reloads. Watching how a 29th overall pick handles his first NHL camp is a small window into whether the next wave is coming fast or slow.

Background and History

The Golden Knights have never really operated like a team that waits on prospects. Since the franchise started, the pattern has been to trade for established players and chase the win right now. That approach produced deep playoff runs, and it also meant fans did not spend a lot of time learning the names of teenagers in the system.

That is part of why this recall gets attention. A first-round pick reporting to camp is a reminder that Vegas does have young players coming, even if the club is built to compete immediately. Piiparinen is the newest name on that list, and at 29th overall he is the highest-drafted player in the 2026 class for this organization.

Finland has been a steady producer of NHL defensemen for a long time, and the Liiga development path is well understood around the league. Players come up through a club's junior system, get a look with the senior team, and get more professional minutes each year. Piiparinen following that exact path with Tappara is normal, and the 29 senior games at 18 is the part that stands out.

The blue line context matters as well. Vegas made a significant investment in defense in July when it signed Rasmus Andersson to a seven-year deal worth $59.5 million. Teams that spend that way on defense are usually also trying to build cheaper young depth behind it, because the salary cap does not allow every spot to be expensive.

There is a local history angle as well. City National Arena opened as the Golden Knights' practice home before the franchise ever played a game, and it doubled as the valley's first serious community rink for a generation of kids who had no local hockey option before that. Every camp held there since has drawn locals who treat it as a neighborhood event.

The broader roster picture is in motion too. The Review-Journal laid out five open questions heading into this camp, including Parker Wotherspoon stepping in for Kaedan Korczak on the blue line. When a defense group is in transition, every extra body with size and skill gets a longer look.

What Happens Next

Piiparinen reports to camp later this week. The first step is the standard one for any camp: medicals, fitness testing, meetings, and then on-ice sessions with the group he is assigned to. Coaches will move players between groups as the week goes on based on what they see.

The bigger factor working against every young player this fall is time. The Golden Knights are heading into a shortened camp with a four-game preseason instead of the usual seven games. That is fewer exhibition minutes for everybody, and prospects are usually the ones who feel that squeeze first, because veterans still need their reps to get ready for opening night.

So the realistic outcomes for Piiparinen look like this. He could get into a preseason game or two, which would be a genuine milestone for an 18-year-old. He could practice through camp, get a full evaluation, and then head back to Finland to keep playing regular professional minutes with Tappara. Either result would be normal, and neither one would mean anything is wrong.

What to watch for is simpler than the roster math. Watch whether he looks comfortable with the speed. Watch whether he can make a clean first pass under pressure. Watch how he uses that 6-foot-3 frame in board battles against grown NHL forwards. Those three things tell you more than a point total in an exhibition game ever will.

Keep the age in mind while you watch. An 18-year-old defenseman is almost never finished. Strength, reading the play, and decision speed usually arrive over two or three more seasons of professional minutes. A prospect who looks a half step behind in September can look completely different two years later, and that gap is normal rather than alarming.

There is also the injury picture around camp. Two VGK rookies came back from the San Jose rookie showcase with injuries, which can open up practice reps for whoever is healthy. Camp opportunity often comes from circumstance, not just from performance.

Ryan's Take

I have lived and worked in this valley long enough to remember when hockey was a punchline here. Now we have a Stanley Cup banner, a shortened camp because the team went to the Final again in June, and an 18-year-old flying in from Finland because Las Vegas is where he wants to build a career. That shift is genuinely remarkable, and it happened fast.

From the real estate side, I do not want to overstate what one prospect means. A recall does not move home values in Summerlin. But the thing underneath it does matter. City National Arena, Lifeguard Arena in Henderson, the youth programs, and the year-round hockey calendar are now part of the fabric of specific neighborhoods, and buyers notice. I have had families relocating here ask me about rink drive times before they asked about anything else.

I also think locals undersell how unusual our situation is. Most NHL markets got their team decades ago and built the youth base slowly around it. Vegas compressed that into a few years, and the result is that a kid who started skating the year the team arrived is now old enough to play high school hockey. Camp week is when that timeline becomes visible in one building.

That is the part people from outside Nevada still do not expect. Las Vegas has become a hockey town with a real youth base and real facilities, and those facilities sit in master-planned communities that families already want. When a first-round pick shows up for camp in Summerlin this week, he is walking into a community that built itself around the sport in under a decade.

What You Can Do

If you want to see Piiparinen and the rest of the camp roster in person, check the Golden Knights' official schedule for training camp practice times at City National Arena before you drive over. Camp schedules shift, sessions get moved, and public access depends on what the team announces. Confirm the day and time from the team directly rather than from a social post.

If your family is new to hockey, camp week is a good on-ramp. You can watch a practice, see what the drills actually look like, and then look into local youth and learn-to-skate programs at rinks around the valley. Summerlin and Henderson both have facilities with beginner programs, and fall is when most sessions start.

If you follow the roster side, keep an eye on the four preseason games. With a shortened exhibition schedule, the lineup sheets for those games are the clearest signal of who the coaching staff wants to see against real competition. If Piiparinen's name shows up, that tells you something.

It is also worth bookmarking the Golden Knights' own news page rather than relying on reposts. Prospect news moves quickly in September, and the difference between a player being sent back and a player sticking around often gets garbled by the time it reaches a feed. The team's own announcements are the cleanest source.

And if you are thinking about buying or selling near one of these hockey hubs, it helps to know the neighborhoods block by block rather than just by zip code. Proximity to a rink, a school, and a freeway on-ramp all price differently, and those differences are easy to miss from a listing photo.
