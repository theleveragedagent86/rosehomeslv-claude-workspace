Darnell Elementary Went From 1 Star to 5 Stars in Two Years

Marshall C. Darnell Elementary School in Las Vegas climbed from a 1-star rating in 2023-2024 to a 5-star rating in 2025-2026, and the Clark County School District held a celebration at the campus on September 17, 2026 to mark it. That is a four-star jump in two school years, which is about as fast as a state rating can move.

The number driving most of that climb is reading. CCSD reports that proficiency in English language arts for students in grades 3 through 5 went from 43.7 percent to 64.7 percent over the same stretch. That is roughly a 21 point gain in the share of upper elementary students reading at grade level.

For families shopping for a home in Clark County, this is the story that should change how you read a school report. A star rating is a snapshot of a specific school year, not a permanent label. Darnell is proof that the snapshot can look completely different two years later.

What Happened at Darnell Elementary

On September 17, 2026, the Clark County School District publicly recognized Darnell Elementary for what it called a four-star improvement in two years. The school sat at 1 star under the Nevada School Performance Framework in 2023-2024. In the 2025-2026 results, it landed at 5 stars, the top rating the state gives.

CCSD Trustee Clerk Lydia Dominguez attended the event at the school. Trustee visits to individual campuses are usually reserved for openings, emergencies, or moments the district wants parents and staff to see, and this one fell in the last category. The district framed the visit as a thank-you to the staff and families who did the work.

The celebration also included a separate honor for one teacher. The College Football Playoff Foundation gave first-grade teacher Christine O'Neal a $1,000 DonorsChoose award. DonorsChoose is the platform teachers across the country use to fund classroom projects, from book sets to seating to science materials, and a $1,000 award covers a meaningful piece of a first-grade classroom wish list. The Foundation has run teacher recognition programs in host cities around the College Football Playoff for several years, and Las Vegas is on that circuit.

The headline academic figure is the grades 3 through 5 English language arts proficiency rate. CCSD puts the two-year move at 43.7 percent to 64.7 percent. One note on the numbers: FOX5 Vegas reported the ending figure as 65.2 percent rather than 64.7 percent, so the exact endpoint differs slightly between the two accounts, and we are using the district's own 64.7 percent here because CCSD is the source that owns the data.

Either way, the size and direction of the change are the same. Roughly two out of every three upper elementary students at Darnell are now testing proficient in reading, up from fewer than half. In a district the size of Clark County, which serves more than 300,000 students, a single elementary school moving that far in that little time is unusual enough that the district built an event around it.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

School ratings follow Clark County families around whether they want them to or not. They show up on real estate listing pages, on school search sites, in relocation packets from employers, and in conversations at backyard barbecues. A 1-star label next to a zoned elementary school is one of the fastest ways a buyer talks themselves out of an entire area.

That is exactly why Darnell matters beyond its own attendance zone. It is a working example that a rating attached to a school today may not describe that school in two years. Ratings come out on a lag. A family looking at a listing this fall may be reading a number that reflects testing from a prior school year, filtered through whatever a third-party site decided to display.

For current homeowners in the zone, the practical effect is simpler. Families already living there now have a stronger case to make when they talk about their neighborhood, and parents who were considering a move to a different zone have one fewer reason to pack. Enrollment stability matters to a campus, because steady enrollment supports steady staffing, and steady staffing is a big part of how academic gains hold.

For renters and for parents choosing between a zoned school and a magnet or charter application, the takeaway is to look at the direction of the trend, not just the current number. A school that has been climbing for two straight years is telling you something different than a school that has been flat, even if both currently show the same rating. The trend line is the part most listing sites do not show you.

There is also a plain civic point here. CCSD gets a lot of coverage on budget cuts, staffing, and facility problems, and that coverage is legitimate. Stories like Darnell are the other side of the same district. Both are true at once, and residents deserve to see both before they form an opinion about the schools their property taxes support.

One more piece worth noticing is the teacher award. A $1,000 DonorsChoose grant to a first-grade teacher is small money next to a district budget, but it lands in the exact grade level where reading habits get built. First grade is where a student either learns to decode words comfortably or starts falling behind quietly. Community support at that level, whether it comes from a national foundation or from a neighbor funding a classroom project, tends to show up in test results several years later.

Background and History

Nevada rates public schools on a five-star scale through the Nevada School Performance Framework. The framework combines several measures, and for elementary schools the biggest pieces are student proficiency in English language arts and math, academic growth over the year, and how English learners are progressing toward proficiency. Chronic absenteeism and a school climate measure also factor in.

Because growth carries real weight in the elementary formula, a school starting from a low base has room to move quickly if a group of students posts a strong year. That is part of why a 1-star school can climb faster than people expect. It is also why a school at the top has to keep performing to stay there, since the same formula that rewarded the climb will register a slide.

Ratings were paused during the pandemic years, which created a gap in the record and left several Clark County campuses carrying pre-pandemic or early post-pandemic labels far longer than anyone intended. When the state resumed publishing, some schools looked much worse on paper than families recognized from the inside, and a few looked better. Darnell's 2023-2024 1-star mark sits in that recovery window.

The district has also pushed hard on early literacy in recent years, including more structured reading instruction in the early grades and more attention to which students are behind and by how much. Nevada's Read by Grade 3 requirements keep that focus on the K through 3 band. A school that gets first and second grade reading right tends to see the payoff show up two or three years later in the grades 3 through 5 numbers, which is the exact pattern Darnell's results follow.

It is also worth understanding what a star rating does not measure. It says nothing about class size in a specific grade, nothing about whether the school has a music teacher or a full-time nurse, nothing about how long the principal has been there, and nothing about the commute from a particular street. Those are the things families notice day to day, and they are the things you only learn by calling the school or talking to a parent who is already there. The rating is a starting point for questions, not an answer.

What Happens Next

The next checkpoint is the 2026-2027 star ratings, which will show whether Darnell holds at 5 stars or settles somewhere in between. Holding is genuinely harder than climbing, because the growth measure that rewards rapid improvement gives less lift once a school is already scoring well. Watching whether the school stays in the four to five range over the next two cycles is the real test of whether this was a durable change or a strong single year.

Staffing is the variable to watch most closely. Turnaround results usually trace back to a specific group of teachers and a principal working a specific plan. If that team stays together, gains tend to stick. If several of them move on at once, the campus has to rebuild the practice from scratch, and that shows up in scores within a year or two. This is true at every school in the valley, not just this one.

Statewide, Nevada continues to adjust how it reports school performance, and the measures inside the framework can shift between cycles. That means year-over-year comparisons are not always perfectly clean. When the next release comes out, read the fine print on what changed in the formula before you read too much into a one-star move at any campus.

In the shorter term, expect CCSD to point at Darnell when it talks about what is working, and expect other campuses to ask what the school did. That is the useful part of a celebration like this one. A specific set of practices at a specific school is a lot easier for another principal to borrow than a district-wide directive.

Families weighing a move should also remember that ratings can travel in both directions. A campus that is riding a strong year today can slip next cycle, and a campus sitting at two or three stars may be mid-climb right now with nothing published yet to show it. Neither situation is a reason to rule an area in or out on its own. It is a reason to check the current data rather than the number you remember hearing a couple of years ago.

Ryan's Take

I get asked about school ratings constantly, and my honest answer is that most buyers are reading them wrong. They treat a star rating like a permanent property of a neighborhood, when it is really a measurement of one school year that arrives months after that year ended. Darnell is the cleanest example I have seen lately of how far a number can move while the houses around it stay exactly the same.

So here is what I tell people. Do not take a school rating off a listing site or off a page that has not been updated since the last cycle. Go to the Nevada Report Card at nevadareportcard.nv.gov, look up the actual zoned school, and read the current year plus the two years before it. Three data points tell you the direction. One data point tells you almost nothing, and a number copied onto a listing page two years ago tells you less than that. Then, if the school matters to your decision, call the front office and ask to tour. Ten minutes on a campus will tell you things no rating captures.

I am not going to tell anyone which part of the valley to buy in based on a school rating, and I would be skeptical of anyone who does. Schools change, families have different needs, and the right zoned school for one household is the wrong fit for another. A rating is one factor in a decision that also includes price, commute, lot size, HOA rules, and how a house feels when you walk through the front door. What I will say is that checking the current data yourself costs nothing and takes about five minutes, and it beats making a six-figure decision on a number you saw on a real estate portal.

What You Can Do

Start with the primary source. The Nevada Report Card at nevadareportcard.nv.gov is the state's official school accountability site, and it lets you look up any Nevada public school by name and see its star rating, proficiency rates, growth data, and enrollment. It is free, it does not require an account, and it is the same data every third-party school site is repackaging.

If you are buying, confirm the zoned school before you rely on any of it. Zoning is set by CCSD, not by the listing, and boundaries do get redrawn. The district's school locator tool on ccsd.net lets you enter an address and see the assigned elementary, middle, and high school. Do that with the actual property address rather than assuming the nearest school is the zoned one.

If you already live in the area, the highest-value thing you can do is show up. School Organizational Teams meet at every CCSD campus and include parent seats, and those meetings are where campus-level decisions about staffing and spending actually get discussed. Board of Trustees meetings are open to the public and streamed, and public comment is open to any resident. Teachers also post classroom projects on DonorsChoose, so if you want to support a specific campus directly, that is a straightforward way to do it.

Finally, keep a little perspective when you read any of this. Darnell's jump is a real accomplishment and worth celebrating, and it is also one school in a district with hundreds of campuses. The useful lesson is not that every low-rated school will climb four stars. It is that the rating you see attached to a school is a description of a past year, produced by a formula that changes, published on a delay. Treat it as one input among several, alongside a campus visit, a conversation with the office, and a look at how the numbers have moved over time.
