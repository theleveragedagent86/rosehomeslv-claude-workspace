PWHL Las Vegas Plays Its First Ever Games at America First Center November 22 to 24

PWHL Las Vegas now has dates for the first games in team history, and they are in Henderson, not on the Strip. The new pro women's hockey club will host the Minnesota Frost and the Vancouver Goldeneyes at America First Center from November 22 through November 24.

The matchups are set. Las Vegas plays Minnesota on Saturday, November 22 at 6:50 p.m. PT. Vancouver plays Las Vegas on Monday, November 24 at noon PT. FOX5 Vegas reported the schedule, and it is the first time anyone has been able to put a real date and a real puck drop on this franchise.

We covered the calendar side of this when the league said camps would open in November in PWHL Las Vegas Camp Opens. This piece is about the games themselves, the times, the opponents, and what a brand new pro team means for the neighborhood that gets to host it.

What Happened

The Professional Women's Hockey League confirmed that training camps open in November. The league is working around the IIHF Women's World Championship in Denmark, which starts November 6. A lot of the best players in the world are on national teams, so the league waits for that tournament before it opens camp. That is why this season starts later than a typical hockey calendar.

PWHL Las Vegas will use that November window for its first games. The team hosts two clubs at America First Center over a three day stretch. The Minnesota Frost come in first. Las Vegas versus Minnesota is scheduled for Saturday, November 22 at 6:50 p.m. PT. That is a night game on a weekend, which is about the best slot a new team could ask for.

Then the Vancouver Goldeneyes take the ice. Vancouver versus Las Vegas is set for Monday, November 24 at noon PT. A noon Monday start is unusual, and it fits the preseason format. These are scrimmage style games built around camp, not a regular season opener with a full arena show. Still, they count as the first games this franchise has ever played.

One more date matters if you want to know who is actually on the ice. PWHL training camp rosters get announced the week of October 5. That is when Las Vegas fans will finally see names, numbers, and positions instead of just a logo and a league press release. Until then, nobody outside the organization knows the roster.

The opponents are worth a note on their own. The Minnesota Frost and the Vancouver Goldeneyes are two different clubs from two different corners of the league map, and Las Vegas draws both inside a three day window. For a first camp, seeing two separate systems back to back gives a coaching staff a much better read than facing the same team twice would.

Schedules at this stage can move. Preseason dates, times, and opponents shift for travel, ice availability, and league scheduling. Before you plan a night around it, check the team and the arena for the current details.

It is worth being clear about what these games are and are not. These are preseason scrimmages tied to training camp. Coaches use them to look at line combinations, test goalies, and see how players handle real game speed after a long summer. The result does not go in a standings column. The value is that the team exists on the ice for the first time, in front of people, in Henderson.

Two different opponents in three days also tells you something about how camp is built. A team gets one look at a club, makes adjustments, and gets a second look against a different style two days later. That is a compressed evaluation window, and it is why the noon Monday start is not as strange as it sounds.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Henderson is getting a pro sports first. That is the headline for anyone who lives near Water Street or anywhere in the eastern half of the valley. Las Vegas has spent a decade building a reputation as a sports town, and almost all of that has happened on or near the Strip. This one lands downtown Henderson instead.

America First Center sits in the Water Street District. That area has been the subject of city planning talk for years. It has restaurants, small shops, and a walkable core that city leaders have wanted to grow. A pro team playing there in November gives that district three evenings and afternoons of real foot traffic, from people who may not have had a reason to go before.

For families, the practical piece is distance and cost. Getting to a Strip event means Strip traffic, Strip parking, and Strip pricing. Getting to Henderson means a normal drive on a normal road for most people in Green Valley, Anthem, Inspirada, Seven Hills, and the southeast valley. If you have kids in youth hockey or kids who play any sport, a first ever pro game twenty minutes from the house is a different kind of Saturday than a trip to the resort corridor.

There is also a plain civic value in it. Kids in Clark County can now watch professional women play hockey in their own county. That was not true before. Whether or not you follow the sport, the presence of a women's pro team changes what local kids picture when they think about where the game can take them.

And for renters and owners who live in the area, events like this are part of what makes a neighborhood feel like a place. Not every amenity shows up in a listing. A walkable district with a real arena in it is the kind of thing people mention when they explain why they picked their street.

Small businesses near the arena get a direct read on this too. A Saturday night game and a Monday noon game are two very different crowds. The night game brings people who will eat before and grab a drink after. The midday game brings people who need lunch. Owners on and around Water Street have been through this cycle with other events at the building, and they know how to staff for it.

There is a commuting note as well. A 6:50 p.m. Saturday start means you are not fighting a weekday rush to get there. A noon Monday start means you are, if you are coming from a job. Anyone planning to go on the 24th should treat it like a midday errand in a busy part of Henderson, not a casual drop in.

Background and History

The PWHL launched as a single, unified professional women's hockey league after years of the sport being split across smaller leagues and short lived organizations. The league started with a small group of teams and has been adding markets. Las Vegas is one of the newer additions, which is why everything about this team is still a first.

Southern Nevada did not have pro hockey at all until fairly recently. The Vegas Golden Knights arrived and turned a desert city into a hockey market faster than almost anyone predicted. The Henderson Silver Knights followed and gave the eastern valley its own team to claim. America First Center came out of that same wave of investment in Henderson.

That order matters. The valley already has ice, already has youth programs, and already has thousands of families who know the sport. A women's pro team is not landing in a market that has to be taught what a blue line is. It is landing in a market that has been building hockey habits for close to a decade.

The November calendar also has history behind it. The world championship in Denmark starting November 6 pulls national team players out of club rosters. Leagues that want their best players available plan around those tournaments. So the league opens camp after, and the first games in Las Vegas land in the back half of the month.

The Water Street District itself has a longer story. Henderson's downtown was the city's original core, and it went through decades of the valley's growth pushing west and south. The push to bring life back to Water Street has been a stated city goal for years. An arena and a pro tenant are a large piece of that plan actually working.

Women's hockey has its own history in the middle of all this. For a long time, the best players in the world finished college or a national team run and had nowhere stable to play professionally in North America. Rosters formed and folded. Seasons got announced and canceled. The current league exists because that instability finally got addressed with one organization, real contracts, and a real schedule. Las Vegas joining that is a sign the league is past the survival phase and into the growth phase.

What Happens Next

The next real date is the week of October 5, when PWHL training camp rosters are announced. That is the first time Las Vegas fans see who is on the team. Expect a mix of established pros and players competing for spots, which is normal for any camp roster.

After that, camps open in November once the world championship in Denmark is underway or wrapping up. Then the November 22 through 24 window at America First Center. Las Vegas versus Minnesota on the 22nd at 6:50 p.m. PT, and Vancouver versus Las Vegas on the 24th at noon PT.

Ticketing details, pricing, and any public access rules for these games have not been laid out in what has been reported so far. Preseason scrimmages are sometimes handled differently than regular season games, and the team will be the one to announce how it works. Watch the team's own channels and the arena for that.

The honest caution is worth repeating. Preseason schedules change. Times move, opponents swap, and games get adjusted. Confirm the date and time with the team or the arena before you build a weekend around it.

Past November, the thing to watch is whether these two games turn into a pattern. If Henderson draws well for a pair of preseason scrimmages, that is an argument for more dates at America First Center down the road. New teams get judged on early turnout, fairly or not, and the people who show up in November are effectively voting on how much hockey this building gets.

Ryan's Take

I have watched what the Golden Knights did to this valley, and I have watched what having the Silver Knights did specifically for Henderson. Sports do not move home prices by themselves. What they do is change how people talk about a place. Henderson used to get described as the quiet suburb. Now it gets described as the city with the arena and the walkable downtown, and that shift shows up in how buyers rank it.

When I show homes in Green Valley, Anthem, and the neighborhoods that feed into Water Street, people ask about what there is to do. They ask about restaurants, walkability, and whether the area feels alive on a weeknight. Adding a brand new professional team to that answer is not nothing. It is another reason a family from out of state picks the east valley over a comparable neighborhood somewhere else in the county.

I would also say this. The first games of a franchise happen once. If you have kids who play, or kids who might, taking them to a November afternoon game at America First Center is a cheap memory compared to most things in this town. That is my actual recommendation, not a market call.

The longer view is about the east valley in general. Buyers who look at Henderson are usually comparing it to the northwest or to Summerlin, and the comparison almost always comes down to lifestyle rather than square footage. Every year Henderson adds another reason to answer that question in its favor, and a pro team with its own arena downtown is a bigger reason than most.

What You Can Do

Put the dates on the calendar in pencil. Saturday, November 22 at 6:50 p.m. PT for Las Vegas versus Minnesota, and Monday, November 24 at noon PT for Vancouver versus Las Vegas, both at America First Center in Henderson. Then check back closer to the date, because preseason schedules move and you do not want to drive down there on a changed date.

Watch for the roster announcement the week of October 5. That is when the team stops being an idea and starts being a group of names. If you want to follow along in a way that actually sticks, pick a player after the roster drops and track her through camp. That is how most people end up caring about a new team.

If you are going, plan to make an afternoon of it. The Water Street District has places to eat within walking distance of the arena, and a noon Monday game in particular leaves the rest of the day open. Parking in downtown Henderson is far easier than anything near the resort corridor, but give yourself extra time for the first game since nobody has done this before.

And if you have kids in a local youth hockey program, check whether your club is organizing a group. Youth programs in the valley often pull together outings for events like this, and a first ever game is exactly the kind of thing they will try to get a block of seats for.

One more thing that costs nothing. Tell a neighbor. A brand new team in a brand new market lives or dies on word of mouth in the first season, and most people in the valley still do not know PWHL Las Vegas exists, let alone that it plays in Henderson in November. Mentioning it at a school pickup line does more for turnout than any ad the team can buy.
