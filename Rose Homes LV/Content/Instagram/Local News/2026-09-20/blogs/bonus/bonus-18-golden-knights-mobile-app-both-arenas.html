New Golden Knights App Now Handles Tickets for Both the Vegas and Henderson Teams

The Vegas Golden Knights launched an all new mobile app on Friday, September 18, and it is now the app you use to get into a home game. It carries full AXS ticket management, and it handles entry into both T-Mobile Arena on the Strip and Lee's Family Forum in Henderson.

The part that matters most for local families is the toggle. Inside the same app, you can switch over to the AHL's Henderson Silver Knights. One download, both teams, both buildings. If your household holds tickets to Vegas games, Henderson games, or both, this is the tool that gets you through the gate.

The team also says the new app will carry exclusive offers and rewards for fans. That part is still rolling out, so treat it as something to watch rather than something to count on. The ticket piece is the part with a hard deadline attached, because the home schedule is about to start and the gate does not care whether you got around to updating your phone.

Consider this a heads up. If you have a game on your calendar in the next few weeks, spend five minutes on your phone now instead of five minutes in a line later.

What Happened

On Friday, September 18, 2026, the Vegas Golden Knights announced and released an all new mobile app. The team described it as a full rebuild rather than a small update, and the headline feature is that it now serves as the single home for tickets across the organization's two local clubs.

The app includes full AXS ticket management. AXS is the ticketing platform the arenas use, so that means the usual list of things fans need on game day lives inside the app now. You can pull up your tickets, and you can manage them without bouncing between different apps or digging through an email thread from four months ago.

The app also handles entry. According to the team, it provides entry into both T-Mobile Arena and Lee's Family Forum for home games. T-Mobile Arena is the Golden Knights' building on the Strip. Lee's Family Forum is the Henderson arena where the Silver Knights play. Two teams, two buildings, one app on your phone.

Then there is the toggle. Inside the app, fans can switch between the Vegas Golden Knights and the Henderson Silver Knights. The Silver Knights are the American Hockey League affiliate, which is the level right below the NHL. A lot of Southern Nevada families follow both, especially families who live closer to Henderson than to the Strip and go to Silver Knights games because the drive is short and the tickets cost less.

Beyond tickets, the Golden Knights say the app will carry exclusive offers and rewards for fans. The team has not spelled out a full list of what those look like week to week, and we are not going to guess at it here. What we can say is that the team is positioning the app as more than a ticket wallet.

A few things are worth saying plainly because we do not have them confirmed. We are not listing app store ratings, download counts, or a feature checklist beyond what the team stated. If you want the exact details for your account, the team's own channels are the place to check.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Here is the practical version. If you hold tickets to a Golden Knights home game or a Silver Knights home game, you need the new app to get in. That is not a nice to have. That is the door.

Think about how a normal game night goes in this valley. You get off work, you fight traffic on the 215 or the 15, you park, and you walk up to the gate with a phone that is at 12 percent battery. That is the worst possible moment to discover that the app you used last season is not the app you need this season. The fix takes a couple of minutes at home. It takes a lot longer standing in a line with a kid on your hip.

The two arena angle is the real local story. Henderson families have been quietly building a hockey routine around Lee's Family Forum for a few seasons now. Silver Knights games are cheaper, the building is smaller, and for a family in Green Valley, Anthem, or Inspirada the drive is nothing compared to a Friday night run to the Strip. Plenty of households do both: a handful of Silver Knights games through the winter, plus a Golden Knights game or two as a bigger night out.

Until now, following both teams meant juggling. Different apps, different logins, different ticket transfers. Putting both under one toggle is a small thing on paper and a real convenience in practice, especially for the parent who is the designated ticket holder for a group of four families.

There is also a sharing angle. Ticket transfers are how season tickets actually work in this town. People split plans, hand games to coworkers, or send a pair to the neighbor who watched the dog. When the transfer flow moves into a new app, everyone in that chain needs to be on the same page. If you are the person who sends tickets out, you are also now the person fielding texts from people who cannot find them. Get ahead of it.

Renters and new arrivals feel this differently than longtime season ticket holders. If you moved here in the last year and bought single game tickets for the first time, you may not have any of the old habits to unlearn. That is actually the easier position. Download the current app, log in, and you are done. The people most likely to hit a snag are the ones who have been doing this the same way since 2018 and assume nothing changed.

There is a cost angle too. Hockey is one of the more affordable ways to see pro sports in this town, especially in Henderson, and the valley does not have unlimited affordable family outings. Anything that makes the ticket side less confusing keeps that option easy to use. Confusion at the gate is the kind of small friction that quietly turns a night out into a hassle people stop repeating.

One more thing for people new to the valley. Hockey is a genuine neighborhood institution in Southern Nevada now, not a novelty. Youth programs feed it, both arenas fill for it, and game nights shape traffic patterns around the Strip and around Henderson. If you just moved here, this is one of those small local systems worth learning early.

Background and History

The Golden Knights arrived in 2017 and changed how this city spends a weeknight. Before that, Southern Nevada had no major league team of its own. Since then the sports map has filled in fast, and hockey has been the anchor of it.

The Henderson piece came later. The Silver Knights gave the organization an affiliate that plays inside the same metro area, which is unusual in pro hockey. Most NHL clubs have their minor league team in another state, sometimes several states away. Here, the pipeline is a short drive down the 215. A player can get called up in the morning and be on the ice at T-Mobile Arena that night.

That closeness is exactly why a shared app makes sense. The organization already treats the two teams as one connected operation, from coaching to development to community events. The fan base overlaps heavily. A single app with a toggle just matches what was already true about how people follow these teams.

The wider shift is mobile ticketing itself. Paper tickets are largely gone from major venues. Digital entry cut down on counterfeits and made transfers instant, but it also put the whole night on your phone. That is why an app change is not a minor technical footnote anymore. When the app changes, the experience of walking into the building changes with it.

It is worth remembering how fast this all came together. A city that had zero major league franchises a decade ago now has hockey at two levels in two buildings, plus the other pro teams that followed. Neighborhoods reorganized around it. Restaurants near both arenas built their weeks around game nights, and families built traditions around them. An app update is a small chapter in that story, but it is part of the same arc: the everyday machinery of being a fan here keeps getting more built out.

Venue names in this valley change often enough that it is worth keeping them straight. Lee's Family Forum in Henderson has carried more than one name since it opened, which is why you will still hear locals use the old one. When you are searching for your tickets or your parking pass, search by the team and the date rather than by the building name, and you will have an easier time.

What Happens Next

The short term is simple. Preseason is underway, the regular season follows, and Silver Knights home dates fill in the calendar through the winter. Every one of those home games at either arena runs through the new app for entry.

Expect the usual pattern for a brand new app. There will be updates in the first several weeks as the team works through the issues that only show up when tens of thousands of people use something at once. Turn on automatic updates, or at least check for one the day before a game. An out of date version is one of the most common reasons a ticket will not load at a gate.

Watch for the offers and rewards piece to take shape. The team has said it will be there. What it actually includes, how often it refreshes, and whether it is tied to attendance or purchases is not something we can confirm today, so do not make plans around it yet. When the details are public, they will come from the team.

If you are a season ticket holder, expect direct communication from the team's ticketing side about your account and your transfers. That will be the most reliable guidance for your specific situation. Group buyers and plan splitters should assume they will need to walk a few people through the new flow.

It is also fair to expect the app to keep changing through the season. That is how these products work now. Features get added, screens get rearranged, and the version you learn in October may look a little different by February. None of that is a problem as long as you keep the app updated and know where your tickets live. The one habit worth building is checking the app the morning of a game rather than the minute you park.

The bigger picture is that the valley keeps adding reasons to be downtown, on the Strip, and in Henderson on a weeknight. The PWHL is bringing pro women's hockey to Henderson this fall. Youth programs keep growing. More game nights across more buildings means more traffic to plan around and more households learning which arena is their arena.

Ryan's Take

I sell houses in this valley, and I will tell you that sports nights come up in buyer conversations more than people expect. Not as a deal breaker, but as a quality of life question. Someone relocating from the Midwest will ask how far they are from T-Mobile Arena. A family looking in Henderson will ask about the Silver Knights and whether a school night game is realistic. Those are legitimate questions about how you actually live in a place.

What I like about the two arena setup is that it gives families a range. Not every hockey night has to be a Strip night with Strip parking and Strip prices. Henderson gives you a version that fits a Tuesday. That flexibility is part of why the southeast valley has held its appeal for families, and it is one of the things I point out when buyers are comparing Henderson to the northwest side.

On the app itself, my advice is boring and practical. Download it now, log in now, and confirm your tickets are visible now, while you are sitting on your couch. Then screenshot nothing and assume nothing, because screenshots of digital tickets do not scan. Every year people learn that the hard way in a line. Do not be that person at the home opener.

What You Can Do

Start with the download. Go to your phone's app store, search for the Vegas Golden Knights app, and install the new version. If you had an older version, make sure it actually updated rather than sitting on the old build. Then open it and sign in with the account tied to your tickets.

Once you are in, do three checks. Confirm your tickets appear for the games you hold. Find the Silver Knights toggle and confirm it shows any Henderson games you have. And walk through where the transfer button lives, so you are not hunting for it at 6:40 on a game night. If something does not look right, that is the moment to contact the team's ticketing support, not the night of.

A few game day habits help regardless of the app. Charge your phone before you leave, because a dead battery is a dead ticket. Add the tickets to your phone's wallet if the app offers that option. Arrive a little earlier than usual for the first game of the season while everyone else is figuring out the same new system. And if you are bringing people, send their tickets a day ahead rather than in the parking lot.

Finally, confirm details with the team directly. Schedules, entry rules, parking, and account questions all change, and the Golden Knights are the only source that can tell you exactly what applies to your seats. Use their official channels rather than a secondhand post.
