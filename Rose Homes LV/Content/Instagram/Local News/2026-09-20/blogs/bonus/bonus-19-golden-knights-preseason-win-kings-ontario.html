The Golden Knights Beat the Kings 4-2 and Gave Us Our First Real Look at the New Roster

The Vegas Golden Knights opened the 2026-27 preseason with a 4-2 win over the Los Angeles Kings on Saturday night, September 19, at Toyota Arena in Ontario, California. The score will be forgotten in a week. What the game actually told us about the bottom of the roster is the part worth keeping.

Preseason results do not count. Nobody hangs a banner for a September win in a building three and a half hours from T-Mobile Arena. But the first exhibition game of the year is the first honest look fans get at the players who are fighting for the last few jobs on an NHL roster, and this one gave Las Vegas plenty to look at. Viliam Kmec scored late in the first period. Raphael Lavoie scored 52 seconds later. Jackson Hallum scored twice, including an empty-netter to close it out. Marc Gatcomb added two assists.

If none of those names are familiar to you yet, that is exactly the point. Read this as a roster preview, not a game recap, and you will get a lot more out of it.

What Happened in Ontario

Vegas played the Kings on Saturday night, September 19, at Toyota Arena in Ontario, California. It was the first game of the 2026-27 preseason for both teams, and it went final 4-2 in favor of the Golden Knights.

The scoring started late in the first period. Viliam Kmec put Vegas on the board, and 52 seconds later Raphael Lavoie made it two. That kind of quick back-to-back sequence is what coaches look for in September. It usually means a line or a pairing got comfortable, found some rhythm, and pushed while the other team was still catching its breath. Two goals in under a minute in a preseason opener is not an accident.

Jackson Hallum handled the rest. He scored twice, and the second one was an empty-netter, which is a tidy way to end a night when your team is protecting a lead and the other side pulls the goalie. Two goals in your first exhibition game of the year is the kind of performance that gets a young player another shift, another game, and another look from the people making decisions.

Marc Gatcomb added two assists. Assists in preseason games tend to get overlooked because the goal scorer gets the headline, but a two-assist night from a player trying to make a roster is meaningful. It says he was on the ice when good things happened, more than once, and that he was a reason they happened.

That is the entire verified box of details from this game, and it is enough. Four goals, four different contributors, and a group of young players who did not look out of place against an NHL opponent. The Golden Knights play at San Jose on Tuesday, September 22, before coming home.

One more thing about the venue is worth noting, because it explains why a lot of local fans could not watch this one live. Toyota Arena is in Ontario, California, about three and a half hours from Las Vegas on a good traffic day. NHL teams often open the preseason in a neutral or secondary building to spread the sport around and to give their younger groups a lower-pressure environment. It is a normal scheduling choice, but it does mean the first game of the year happened outside the market. The home portion starts this week.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Here is the practical reason a Las Vegas family should care about a September exhibition game played in California: these are the players you are going to be watching all year, whether you realize it yet or not.

The Golden Knights organization runs a two-city pipeline. The NHL team plays at T-Mobile Arena. The AHL affiliate, the Henderson Silver Knights, plays in Henderson. Players move between the two all season long, especially in a year with a long schedule and a shortened camp. The kids who look good in September are the kids who get the call in January when somebody gets hurt. Knowing their names now means the mid-season call-up is not a mystery to you later.

It also matters because hockey in this valley has stopped being a novelty and started being infrastructure. There is an NHL team on the Strip, an AHL team in Henderson, a practice facility in Summerlin that is open to the public, and youth programs feeding into all of it. That is not a tourist attraction. That is a set of amenities that families use, and families make housing decisions around the amenities they actually use.

The most useful thing in this story for a local is not the score at all. It is the calendar. The Golden Knights host two preseason home games at T-Mobile Arena, September 24 against the Utah Mammoth and September 26 against the San Jose Sharks, both at 7 p.m. Pacific. Those are real NHL games in your own city at preseason prices, with young players skating hard because their jobs depend on it. For a lot of families, a preseason game is the most affordable way to get kids into an NHL building all year.

And if even that is more than you want to spend, the on-ice sessions at City National Arena, 1550 S. Pavilion Center Dr. in Summerlin, are free and open to the public. That is the part most people in this valley still do not know. You can walk into an NHL practice facility, sit down, and watch professional hockey players work, for nothing.

There is a second effect that shows up in neighborhoods rather than in box scores. Every year a group of young players and their families land in Clark County for a few months, sometimes a few weeks, while camp and the early season sort itself out. They rent. They enroll kids in schools. They shop and eat in the neighborhoods near the rinks. It is a small piece of the local rental picture, but it is a real one, and it is one more reason professional hockey here functions as an economic fixture and not just entertainment.

For homeowners, none of this is a reason to buy or sell anything. It is context. Summerlin and Henderson both market themselves partly on amenities that regular families can reach on a weekday, and two professional hockey facilities with public access qualify. When you are explaining your neighborhood to somebody moving here from out of state, that is a concrete thing to point at.

Background and How the Roster Got Here

This September is unusual, and the reason shows up in how the preseason lineups are being built. General manager Kelly McCrimmon said the 2026 camp was shortened by eight days, which creates real urgency to install systems before an 84-game regular season begins. Less practice time and more games is a hard combination for any coaching staff.

McCrimmon also laid out how the four preseason games would be used. Veterans can play in only two of the four. Younger lineups go early, and the projected opening-night group gets featured in games three and four. That explains exactly what Las Vegas saw in Ontario. Game one was the young group. The names on the scoresheet were not accidental, they were the plan.

Camp opened with 66 players: 36 forwards, 23 defensemen, and 7 goaltenders. Opening practices began Thursday, September 17, at City National Arena in Summerlin. All 66 are participating except Ben Hemmerling, who was injured at the San Jose rookie tournament, and Lukas Cormier, who had offseason surgery and is practicing in a non-contact jersey.

Sixty-six players and 23 roster spots means most of the people who were on the ice Saturday night will not be in Las Vegas when the regular season starts. Some go to Henderson. Some go back to junior or to Europe. That math is what makes a preseason opener genuinely interesting to watch. Everybody out there knows the numbers.

There is also a recent example of how this path works. Braeden Bowman came up through the Henderson Silver Knights, put up 62 points in 88 AHL games, was named AHL Player of the Month for April 2026, and then signed a six-year extension with Vegas at $3.4 million per year. He was a name on a preseason roster once too. That is the template every young player in Ontario on Saturday is chasing.

What Happens Next

The schedule from here is short and busy. Vegas plays at San Jose on Tuesday, September 22. That is the second of the four preseason games, so expect another look at the younger group before the veterans take over.

Then the team comes home. T-Mobile Arena hosts the Utah Mammoth on Thursday, September 24, at 7 p.m. Pacific, and the San Jose Sharks on Saturday, September 26, at 7 p.m. Pacific. Based on how McCrimmon described the plan, those two home games are where the projected opening-night lineup gets featured. If you want to see the actual team, those are the nights.

The shortened camp changes what those final games are for. In a normal year a coaching staff has extra practice days to walk through systems at half speed. This year the games themselves carry more of that load, which means the last two preseason nights function as full dress rehearsals rather than as auditions. That is good news for anyone buying a ticket, because it means the hockey should look closer to the real thing.

Between now and then, on-ice sessions continue at City National Arena in Summerlin, and they remain free to attend. Practice times are posted by the team, and they can shift, so check before you drive out there. Weekday sessions tend to be calmer than weekend ones if you are bringing small kids.

After the preseason ends, cuts happen fast. Players get reassigned to Henderson, and the opening-night roster gets set. Watch for those transaction announcements, because that is when you find out which of Saturday night's scorers stuck. Anyone paying attention to this week's games will recognize the names in those announcements, which makes the whole season easier to follow.

Ryan's Take

I have watched this valley change around hockey for almost a decade now, and the thing that still surprises people who move here is how deep it goes. They expect a Strip attraction. What they find is a practice facility in Summerlin with open doors, a second professional team in Henderson, youth leagues with waitlists, and neighbors who talk about prospects by name in September.

From a housing standpoint, that matters more than people assume. When a buyer asks me what there is to do on the west side, City National Arena is a real answer, not a filler answer. Same with Henderson and the AHL team. Amenities that a family uses more than twice a year are the ones that hold value, and a free NHL practice you can walk into on a Tuesday morning is exactly that kind of amenity. It shows up in how people describe their neighborhood, which eventually shows up in what they are willing to pay to live there.

The other thing I would say is this: do not skip the preseason because it does not count. A September game is the cheapest and calmest way to introduce a kid to a live NHL building. Smaller crowd, shorter lines, lower ticket price, and the hockey is honest because the players are fighting for something. I would rather take a family to a preseason game than to a February Saturday sellout, and I say that as somebody who likes the sellouts.

What You Can Do

If you want to see this team before the regular season starts, you have two easy options this week and both of them are local.

The first is the home preseason schedule. T-Mobile Arena hosts Utah on Thursday, September 24, and San Jose on Saturday, September 26, both at 7 p.m. Pacific. Tickets are handled through the team's official channels, and the Golden Knights recently launched a new mobile app that includes full ticket management and entry for home games at both T-Mobile Arena and the Henderson arena. If you hold tickets to games in both cities, that one app now covers you.

The second is free. On-ice sessions at City National Arena, 1550 S. Pavilion Center Dr. in Summerlin, are open to the public at no cost. Go early, because parking fills up faster than people expect, and bring a jacket. It is colder in there than the temperature outside would ever suggest. Kids get close enough to hear the skates cut and the pucks hit the boards, which is something television cannot reproduce.

If you have kids who show any interest after watching, the youth programs in this valley are worth a phone call. Clark County has house leagues, travel programs, and learn-to-skate sessions running out of several rinks, and most of them have a low-commitment entry option so you can find out whether a kid actually likes it before you buy equipment. Parents who have been through it will tell you the gear is the expensive part, and that used gear is completely normal.

Beyond that, follow the roster moves over the next two weeks. When the reassignments to Henderson come out, you will recognize the names from Saturday night, and you will know where to go watch them next. Hockey in Clark County is a two-city sport now, and the people who follow both teams get twice the season.
