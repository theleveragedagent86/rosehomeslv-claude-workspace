A Free Glow-in-the-Dark Bike Ride Opens Halloween Season at Craig Ranch

The City of North Las Vegas is kicking off Halloween season with a free glow-in-the-dark family bike ride called Glowtoberfest, Gears and Ghouls, on Friday, October 2, 2026. Check-in starts at 5:30 p.m. at the Garden Plaza inside Craig Ranch Regional Park, 628 W. Craig Road, the ride rolls at 6:30 p.m., and the whole event wraps up around 8 p.m.

This is a partnership between the city, the RTC, and Club Ride, and it comes with free glow-in-the-dark items, prizes for the best decorated bikes and costumes, food trucks, and technicians on site checking bikes and helmets before anyone rolls out. It is a no-cost way to spend a Friday night as a family, and it sets up the Craig Ranch Pumpkin Patch, which opens October 9.

What Is Happening at Craig Ranch on October 2

Glowtoberfest, Gears and Ghouls, is a family bike ride organized by the City of North Las Vegas, the Regional Transportation Commission of Southern Nevada, and Club Ride. It happens on Friday, October 2, 2026, at the Garden Plaza inside Craig Ranch Regional Park, which sits at 628 W. Craig Road in North Las Vegas. The timeline is straightforward. Check-in opens at 5:30 p.m., giving riders and families time to get decked out in glow gear and get their bikes looked over before the group heads out. The ride itself rolls at 6:30 p.m., once the sun has gone down and the glow-in-the-dark theme actually shows up the way it is supposed to. The full event wraps around 8 p.m.

The name says a lot about the tone. Gears and Ghouls means this is not just a plain community ride, it is dressed up for the season. There are prizes for the best decorated bikes and the best costumes, so families who want to go all in on lights, streamers, and Halloween themes have a reason to put in the effort. Free glow-in-the-dark items are handed out for anyone who wants to light up their bike, helmet, or outfit for the ride.

Food trucks will be on site, which means this can double as dinner. Bring the family, eat at the park, get your bike checked, and then join the ride once it gets dark. Technicians will be checking bikes and helmets before the ride starts, which is a real safety feature and not just decoration. A quick check can catch a loose brake or a helmet that does not fit right before it becomes a problem on the road.

One rule to know ahead of time: motorized vehicles and e-bikes are not permitted in North Las Vegas parks. If your household relies on an e-bike for family rides, plan to bring a standard pedal bike for this one instead, since the rule applies inside the park regardless of the event.

The Garden Plaza location matters for logistics too. It gives families a defined spot to gather, meet up with friends, and stage their bikes before the ride starts, rather than trying to organize a check-in across a sprawling open field. A plaza setting also makes it easier for food trucks to park in a predictable spot, and for the technicians running bike and helmet checks to set up a clear line so families are not waiting around wondering where to go. For a first-time attendee, that kind of layout takes a lot of the guesswork out of showing up to a new event.

Because the ride itself starts at 6:30 p.m. in early October, families should expect the early part of check-in to still be in daylight, with dusk settling in by the time the group actually rolls out. That timing is intentional. It gives everyone enough natural light to get organized, find their friends, and get glow gear attached to bikes and costumes, while still delivering a true after-dark ride once the group is moving. By the time riders finish around 8 p.m., North Las Vegas will be fully dark, which is exactly when the glow-in-the-dark theme looks its best.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Free family events are always worth flagging, but a free event that happens after sunset and is built around lights and costumes is a specific kind of win for families in the north valley. Kids get excited about Halloween well before October 31 actually arrives, and Glowtoberfest gives them an early outlet for that energy that does not cost a family anything beyond the gas to get to the park.

For North Las Vegas residents specifically, this is programming that happens close to home. Craig Ranch Regional Park is one of the largest and most used parks in the city, and events that draw families into it on a Friday night are good for the neighborhood in a few ways. They give people a reason to be outside together, they put more eyes and more activity into a public space in the evening, and they build the kind of casual community connection that does not happen when everyone is just driving past the park on their way somewhere else.

There is also a practical safety angle that should not get lost in the fun. Getting free bike and helmet checks from actual technicians is a real service. A lot of families have bikes sitting in the garage that have not been looked at in a year or more, and a loose chain, worn brake pads, or an improperly fitted helmet are the kind of small issues that only get noticed when something goes wrong. Having someone check that equipment for free, right before a ride, is a meaningful benefit on top of the entertainment.

For parents specifically, an event like this is also a low-stakes way to introduce kids to riding as a group in a controlled setting, with an organized route and other families around, rather than just riding around the neighborhood on their own. That kind of structured, supervised group ride can build confidence for kids who are still getting comfortable on two wheels.

It also matters that this event costs nothing. Halloween season in Las Vegas comes with plenty of paid options, from haunted houses to ticketed festivals, and those add up fast for a family with more than one kid. A free night that still delivers costumes, prizes, food trucks, and an organized activity gives families a real alternative that does not force a choice between having a fun October and staying on budget. For households in North Las Vegas who might otherwise skip the pricier seasonal events entirely, Glowtoberfest fills that gap directly.

Finally, this kind of event builds a habit. A family that comes out for Glowtoberfest and has a good experience is more likely to come back for the Craig Ranch Pumpkin Patch the following week, and more likely to check the city's parks and recreation calendar for the next free event after that. Cities benefit from that kind of repeat engagement just as much as families do, since it turns a single night out into an ongoing relationship between residents and the public spaces the city maintains for them.

Background and History

Craig Ranch Regional Park has become one of the anchor public spaces in North Las Vegas, and it regularly hosts city-run programming throughout the year. The park's size and layout, including open plaza space like the Garden Plaza where this event is centered, make it a natural fit for gatherings that need room for a crowd to check in, mill around food trucks, and then head out together as a group.

The partnership behind this event is worth noting too. The Regional Transportation Commission of Southern Nevada, known as the RTC, works on transit and active transportation projects across the valley, including bike infrastructure. Club Ride brings organizing experience for group rides. Pairing those two groups with the City of North Las Vegas's parks and recreation programming is what turns a simple idea, a nighttime bike ride, into an event with real logistics behind it: a defined check-in window, safety checks, a planned route, and a clear start and end time.

Glow-themed night rides have become a familiar format in cities around the country over the past several years, largely because they solve a real problem. Daytime bike events in Las Vegas can be brutal for much of the year because of the heat, and even by early October, afternoons are still warm. Moving a ride to the evening, after the sun goes down, makes it far more comfortable for families with young kids, and the glow lights add a layer of fun and, frankly, visibility that a typical daytime group ride does not have.

This event also lines up with the broader fall programming push across North Las Vegas parks. The Craig Ranch Pumpkin Patch opens the following week, on October 9, which means Glowtoberfest effectively functions as the opening night of the fall and Halloween season at the park. Families who enjoy the bike ride now have a reason to come back a week later for the pumpkin patch, and the city gets two strong turnout events back to back at the same location.

Free bike and helmet safety checks tied to community rides are also becoming a more common feature at these kinds of events nationally, and for good reason. Bike shops often charge for a basic tune-up, and not every family budgets for that kind of maintenance every year. Building a safety check directly into a fun, free event removes the cost barrier and gets more people to actually have their equipment looked at, rather than putting it off indefinitely. It is a small piece of the puzzle, but it is the kind of detail that quietly makes an event more useful than it might first appear.

What Happens Next

The event is set for Friday, October 2, 2026, with check-in at 5:30 p.m. and the ride rolling at 6:30 p.m. Registration is required and available at cityofnorthlasvegas.com/glowtoberfest. Families planning to attend should register ahead of time rather than assuming they can just show up, since organized group rides typically use registration numbers to plan routes, staffing, and how much glow gear and food to have on hand.

After Glowtoberfest wraps around 8 p.m., the next date to have on the calendar for the same park is October 9, when the Craig Ranch Pumpkin Patch opens. Families who enjoy this ride and want to keep the momentum going into the rest of Halloween season have a built-in second trip to the same location just one week later.

As with any scheduled outdoor city event, details can shift closer to the date, whether that is weather, route changes, or staffing. Confirm the specifics on the official registration page before you head out, especially start times and whether any changes have been made to check-in procedures.

What You Can Do

If you want to go, register ahead of time at cityofnorthlasvegas.com/glowtoberfest. Plan to arrive by 5:30 p.m. for check-in so your bike and helmet can be looked over before the 6:30 p.m. ride start, and remember that motorized vehicles and e-bikes are not allowed inside North Las Vegas parks, so bring a standard pedal bike.

Bring the kids something to decorate their bikes or costumes with if you want a shot at the prizes for best decorated bike and best costume, and plan to grab dinner from one of the food trucks on site rather than eating beforehand. If your family bikes have not been checked or tuned in a while, this is a good, free opportunity to get that done before the ride even starts.

Confirm all details, including check-in time and location, on the official city registration page before you head out, since event logistics can be updated as the date gets closer.
