Two Free Concerts Are Coming to Centennial Hills Park Amphitheatre This Fall

The City of Las Vegas is bringing two free outdoor concerts to the Centennial Hills Park Amphitheatre at 7101 N. Buffalo Drive this fall, one on Saturday, September 26, and one on Saturday, October 3, both starting at 7 p.m. No tickets, no cost, and no reason to drive across town when the show is right in your own neighborhood.

This is part of the city's free outdoor fall concert series, and it lands squarely in the northwest valley, an area that does not always get top billing when people talk about Las Vegas entertainment. Grab a low-back chair or a blanket and plan on a relaxed Saturday night under the stars.

What Is Happening and When

The lineup covers two very different nights of music. On Saturday, September 26, at 7 p.m., Tom Stevens takes the amphitheatre stage for "Bennett: The Good Life, A Tribute to Tony." It is a 90-minute set built around 27 songs from the Tony Bennett songbook. For anyone who grew up with that voice on the radio or has just always loved the great American songbook, this is a rare chance to hear that catalog performed live and outdoors, close to home, for free.

One week later, on Saturday, October 3, also at 7 p.m., the amphitheatre hosts Suika T and Band, an eight-piece Latin fusion group. Where the Tony Bennett tribute is a night of classic standards, this show is built for movement and energy, blending Latin rhythms with a full band arrangement. Back to back Saturdays gives northwest valley residents two completely different musical experiences within the same short walk or short drive from home.

Both shows are free and open to the public, and both are at the same location, the Centennial Hills Park Amphitheatre, 7101 N. Buffalo Drive. The city recommends bringing a low-back chair or a blanket to sit on the lawn, since this is an outdoor amphitheatre setting rather than reserved seating. Refreshments will be sold on site, so you do not need to plan a whole meal around the evening, though you are welcome to eat beforehand if you would rather.

There are two rules worth knowing before you go. No pets are allowed at the venue, and no glass containers are permitted. If you are bringing drinks from home, use plastic or cans. Beyond that, this is meant to be an easy, low-key evening out. Show up, find a spot on the grass, and enjoy the music.

A 90-minute set built around 27 songs is a substantial evening of music, not a short opening act tacked onto something else. That kind of run time means the September 26 show is really the main event of the night, and the same is true of the eight-piece band arrangement on October 3. An eight-piece group is a full band, with horns, percussion, and vocals working together rather than a single act with a backing track. Both nights are built to stand on their own as a real concert experience, not a background performance you half pay attention to while doing something else.

Parking and arrival planning are worth a mention too. Community park amphitheatres like this one typically fill in from the lawn area near the stage outward, so arriving with enough time to find a comfortable spot before the show starts at 7 p.m. is a good idea, especially if you want to sit together as a group. Since seating is general admission on the grass rather than assigned, getting there a little early is the only real strategy you need.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Free is doing a lot of work in this story. A family night out in this town can add up fast between tickets, parking, and food, and that adds up even faster once you are talking about live music. A free concert series removes that barrier completely. You can bring the kids, bring the grandparents, bring a group of neighbors, and the only real cost is whatever you spend on refreshments once you get there.

For residents of Centennial Hills, Skye Canyon, Aliante, and the surrounding northwest neighborhoods, this also solves a problem that comes up a lot in this part of the valley. A lot of the big, well known entertainment in Las Vegas sits on or near the Strip, which means a 30 or 40 minute drive each way for anyone living out here. A concert series that happens inside your own community park means you do not need to plan your whole evening around traffic and parking downtown.

It also says something about how the northwest valley is developing as a place to live, not just a place to commute from. Centennial Hills Park already functions as a gathering point for the surrounding neighborhoods, with its amphitheatre, open space, and recreational facilities. Programming like this two-week concert series turns that park from a nice amenity on a map into a place where actual community life happens on a Saturday night.

There is a real appeal here across generations too. The Tony Bennett tribute on September 26 is likely to draw an older crowd nostalgic for that music, while the Latin fusion show on October 3 pulls in a younger, more dance-oriented audience. Together, the two nights give the northwest valley a broader cultural offering than a single genre concert would, and that kind of variety is part of what makes a neighborhood feel complete.

Consider what a night like this replaces for a lot of families. Instead of a screen-heavy evening at home, or a long drive to find something to do, a household in Centennial Hills or Skye Canyon can walk out the door, drive a few minutes, and be sitting on the lawn with neighbors before the sun even fully sets. That kind of low-friction outing is exactly what gets used, week after week, in a way that a once-a-year ticketed event never quite manages. Free, local, and recurring beats expensive and far away almost every time when it comes to what a family actually does on a given Saturday.

It is also worth pointing out that this kind of programming tends to build on itself. A well-attended concert series encourages the city to keep funding similar events, which in turn gives residents another reason to think of their own park as a destination rather than just green space between subdivisions. Over time, that is how a neighborhood park becomes part of a community's identity instead of just an amenity listed on a real estate flyer.

Background and History

The Centennial Hills Park Amphitheatre has become one of the anchor gathering spaces for the northwest valley. The broader Centennial Hills Park complex includes sports fields, playgrounds, walking paths, and the amphitheatre itself, which the City of Las Vegas uses throughout the year for community programming. An outdoor amphitheatre is a fairly efficient piece of civic infrastructure. Once it is built, it can host everything from concerts to movie nights to community meetings, all without the overhead of an indoor venue.

Free outdoor concert series are a long-running tradition for cities looking to activate their parks, and Las Vegas has leaned into that idea for years, spreading programming across different parts of the valley rather than concentrating everything in one central location. Putting a concert series in Centennial Hills specifically acknowledges that the northwest valley has grown into a substantial, established community with its own population base that deserves the same kind of city-run entertainment that other parts of the valley already get.

Fall is also simply the right season for this. Outdoor evening events in Las Vegas are much more comfortable once the calendar turns past Labor Day. September and October evenings cool down enough to make a lawn chair and a two-hour concert genuinely pleasant, which is exactly why so much of the valley's outdoor event calendar clusters into these months. A 7 p.m. start time in late September and early October means the worst of the daytime heat is already behind you by the time the music starts.

The choice of artists also reflects a deliberate attempt to reach more than one type of resident. A tribute act built around a legendary American songbook performer speaks to longtime valley residents and retirees who remember that music from decades of radio and television. An eight-piece Latin fusion band speaks to a younger, increasingly Latino population that has grown steadily across the northwest valley in recent years. Programming both genres within the same short concert series, at the same venue, in the same neighborhood, is a small but meaningful way for the city to serve the actual makeup of the community rather than a single demographic.

What Happens Next

The two dates to have on your calendar are Saturday, September 26, and Saturday, October 3, both at 7 p.m. at the Centennial Hills Park Amphitheatre. Between now and then, the main thing to do is plan your evening, decide who is coming with you, and figure out what you want to bring to sit on.

Confirm the details before you head out. Event details for city-run programming can shift, whether that is a small schedule adjustment or a weather-related change, so it is worth checking the City of Las Vegas parks and recreation channels the day of or the day before each show. That is a five-minute step that saves you a wasted drive if anything changes.

If these two concerts go well, it would not be surprising to see the city continue or expand this kind of free outdoor programming at Centennial Hills Park Amphitheatre in future seasons. Turnout at events like this tends to shape whether a city keeps investing in that kind of programming for a given park, so showing up is itself a small vote for more of this in your neighborhood going forward.

Between the two Saturdays, there is nothing else you need to plan for or register for in advance. There are no tickets to reserve and no RSVP required. That simplicity is part of the appeal, but it also means the details that do exist, the date, the time, and the location, are the ones worth confirming again right before you go, since those are the only things that could realistically change.

What You Can Do

Put both Saturdays on the calendar, September 26 for the Tony Bennett tribute and October 3 for the Latin fusion show, both at 7 p.m. Bring a low-back chair or a blanket, since seating is on the lawn rather than in fixed rows. Leave the pets and the glass containers at home, since neither is allowed at the venue, and plan to buy refreshments on site if you want a snack or a drink during the show.

If you have neighbors in Centennial Hills, Skye Canyon, or Aliante who might not have heard about this yet, this is an easy one to pass along. Free, close to home, and family friendly covers most of what people are looking for in a Saturday night plan. Before you go, double check the current details through the City of Las Vegas so you know the show is still on as scheduled.

If you can only make one of the two nights, think about which crowd you want to be part of. The September 26 tribute leans toward a quieter, more seated audience settling in for a full set of classic songs. The October 3 Latin fusion show leans toward a livelier, more social crowd that may end up on their feet by the end of the night. Either way, both events are free, both are close to home for a huge stretch of the northwest valley, and both are the kind of low-key plan that is easy to say yes to on a Saturday.
