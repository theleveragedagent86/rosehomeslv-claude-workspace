CCSD Police Arrested a Rex Bell Elementary Teacher on Child Abuse and Battery Charges

Clark County School District Police announced on Wednesday, September 16, 2026, that a 54 year old elementary school teacher was arrested on child abuse and neglect and battery charges tied to an investigation at Rex Bell Elementary School in Las Vegas. According to KTNV 13, the teacher, identified as Dina Fanella, was arrested on September 1 and booked into the Clark County Detention Center, and she has been placed on paid leave.

Before you read another word, here is the part that matters most. These are charges, not convictions. Nothing in this case has been decided by a court. Every detail below comes from CCSD Police or from KTNV's reporting on the announcement, and a person facing charges is presumed innocent until a court says otherwise.

This post is not about guessing what happened inside a classroom. It is about the part parents in Clark County can actually act on: how the district handles an arrest of one of its own employees, how long the public waits to hear about it, and what a family should do when the school in question is the one their kid walks into every morning.

What Happened

CCSD Police released the information on Wednesday, September 16, 2026. KTNV 13 reported that the department said Dina Fanella, 54, was arrested on September 1, 2026, and booked into the Clark County Detention Center on charges of child abuse and neglect and battery.

The investigation began in August at Rex Bell Elementary School, which is where the teacher was last assigned, according to the department's announcement as reported by KTNV. The station reported that she was initially cited, and that the charges were later amended to the ones she was booked on. That sequence, a citation first and amended charges later, is a normal part of how a case can develop once investigators finish their work and a prosecutor reviews the file.

KTNV also reported that Fanella has worked for the Clark County School District since 2006, which puts her roughly two decades into a career with the district. The district placed her on paid leave. Paid leave is the standard status for a CCSD employee who is under investigation, and it is an administrative step, not a finding of guilt.

That is the full set of confirmed facts. We are not going to go past it. CCSD Police did not publicly detail the alleged conduct in the reporting available, and no student involved has been identified or described. We will not speculate about what the allegations involve, we will not name or hint at any child, and we will not fill in gaps with guesses. Anything you may see circulating on social media that adds detail beyond what CCSD Police released should be treated as unverified.

KTNV noted in its coverage that this is the second CCSD teacher arrest the station has reported in recent months. That single line is what turned a campus story into a district-wide conversation, because parents naturally start asking whether they are seeing a pattern or simply seeing more reporting on cases that were always handled the same way.

It is worth being precise about that question, because the honest answer is that two reported arrests do not tell you whether anything has changed. CCSD employs tens of thousands of people across more than 300 schools. Without a multi year count of arrests and dispositions from the district, nobody can responsibly say arrests are up or down. Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

For families zoned to Rex Bell Elementary, this stopped being a news story the second they read the school's name. The first reaction is always the same. Parents check the campus name, check the grade level, and then try to figure out whether their child had any contact with the staff member involved.

The second reaction is about timing. The arrest happened on September 1. The public announcement came on September 16. That is roughly two weeks between the booking and the moment most parents learned anything. For a lot of families, that gap is the story. They want to know who knew what during those two weeks and what, if anything, the school communicated to parents directly in that window. There is a broader civic point here that applies far beyond one campus. CCSD runs its own police department. That is unusual for a school district and it gives CCSD its own investigators, its own arrest authority, and its own decisions about when to release information to the public. Most Clark County residents do not realize their school district operates a full police agency until a story like this puts it in a headline. How that agency communicates, and how fast, is a legitimate public accountability issue that residents get to weigh in on.

It also matters to families making housing decisions, and I am going to be careful about how I say this. School quality is one of the top factors buyers ask me about, and it should be. But a single arrest at a single campus is not a measure of school quality, and treating it like one is a mistake. Schools are large organizations staffed by hundreds of adults. One person facing charges does not tell you whether the teaching is good, whether the principal is effective, or whether your kid will thrive there.

What a story like this should do is remind families to look at real, repeatable information when they evaluate a school. Nevada's star ratings, proficiency data, teacher retention, class sizes, and the school's own safety reporting are all public. Those are the things that actually describe a campus year over year. A single news cycle is not.

Background and History

Rex Bell Elementary School is a Clark County School District elementary campus in Las Vegas. Like every CCSD school, it sits inside a district that is one of the largest public school systems in the country, serving roughly 300,000 students across Clark County.

Scale is the piece of context most people skip. A district that size employs a workforce comparable to a mid sized city's entire government. Any organization with tens of thousands of employees will, over time, have employees who are arrested. That is true of hospitals, casinos, airlines, and police departments. It is not a defense of anything, and it is not a dismissal of a serious allegation. It is simply the reason a single case cannot be read as a verdict on an entire district.

The structure that matters here is CCSD's own police department. The district operates a sworn police force whose officers investigate crimes on district property and involving district employees and students. When something is reported at a campus, CCSD Police can investigate it directly rather than handing it to Las Vegas Metro or Henderson Police. That is why the announcement in this case came from CCSD Police and not from a city agency.

The employment side runs on a separate track from the criminal side, and the two do not move at the same speed. When a CCSD employee is under investigation, the district can place that person on leave immediately. That is an administrative decision the district makes on its own. The criminal case, meanwhile, moves through the Clark County justice system on its own timeline, through charging decisions, court dates, and eventually either a resolution or a trial. An employee can be cleared in court, or the charges can be dropped, and the employment outcome can still be different. The reverse is also true. Those are two different systems with two different standards of proof.

That separation explains the thing parents find most frustrating: why the district often says very little while a case is open. Districts generally limit what they release about a pending criminal investigation and about personnel matters, both for legal reasons and because saying the wrong thing can damage a case or an employee's rights. It is a real constraint. It is also, understandably, cold comfort to a parent who just wants to know what happened at their kid's school.

What Happens Next

The criminal case moves through the Clark County court system from here. Charges filed after a booking go through the normal steps: an initial appearance, a prosecutor's review, and court dates that get set, continued, and reset over weeks or months. Cases at this stage routinely take a long time to resolve, and the charges a person is booked on are not always the charges that end up filed. Again, nothing here has been decided, and no court has ruled on anything.

On the district side, the employee remains on paid leave for now, per KTNV's reporting. CCSD's next steps on the employment question are an internal personnel process, and districts typically do not narrate those publicly while they are underway. There is no announced date for any district decision in the available reporting. [NOT VERIFIED]

What parents can reasonably watch for is communication from the school itself. When a case involves a specific campus, families at that campus are usually the first to hear about support resources, counseling availability, and any changes to staffing in the affected classroom. If you are a Rex Bell family and you have not received anything, asking the front office directly is the fastest route to a real answer.

The bigger thing to watch is whether this prompts any district level conversation about how quickly CCSD notifies the public and affected families after an employee arrest. Two weeks between a booking and a public announcement is the kind of detail that shows up in public comment at a Board of School Trustees meeting. If enough parents raise it, it becomes an agenda item. If nobody raises it, it does not.

Ryan's Take

I sell real estate in Clark County and I talk to buyers about schools almost every single day. So let me say the thing I would say to a client who called me about this in a panic.

One arrest is not a school rating. It is not a neighborhood rating either. If you are zoned to Rex Bell and you were happy with the school in August, a criminal case involving one adult, a case that has not been adjudicated, does not change what the school is. I would not move a family, pull a kid, or walk away from a home over a headline. I have seen buyers make housing decisions on a single news cycle and regret it a year later when the story turned out to be more complicated than the first report.

What I do think is fair is to use a moment like this as a prompt to actually look at your school. Not the headline, the school. Pull the Nevada Report Card data. Ask the principal about teacher turnover. Ask how the campus communicates with families when something serious happens, because that answer tells you a lot about how the school is run. Those questions will serve you whether or not this particular case ever touches your family.

And be careful with the neighborhood chatter. In a case involving allegations against a specific person, with a child somewhere in the middle of it, the rumor mill does real damage to real people, including people who have not been found guilty of anything. Wait for the court. That is not a soft position. That is just how this is supposed to work.

What You Can Do

If your child attends Rex Bell Elementary, start with the school. Call the front office or email the principal and ask directly whether your child was in any affected classroom and what support the school is offering students. You are entitled to ask, and a direct question to the school gets you a better answer than anything you will find in a Facebook group.

If you want to ask about the district's notification timeline, the Board of School Trustees is where that question belongs. CCSD board meetings include a public comment period, and the meeting schedule and agendas are posted on the district's website at ccsd.net. Public comment is also where parents can push for clearer policies on how fast families are notified after an employee arrest, which is a policy question rather than a question about any one case.

For the criminal case itself, court records in Clark County are public. You can follow the actual docket rather than relying on secondhand summaries, which is the single best way to avoid spreading something that is not true. Court information is available through the Clark County courts' public case search. Watching the docket yourself also keeps your expectations realistic, because court timelines are slow and long stretches with no visible activity are normal rather than a sign that something is being hidden.

It also helps to talk to your kids in an age appropriate way about who they can go to at school when something feels wrong. Every CCSD campus has counselors and administrators, and the district publishes reporting options for families who want to raise a safety concern. A child who knows exactly which adult to find is better protected than a child who only knows that something scary was on the news.

Finally, if you are choosing a school or a neighborhood, use the durable information. Nevada's school star ratings, proficiency scores, and enrollment data are published every year and are far more useful for a housing decision than any single news story.
