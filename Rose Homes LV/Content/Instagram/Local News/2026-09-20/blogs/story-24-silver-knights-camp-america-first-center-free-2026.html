Henderson Silver Knights Camp Opens Monday and Every Practice Is Free to Watch

The Henderson Silver Knights open 2026 training camp Monday, September 21, with the first skate at noon at America First Center, 222 S. Water St. in downtown Henderson. Every practice is open to the public and free to watch, so you can walk in, sit down, and watch pro hockey without paying a dollar.

That is a rare thing in a valley where most live sports cost real money. Camp is also the first time anyone gets to see the team under new head coach Joel Ward, which makes this week more interesting than a normal skate. If you have never been inside the building on Water Street, this is the cheapest excuse you will get all year.

The team announced the full camp schedule on Saturday, September 20. Camp runs into the following weekend, then the Silver Knights close it out with two exhibition games at the Southwest Showdown in Tucson on September 26 and 27. The regular season opens Friday, October 3, at Lee's Family Forum against the Bakersfield Condors.

What Happened

The Henderson Silver Knights released their 2026 training camp practice schedule on September 20. The headline detail for locals is the access. All camp practices at America First Center are open to the public, and there is no ticket and no cost to sit and watch.

The first on-ice session is Monday, September 21, at noon Pacific time. The arena sits at 222 S. Water St., right in the middle of downtown Henderson. That is the Water Street District, the stretch of older downtown that the city has been rebuilding for years with new restaurants, new housing, and public events.

This camp carries more weight than usual because it is the first team activity under new head coach Joel Ward. A coaching change resets a lot of things at this level. Line combinations, defensive pairings, power play looks, and the pecking order for ice time all get sorted out over a handful of practices. Camp is where you actually see that happen in real time instead of reading about it later.

After the camp practices wrap, the Silver Knights travel to Tucson for the Southwest Showdown on September 26 and 27. Those are the two exhibition games that close out the preseason. Then the team comes home and opens the regular season on Friday, October 3, at Lee's Family Forum against the Bakersfield Condors.

One practical note before you make plans. Practice schedules during camp can shift. Times move, sessions get combined, and teams sometimes close a day to the public without much warning. Check the team's schedule the morning of the day you plan to go so you are not driving to Water Street for a rink that is already dark.

It also helps to know what a camp practice actually looks like if you have never sat through one. It is not a game. There is no anthem, no scoreboard drama, and no intermission show. What you get instead is drills, small group work, and a lot of coaching. Players skate in waves, work on breakouts and zone entries, and take shots on goaltenders who face far more pucks in an hour than they would in a real game.

People often stay longer than they planned. Without crowd noise you can hear the skates cutting, the pucks hitting the boards, and the coaches calling things out. It is a different experience than a ticketed game and, for a lot of people, a more interesting one.

Why It Matters to Las Vegas Residents

Free is the whole story here. A family of four can go to a Silver Knights camp practice, spend nothing on admission, and still get a couple of hours of real entertainment. Compare that to almost any other live sports option in Clark County and the math is not close.

It is also a low-pressure way to introduce kids to hockey. At a practice there is no crowd noise, no scoreboard, and no rush. Kids can actually see the drills, watch the goalies work, and hear the sounds of the game. A lot of parents in this valley grew up somewhere without hockey and do not know where to start. A free practice is the start.

For downtown Henderson, this is foot traffic. When a few hundred people show up on Water Street at midday, they park, they walk, and some of them eat lunch or grab coffee before or after. Small businesses on that corridor notice weeks like this one. Henderson has spent years and real money trying to turn Water Street back into a place where people spend an afternoon, and events that pull people in for free are part of how that works.

There is a housing angle too, and it is simpler than people expect. Buyers looking at Henderson almost always ask what there is to do. Being able to say that downtown has an arena with pro hockey, concerts, and free public practices is a genuine answer. Amenities that people actually use are what make a neighborhood feel lived in, and that is what shows up in demand over time.

Finally, this is a reminder for anyone who drives through downtown Henderson during the week. Midday events at the arena can put more cars on Water Street and the side streets around it. It is not the Strip, but if you are running errands in that area on Monday around noon, give yourself a few extra minutes.

There is one more thing worth saying about timing. A noon practice on a Monday is awkward for most working adults, and that is exactly why it stays uncrowded. If you have a flexible schedule, work from home, homeschool, or are retired, this is close to a private showing of a pro hockey team. People with traditional schedules should look at the rest of the week's published times instead of assuming the whole camp is midday.

Seniors in the Henderson and Green Valley area tend to love this kind of outing for the same reasons. It is short, it is indoors, it is free, there is real parking, and you can leave whenever you want. Not every valley event checks all five of those boxes.

Background and History

The Henderson Silver Knights play in the American Hockey League, which is the top development league below the NHL. The team is the affiliate of the Vegas Golden Knights, so the players skating at America First Center this week are the group the NHL club is developing and drawing from during the season.

That connection is why camp matters to more than just AHL fans. Roster moves flow both directions during a season. Players who look good in Henderson in September often turn up in a Golden Knights lineup later. If you follow VGK closely, camp in Henderson is a preview of the names you will hear in January.

America First Center anchors the Water Street District, which is downtown Henderson's original commercial core. For a long stretch, that corridor struggled while growth in the valley moved west and south. Henderson has been working to reverse that with street improvements, events, new dining, and residential projects near the core. Putting a pro sports venue in the middle of it was the single biggest bet in that effort.

The Silver Knights arrived as part of a broader run of hockey growth in Southern Nevada. The Golden Knights opened the door in 2017, and the sport went from a novelty to something with youth leagues, learn to skate programs, and real local roots. A Las Vegas kid can now grow up watching hockey in person, which was not true a decade ago.

Coaching changes are a normal part of the AHL. Development leagues move coaches up, down, and sideways often, and a new voice usually brings a different system and a different set of priorities. Joel Ward stepping in as head coach means this camp is a genuine reset rather than a continuation, and that is part of why the practices are worth watching this particular year.

Henderson itself is worth a word here, because the city is not a Las Vegas suburb in the way outsiders assume. It is its own municipality with its own council, its own police department, and its own downtown. Water Street was that downtown long before the master planned communities went up on the edges of town, and the effort to bring it back has been a deliberate, multi year policy choice rather than an accident of growth.

You can see the results in pieces. Restaurants that would once have gone to a strip center off a major arterial now open on Water Street instead. Events that used to happen in a park downtown now anchor to the arena. Each addition makes the next one a little easier to justify, which is how a corridor turns around.

Free public practices fit that pattern neatly. They cost the team almost nothing, they cost the visitor nothing, and they put people on the sidewalk in the middle of a weekday. That is the kind of small, repeatable thing that actually builds a downtown, more than any single big announcement does.

What Happens Next

The immediate calendar is short and easy to follow. Camp practices start Monday, September 21, at noon at America First Center and continue through the week, with all sessions open to the public. The team's published schedule is the place to confirm the exact times day by day.

Next comes the road trip. The Silver Knights play two exhibition games at the Southwest Showdown in Tucson on September 26 and 27. Preseason games are where the roster starts to firm up. Coaches use them to see who handles game speed and who does not, and cuts usually follow.

Then the season starts at home. Friday, October 3, the Silver Knights open the regular season at Lee's Family Forum against the Bakersfield Condors. That is a ticketed game, unlike camp, so if you want to be there you will want to plan ahead rather than deciding that afternoon.

Between now and October 3, expect roster news. Assignments from the NHL club, cuts, and signings all tend to land in that window. If you watched camp and picked out a player you liked, that is the stretch where you find out whether he made it.

Watch the venues too, because the Silver Knights operate across more than one building. Camp practices this week are at America First Center on Water Street, while the October 3 season opener is at Lee's Family Forum. If you are buying tickets or making plans with friends, confirm which building you are headed to rather than assuming.

The broader thing to watch over the next month is how the team looks under a new coach once games count. Camp tells you intentions. Preseason in Tucson tells you a little more. The first few regular season games at home tell you whether any of it took. Fans who go to camp get the earliest look at that story.

Ryan's Take

I talk to buyers about Henderson constantly, and the question underneath almost every conversation is the same one: what is actually here? Schools and commute times matter, but people also want to know what their weekends look like. Downtown Henderson having an arena that hosts pro hockey, concerts, and free open practices is a real answer, not a brochure answer.

What I would point out is how much the Water Street District has changed in the time I have been working this valley. It went from a place most people drove past to a place people drive to. That shift did not happen because of one project. It happened because the city kept investing and because there are now reasons to be there on a random Monday. A free camp practice at noon is exactly that kind of reason.

If you are shopping for a home in Henderson, I would genuinely suggest spending an afternoon downtown this week. Go watch twenty minutes of practice, then walk Water Street and eat somewhere. You will learn more about whether that part of town fits you than you would from any listing photo.

What You Can Do

If you want to go, here is the short version. Camp practices are at America First Center, 222 S. Water St., Henderson. The first skate is Monday, September 21, at noon. It is free and open to the public. Bring a jacket, because rinks are cold even in September in Nevada, and bring kids if you have them.

Check the schedule before you drive over. Practice times during camp are the kind of thing that changes on short notice, and the team's own schedule page is the reliable source. Confirming the morning of takes thirty seconds and saves you a wasted trip.

Plan on arriving a few minutes early and sitting wherever staff directs you. Public access at a practice is a courtesy, not a ticketed seat, so the usual rules apply. Keep the aisles clear, keep noise reasonable during drills, and do not expect autographs or interaction while players are working. Most teams are generous about this when fans are respectful about it.

Make an afternoon of it. Water Street has restaurants and coffee within an easy walk of the arena, and parking downtown is far less painful than anything near the Strip. If you have been meaning to check out downtown Henderson and never had a reason, this is the reason.

If hockey sticks with your family, the next step is the October 3 home opener at Lee's Family Forum against the Bakersfield Condors. Tickets for that one go through the team. And if you want to see the NHL side, the Vegas Golden Knights also hold free public practices at their Summerlin facility during their camp window.
