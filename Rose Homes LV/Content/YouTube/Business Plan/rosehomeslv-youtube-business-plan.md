# Rose Homes LV YouTube Business Plan

This plan reverse-engineers youtube.com/@LIVINGINDALLAS, the most thoroughly documented real estate YouTube channel in the business, and maps its proven playbook directly onto the content engine Ryan has already built. Levi Lascsak and Travis Plumb turned a brand-new channel into a full-time relocation business in roughly a year using nothing but search-optimized video, then packaged the exact method into a book, a podcast, and a coaching program, so almost every move is on the public record and we can copy what works. The difference for Rose Homes LV is that the most labor-intensive parts of their system, the research, the scripting, the SEO, and the local-news production, already exist here as working skills. The goal of this document is to turn @rosehomeslv from a name-based agent channel into a "Living in Las Vegas" relocation lead machine that prospects for us around the clock.

## 1. The @LIVINGINDALLAS teardown (why it works)

### The numbers (the channel owner's public claims)

- The channel "Living in Dallas Texas" was started by Levi Lascsak and Travis Plumb, agents at eXp Realty, with their first video uploaded December 5, 2020 (livingindallastx.com/about).
- It now sits at roughly 44,500 subscribers and about 4.5 million views, and the owners say it generates 3 to 5 inbound leads per day (livingindallastx.com/about; ShoutOut DFW interview).
- In their first 12 months they closed roughly 164 sales for about $33.5M in volume and around $1M in GCI, with zero ad spend and zero cold calling (Keeping It Real podcast, "164 Sales in 12 Months ALL From YouTube"; Passive Prospecting).
- Their first closing came around April 2021, roughly four months after the first upload, and they report about $30K in commission that month (Passive Prospecting).
- They say they went from $0 to roughly $2.6M GCI in under two years and have sold 500-plus homes through the method (Investor Fuel; Passive Prospecting).
- Levi packaged the system as "Passive Prospecting," now a book, a podcast, and a coaching business.

Read those claims as marketing from the person selling the method, not as audited results. The point for us is not the exact dollar figures, it is the shape of the outcome: a single channel, built with free organic video, replaced an entire cold-prospecting operation. That outcome is repeatable because it rests on four structural advantages, not on luck or charisma.

### The four pillars

**Pillar 1: Intention over interruption.** This is the core philosophy and the reason the whole thing works. Levi frames it as intention-based marketing versus interruption marketing. Cold calls, door knocking, and paid ads interrupt people who were not thinking about real estate, so most of the audience is annoyed and unqualified. YouTube does the opposite. It catches people who are already typing "moving to Dallas" into a search bar. They have raised their hand before you ever say a word. As Levi puts it, "You provide the value upfront for free through video. When the client has consumed enough free value, they reach out when they are ready." A relocation searcher is one of the highest-intent leads in real estate: they are moving to a city they do not know, they need a local expert, and they have no agent yet. The video gets to be that expert first.

**Pillar 2: Evergreen compounding.** A cold call ends the second you hang up. A ranked video keeps working forever. This is the idea behind two of Levi's most-quoted lines. "Making videos does not take you time, it makes you time," and the claim that their library's accumulated watch time "prospects for us 16 days worth of time every day." One well-targeted "Moving to Dallas" video answers the same question for a new searcher every single day, for years, while the team sleeps. Effort goes in once, leads come out indefinitely, and every additional video stacks on top of the last one instead of resetting to zero. A prospecting team that stops dialing stops eating. A video library that you stop adding to still keeps producing.

**Pillar 3: A tight, repeatable content formula.** Levi does not freestyle topics. He teaches a short list of formats that reliably rank because they match what relocators actually search:

- Neighborhood and community tours.
- "Pros and cons of living in [place]."
- Cost of living in [place].
- Suburb-versus-suburb comparisons.
- "Moving to [city]" hero videos.
- Local development and news.

The formula matters more than the personality. Any agent who consistently fills these buckets for their own metro can rank, because the search demand already exists and most local agents are not serving it on video.

**Pillar 4: Keyword-first SEO plus a gated lead funnel.** Ranking is upstream of everything, so every video is engineered around a search keyword before a single frame is shot (the title rules, description rules, and filename trick are detailed in Section 4). Then the traffic has somewhere to go. The funnel is simple and gated: the video offers a free relocation guide, the viewer trades an email to get it, the email drops into the CRM, and that contact is nurtured toward a discovery call. Free value first, contact second, conversation third. The video earns the click, the guide earns the email, and the nurture earns the appointment.

### The advantage we exploit

Here is the most useful thing Levi has said publicly, and it is the seam we pry open. He states that local content, meaning city development, infrastructure projects, and specific-suburb coverage, spreads FASTER than pure relocation content. It hits both the people planning a move and the people who already live there, so it earns more views, more shares, and more subscribers per video. The catch for him was production. Local news is a different cadence and a different research job than evergreen relocation tours, so he had to stand up an entirely SECOND channel just to feed it.

Ryan does not have that problem. The hardest part of the local-content engine, the weekly Clark County news research and production, already exists as the local-news skill in this workspace, which researches Government and Development, School Board, Hockey, and Real Estate Market every week, ranks stories by viral potential, and outputs finished green-screen video transcripts, blogs, and social copy. So the thing that forced Levi into a second channel and a second workflow is, for us, a button we already press. Running a hybrid of evergreen relocation video and fast-moving local news is not a compromise or a dilution of the Dallas model. It is the Dallas model plus the exact accelerant Levi had to bolt on later, available to us on day one.

## 4. The keyword + SEO system (Levi's exact rules, adapted to Las Vegas)

Levi's whole edge is that he treats YouTube as a search engine, not a video site. Ranking comes before production. Below are his exact rules, translated to Las Vegas. The skills already generate most of this, so this section is the standard every video is held to and the spec the skills are tuned against.

### Title formulas per pillar

Titles must be search-intent titles, never vague. "House Tour" or "Las Vegas Vlog" is banned because nobody searches it. Every title is built around the phrase a relocator would actually type.

- **Relocation hero:** "Moving to [place] in [year]", "Pros and Cons of Living in [place]", "Don't Move to [place] Until You Watch This".
- **Neighborhood tour:** "Living in [neighborhood] | Everything You Need to Know", "[Neighborhood] Las Vegas: The Honest Tour".
- **Cost / comparison:** "Cost of Living in [place]", "[Place A] vs [Place B]: Which Should You Choose?".
- **Local news / development:** "[Project or place] Is Changing Las Vegas", "What's Being Built in [area] Right Now".
- **Market authority:** "Las Vegas Housing Market Update [Month Year]".

### Concrete example Las Vegas titles

1. Moving to Las Vegas in 2026: Watch This Before You Move
2. Pros and Cons of Living in Henderson, Nevada
3. Henderson vs Summerlin: Where Should You Actually Live?
4. Cost of Living in Las Vegas (What Nobody Tells You)

### The description template

The description is ranking real estate, not an afterthought. Every video uses this structure:

- **Keyword in the first 3 sentences.** The exact search phrase from the title must appear, naturally, within the first three sentences. YouTube weights the opening lines most heavily, and they double as the snippet in search results.
- **250-plus words.** Write a real, useful description of at least 250 words. Restate what the video covers, the neighborhoods or comparisons it includes, and who it is for, using the keyword and close variants throughout.
- **CTA link first.** The single most important link, the free Las Vegas relocation guide that captures the email, goes at the very top of the description, above the fold, before anything else.
- **Chapters.** Timestamped chapters (00:00 style) for every section. Chapters improve watch time, give the video more ranking surface, and earn extra placement in search.
- **Tags.** A focused set of tags built from the primary keyword and its close variants (place names, "moving to," "cost of living," "pros and cons"). Tight and on-topic beats a long, scattered list.
- **Contact and links below the body.** Ryan's contact info and standard links go beneath the description body, after the value and the chapters.

### The filename trick

Before upload, rename the video file to the target keyword. Not "final_v3.mp4," but a filename that is the search phrase itself, for example `moving-to-las-vegas-2026.mp4`. YouTube can read the uploaded filename, and matching it to the keyword is a free, easy ranking signal that almost no local agent bothers with.

### Thumbnail rules

- **High contrast.** The thumbnail has to pop at a tiny size on a phone feed. Bold colors, strong separation between subject and background, no muddy mid-tones.
- **A face plus a few words.** Human faces with clear expressions earn clicks, so put Ryan (or a strong subject) in frame, paired with a very short text overlay, three or four words maximum. Walls of text on a thumbnail do not read at feed size.
- **Title and thumbnail tease DIFFERENTLY.** This is the rule almost everyone gets wrong. The thumbnail should not repeat the title word for word. The title states the topic; the thumbnail adds a second, complementary hook, the tension, the surprise, or the payoff, so that together they give the viewer two reasons to click instead of saying the same thing twice. Example: title "Pros and Cons of Living in Henderson, Nevada," thumbnail text "Would I move here?" over Ryan's face.

These rules are not optional polish. They are the difference between a video that ranks and earns leads for years and a video that is invisible the day after it posts. Every skill in the architecture is held to this spec, and every video ships against this checklist.

## 5. Cadence and publishing rhythm

The single biggest lever in year one is volume plus consistency. Levi Lascsak did not win with one perfect video. He won by publishing 2 to 3 times a week, every week, without missing, while the library compounded behind him. We copy that exactly. The goal is not a viral hit. The goal is to never miss a publish slot for 12 straight months.

**The commitment: 2 to 3 videos per week, every week, for 12 months.**

Quantity early is what builds the back catalog that does the selling later. A relocation channel is a library, not a broadcast. A video filmed in month 2 can still be generating a discovery call in month 11. The faster we stack videos, the faster that library effect kicks in.

**Weekly slot plan (one production day feeds the whole week):**

We batch on one filming day so Ryan is on camera once, not three times. Research and scripts are prepped before that day using the skills, so filming is just reading the teleprompter.

| Slot | Day | Source skill | What it is |
|------|-----|--------------|------------|
| Long-form relocation / neighborhood | Monday | /yt-long | The anchor video. 5 to 15 minutes. "Living in Summerlin", "Moving to Henderson", cost of living, best neighborhoods for families, etc. This is the SEO and authority piece. |
| Local news / development | Wednesday | /local-news | Weekly Clark County news and development roundup. New construction, openings, growth, "what is happening in Las Vegas this week". Keeps the channel current and feeds the algorithm fresh topics. |
| Market update | Friday | /youtube-manager | Weekly MLS market-update video. Prices, inventory, days on market, interest-rate context. The recurring data piece that relocating buyers and sellers search for. |
| 2 to 3 Shorts | Throughout the week | Cut from the Monday long-form | Vertical clips pulled straight out of the long-form video. One filming session yields the long-form plus several Shorts. Maximum output for minimum extra camera time. |

That is 3 long videos plus 2 to 3 Shorts most weeks. In a heavier week the Shorts push total output past 5 pieces. The long-form is always the priority. If a week gets compressed, protect the Monday long-form first, then the market update, then local news, then Shorts.

**Weekly batching and filming workflow:**

1. Earlier in the week, run /local-news, /youtube-manager, and /yt-long to research topics and generate the scripts and SEO packages. All output lands under /Users/ryanrose/Downloads/Claude/Rose Homes LV/Content/YouTube/.
2. On the dedicated filming day, film all three long-form scripts back to back off the teleprompter. Same setup, same lighting, one block of camera time.
3. Edit, then cut 2 to 3 Shorts out of the long-form footage.
4. Publish on the slot schedule (Monday, Wednesday, Friday) with the SEO package attached to each: title, description with the funnel CTA, tags, and thumbnail.
5. Engage with comments within the first few hours of each upload. Early engagement signals the algorithm and starts conversations that turn into leads.

**Expectation setting on timeline:**

This is a slow-burn channel that pays off big. Based on Levi's public results and how YouTube SEO ranks, the realistic curve is:

- Months 1 to 2: building the library, low views, near-zero leads. This is normal. Do not change course.
- Months 3 to 4: first inbound relocation-guide leads typically start arriving as videos begin to rank and the catalog reaches critical mass.
- Months 5 to 12: compounding. The library is large enough that new and old videos both pull traffic, leads become steadier, and the first closings land.

The mistake that kills channels is quitting in months 1 to 2 because the leads have not shown up yet. The plan assumes a quiet start by design. Consistency through the quiet stretch is the whole strategy.

## 6. The lead-capture funnel

Every video exists to move a viewer one step: from watching to raising their hand. We do that with a single free offer, the same one on every video, with no friction.

**The flow, in words:**

YouTube video (long-form, news, market update, or Short)
-> viewer hears the outro CTA and reads the description CTA
-> clicks the link to the free "Moving to Las Vegas Relocation Guide"
-> lands on the Lofty landing page at https://rosehomeslv.com/moving-to-las-vegas
-> enters name, email, and phone to unlock the guide (this is the lead capture)
-> entry triggers a Lofty smart plan that nurtures by email over the following days
-> nurture invites them to book a discovery call with Ryan
-> discovery call converts to a client.

**Second on-ramp (search, not video):**

Google search for "moving to Las Vegas" and related terms
-> lands on the supporting SEO blog at https://rosehomeslv.com/blog/moving-to-las-vegas
-> the blog offers the same free relocation guide
-> sends the reader to the same landing page at https://rosehomeslv.com/moving-to-las-vegas

So we have two doors, video and search, both feeding one landing page, one smart plan, and one call funnel. Everything points at the same offer, which keeps the funnel simple to run and easy to measure.
