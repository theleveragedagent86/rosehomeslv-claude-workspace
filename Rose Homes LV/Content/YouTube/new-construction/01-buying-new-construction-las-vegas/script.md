[GFX: chapter — 3 "Your First Visit and Your Agent"]

BODY 1

[GFX: transition — medium]
Let's start at the very beginning, because this is where that expensive day-one mistake lives.

[GFX: broll — model home sales office, friendly staff at a desk]
When you walk into a model home, the friendly person who greets you seems like they're on your side. They're not. The on-site staff work for the builder. They represent the builder's interests, not yours.

[GFX: stat — first visit only | when your agent must register | src: hendersonredevelopment.com; neighborhoodsinlasvegas.com]
And here's the rule that trips people up. The builder needs your real estate agent to register, or come with you, on your very first visit. Not the second visit. The first.

[GFX: title-card — "Walk in alone first, and your agent can be locked out"]
If you walk in alone and fall in love with a floorplan, you may have just locked your own agent out of the deal. That means no one in the room is looking out for you when it's time to negotiate.

[GFX: stat — $0 | what your own agent costs you | src: homesforsale.vegas/new-construction-realtors]
Now here's the part that surprises almost everyone. Having your own agent costs you nothing. The builder sets aside a commission for the buyer's agent. So you get someone in your corner for free, but only if you bring them on that first visit.

[GFX: quote — "On-site staff represent the builder, not the buyer." — neighborhoodsinlasvegas.com]
Think about it this way. The sales agent is paid to sell that builder's homes at that builder's prices. They're good people, but they are not your people. Your agent is the one person in that room whose only job is to get you the best deal.

[GFX: chapter — 5 "Contract, Design Center, and Upgrades"]

BODY 3

[GFX: broll — buyer signing a contract at a desk]
First you'll sign the builder's contract and put down a deposit.

[GFX: compare — 5 to 10% builder deposit vs. typical resale earnest money | src: houzeo / StyleCraft synthesis]
Expect that deposit to run about five to ten percent of the price. That's usually more than the earnest money on a resale, so plan for it.

[GFX: broll — design center wall of finishes, cabinets, fixtures]
Then comes the design center, where you pick countertops, flooring, cabinets, and fixtures. It feels like a candy store. It is also where the markups live.

[GFX: stat — 50 to 100% | builder flooring markup | src: rosehomeslv.com/blog/new-construction-upgrades-worth-it-las-vegas]
Remember that flooring number from the start? Builder flooring is commonly marked up fifty to a hundred percent over what it would cost to install after you close.

[GFX: title-card — "Some upgrades wait. Some can't."]
So here's the simple rule I give my buyers. Some upgrades can wait until after closing, and some absolutely cannot.

[GFX: stat — before the foundation pour | when structural options lock in | src: homesforsale.vegas/builder-options]
Structural choices, like an extra bedroom, a bonus room, or a covered patio, have to be selected before they pour the foundation. Once that concrete is down, that door is closed.

[GFX: stat — 8 to 12 weeks before closing | when design selections are made | src: homesforsale.vegas/builder-options (search synthesis)]
Your finish selections usually happen about eight to twelve weeks before closing. So you've got time, but not unlimited time.

[GFX: broll — couple looking thoughtful, comparing two flooring samples]
My honest advice. Spend on the things that are hard to change later, like layout and structure. Be careful with the easy stuff, like flooring and light fixtures, where the markup is steep and a contractor can do it cheaper down the road.

[GFX: chapter — 6 "The Build, Inspections, and Warranty"]

BODY 4

[GFX: stat — 6 to 12 months | production home build time, contract to closing | src: rosehomeslv.com/blog/new-construction-timeline-las-vegas-2026]
For a typical production home, plan on about six to twelve months from contract to closing.

[GFX: stat — 30 to 90 days | wait after signing before ground breaks | src: rosehomeslv.com/blog/new-construction-timeline-las-vegas-2026]
And don't expect dirt to fly the day you sign. It can be thirty to ninety days after signing before they even break ground on your lot. That's normal, so don't panic.

[GFX: list — months 1-2 permits; 3-4 foundation and framing; 5-6 rough-in; 7-9 finishes; 10-12 inspections and close]
From there it moves in phases. The first couple of months are permits and site prep. Then foundation and framing. Then the rough-in for electrical, plumbing, and HVAC. Then drywall, cabinets, and flooring. And finally inspections, the punch list, and your closing.

[GFX: stat — 30 to 60 days | buffer to add past the builder's projected date | src: rosehomeslv.com/blog/new-construction-timeline-las-vegas-2026]
And here's a tip that saves a lot of stress. Take whatever close date the builder gives you and quietly add thirty to sixty days in your own head. Weather, supplies, and inspections push dates. If you've planned a buffer, a delay is a shrug instead of a crisis. If you've already given notice on your apartment, it's a nightmare.

[GFX: broll — framed house, exposed studs and wiring before drywall]
Now, the first big moment. The pre-drywall walk, sometimes called the frame walk.

[GFX: title-card — "See it before the walls hide it"]
This happens while the framing, plumbing, electrical, and HVAC are still open and exposed. Once the drywall goes up, all of that disappears behind your walls. So this is your one clean look at the bones of the house. Bring your agent. Take photos of everything.

[GFX: broll — buyer placing blue tape on a wall imperfection]
The second big moment comes a few days before closing. It's called the blue tape walkthrough.

[GFX: stat — a few days before closing | blue tape walkthrough timing | src: rocketmortgage.com/learn/blue-tape-walkthrough]
You walk the finished home and put a piece of blue tape on every flaw you find, a scratch, a gap, a paint miss, anything. The builder fixes the taped items before you close. Be picky here. This is the easiest time to get things fixed for free.

[GFX: list — 1 year workmanship; 2 years systems; 10 years structural]
After you move in, you're not on your own. Many builders offer what's called a 1-2-10 style warranty. Roughly, one year on workmanship, two years on systems like plumbing and electrical, and ten years on the big structural pieces.

[GFX: title-card — "Warranty terms vary by builder, so read yours"]
I want to be straight with you. That 1-2-10 setup is a common industry model, not a one-size-fits-all Nevada law. Every builder's actual terms are different, so read your specific warranty.

[NOT FOUND: Nevada statute-of-repose hard number for construction-defect claims, 6 vs 10 year conflict unresolved in research]
On top of the warranty, Nevada law gives homeowners a window of years to bring construction-defect claims under the state's construction-defect rules. The protection is real. Just don't bank on a specific deadline without checking the current statute, because the exact window is something to confirm with a professional.

[GFX: chapter — 7 "Closing and Your Action Plan"]

ACTION / TAKEAWAYS

[GFX: transition — medium]
So let's bring it home, literally. Closing on a new build has a few twists a resale doesn't.

[GFX: stat — 2 to 5% | new-construction closing costs in Las Vegas | src: rosehomeslv.com/blog/new-construction-closing-costs-las-vegas]
Plan for closing costs of roughly two to five percent of the purchase price.

[GFX: stat — $5.10 per $1,000 | Nevada transfer tax | src: rosehomeslv.com/blog/new-construction-closing-costs-las-vegas]
And watch one line item. Nevada's transfer tax runs five dollars and ten cents per thousand dollars of value. On a resale the seller usually pays it. In a builder contract, that cost can get shifted to you, so read for it.

[GFX: stat — up to $30,000 | 2026 builder closing-cost incentive with a preferred lender | src: rosehomeslv.com/blog/new-construction-closing-costs-las-vegas]
Now the good news. In 2026, some builders are offering serious incentives if you use their preferred lender. Richmond American has gone up to thirty thousand dollars toward closing costs, and others run periodic promotions. Always compare that against an outside lender's rate, because a lower rate can beat a credit.

[GFX: list — bring your agent first; spend on structure not finishes; show up for both walks]
So here are your three takeaways. One, bring your agent on that very first model visit, because it's free and it locks in your representation. Two, spend your upgrade money on structure and layout, not on the flooring with the fifty to a hundred percent markup.
