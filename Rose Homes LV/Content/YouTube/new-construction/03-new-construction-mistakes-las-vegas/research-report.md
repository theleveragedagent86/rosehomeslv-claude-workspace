# Research Report: What Builders Won't Tell You: New Construction Mistakes in Las Vegas (Video 3 of 3)

## Overview
This is the cautionary "watch out for this" video in the new-construction batch. It is a tight, money-and-representation focused walkthrough of the traps a Las Vegas valley buyer can fall into on a builder home: treating the friendly person in the model home as your agent when they work for the builder, paying lot premiums without understanding what drives them, overspending at the design center on marked-up upgrades, taking a builder rate buydown or incentive without comparing it to an outside lender, and signing away the appraisal, inspection, and financing protections a resale buyer normally keeps. The angle is protective, not anti-builder: how the system actually works and how a buyer protects themselves. Facts are local-first to Las Vegas and Clark County where the topic allows, and the representation, lot-premium, upgrade, and lender mechanics draw on national sources where they describe how the builder system works everywhere.

## Entities
- **Builder on-site sales agent (sales counselor):** The licensed agent staffing the model home; legally represents the builder/seller, not the buyer. Source: facts #1, #2.
- **Buyer's agent (independent):** The buyer's own licensed agent, paid by the builder's co-broke, who must be registered on the first model visit. Source: facts #4, #5, #6.
- **Richmond American Homes:** National builder active in Las Vegas; runs an up-to-$30,000 incentive tied to its in-house lender. Source: facts #26, #27.
- **HomeAmerican Mortgage Corporation:** Richmond American's in-house/affiliated lender; funds the builder's advertised Las Vegas financing offers. Source: fact #27, https://www.homeamericanmortgage.com/.
- **RESPA (Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act, 1974) / CFPB:** Federal law and enforcing agency; bars sellers from requiring a specific lender or title company, governs affiliated business arrangements. Source: facts #28, #29.
- **Affiliated Business Arrangement (AfBA):** The disclosed financial relationship between a builder and its in-house lender or title company; requires a written disclosure to the buyer. Source: fact #29.
- **2-1 buydown:** A temporary rate buydown, 2 points off year one, 1 point off year two, full rate year three. Source: fact #20.
- **Las Vegas builders offering 2026 incentives:** KB Home, DR Horton, Lennar, Richmond American and others; valley-wide incentive types from VEGAS INC. Source: fact #25.

## Local Context
- **First-visit registration is the local trap.** In Las Vegas, if a buyer walks into a model home alone, the builder's sales rep records them, and an agent brought in later can be denied registration, leaving the buyer without free representation. This is the single most repeated local warning. Source: facts #5, #6.
- **Las Vegas upgrade markup runs hot.** Local reporting puts builder flooring/upgrade markups at 50% to 100% over post-close pricing, on the high end of the national 30% to 80% range, so the "do cosmetic work later" advice matters more here. Source: facts #11, #12, #13.
- **Window coverings and yards are usually buyer expenses on a Las Vegas new build,** which buyers often discover after closing; quality shades run $150 to $300 per window locally. Source: fact #16.
- **Local 2026 incentive shapes:** rate buydowns ~1 to 1.5 points, design credits $5,000 to $15,000, HOA dues 30 to 60 days, and Richmond American's up-to-$30,000 program through HomeAmerican Mortgage. The lesson is to price the same home with an outside lender to see what the incentive is really worth. Source: facts #25, #26, #27.
- **Nevada transfer tax ($5.10 per $1,000) can be shifted to the buyer in a builder contract,** a cost a resale buyer usually does not carry. Source: fact #36.

## Conflicts and Open Questions
- **[Conflict] Can a builder incentive be conditioned on using the preferred lender?** Most sources (BuilderOnline summarizing RESPA, the CFPB AfBA safe-harbor framework, mortgage-industry FAQs) say YES, a builder may offer an incentive for using its affiliated/preferred lender as long as the buyer is not "required" to and an AfBA disclosure is given. One source (Silberman Law Firm, 2024-07-10) states more strictly that "incentives must not be contingent on using a specific lender." In practice many Las Vegas builders DO tie their best incentive to the in-house lender (e.g. Richmond American / HomeAmerican). QA should resolve the precise legal line before the script asserts it; the safe on-camera framing is "the builder can't force you to use their lender, but their biggest incentive is usually only on the table if you do, and that is legal as long as you are free to walk." Sources: facts #28, #29.
- **NOT FOUND: A Las Vegas-specific lot-premium dollar figure or percentage.** No local source gave a defensible Las Vegas valley figure for what a view, corner, or cul-de-sac lot premium costs. The allhomeslasvegas.com lot-premium blog returned HTTP 403 and could not be read. The script should explain what drives premiums (view, size, corner, privacy, cul-de-sac) and that they range from a few thousand to six figures and are baked into the price, but must NOT state a specific Las Vegas premium dollar amount. Sources: facts #7, #8, #9, #10.
- **NOT FOUND: Whether Las Vegas builders negotiate or waive lot premiums.** National sources say premiums fluctuate with the market and shrink in down markets, and the Las Vegas market is softening in 2026 (Video 2 facts: 2025 permits down ~20%, builders cutting prices), which suggests room to ask, but no source confirms Las Vegas builders are currently waiving or discounting lot premiums. Do not claim they are negotiable here without a source. Source: fact #9; Video 2 context.
- **NOT FOUND / VERIFY: The mortgage-shopping savings figures ($600 for two quotes, $1,200 for four).** These came from a search summary inside builder-lender articles and trace back to Freddie Mac-style shopping studies, but the exact primary citation and year were not pulled. QA should source or drop the specific dollar figures before they are spoken; the directional point (shopping more lenders saves money) is safe. Source: fact #24.
- **VERIFY: The $20,000 buydown vs. $25,000 markup example (fact #21).** This is an illustrative figure from a National Mortgage Professional article reached via search synthesis, not a directly fetched page. Treat it as an illustration of the mechanism, not a Las Vegas-specific number; QA should confirm the source text or present it as a generic example.
- **VERIFY: Design-center category markups (fact #12) are from a Dallas-Fort Worth 2026 guide,** not Las Vegas. They illustrate the pattern (cosmetic = highest markup). Pair them on camera with the local 50% to 100% flooring figure (#13) rather than presenting the DFW percentages as Las Vegas numbers.
- **Date staleness flags:** Several representation and contract sources are undated (n/d) agent/law blogs; their descriptions of how the builder system works are evergreen and stable, but no year should be attached. The Richmond American campaign intro-rate (2.999%) is a live promo that changes; the script should say "for example" and not present it as a standing rate.
