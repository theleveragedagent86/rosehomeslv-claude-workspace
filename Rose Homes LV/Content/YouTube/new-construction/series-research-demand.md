# Series Research: Demand, Competition, Local Opportunity

Research date: 2026-05-29. Purpose: pick which of three new-construction angles Ryan Rose should expand into a multi-episode YouTube series for the Las Vegas / Henderson market.

## Summary (bottom line up top)

The three candidate angles:

1. **The Full Process** (broad how-to roadmap)
2. **New vs. Resale** (decision guide)
3. **What Builders Won't Tell You / Mistakes** (cautionary insider warnings, money and representation)

Recommendation: build the **series around Angle 3 ("What Builders Won't Tell You: New Construction Mistakes in Las Vegas")**, anchored by a strong "Full Process" pillar video (Angle 1) that the mistakes episodes link back to. Angle 3 wins on three of the most important factors at once: it maps to high-intent, decision-stage search queries, it has the clearest local ranking opening because national generic content dominates the "process" and "vs resale" keywords, and it has by far the deepest bench of distinct, non-repeating episode topics (a comfortable 8 to 10 episodes). It is also the angle that most rewards real Las Vegas specifics (actual builders, actual incentive structures, actual lot premiums), which both ranks for local intent and builds trust with buyers who could hire him.

Angle 1 (Full Process) is the best single evergreen pillar video but a weak series spine, because a "series" of process steps just re-fragments one video. Angle 2 (New vs. Resale) is genuinely high-volume but is the most saturated by national and builder-owned content, and it is a one-and-done decision video, not a series.

## Question 1: Search demand and intent

### The query landscape

People searching around new construction cluster into a few intent buckets. The volume and the buyer-readiness ("how close to needing an agent") differ a lot by bucket.

**Decision-stage queries (high volume, mid intent):**
- "new construction vs resale" / "new construction vs resale which is better" is a heavily produced topic with dedicated guides from builders, brokerages, and national portals (Jagoe Homes, NewHomeSource, Shea Homes, REMAX, HousingWire). The sheer number of publishers targeting it signals high search volume. Source: [NewHomeSource](https://www.newhomesource.com/learn/new-homes-vs-resale-which-is-right-for-you/), [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/new-construction-vs-resale-homes-how-agents-can-guide-buyers-in-todays-market/). Intent here is "I haven't decided what kind of home to buy yet," which is earlier in the journey and less close to hiring an agent.

**Process / how-to queries (high volume, mid intent):**
- "buying new construction home process," "buying new construction tips," "new construction steps." Bankrate, Allen Tate, and many brokerages publish evergreen guides here. Source: [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/real-estate/buying-new-construction-avoid-these-mistakes/). Intent is "I think I want new construction, walk me through it." Mid-stage.

**Cautionary / money / representation queries (high intent, agent-ready):**
These are the queries that signal a buyer who is close to needing an agent, because the searcher has decided on new construction and is now worried about getting burned:
- "new construction mistakes to avoid" and "regrets buying new construction" are widely produced (City Ventures, Southern Bell Living, Bankrate, Waypoint Idaho's "11 regrets" piece). Source: [City Ventures](https://cityventures.com/blog/mistakes-to-avoid-when-buying-new-construction-homes/), [Waypoint Idaho](https://www.waypointidaho.com/blog/11-instant-regrets-when-buying-new-construction/).
- **"do I need a realtor for new construction"** is its own dense keyword cluster with dedicated pages from Orchard, FastExpert, HomeLight, U.S. News, and many brokerages. Source: [HomeLight](https://www.homelight.com/blog/buyer-do-i-need-a-realtor-to-buy-a-new-house/), [Orchard](https://orchard.com/blog/posts/do-you-need-a-real-estate-agent-when-buying-new-construction). **This is the single most agent-ready query in the whole space.** A person searching this is one good answer away from booking a buyer's agent, and the honest answer ("yes, and the builder usually pays your agent, so it costs you nothing") is exactly the on-ramp Ryan wants.
- "builder upgrades worth it" / "design center upgrades to avoid" / "which new construction upgrades hold value" is a large, evergreen cluster with strong YouTube presence (multiple videos: "6 New Construction Upgrades You Should AVOID," "Which New Construction Home Upgrades Are Worth It?," "New Construction Upgrade Mistakes"). Source: [YouTube search results](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LvbWv7oNb28), [Nitin Gupta DFW design-center guide](https://www.nitinguptadfw.com/post/design-center-secrets-which-upgrades-are-worth-it-and-which-are-ripoffs-2026-guide-dallas-for).
- "builder preferred lender rate buydown catch" / "should I use builder's lender" is a rising, high-intent cluster with coverage from Bankrate, The Mortgage Reports, U.S. News, and National Mortgage Professional. Source: [Bankrate on builder buydowns](https://www.bankrate.com/mortgages/builders-are-dangling-super-low-mortgage-rates-but-theres-a-catch/), [The Mortgage Reports](https://themortgagereports.com/80414/builders-preferred-lender-pros-cons).
- "lot premium new construction" / "are lot premiums worth it" rounds out the money cluster.

### Which angle maps to the highest-volume, highest-intent queries

- Angle 2 (vs resale) maps to the **highest raw volume** but **lower intent** (still deciding).
- Angle 1 (process) maps to **high volume, mid intent**.
- Angle 3 (mistakes / what builders won't tell you) maps to a **basket of multiple high-intent clusters at once** (mistakes, do-I-need-a-realtor, upgrades worth it, builder lender catch, lot premiums). Each of those is its own searchable topic, and every one of them signals a buyer who has already decided on new construction and is now nervous about money and representation. **That is the buyer most likely to hire an agent.** So while "vs resale" may edge it on raw volume, Angle 3 wins on intent and on agent-readiness, and it covers more distinct money-keywords than either of the other two.

## Question 2: Competition and saturation

**Angle 1, Full Process: crowded, generic-dominated.** "Buying new construction process / tips" is saturated by national publishers (Bankrate, Allen Tate, builder blogs) and large national YouTube channels. A single local agent will struggle to outrank generic national how-to content on the bare "process" keyword because the content is interchangeable and the big sites have domain authority. The local angle helps, but the topic itself is the most commoditized. Source: [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/real-estate/buying-new-construction-avoid-these-mistakes/), [Allen Tate](https://blog.allentate.com/how-to-be-savvy-when-buying-new-construction/).

**Angle 2, New vs. Resale: the most saturated, and partly conflicted.** This keyword is produced not just by neutral publishers but heavily by **builders themselves** (Jagoe, Shea, M/I, ADI) and by portals (NewHomeSource is owned by the new-home industry). Source: [Shea Homes](https://www.sheahomes.com/blog/sh-buying-new-vs-resale), [NewHomeSource](https://www.newhomesource.com/learn/how-to-choose-between-new-construction-and-resale-homes/). Builder-owned content naturally tilts toward "new." An independent local agent can carve a credible "honest, both-sides, here's-when-resale-actually-wins" lane, but it is one video's worth of differentiation, not a series, and the competition is the deepest of the three.

**Angle 3, Mistakes / What Builders Won't Tell You: clearest local opening.** Two things make this the least defensible by national/generic players:
1. The "what builders won't tell you" framing is inherently **adversarial to the builders**, so the builder-owned content machine (which dominates the other two angles) cannot and will not produce it. That removes the highest-authority competitors from the field.
2. The strongest, most trustworthy version of each "mistake" requires **local specifics**: real Las Vegas lot premiums (which here range from about $5,000 to $200,000+), the actual builders' preferred-lender structures, the actual incentive packages on offer in Summerlin, Cadence, Skye Canyon, Inspirada. Source: [Nevada Real Estate Group / Las Vegas Home Search Experts](https://www.lasvegashomesearchexperts.com/new-construction), [recngroup Henderson guide](https://recngroup.com/blog/henderson-nevada-new-home-construction-2025). National "10 mistakes" listicles cannot match a local agent who names the community and quotes the actual incentive. There is existing YouTube content on "upgrades to avoid," but it is generic and national, leaving the **local-mistakes** niche wide open.

**Where a local agent can realistically rank and own a niche:** the intersection of "new construction mistakes" + "Las Vegas / Henderson" + named communities and builders. That intersection is under-served on YouTube today and is exactly Angle 3.

## Question 3: Series expandability (candidate episode counts)

A good series needs many distinct sub-topics that don't repeat. Here is the realistic count for each angle.

### Angle 1, Full Process: ~3 to 4 episodes, and they cannibalize the pillar.
The process is essentially one linear story (pick community -> model home -> design center -> contract -> build -> walkthrough -> close). Source: [recngroup](https://recngroup.com/blog/henderson-nevada-new-home-construction-2025). Splitting it into episodes ("Episode 2: the design center step," "Episode 3: the walkthrough step") just fragments the pillar video and forces viewers to watch in order. Weak series spine. Best used as a single evergreen pillar.

### Angle 2, New vs. Resale: ~2 to 3 episodes, mostly forced.
The decision is largely a single weighing of trade-offs (price/sqft, move-in timing, lot maturity, warranties, HOA, negotiation). Source: [NewHomeSource](https://www.newhomesource.com/learn/new-homes-vs-resale-which-is-right-for-you/), [Jagoe Homes](https://jagoehomes.com/new-construction-vs-resale-homes-2025-buyers-comparison-guide/). You could spin off "new vs resale for first-time buyers," "for downsizers," "by budget band," but those are audience re-slices of the same comparison, not genuinely new content. One-and-done by nature.

### Angle 3, Mistakes / What Builders Won't Tell You: 8 to 10 strong, distinct episodes.
Each "mistake" is its own self-contained, searchable, click-worthy topic with its own keyword and its own real Las Vegas data. They do not depend on watch order. Candidate episodes, each backed by a sourced fact:

1. **Using the builder's sales rep as your agent** (and why the builder usually pays your agent, so it costs you nothing). Source: [HomeLight](https://www.homelight.com/blog/buyer-do-i-need-a-realtor-to-buy-a-new-house/), [FastExpert](https://www.fastexpert.com/blog/do-you-need-a-realtor-for-new-construction/). This is also the most agent-ready keyword in the space.
2. **Lot premiums** (in Las Vegas these run $5,000 to $200,000+, are generally less negotiable than upgrades, and you can never change them later). Source: [Las Vegas Home Search Experts](https://www.lasvegashomesearchexperts.com/new-construction).
3. **Design-center markups and which upgrades to skip** (paint marked up 200 to 400%, light fixtures 300 to 400%, do these after closing). Source: [Nitin Gupta DFW](https://www.nitinguptadfw.com/post/design-center-secrets-which-upgrades-are-worth-it-and-which-are-ripoffs-2026-guide-dallas-for).
4. **Which upgrades actually hold value** (structural and quartz at 80 to 90% ROI vs cosmetic at 40 to 70% recovery). Source: [M/I Homes](https://www.mihomes.com/blog/new-construction-upgrades-that-add-value), [Nitin Gupta DFW](https://www.nitinguptadfw.com/post/design-center-secrets-which-upgrades-are-worth-it-and-which-are-ripoffs-2026-guide-dallas-for).
5. **The builder's preferred-lender "catch"** (rate buydowns of $20k to $40k per loan are often rolled back into the home price; ~64% of large builders used permanent buydowns as of June 2025). Source: [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/mortgages/builders-are-dangling-super-low-mortgage-rates-but-theres-a-catch/), [AEI](https://www.aei.org/articles/three-years-later-permanent-rate-buydowns-continue-to-prop-up-new-home-prices/).
6. **Builder contracts, forced arbitration, and lost contingencies** (warranty terms often withheld until closing; arbitration clauses in the sales contract and the third-party warranty tilt toward the builder). Source: [Fair Arbitration Now](https://fairarbitrationnow.org/home-building-arbitration/), [Nolo](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/new-home-construction-defects-new-york-buyers-rights-against-the-builder.html).
7. **The model-home illusion** (the model is loaded with upgrades rarely in the base price; standard finishes are basic). Source: [City Ventures](https://cityventures.com/blog/mistakes-to-avoid-when-buying-new-construction-homes/).
8. **Skipping the independent inspection and rushing the walkthrough** (NAHB: 61% of new-home owners found a warranty-eligible defect in year one that the builder's final walkthrough missed; ~25% of new homes see some structural distress). Source: [Charlotte Inspector / AHI](https://charlotte-inspector.com/blog/how-to-take-advantage-of-your-builder-home-warranty/), [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/real-estate/buying-new-construction-avoid-these-mistakes/).
9. **Warranty gotchas** (what the 1/2/10 structure really covers vs the implied-warranty protections the warranty quietly excludes). Source: [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/homeowners/what-is-a-homebuilders-warranty.html), [FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/warranties-new-homes).
10. **The hidden move-in costs** (landscaping, backyard finishing, window coverings, and HOA timelines that force you to landscape fast and cost thousands out of pocket). Source: [City Ventures](https://cityventures.com/blog/mistakes-to-avoid-when-buying-new-construction-homes/).

Optional 11th: **Construction delays and how the contract treats them** (move-in deadlines, temporary-housing exposure). Source: [Allen Tate](https://blog.allentate.com/how-to-be-savvy-when-buying-new-construction/).

That is a comfortable 8 to 10 strong episodes, with an 11th in reserve, each with its own keyword and its own local data point. No other angle comes close.

## Question 4: Local angle (which benefits most from being Las Vegas-specific)

**Angle 3 benefits the most from being local.** Every mistake episode is dramatically stronger when grounded in real Las Vegas facts:
- Real lot-premium ranges in valley communities ($5,000 to $200,000+). Source: [Las Vegas Home Search Experts](https://www.lasvegashomesearchexperts.com/new-construction).
- Real builder incentive packages: Lennar promotional FHA buydowns starting near 2.75% year one and up to 3% toward closing through Lennar Mortgage; Pulte up to $50,000 in credit through Pulte Mortgage with first-year rates as low as 2.5%. Source: [Julia Grambo builder comparison](https://www.juliagrambo.com/blog/las-vegas-home-builders-compared-toll-brothers-lennar-kb-and-more), [Lauren Sells Las Vegas](https://www.laurensellslasvegas.com/post/new-build-incentives-heating-up-as-2025-fiscal-year-ends-in-las-vegas).
- Real local market context: new construction is now roughly a quarter of Southern Nevada sales, and 2025 was the slowest resale year in nearly two decades (~28,500 sales) with builders pulling 9,734 permits (down 20%, lowest since 2016) and Lennar, D.R. Horton, Pulte, and KB leading. Source: [Review-Journal](https://www.reviewjournal.com/business/housing/new-southern-nevada-homes-now-make-up-25-of-sales-as-builders-offer-incentives-3068164/), [Las Vegas Business Press](https://businesspress.vegas/real-estate/despite-decline-builders-gear-up-for-more-sales-in-2026-100066/). With builders this hungry, the "what they won't tell you" framing is timely and credible.
- Real named communities (Summerlin, Skye Canyon, Cadence, Inspirada, Lake Las Vegas) where Ryan can speak from experience. Source: [recngroup](https://recngroup.com/blog/henderson-nevada-new-home-construction-2025).

Angle 1 (process) also benefits from local communities and timelines, but the steps themselves are generic. Angle 2 (vs resale) benefits from local price-per-sqft comparisons, but the national "median new home is now cheaper than median resale" story partly overrides the local angle. Source: [Jagoe Homes](https://jagoehomes.com/new-construction-vs-resale-homes-2025-buyers-comparison-guide/). Angle 3 is the only one where local specificity is the entire competitive moat: a national channel literally cannot make Ryan's version.

Note: Rose Homes LV already publishes supporting written content on these exact local topics (a "New Construction Upgrades: Which Are Worth It in Las Vegas?" blog and a "Lennar vs KB Home vs DR Horton Las Vegas" blog), which means the website can cross-link the videos and the agent already has a documented local point of view. Source: [rosehomeslv.com](https://www.rosehomeslv.com/blog/new-construction-upgrades-worth-it-las-vegas), [rosehomeslv.com](https://www.rosehomeslv.com/blog/lennar-vs-kb-home-vs-dr-horton-las-vegas).

## Bottom-line ranking

### (a) By search demand and intent
1. **Angle 3 (Mistakes / What Builders Won't Tell You)** — covers the most distinct high-intent, agent-ready keyword clusters (mistakes, do-I-need-a-realtor, upgrades worth it, builder lender catch, lot premiums); the searcher has already decided on new and is the most likely to hire an agent.
2. **Angle 2 (New vs. Resale)** — highest raw volume but earlier-stage, lower intent.
3. **Angle 1 (Full Process)** — solid volume, mid intent, but commoditized.

### (b) By local ranking opportunity
1. **Angle 3** — adversarial framing locks out builder-owned content; local specifics are the moat; the local-mistakes niche is wide open on YouTube.
2. **Angle 1** — local communities/timelines help, but the topic is generic and crowded.
3. **Angle 2** — most saturated, heavily produced by builders and national portals; hardest to outrank.

### (c) By series expandability
1. **Angle 3** — 8 to 10 distinct, non-repeating episodes (11 with the delays topic).
2. **Angle 1** — 3 to 4, and they cannibalize the pillar video.
3. **Angle 2** — 2 to 3, mostly forced audience re-slices.

### Combined recommendation
Build the **series on Angle 3: "What Builders Won't Tell You: New Construction Mistakes in Las Vegas."** It is the only angle that wins demand-intent, local ranking opportunity, and expandability simultaneously, and it is the angle most likely to convert viewers into buyer-clients (the searchers are decision-stage and worried about money and representation).

Structure the series as: keep **Angle 1 (Full Process) as a single evergreen pillar video** that every mistakes episode links back to ("new to all this? start with my full-process video"), and keep **Angle 2 (New vs. Resale) as one standalone decision video** that feeds the top of the funnel. Then run Angle 3 as the recurring series.
