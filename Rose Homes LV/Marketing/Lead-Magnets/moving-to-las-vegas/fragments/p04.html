Where to live  ·  The valley at a glance

Las Vegas is really seven or eight towns.

Pick the feel first. Pick the street later.

Each part of the valley has its own pace, its own drive times, and its own price range. Start with the two or three that sound like your life, then look at homes inside them.

Seven areas people ask about most, and who each one tends to suit.
AreaWhere it is, and how it feelsWho it tends to suit
HendersonSoutheast side. Its own city. Clean, quiet, well run, heavy on parks and trails.Families and professionals who want a calm suburb with strong day to day services.
SummerlinWest side, against Red Rock Canyon. A large master plan built around trails, golf, and Downtown Summerlin.Outdoor people and buyers who want somewhere planned, with places to walk to.
Centennial HillsNorthwest. Newer homes, a little more room, easy access to the 215 Beltway.Buyers who want newer construction and more square footage per dollar.
Skye CanyonFar northwest. A newer master plan built around fitness, trails, and a resort style pool.Active families and first time buyers who want amenities without a long list of compromises.
North Las VegasNorth end, and its own city. A mix of established streets and brand new construction.Value minded buyers who want the most home for the money.
Green ValleyInside Henderson, and older. Mature trees, shopping and dining already built in.Buyers who want an established, convenient neighborhood with everything nearby.
InspiradaSouth Henderson. A newer master plan laid out around parks and sports fields.Young families who want a newer home in a parks first, event driven community.

The next two pages take these one at a time.

Prices, home ages, and lot sizes vary inside every one of these, so treat the table as a starting point rather than a ranking. The one thing that does hold across the valley is the split between the newer master plans out at the edges and the established neighborhoods closer in.
