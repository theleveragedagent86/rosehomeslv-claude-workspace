What it costs to live here The practical part

Four numbers that actually move your budget.

These are valley wide figures for 2026, each one from a named public source with the date it was checked. Your real costs depend on the area, the home, and how you use it.

Housing

About $490,000

The median price of an existing single family home in the valley. It is the biggest line in almost every budget, and it moves month to month, so check a fresh number before you plan around it.

Las Vegas REALTORS, June 2026

State income tax

None

Nevada does not tax personal income. Coming from a state that does, this is the one number that quietly offsets several of the others, and it shows up every paycheck rather than at tax time.

Nevada Department of Taxation, verified July 2026

Summer cooling

Around $135 a month

A typical valley power bill, closer to $80 in the mild months and higher at the peak of summer. Nevada averaged about $114 a month across 2025. Cooling is the utility to plan for here, not heating.

U.S. Energy Information Administration, 2025 average

Everyday utilities

Water about $32 to $45

Per month, on steep tiered rates that reward lower outdoor water use, which is part of why desert landscaping is everywhere. Power, gas, water, and internet together run about $325 to $345 a month.

Published utility rates and 2026 valley averages

What do these four leave out?

Four more lines, and they are all address specific.

  1. 1Property taxes. Set per parcel, so a valley wide figure is Not found here on purpose. The Clark County Assessor publishes the actual number for any address.
  2. 2HOA dues. Common across the valley and they vary widely by community. Always ask for the amount in writing before you are under contract.
  1. 3Homeowners insurance. Priced off the home, not the zip code. Get a real quote once you have an address in mind, not before.
  2. 4Vehicle registration. A new resident cost that catches people from states with cheap registration. Budget for it in month one.
