Why Las Vegas
WHY PEOPLE FALL FOR THIS CITY

People move to Las Vegas for a lot of reasons. Here are the ones that come up the most when I talk with folks making the move.

No state income tax. Nevada is one of the few states with no state income tax. That means more of your paycheck stays in your pocket. A lot of people moving from higher-tax states feel the difference right away.

The lifestyle is easy to love. Las Vegas is more than the Strip. Most of the people who live here spend their time in quiet suburbs with parks, trails, golf, and great restaurants. You get a big-city feel without a lot of the big-city stress.

Sunshine almost every day. The valley sees sun on most days of the year. Winters are mild, so you can hike, bike, and play outside in months that would be freezing in other places. Summers are hot and dry, and locals plan around it. More on that in the cost section.

Entertainment any night you want it. World-class shows, concerts, dining, and sports are minutes away. The Raiders, the Golden Knights, and more call Las Vegas home. When friends and family visit, you'll never run out of things to do.

Close to Southern California. The beaches and mountains of Southern California are about a four-hour drive. You can keep the coast in your life for weekend trips without paying coastal prices to live there every day.

One thing to know: Las Vegas grows fast and neighborhoods can feel very different from one another. The right area for you depends on your commute, your stage of life, and the vibe you want. That's exactly what the next section helps with.
Neighborhoods
WHERE TO LIVE: A QUICK GUIDE

Las Vegas is really a group of communities spread across the valley. Each one has its own feel. Here's a friendly overview to help you narrow things down. When you're ready, ask me and I'll match areas to your budget and your must-haves.

HENDERSON

Safe, suburban, family-friendly

Henderson sits on the southeast side of the valley. It's known for being clean, well-run, and quiet, with lots of parks and walking trails. It feels like its own city while still being close to everything.

Who it suits: Families, professionals, and anyone who wants a calm suburb with strong day-to-day services.

SUMMERLIN

Master-planned, active, west side

Summerlin is a large master-planned community on the west side, tucked up against Red Rock Canyon. It's built around parks, trails, golf, shopping, and Downtown Summerlin. The hiking out here is some of the best in the valley.

Who it suits: Outdoor lovers, families, and buyers who want a planned community with lots to walk to.

CENTENNIAL HILLS

Newer, spacious, northwest

Centennial Hills is in the northwest, where you'll find newer homes and a bit more space. It's a popular pick for people who want a newer build without the price of the most central areas, plus easy access to the 215 Beltway.

Who it suits: Buyers who want newer construction, more square footage, and an easy commute north and west.

SKYE CANYON

Outdoorsy, newer master plan, northwest

Skye Canyon is a newer master plan in the far northwest, built for an active, outdoor lifestyle. It has a fitness center, a resort-style pool, parks, and a big trail system. You're close to US-95 and a quick drive to Mount Charleston for cooler air.

Who it suits: Active families and first-time buyers who want newer homes and lots of amenities.

NORTH LAS VEGAS

Growing, good value, north

North Las Vegas is its own city on the north end of the valley. It has grown a lot in recent years and is often one of the more affordable places to find a newer home. There's a mix of established neighborhoods and brand-new construction.

Who it suits: First-time buyers and value-minded families who want more home for the money.

GREEN VALLEY

Established, convenient, southeast

Green Valley is an established area within Henderson. It's mature and leafy, with shopping, dining, and parks close by. Because it's been around a while, the trees are grown in and a lot of services are right around the corner.

Who it suits: Buyers who want an established, convenient neighborhood with everyday needs nearby.

INSPIRADA

Newer master plan, parks-focused, south Henderson

Inspirada is a newer master-planned community in south Henderson, built around a web of parks, sports fields, and trails. It has a younger, family feel and lots of newer homes, with community events that make it easy to meet neighbors.

Who it suits: Young families and buyers who want a newer home in a parks-first, community-driven setting.

Schools
SCHOOLS AND FAMILY LIFE

If you have kids, schools are a big part of the decision. Here's a clear, no-spin overview so you know how it works before you pick an area.

The public district is Clark County School District (CCSD). It serves the whole Las Vegas valley, so wherever you land, you'll be inside CCSD. The school your child attends usually depends on your home address.

You have options beyond your zoned school. The area also has magnet schools, public charter schools, and private schools. Each works a little differently, and spots can be competitive, so it helps to plan ahead.

Ask me for area-specific guidance. Schools matter to a lot of families, and the right fit is personal. I'm happy to point you toward the resources to research schools by address, and to share what families in each area tell me. I'll keep it factual and let you make the call.

Good to do early: If schools drive your move, look at them before you settle on a neighborhood. The home you choose can decide your zoned school, so it's worth lining up first.

Hidden Costs
COSTS RELOCATORS FORGET

Moving has a few costs that sneak up on people, especially when you're crossing state lines. None of these are surprises if you plan for them. Here's the short list so your budget stays on track.

  • Earnest money. A good-faith deposit you put down when your offer is accepted. It goes toward your purchase, but you need it ready up front.
  • Home inspection. A pro checks the home before you buy. Worth every penny for the peace of mind, and you pay for it during the process.
  • Appraisal. Your lender orders this to confirm the home's value. It's usually a separate cost you cover along the way.
  • HOA dues. Many Las Vegas communities have a homeowners association. Dues cover shared amenities and upkeep, and they're an ongoing monthly cost.
  • Moving costs. Movers, a truck, packing supplies, and maybe storage. Long-distance moves cost more, so get quotes early.
  • Utility deposits. Some utility providers ask for a deposit when you set up new service, especially if you're new to the area.
  • New-resident DMV and registration. When you become a Nevada resident, you'll register your vehicle and update your license. Plan for the time and the fees.
How to stay ahead of it: Set aside a small cushion for these one-time costs on top of your down payment. When you ask me for a plan, I'll flag which of these apply to your move so there are no surprises.
First 30 Days
YOUR FIRST 30 DAYS CHECKLIST

You've got the keys. Now let's get you settled. Here's a simple checklist for your first month so the boring stuff gets handled and you can enjoy your new city.

  • Turn on your utilities. Set up power, water, trash, gas, and internet. Do this before move-in day if you can so you arrive to a working home.
  • Update your driver's license. New Nevada residents update their license. Check what documents you need and book an appointment to save time.
  • Register your vehicle. Nevada requires you to register your vehicle after you move. There's usually an inspection step, so look up the order of operations first.
  • Register to vote. Once you're a resident, update your voter registration with your new address so you're ready for the next election.
  • Find your everyday services. Pick a grocery store, pharmacy, doctor, dentist, vet, and bank branch near home. Getting these set early makes life feel normal fast.
  • Learn your routes. Drive your commute, your kids' school run, and the way to the nearest hospital. Knowing your roads takes the stress out of week one.
  • Explore your area. Walk the parks, try a few local restaurants, and find your coffee spot. This is the fun part, and it's how a new place starts to feel like home.
Settling-in tip: Tackle the official to-dos like license, registration, and voting in your first couple of weeks, while you have momentum. Then spend the rest of the month exploring. Need a recommendation? I'm a text away.
