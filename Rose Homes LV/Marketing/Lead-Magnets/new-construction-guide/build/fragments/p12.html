Your cap does not move Path B  ·  Moving up

I have had the 3 percent cap for years. Does it come with me?

No. A new house has nothing to cap against.

A brand new home gets no cap at all in its first fiscal year, and the cap starts the year after.* The law only caps property that had a separate assessed value the year before, and it excludes the value added by new improvements.* A house that did not exist last year has nothing to compare to.

Then there is the part people lose by mail.

Recording any ownership document knocks the cap off. You claim it back.* The Assessor mails a postcard after July 1 to properties whose ownership changed.* Sign it and send it back.* It looks like junk mail. It is worth real money every year.

Only one property per person statewide can carry the 3 percent cap.** Keep the old house as a rental and it moves to the higher cap.* Claiming the primary residence cap falsely carries a penalty of three times the tax deficiency, plus the tax and any other penalty.* Pick one house and be right about it.

The escrow trap on the new house

Your first bill may be for the dirt.

The Assessor values property as it stands on July 1 and bills the July 1 owner.* If your house was not standing yet, that first bill can reflect the lot only.

So ask your lender to escrow on the fully assessed completed home, not a land only bill,* or your payment jumps in year two.
