Why is the math different here?

Nevada does not tax what you paid for the house.

It taxes 35 percent of taxable value.* The Assessor builds that from replacement cost, minus 1.5 percent a year for age up to fifty years, plus the land.* Costs update yearly from a national building cost service.*

The Assessor's own example: $200,000 taxable value gives $70,000 assessed, and at 3.2782 per $100 the tax is $2,294.74.*

So what happens in year one?

There is no cap at all.

You have heard Nevada holds your main home to 3 percent a year,* and up to 8 percent on everything else.* Both are true. Neither covers a brand new house in its first fiscal year.*

The reason is in the statute. The cap only reaches property that had a separate assessed value the year before, and it leaves out value added by new improvements. A house that did not exist last year has nothing to cap against.*

The Assessor values property as it stands on July 1 and bills that owner,* so your first bill can cover the lot only.*

If you came through Path B you have seen part of this already. This is the whole picture.

The calendar

  • Due the third Monday in August.*
  • Over $100? Four installments,* in August, October, January, and March.*
  • No bill by August 1? Call the Treasurer.*
  • Ten days of grace,* then penalties from 4 up to 22 percent.*
  • Do this one thing. Have your lender escrow on the finished home, not a land only bill,* and watch for the postcard.*
