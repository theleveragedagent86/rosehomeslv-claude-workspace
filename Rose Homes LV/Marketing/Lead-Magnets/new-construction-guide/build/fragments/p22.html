What is an incentive, really?

A pool of money the builder decides how to spend.

It is what a builder will spend to close your deal. Your loan program sets the ceiling.

The cap ladder is in the diagram. Three rules ride with it. Buydowns count against the cap.* None can fund your down payment or reserves.* Twelve months of HOA dues can be covered.* On a VA loan it caps at 4 percent of reasonable value.* The FHA cap is lower at a small down payment. Its figure is Not found anywhere.

A buydown is not a lower rate.

A lump sum sits in a separate escrow account, and a slice comes out monthly to cut your early payment.** Your real rate never moves.* It steps up 1 percent a year, 3 percent total, three years maximum,* usually 2-1 or 3-2-1.* One builder published $20,125 as the fee on a $500,000 example.*

Where does it get quiet?

When part of the money is tied to their lender.

Century states $15,000 of an advertised $50,000 in flex cash is contingent on its own lender,* capped at 3 percent of price.* D.R. Horton needs its lender for the advertised rate, not to buy.* Taylor Morrison conditions on two affiliates,* after a pre application.* Toll Brothers* and Lennar* require their own affiliate for the offer. One volume builder has no affiliate at all* and compares several instead,* though some offers still require one.*

None of this is shady. A builder may price a discount around its own lender if you can still buy without it and it is not clawed back in fees.* A referral to a company the builder owns requires a written Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure.**
