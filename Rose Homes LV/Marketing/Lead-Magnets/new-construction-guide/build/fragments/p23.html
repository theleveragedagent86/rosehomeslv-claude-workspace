What happens to my loan while the house goes up?

Your price is set today. Your loan is not.

Nothing I have published explains this. It is the biggest hole in it.

How long is the build?

Mostly, nobody will say.

Eleven of twelve volume builders publish no contract to close time.* One publishes a four to five month build* plus thirty to fifty days before start,* roughly five to six and a half months in two pieces.* Another publishes six to twelve months.* One contract commits only to finishing within two years of signing.*

What moves underneath me?

In July 2026, the rate moved every week.

The national thirty year average went 6.43,* 6.49,* 6.55,* then 6.58 percent,* a range of 6.43 to 6.58 in one month.* The fifteen year sat at 5.96.* A year earlier it was 6.74.* That is a survey average, not a quote.*

Builders pay a lender up front to lock a below market rate on a pool of loans, offered only to their buyers.* Two publish that it lasts until the pool runs dry or the rate expires.** An offer can be gone before your house is.

What I will not print

Not found for current builder forward commitment rates and terms, and for lock extension and float down fee schedules.

Ask six things. How long is the lock. What does an extension cost. Is there a float down. When does the pool expire. What if the house is late. What if the rate moves.

And the appraisal?

A price set today gets appraised next year.

No Clark County source publishes how often new construction appraises below contract, or who absorbs the gap. That is Not found and I am not inventing a rate. Ask who orders it, what happens if it lands low, and whether the builder renegotiates. A loan over $832,750 here is jumbo,* and a low appraisal can move you across it.
