Can I check a builder before I sign?

Yes. It is free, and it takes about ten minutes.

Everything I have written on this tells you how to complain after something went wrong. Nothing tells you how to look first.

Step one

Search the company, not the sign out front.

Plenty of builders hold the state license under a name nothing like the brand. Lennar builds as Greystone Nevada LLC, Pulte as P N II Inc, Signature Homes as Plaster Development Company Inc.**** One smaller builder publishes that its brand is a DBA running its own brokerage, so the salesperson may work for it.* Ask what name is on the license.

Step two

Run the two free state searches.

  • The Nevada State Contractors Board license search.* Look for Active status, a classification, usually B-2, and the expiration date. One builder's expired 07/31/2026, days after I pulled it, so pull it again.*
  • The Board's disciplinary action search.* Read what it says. The reason matters more than the record existing.

How to read a BBB line

A Better Business Bureau letter is that group's own measure, not a government rating, and accreditation is paid for or declined.* A complaint count is complaints filed, not findings, and a bigger builder shows a bigger number. "Not Rated" is a status, not a low grade.*

Same for awards. There is no current J.D. Power builder study. It was cancelled in 2011, so anyone citing it is citing 2010 or earlier.*

What I did not check

Permit histories for the county and the city, Secretary of State entity standing, District Court defect filings, and NRS Chapter 40 notices.* Any of it could matter. Pages 32 to 35 carry the builder pages and the disclaimer that governs them.

Three documents. In writing. Every time.

  • The warranty document with its term lengths, plus the one page rights sheet your builder owes you.*
  • The HOA budget and resale package. Nevada law entitles you to it.*
  • The Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure, if you are sent to a company the builder owns.*
