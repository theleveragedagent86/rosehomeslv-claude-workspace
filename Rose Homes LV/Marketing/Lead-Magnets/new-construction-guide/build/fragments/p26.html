When can I actually catch something?

Four times. One of them closes forever.

Pre drywall is the only look at the bones: framing, straps and hangers, plumbing and electrical runs, duct routing, flashing, waterproofing.* One builder calls it your chance to confirm the options you ordered got installed.* Then blue tape, the final walk, and month eleven.*

Insist the punch list is written.

Your statutory one year clock starts when that written punch list is finished, not at closing.*** Get the date. File it.

That year covers home systems, workmanship, materials, plumbing, electrical, mechanical, contractor installed appliances, fixtures, and structural components,* unless a manufacturer warrants it separately. It transfers to the next owner* and cannot waive your other warranties.*

Do the warranty walkthrough at eleven months, not twelve, because three deadlines land at the end of year one.* Inside that year, written notice of a defect starts a forty five day repair clock,** and missing it opens the builder to state discipline.*

Nevada does not require 1-2-10. The two and ten year pieces come from your builder's warranty company.* The statutory ten years limits how long you have to sue,* running from the latest of final inspection, notice of completion, or occupancy.* It was six years from 2015 to 2019,* so anything claiming six is stale.

One honest limit

No Nevada statute lets you bring your own inspector onto a builder's job site.* Get it in the contract. A municipal inspection checks code minimums, and that inspector does not work for you.* A certified Nevada inspector does, and carries at least $100,000 in errors and omissions plus $100,000 in liability.**

Assume an arbitration clause in a builder contract is enforceable. A 2019 Nevada Supreme Court decision held federal law controls.* Ask a Nevada attorney. Blog: pre drywall, blue tape, month eleven, 1-2-10.
