What they mean The numbers, Q3 2026

What the numbers mean, and what they do not.

New against resale, measured two ways, because they answer different questions. Same month, June 2026: a new median of $525,000 against $490,000 for an existing single family home, a gap of about $35,000. That subtraction is mine.* A national study published June 22, 2026 counted differently: $504,460 against about $425,000, a gap of $79,460, ranking Las Vegas 45th against a $52,565 national average.** New homes were about 18 percent of the local market against 15 percent nationally.* They count different pools. Neither is wrong.

Resale context, June 2026. Condo and townhome median $292,000, down 4.3 percent.* 2,823 existing sales.* 7,147 single family listings without an offer, up 2.2 percent, and 2,690 condo listings, up 4.9 percent.** 78.5 percent sold within 60 days, down from 82.8 percent, and 23.2 percent paid cash.** From the local MLS, which misses builder sales.*

The incentive climate, dated May 2026, not a promise from any builder: buydowns of 1 to 1.5 points, design center credits of $5,000 to $15,000, HOA dues covered 30 to 60 days.* The builders association chairman described the same pattern.*

Two cautions come from a competing brokerage's own survey, not a neutral source: design center prices above what the same work costs elsewhere, and builder lenders quoting above the best outside rates in May 2026.**

One limitation I cannot fix. Every source here reports at the county or Southern Nevada level, so Mesquite and Boulder City sit inside these medians.

This page is rebuilt each quarter. The local association's domain did not resolve to its statistics page on July 25, 2026, so its figures come from two outlets reprinting the release.
