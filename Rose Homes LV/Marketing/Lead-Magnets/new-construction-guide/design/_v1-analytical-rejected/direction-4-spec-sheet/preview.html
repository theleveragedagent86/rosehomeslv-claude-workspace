19.0 SID and LID Sheet 19  ·  Rev A

The bill that does not come with your mortgage

A special assessment can ride along with a new home for thirty years. Nobody at the model home has to bring it up.

Las Vegas builds new neighborhoods on empty desert. Somebody has to pay for the streets, the sidewalks, the curbs, the streetlights, and the sewer and water lines that go in before the first house does.

Often that somebody is you.

A Special Improvement District, usually shortened to SID, is a charge on the properties inside the district. It pays for exactly that kind of public work. 1 In Henderson the same thing is normally called a Local Improvement District, or LID. Different letter. Same idea. 2

Here is the part that surprises people. The district spreads that cost over ten to thirty years at a low interest rate. 3 You do not clear it at closing. You pay a piece of it, twice a year, for decades.

19.2  /  CheckpointsFive things to know before you sign

  1. 01It is billed twice a year. The bill goes out about sixty days before it is due. 5
  2. 02It is not part of your property tax bill. It is billed separately, and it is usually not inside your monthly mortgage payment. 6
  3. 03It is a lien on the home until it is paid off. 6
  4. 04Missing a payment is serious. In unincorporated Clark County a late payment adds 2 percent a month on the unpaid balance plus interest, 7 and the county can begin foreclosure within sixty days of a delinquency. 8
  5. 05Two houses on the same street can owe different amounts. The cost is split by how much each lot benefits, calculated on a front foot, area, zone, or other fair basis. 9

Can you pay it off early? In unincorporated Clark County, yes, at any time, with a 3 percent prepayment penalty on the unpaid balance. 10 That is the county's rule. Districts inside a city can work differently. So ask for a written payoff quote for your district instead of assuming.

Detail A 19.1  /  Aside
Why you never voted on it. Most districts in new communities are created by what the county calls the developer method. It is negotiated between the developer and the government, and it does not require a protest hearing. 4 The district was in place before your lot had a house on it. There was no meeting for you to attend.

I am not going to hand you a number.

No government office and no developer publishes a per home or per community dollar amount for these assessments. I checked the Clark County Treasurer, Clark County Public Works, the City of Henderson, and the company that does the billing. None of them publishes one.

Third party websites do publish figures. They disagree with each other by more than five times for the same master plan.

A wrong number here would steer your budget for thirty years. So I would rather give you the lookup. It takes about five minutes, and it gives you the real number for your actual lot.

Look at districts 151 and 819. Both are in Summerlin. One made its final payment on June 1, 2025. 12 The other collects until April 1, 2055. 11

Same master plan. Thirty years apart.

That is why the community name tells you almost nothing and the lot number tells you everything.

Two communities publish that they carry none at all. The developer of Cadence states its homes have no SID or LID fees. 13 StoryBook publishes the same for Wellston Ridge. 14 Take that as a good sign, and still run the parcel. A community that is not on a published list is not proof that your lot is clear. 15

19.4  /  ProcedureRun this before you sign. It takes about five minutes.

  • 1.Get the parcel number. Ask the builder for the assessor parcel number for the exact lot you are buying. Not the community. The lot.
  • 2.Run it. Search that number at amgnv.com. The search covers Clark County and the cities of Las Vegas, North Las Vegas, and Henderson. 16
  • 3.Ask for four numbers in writing. The amount each year. The balance left. The year it ends. The penalty to pay it off early.
  • 4.Watch your closing date. If you close within about fifteen days of a billing date, tell escrow to pay the assessment at closing. It takes about fifteen days for the Assessor to change the mailing address, so the bill can go to the builder's old address and you will never see it. 17
Two numbers worth saving.

Assessment Management Group, which handles billing, payoffs, and lien releases for most valley districts, and also bills Henderson's districts: (702) 796-0082 18

Clark County Public Works, for unincorporated county districts: (702) 455-6000 19
