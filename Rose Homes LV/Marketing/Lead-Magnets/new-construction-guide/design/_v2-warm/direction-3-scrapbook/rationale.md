# Direction 3, Scrapbook. Rationale

## Thesis

A friend who already did this walked you through it and wrote in the margins.

One split governs everything. **The data is crisp. Everything around the data is handmade.** The twenty row district table is set clean, tight and deliberately boring. The arrow pointing at it, the highlighter on two of its rows and the note in the margin are the parts that look like a person. That split lets a scrapbook carry a lien schedule without becoming a craft project.

## Why it warms up protective content

Round one made the sourcing apparatus into the design, and the reader felt audited. The rigor is still here, it just stopped being the thing you look at. Sources are a quiet superscript plus one inline attribution, with full URLs in the appendix. That gave back roughly a quarter of every page, and it went to photography, white space and the margin.

The brief's real test is the NOT FOUND callout, answered here as literally as possible. **The refusal to print a number is a sticky note**, in real handwriting, with a terracotta sticker reading NOT FOUND, ON PURPOSE. A sticky note is what a friend leaves you, not a hole in the research. The headline is Caveat at 25pt because a person wrote it. The paragraphs under it are Nunito in two columns because a person still expects you to read them.

Same logic elsewhere. Five hard facts become "Five things I would tell a friend first". The lookup becomes "Do this before you sign. Five minutes, tops." on a card with real checkboxes. Nothing is softened: the 2 percent monthly penalty, the sixty day foreclosure window and the 3 percent prepayment penalty are at full strength. Only the speaker changed.

## Who it wins, who it loses

**Wins** the first time buyer who drove past a model home and is embarrassed to admit they do not know what they are looking at. Wins cold Facebook traffic, and wins the phone, because handwriting and photographs survive 375px better than a rule based system does.

**Loses** anyone who wants this to look institutional. An investor comparing four master plans will read the handwriting as decorative. If the target skews to move up buyers with large budgets, this is the riskiest of the six.

## Ink coverage, and the honest print answer

Measured off the rendered pages: mean ink density **17.6 percent on the cover, 11.0 on page 19, 10.0 on page 20**, approximate total area coverage 56, 38 and 33 percent. Far under a color blocked design, because this uses pastel objects on cream, not full bleed panels.

The cost driver is the cream flood, not the stickers. Printing `#FBF4E6` edge to edge across 44 duplex pages is a solid tint on 88 sides. **The fix is to not print it. Spec a warm natural uncoated stock and set the page background to nothing.** That kills the largest ink item and makes the texture real rather than simulated. On white house stock, keep the flood, expect a heavier bill, and expect the cream to shift pink on a laser.

## Grayscale survival

Tested by desaturating the rendered pages. It holds, with one fix applied: the highlighted rows sit close to the zebra tint once the yellow is gone, so they also carry a solid dark inset bar on the row header. Nothing depends on hue. NOT FOUND cells carry the words plus a dashed outline, Matured and Paid off carry bold words, and the diagram layers differ in tone as well as color. The circles, arrows and margin notes are pure line work, which is the one thing a home laser is good at.

## Mobile hero

At 1280 the hero is two columns, copy left, three taped photos and a torn map scrap right. At 375 the collage moves above the copy, drops the scrap, shrinks to two photos at 150px and 100px, and flattens rotations toward zero. The button goes full width, loses its hand drawn ring, and still clears the fold at 375 x 812.

## Signposting the three reader paths

A colored sticker tab plus a running head, not a new layout. The chips are set: teal, sage, plum. Each path opener gets its chip at full size with one handwritten line under it, every interior page repeats the path name in the verso running head, and the folio stays terracotta so the reader keeps two fixed anchors.

## What did not fit

The spread is over budget and says so rather than cutting. The copy runs about 900 words against an 840 word budget. Four things moved to hold two Letter pages at readable sizes: the five item list, the sticky note body and the prose after the table all set two column, and the checklist beside the phone card as one toolkit row. Body dropped to 9.2pt, the table to 7.5pt. Page 19 finishes with 11px of slack. **Add one sentence there and something has to leave.** The diagram is the candidate, the only object on the spread carrying no fact of its own.
