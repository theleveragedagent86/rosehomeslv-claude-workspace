# Direction 4: Postcard to Workbook, rationale

## Thesis

The front half sells you on the valley. The back half hands you a pen and gets to work.

One palette, one type system, one voice. What changes at page 18 is posture, not look. Pages 1 to 17
are a postcard: deckled photo edges, a stamp, a postmark, saturated desert sky. Pages 18 to 44 are a
workbook: checkboxes, ruled write in lines, labeled blanks, worksheet panels with a flag tab. The
divider says it out loud. "Put the postcard down. Bring a pen."

## Why this warms protective content

The rejected round made the sourcing apparatus the design and it read as an audit. This one makes the
**reader's own participation** the design, a warmth that survives hard information in a way a photograph
cannot.
The proof is the callout the brief singled out. "I am not going to hand you a number" is an amber panel
with a red border, a red flag reading **This one you fill in yourself**, and three ruled blanks labeled
*Your parcel number*, *Amount each year*, *Year it ends*, each with a hint ("ask the builder for the
lot, not the community"). The refusal stops being a hole and becomes a gift: the reader gets the three
lines where their own number goes. Amber means *a blank you fill in* throughout, so the table's NOT
FOUND cells use the same chip and dotted underline. All five marks read as one idea, not five apologies.

Headings are questions: "Hold on. What is this second bill?", "Q. How did this get decided without me?",
"Is your district on this list?" The lookup carries a flag reading **Five minutes, with a pen**, and its
third step has four blank lines in it, so the checklist becomes the task instead of describing it.

## Who it wins, who it loses

**Wins** the stated reader: already drove past a model home, overwhelmed, responds to being given
something to do. Wins anyone who prints it and anyone standing in a sales office. **Loses** anyone wanting a luxury object, and partially loses the screen only skimmer, because a
ruled blank is inert on a phone. Loses badly if the divider is cut, since without the gear change the
halves read as two different books.

## Print ink coverage

Not a cheap 44 page duplex job. Five to eight pages are full bleed saturated at roughly 180 to 240
percent total area coverage in CMYK, the divider's vermillion band worst of all. Workbook pages average
about 30 to 40 percent tinted area, nearly all light tint, with no full page floods. On a digital press all 44
pages bill at the color rate anyway, so the heavy pages are effectively free. The real cost is stock: coated 100gsm or heavier, or the cream fields blotch and
the 7.4pt table fills in.

## Grayscale survival

Tested. `--sunset` reduces to gray 103, `--cactus` to 98, `--sky` to 96, the same gray on a home
printer. Fatal if hue carried meaning; none does. NOT FOUND prints the literal words plus a dotted
underline, Matured and Paid off print a check glyph plus the word, props carry numerals, compare cards
carry district numbers. Panels stay legible because each is bounded by a border or a flag rather than by
fill, and the fills separate on the light end (249 / 238 / 234 / 230).

## Mobile hero

Single column below 900px. The photograph and scrim stay, because the photo is the warmth and dropping
it turns the page into a form. Below 420px the headline pins to 31px, props stack full width with
numerals intact, the button goes full width, and the trust grid collapses to one field per line so
`Real Broker, LLC` and license `S.0185572` never wrap mid string. No form and no fields, per Lofty.

## The three reader paths

Signposted on the divider under "Three ways to read the back half". *In a hurry* goes to the checklists,
*Doing your homework* to the prose and numbered sources, *Already at a model home* straight to page 20
and the two phone numbers. A tab chip on every workbook page repeats which mode you are in.

## What did not fit, said out loud

Nothing was cut and no fact, number, date, statute, or phone number changed. Four things moved: the
"Look at districts 151 and 819" prose became two compare cards carrying the same dates; "Same master
plan. Thirty years apart." became a bold lead in; the table intro moved into the `<caption>`; the
attribution moved to the running foot.

Two compromises. The table sits at **7.4pt over 1.07**, the price of twenty rows, four columns and a
full worksheet on one page; move the checklist to page 21 and it goes back to 8pt. Figure 4 takes about
**34 percent** of the page 19 column height, not the 45 percent specified, because the write in panel
had to be the larger object for this to work.
