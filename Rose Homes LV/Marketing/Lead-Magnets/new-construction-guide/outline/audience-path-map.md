**Prepared:** 2026-07-25
**Prepared by:** Guide Architect
**Companion to:** `guide-outline.md` and `page-budget.md`.

The guide splits three ways at **page 5** and rejoins at **page 18**. This file locks the reader-facing wording of the split, the wording of the rejoin, the navigation system that makes the split findable in print and on a phone, and every cross-reference in the book.

**Em-dashes in this file: zero.**

This is final copy. It sets on page 5 as written. Do not paraphrase it in the build.

>
> Find the one that sounds like your situation. Turn to that page. Skip the other two.
>

>
> ---
>
> ### A. I do not live in Nevada yet.
>
> You are moving here from somewhere else. Maybe you have visited. Maybe you have not. You will probably sign on a house you have stood inside once, or never.
>

>
> ---
>
> ### B. I own a home here now, and I want a bigger one.
>

>

>
> ---
>
> ### C. This is my first newly built home.
>
> Maybe your first home of any kind. Everything at the model home sounds reasonable, and you have no way to tell which parts are standard and which parts are a decision you are about to make.
>
> **Turn to page 14.**
>
> ---
>
> All three paths end on page 17. Everybody meets again on page 18.

---

### Why the branch is written this way

- **The reader names themselves.** None of the three headings uses a market term. There is no "relocation buyer", no "move-up buyer", no "first-time buyer segment". Each one is a sentence a person would actually say out loud.
- **Permission to read two.** Real readers straddle. A California owner selling a house back home fits A and B. Telling them to read both, and saying how short both are, costs nothing and prevents the feeling of having picked wrong.
- **The exit is stated before the entrance.** "All three paths end on page 17" tells a reader up front that this is a short detour, not a fork in the road they cannot come back from.
- **Second person, present tense, no hedging.** Matches voice exemplar 13, which normalizes the reader's uncertainty before it offers anything.
- **No CTA on this page.** Page 4 just made the primary ask. Page 5 is navigation. Stacking a second ask here would make the guide feel like a funnel instead of a guide.

## 2. The rejoin, in exact wording

### 2A. Foot of every path's last page (pages 9, 13, and 17)

Identical wording on all three, set identically, so a reader who reads two paths sees the same sign twice and is not confused about whether they are in the same place.

> **That is the end of your path.**
>
> Turn to page 18. Everything from there to the back of the guide is the same for all three readers.

### 2B. Head of page 18

> ## Everyone back together
>
> If you came from page 9, you are moving here from out of state. From page 13, you already own a home in the valley. From page 17, this is your first newly built home.
>
> From here on, the guide is the same for all three of you.
>
> The next ten pages are the part of buying new construction that does not care where you came from. The assessment on your lot, the tax reset in year one, the caps on what a builder is allowed to pay, the clock on your rate lock, and what your warranty actually covers. Every one of those lands the same way whether this is your first house or your fourth.

### 2C. Wording rules for the rejoin

1. **The three paths are named by page number, not by label.** "If you came from page 9" is concrete. "If you are a relocation buyer" is a category, and categories make readers second-guess whether they picked the right one.
2. **Never say "as we covered in your path."** A reader who read one path has no idea what the other two said, and reminding them there was content they skipped creates doubt. Each core page stands on its own.
3. **The rejoin does not summarize the paths.** It names what is coming, not what was missed.
4. **The rejoin appears once.** Do not repeat a "welcome back" beat on page 19 or anywhere later.

## 4. Edge tabs

### What they are

A vertical bleed tab on the **outer** edge of pages 6 through 17 only. Twelve tabbed pages, three tabs, four pages each. Nothing before page 6 and nothing after page 17 carries one, so the branching block is physically identifiable in a closed stack.

### Tab text

Set bottom-to-top along the outer edge, in caps, tracked open.

| Path | Tab text | Pages | Vertical position on the outer edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | `PATH A · MOVING HERE` | 6 to 9 | Top third |
| B | `PATH B · MOVING UP` | 10 to 13 | Middle third |
| C | `PATH C · FIRST BUILD` | 14 to 17 | Bottom third |

The stepped vertical position is the whole point. A closed stack shows three bands at three heights, so a reader thumbs straight to their path without opening the guide. This is the same device a phone book or a field manual uses, and it is the reason the guide is printable rather than merely printed.

### Three constraints the design directions must respect

1. **The three tabs must be distinguishable in grayscale.** A large share of readers will print this at home in black and white. Different hues at the same value collapse into three identical grays. Give them different **values**, not just different colors.
2. **The tab bleeds off the outer edge.** It must survive a trim variance of about 3mm on a home printer, so no text sits within 5mm of the trim.
3. **Tab text is redundant with the running head and the folio letter, on purpose.** If the tab does not print, the reader still knows which path they are in from two other places on the same page.

### On a phone, tabs do not exist

The guide is print-first and is also read on phones. Edge tabs are meaningless on a screen. So every path page carries a **text path label at the top of the page content**, above the headline, in the path's own color:

`PATH A · MOVING HERE`

That label is what the phone reader sees. It is set as real text, not baked into the tab graphic, and it must not be hidden by a print-only CSS rule.

## 5. The cross-reference map

Every pointer in the book. A cross-reference is written as **"see page N"** in body text, never as a bare number and never as "see above" or "see below", which are meaningless once a reader has jumped.

### 5A. The one rule that governs all of them

**No path page may point to another path page as required reading.** A reader who reads only Path C must never be sent to page 11. The only place the three paths are mentioned together is page 5, and there it is an invitation, not a dependency.

Path pages point **forward into the core**, **out to the blog**, or **out to the relocation guide**. Never sideways.

## 6. What a single-path reader misses, and how the guide covers it

A reader who reads one path reads 31 of 39 pages. Here is what falls outside their read and whether it matters.

| Content | Only in | Does a reader outside that path need it? |
|---|---|---|
| Remote signing, remote inspections, mail-away closing | Path A, p7 | Rarely. A local buyer is on site. **No coverage needed.** |
| Cash buyers losing the lender-tied incentive | Path A, p8 | Yes, and this is the one real leak. **Covered on p22**, which every reader sees, in the lender conditioning section. p8 is the expanded version, not the only version. |
| The 5 day cancellation right and its personal-inspection condition | Path A, p8 | Yes. **Covered again on p18** in the sequence, as one of the three legal deadlines, so every reader meets it once. |
| Selling while under contract, bridge and contingency questions | Path B, p11 | Only if they own a home. A Path C first-time buyer does not. **No coverage needed.** |
| **The seller CTA** | Path B, p11 | By design. It appears exactly once in the whole guide and is not repeated anywhere. A Path A reader selling a house in another state is not a Nevada listing lead, so there is no leak to fix. |
| The 3 percent cap you give up when you move up | Path B, p12 | Yes. **Covered in full on p21**, which every reader sees. p12 is the short move-up framing of the same facts. |
| The interested party contribution ladder | Path B, p13 | Yes. **Covered in full on p22** with the diagram. |
| The three builder business models | Path C, p14 | Yes, mildly. **Covered on p33 and p34** in the upgrades column, one line per builder. |
| Nevada down payment assistance programs | Path C, p15 | Only for buyers who qualify. Move-up and relocating buyers mostly do not, and the Worker Advantage residency rule excludes brand new arrivals outright. **No coverage needed.** |
| The Loan Estimate comparison method | Path C, p16 | Yes. **Referenced from p22 and p23**, which every reader sees, with a pointer to p16 for the detail. |
| The full monthly payment stack | Path C, p17 | Yes. **Partially covered on p19, p20 and p21**, which carry the two layers people actually miss. |

**Net result:** every fact that a reader genuinely needs regardless of path already appears somewhere in the shared 22 pages. The path pages carry framing, sequencing, and the two or three facts that only matter to that reader. That is the correct division of labor for a branching guide, and it means no reader can be harmed by picking the wrong path.
