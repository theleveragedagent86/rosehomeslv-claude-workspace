### Why this spread and not another

The brief offered a choice between the SID and LID section and the Incentives Decoded section. **SID and LID wins**, for four reasons:

1. It carries **every element type in the guide at once.** Six of the nine content types in `page-budget.md` appear on this one spread.
2. It has the **largest table in the book outside the builder pages**, at 20 rows and 4 columns, so a director cannot dodge the tabular problem.
3. It contains the guide's **defining editorial move**, which is refusing to print a number that cannot be verified and handing the reader a lookup instead. If a design direction cannot make a NOT FOUND callout feel like generosity rather than a gap, that direction fails on 34 of 39 pages.
4. It is the **highest-value section in the guide.** The fact ledger calls the per-lot assessment amount the single largest hidden carrying cost in Clark County new construction, and no public source publishes it.

**Runner-up, for the record.** Incentives Decoded (page 22) would test a flow diagram and a cap ladder harder than this spread does, but it has no long data table and no lookup checklist. If Ryan wants a second comparison round, that is the page to run.

# DELIVERABLE 1: THE COVER

## Recommended title

# Before You Walk Into the Model Home

**Why this one.** The reader has already driven past a model home. That is the stated audience. This title meets them at the exact moment they are in, and it is the only title of the four that also states the guide's single ask, which is to get represented before that first visit. It contains no hype word, no superlative, and nothing that can go out of date.

## Three alternates

1. **What Nobody Tells You at the Model Home**
   More curiosity, slightly more clickbait. Reads well as a Facebook ad headline and slightly less well on a printed cover.
2. **The Buyer's Side of New Construction**
   The most restrained option. Closest to the voice's core sentence about who works for whom. Weakest on urgency.
3. **Read This Before You Sign With a Builder**
   The most direct. Highest implied stakes. Risks sounding like a warning label rather than a guide, which cuts against the warm half of the voice.

## PAGE 19, LEFT

### [HEADLINE]

# The bill that does not come with your mortgage

### [DECK]

A special assessment can ride along with a new home for thirty years. Nobody at the model home has to bring it up.

### [BODY]

Las Vegas builds new neighborhoods on empty desert. Somebody has to pay for the streets, the sidewalks, the curbs, the streetlights, and the sewer and water lines that go in before the first house does.

Often that somebody is you.

A Special Improvement District, usually shortened to SID, is a charge on the properties inside the district. It pays for exactly that kind of public work. [1] <!-- sid-definition --> In Henderson the same thing is normally called a Local Improvement District, or LID. Different letter. Same idea. [2] <!-- sid-henderson-billed-by-amg -->

Here is the part that surprises people. The district spreads that cost over ten to thirty years at a low interest rate. [3] <!-- sid-term-length --> You do not clear it at closing. You pay a piece of it, twice a year, for decades.

### [PULL CALLOUT, labeled aside]

> **Why you never voted on it.** Most districts in new communities are created by what the county calls the developer method. It is negotiated between the developer and the government, and it does not require a protest hearing. [4] <!-- sid-developer-method --> The district was in place before your lot had a house on it. There was no meeting for you to attend.

### [SUBHEAD]

## Five things to know before you sign

### [NUMBERED LIST]

1. **It is billed twice a year.** The bill goes out about sixty days before it is due. [5] <!-- sid-billed-semiannually -->
2. **It is not part of your property tax bill.** It is billed separately, and it is usually not inside your monthly mortgage payment. [6] <!-- sid-separate-bill -->
3. **It is a lien on the home until it is paid off.** [6] <!-- sid-separate-bill -->
4. **Missing a payment is serious.** In unincorporated Clark County a late payment adds 2 percent a month on the unpaid balance plus interest, [7] <!-- sid-late-penalty --> and the county can begin foreclosure within sixty days of a delinquency. [8] <!-- sid-delinquency-foreclosure -->
5. **Two houses on the same street can owe different amounts.** The cost is split by how much each lot benefits, calculated on a front foot, area, zone, or other fair basis. [9] <!-- sid-apportionment -->

### [BODY, continued]

Can you pay it off early? In unincorporated Clark County, yes, at any time, with a 3 percent prepayment penalty on the unpaid balance. [10] <!-- sid-prepay-unincorporated, conflict cft-sid-prepay-penalty --> That is the county's rule. Districts inside a city can work differently. So ask for a written payoff quote for your district instead of assuming.

### [NOT FOUND CALLOUT, the largest visual object on page 19 after the diagram]

> ### I am not going to hand you a number.
>
> No government office and no developer publishes a per home or per community dollar amount for these assessments. I checked the Clark County Treasurer, Clark County Public Works, the City of Henderson, and the company that does the billing. None of them publishes one.
>
> Third party websites do publish figures. They disagree with each other by more than five times for the same master plan.
>
> A wrong number here would steer your budget for thirty years. So I would rather give you the lookup. It takes about five minutes, and it gives you the real number for your actual lot.
>
> <!-- NOT FOUND register item 14. Do not fill this with an estimate, an internal file, or a competitor's blog. -->

## Run this before you sign. It takes about five minutes.

- [ ] **1. Get the parcel number.** Ask the builder for the assessor parcel number for the exact lot you are buying. Not the community. The lot.
- [ ] **2. Run it.** Search that number at amgnv.com. The search covers Clark County and the cities of Las Vegas, North Las Vegas, and Henderson. [16] <!-- sid-parcel-search -->
- [ ] **3. Ask for four numbers in writing.** The amount each year. The balance left. The year it ends. The penalty to pay it off early. <!-- sid-lookup-steps -->
- [ ] **4. Watch your closing date.** If you close within about fifteen days of a billing date, tell escrow to pay the assessment at closing. ## What each design direction has to solve on this spread

A checklist for judging the six directions side by side.

1. **Does the NOT FOUND callout read as generosity or as a hole?** It is the most important object on page 19. If it reads as an apology, the direction has misunderstood the guide.
2. **Is the 20 row table readable at arm's length on paper?** Four columns, mixed data types, two cells reading "Matured" and one reading "Paid off". No zebra stripe so heavy it fights the source strip below it.
3. **Do the three NOT FOUND cells in the table look deliberate?** They appear five times on this spread. They will appear hundreds of times in the guide. They need a treatment, not a default.
4. **Does the checklist look like something a person would carry into a sales office?** Checkbox affordance, real hierarchy between the bold step name and the explanation.
5. **Does the source strip look like a feature or like fine print?** Nineteen entries with full URLs. Long URLs will break. Decide how.
6. **Does the spread read as one canvas or as two pages that happen to be adjacent?** The argument starts on the left and the tools are on the right. The design has to carry the reader across the gutter.
7. **Does the soft close survive being small?** It is one sentence and an offer. It must not look like an ad, and it must not disappear.
8. **Does it hold up in grayscale?** Home printers. Test it.

### Notes on the hero

- **The headline is a fact, not a claim.** One national builder states in its own buyer materials that its new home consultant represents the builder and is not your broker or agent. <!-- law-lennar-nhc-not-your-agent --> That is what makes this headline safe to run in a paid ad. It is also the calibration sentence for the entire guide's voice.
- **The three value props are all ledger-backed** and all sit in the guide's highest-value gap areas. Prop 1 is pages 19 and 20. <!-- sid-definition, sid-term-length, sid-parcel-search --> Prop 2 is page 21. <!-- tax-newbuild-no-cap-year-one, tax-postcard-claim, tax-cap-3-percent-primary --> Prop 3 is page 22. <!-- fin-ipc-cap-over-90-ltv, fin-ipc-includes-buydowns -->
- **No number in the hero can go stale.** No price, no rate, no incentive amount, no builder name. The 3 percent cap and the July 1 postcard are statute and county procedure, not market data.
- **Button alternates**, if a director wants a second option to test: "Get the free guide". Do not use "Download now", "Instant access", or anything with a countdown. Never a deadline, never scarcity.
- **Microcopy decision for Ryan.** "The PDF comes by email" is deliberately the only promise made, because a Lofty smart plan will follow. Do not write "no calls" or "no spam" unless Ryan is willing to hold to it.
- **Compliance.** The trust line carries the brokerage name and the license number, which Nevada requires in a licensee's advertising. <!-- law-nrs645-advertising --> **The license number is NOT FOUND and blocks publication of both the guide and the landing page.**
- **No form lives in this hero.** Per `lofty-constraints.md`, the landing page is an HTML embed and Lofty appends its own form below it at publish. The button is `<a href="#contact">` and page JS scrolls to the bottom of the document. Do not add a `<form>`, do not post to a webhook, do not collect a single field inside the embed.
- **`position: fixed` is safe on the landing page and forbidden in the guide.** Two files, two opposite rules.
