### p4. Register before the first visit, not after

**Points**
1. Registration happens at first contact, not at first signature. Some builders count a website inquiry or a phone call as first contact (`proc-register-agent-first-contact`, `law-lennar-broker-registration`).

### p8. If you are paying cash, read this twice

**Points**
1. Builder incentives are usually built around financing. A cash buyer can walk away from the largest concession in the deal without being told it existed.
2. Named, dated examples of the split. One builder states that up to $15,000 of an advertised up to $50,000 in flex cash was contingent on closing with its affiliated lender, and that any lender-tied incentive may not exceed 3 percent of the sales price (`bld-century-lender-split`, `bld-century-lender-cap`). Another states you are not required to finance with its affiliate to buy, but you must use it to get the advertised rate (`bld-drhorton-lender-conditioned`). A third states its offer requires financing through its affiliate (`bld-lennar-lender-conditioned`).
3. It is legal for a builder to price a discount around its own lender, as long as you can still buy without that lender, the discount is real, and it is not clawed back through higher fees elsewhere (`fin-respa-required-use`). And when a builder sends you to a company it owns, federal law requires a written Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure at the time of referral (`fin-aba-disclosure-required`, `fin-aba-disclosure-timing`).
4. The five day cancellation right has a condition most people never read. If your home is in a common interest community with an HOA, you may cancel in writing until midnight of the fifth calendar day after signing, **but only if you have not personally inspected the unit** (`law-nrs116-5-day-cancel`, `law-nrs116-cancel-condition`, `law-nrs116-cancel-refund`). A buyer who flies in, walks the home, and signs the same day may have signed that right away.
5. Pro tip in the house voice: get the affiliated lender's Loan Estimate anyway, even if you are paying cash, because it prices the concession you are giving up.

**Points**

Nevada taxes 35 percent of taxable value, not market value, and the Assessor builds taxable value from replacement cost minus depreciation plus land (`tax-assessment-ratio`, `tax-taxable-value-method`). ### p12. Your 3 percent tax cap does not move with you

**Points**
1. The move-up surprise. You have been sitting under a 3 percent annual cap for years. A brand new home gets no cap at all in its first fiscal year, and the cap starts the year after (`tax-newbuild-no-cap-year-one`).
2. Why the law works that way. The cap only applies to property that had a separate assessed value the year before, and it excludes value added by new improvements. A house that did not exist last year has nothing to cap against (`tax-cap-statutory-reason`).
3. Recording any ownership document knocks the cap off. You have to claim it back (`tax-recording-removes-cap`). The Assessor mails a postcard after July 1 to properties whose ownership changed. Sign it and send it back or you lose the 3 percent cap (`tax-postcard-claim`, `proc-tax-postcard-after-july1`).
4. Only one property per person statewide can carry the 3 percent cap (`tax-cap-one-property-rule`). If you keep the old house as a rental, it moves to the higher cap of up to 8 percent (`tax-cap-8-percent-other`). Falsely claiming the primary residence cap carries a penalty of three times the tax deficiency, plus the tax and any other penalty (`tax-false-claim-penalty`).
5. The escrow trap on the new house. The Assessor values property as it stands on July 1 and bills the July 1 owner, so your first bill can reflect the lot only (`tax-lien-date-july-1`). Ask your lender to escrow on the fully assessed completed home, or your payment jumps in year two (`proc-escrow-on-full-tax`).

### p13. What your equity actually buys, and selling again in year three

**Points**
1. The one real advantage of buying a new build with equity, and almost nobody explains it. On a conventional loan, how much a builder is allowed to pay toward your costs scales with your down payment: 3 percent of the price when your loan is over 90 percent of value, 6 percent between 75.01 and 90 percent, and 9 percent at 75 percent or less (`fin-ipc-cap-over-90-ltv`, `fin-ipc-cap-75-to-90-ltv`, `fin-ipc-cap-under-75-ltv`).
2. That cap includes builder-paid rate buydowns, which is why a builder cannot stack an unlimited buydown on unlimited closing cost help (`fin-ipc-includes-buydowns`). And builder money can never fund your down payment, your reserves, or your minimum contribution (`fin-ipc-not-down-payment`). Seller or builder money may cover up to 12 months of HOA assessments (`fin-ipc-hoa-dues-limit`).
3. One builder warns in writing that the combined value of seller incentives and credits may exceed your loan program's limits (`bld-century-concession-warning`). Ask your lender to check the cap before you accept the incentive package, not after.
4. Selling again in year three, the structural problem nobody names. When you resell, you compete against the builder's own brand new inventory, in your own neighborhood, with whatever incentive is running that month.
5. Two dated numbers that frame it, both labeled. In June 2026 the new home median was $525,000 and the existing single family median was $490,000, a gap of about $35,000 (`mkt-new-median-june-2026`, `mkt-resale-median-june-2026`, `mkt-gap-same-month`). A national study published June 22, 2026 measured the same gap at $79,460 using a different mix (`mkt-gap-clever-study`). Show both and say why they differ, per ledger conflict `cft-new-vs-resale-gap`.

### p14. "Included" means three completely different things

**Points**
1. Builders in this valley run three different business models, and the word "included" means something different in each one.
2. Model 1, fixed package, no upgrades. One builder has no design center and no upgrades at all, selling a set inclusion package (`bld-lgi-no-upgrades`).
3. Model 2, included features. One builder uses an included-features model instead of a buyer-facing design center for its standard product, and states that features vary by community and homesite (`bld-lennar-upgrade-model`). Another competes on standard inclusions such as owned solar, an EV outlet, a smart home package, and a tankless water heater (`bld-signature-standards`). A third tags nearly every Clark County community with solar included (`bld-beazer-solar`).
4. Model 3, design center. Several builders run studios: a Home Expressions Studio (`bld-pulte-design-center`), a Design Studio in Las Vegas at 5795 Badura Ave. #150 (`bld-kb-design-studio`), and Home Gallery showrooms (`bld-richmond-design-center`). One builder offers nine curated interior collections as an alternative to picking item by item (`bld-toll-design-collections`).
5. Two hard limits nobody puts on a flyer. Your design selections must be final before the build starts, at the one builder that publishes the rule (`bld-kb-selections-deadline`, `proc-selections-before-build`). And one builder states on every community page that buyers may be limited in the structural changes, options, and upgrades they can make (`bld-richmond-upgrade-limit`).
6. ### p26. Inspections, the punch list, and what your warranty really covers

**Points**
1. Four checkpoints where a buyer can catch problems: pre-drywall, blue tape walkthrough, final walkthrough, and the 11 month warranty walkthrough (`insp-four-checkpoints`).
2. Pre-drywall is the only time anyone sees the bones: framing, straps and hangers, plumbing and electrical runs, duct routing, flashing, and waterproofing (`insp-predrywall-purpose`). One builder describes the pre-drywall orientation as your chance to confirm the options you ordered are what was actually installed (`insp-kb-predrywall-verification`).
3. **Insist the punch list is written.** Nevada measures the statutory one year warranty from the completion of a written punch list, not from your closing date (`law-nrs624602-clock-start`, `insp-punch-list-written`, `proc-warranty-clock-punch-list`). Find that date and put it in your file.
4. What Nevada actually requires, which is not 1-2-10. State law requires a written warranty of at least one year covering home systems, workmanship, materials, plumbing, electrical, mechanical, contractor-installed appliances, fixtures, equipment, and structural components (`law-nrs624602-written-warranty`, `law-nrs624602-scope`). It transfers to the next owner (`law-nrs624602-transferable`) and cannot be used to waive other warranties (`law-nrs624602-no-waiver`). The two and ten year pieces come from the builder's warranty company, not from state law (`law-1-2-10-not-required`, `law-no-2-year-statute`). The ten year figure in Nevada law is a **deadline to sue**, not a promise to repair (`law-ten-year-is-deadline-not-warranty`, `law-nrs11202-repose`), running from the latest of final inspection, notice of completion, or certificate of occupancy (`law-nrs11-2055-clock-start`). It was six years from February 2015 to September 2019 and is ten now, so anything claiming six years is out of date (`law-repose-history`).
5. Do the warranty walkthrough at eleven months, not twelve, because three deadlines converge at the end of year one (`insp-11-month-reason`). Inside that first year, written notice of a defect starts a 45 day repair clock, and a builder who misses it is immediately subject to state discipline (`law-nrs40672-45-day-clock`, `law-nrs40672-discipline`, `proc-year-one-45-day-window`).
6. The honest limit on your access. **No Nevada statute gives a buyer the right to bring an independent inspector onto a builder's job site** (`insp-no-statutory-right`). It is a contract and site access question. Get it in the contract. Common objections buyers hear are national industry reporting, not a claim about any Las Vegas builder (`insp-builder-objections`, `insp-city-inspection-difference`, both low confidence, attribute in the sentence). A municipal inspection checks code minimums and the inspector does not work for you.
