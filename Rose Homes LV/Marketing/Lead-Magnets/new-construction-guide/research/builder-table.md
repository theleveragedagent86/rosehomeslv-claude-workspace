## How to read this table

This table is a list of facts. It is not a scorecard.

Ryan does not rank builders. There is no score here, no stars, and no "best" list. Every box holds one of three things: something the builder published on its own website, something an outside group published about the builder, or the words NOT FOUND.

**NOT FOUND means we looked and did not find it.** It does not mean the answer is bad. It usually means the builder does not put that answer on its website. Many builders do not publish their warranty terms or how long a home takes to build. When you see NOT FOUND, that is your cue to ask the sales office for it in writing.

**Every fact has a number next to it.** The number points to a footnote at the bottom of this page. Each footnote gives the web address we read and the date we read it. Every fact here was checked on July 25, 2026.

**Prices move.** Each price is the builder's own "starting at" number on July 25, 2026. Starting prices almost never include the lot premium, options, or upgrades. Builders change them week to week.

**Incentives expire.** Where a builder's rate offer is shown, it is shown with the dates the builder attached to it. Those offers are printed as dated examples of how a builder structures an incentive, not as an offer you can still get. Ask what is running today.

**The builders are listed A to Z.** That is the only order used. Do not read the order as a ranking.

**Check anything that matters to you.** The links in the footnotes are free and take about a minute each. If a line here raises a question, ask the builder directly, and ask Ryan to help you read the answer.

### About the studies named in this table

Two named studies appear in the third-party column. A score with no method attached is not usable information, so here is what each one is.

**Lifestory Research 2026 America's Most Trusted Home Builder Study.** Lifestory Research collected 62,596 opinions from people across the United States who said they were actively shopping for a new home over the previous 12 months, in the largest United States housing markets, and rated the 20 largest home builders in the country. The output is a Net Trust Quotient Score per builder brand. The study is national. It does not publish a Las Vegas result. A builder that is not one of the 20 largest is not in the study at all, and that absence says nothing about the builder [9].

**31st Annual Homebuyers' Choice Awards, published by Zonda, 2026.** Based on more than 210,000 verified homebuyer surveys collected through TrustBuilder CX powered by Eliant, covering more than 250 homebuilders nationwide. The surveys cover the purchase, the move-in, and the first year of ownership. Awards are given by category and by builder division, so a division-level result can be city specific [125].

**On J.D. Power.** J.D. Power cancelled its 2011 United States New-Home Builder Customer Satisfaction Study and no later edition was found. Any Las Vegas builder marketing that cites J.D. Power for new-home builder satisfaction is citing something from 2010 or earlier. Ask what year and what study.

## How this table was built

- Builder facts come from each builder's own live web pages, loaded on July 25, 2026. Where a builder does not publish a fact, the cell says NOT FOUND rather than filling the gap with what the industry usually does.
- Third-party records come from three kinds of source only: Better Business Bureau business profiles found through the directory search at footnote [149] with the location set to Las Vegas, NV, the Nevada State Contractors Board public license search at footnote [8], and named research studies that publish their method. Anonymous review-site star averages, Zillow builder profiles, Yelp, Houzz, and Google review counts were deliberately excluded and must not be added later.
- Where a builder has no Las Vegas area Better Business Bureau profile, the company headquarters profile was used and is labeled as such in that builder's cell. Two builders in the table are in that position: LGI Homes, whose rating shown is on a Texas headquarters profile, and Toll Brothers, whose rating shown is on a Pennsylvania headquarters profile.
- Board disciplinary actions can also be searched directly at footnote [150].
- The Nevada State Contractors Board search is a form submission and produces no permanent link to a single license record. Every state license citation therefore points at the public search page, footnote [8], and the cell names the exact license number that reproduces the record.
- A Nevada State Contractors Board license detail page carries a section headed "Disciplinary Action During Last 5 Years" when such an action exists. Where a cell says no such section appears, that means the section was absent from the public record on July 25, 2026. It does not mean no action exists outside that five year window, and it does not mean no action is pending.
- What was not checked: Clark County and City of Las Vegas building permit histories, Nevada Secretary of State entity standing, Nevada District Court construction defect filings, and any NRS Chapter 40 notices. Those are real public records that could change the picture. They were outside the scope of this research and are named here so nobody assumes they were covered.
