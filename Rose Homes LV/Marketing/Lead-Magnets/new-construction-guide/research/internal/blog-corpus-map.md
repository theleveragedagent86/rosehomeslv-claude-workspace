## READ THIS FIRST: how to use this file

Every number in this document came out of Ryan's own blog corpus. Those posts all carry `"datePublished": "2026-03-02"` in their schema markup, and the source files were last written on 2026-03-06. That makes them **roughly five months old as of 2026-07-25**.

**No number in this file is a citable fact.** Each one is a **verification lead**. It tells an external researcher what Ryan has already told the public, so the researcher can go confirm it, update it, or contradict it against a live primary source. Nothing here may be copied into the guide with a citation. A number only enters the guide once it carries a real citation in the form `value [source: <full URL> | verified: 2026-07-25]`.

Internal claims in this file are marked like this:

`[internal: <file> | published 2026-03-02 | VERIFY]`

**Blog URL pattern:** `rosehomeslv.com/blog/<slug>`. The path segment is singular. `rosehomeslv.com/blogs/<slug>` returns a 404. Every internal link in the guide must use `/blog/`.

**Publish status is NOT FOUND.** These 110 files exist on disk and were formatted for the Lofty CMS, but nothing on disk confirms which of them actually went live on rosehomeslv.com. A `QA-Report-No-Title-Posts.xlsx` in the same folder suggests at least some had publishing problems. **Before the guide links to any slug, someone must load the live URL and confirm it resolves.** Do not ship a lead magnet full of 404s.

### 2H. Builder registration rule (the corpus's strongest single position)

Two full posts assert this, and it recurs in the FAQ of the Ironwood buyer guide:

- You must bring or register your agent **on the first visit**, including phone, email, or online chat contact with a specific community.
- If you visit unregistered, builders will generally refuse to add your agent later.
- The builder pays the buyer agent commission, so representation costs the buyer nothing.
- The corpus explicitly claims the commission is **already priced into the home**, so an unrepresented buyer gets no discount, the builder simply keeps it.
- Named as enforcing this: Lennar, DR Horton, KB Home, Pulte, Toll Brothers, Richmond American, Tri Pointe.

Where: `bring-realtor-first-visit-model-homes`, `do-you-need-realtor-new-construction-las-vegas`

**Risk:** MEDIUM to HIGH. Buyer-agent compensation practice has been in flux industry-wide. The "builder pays, costs you nothing" framing is the corpus's most-repeated commercial claim and it is the one most likely to have changed. **This must be re-verified with current Las Vegas builder registration policies before the guide repeats it**, and the guide should describe how compensation is documented rather than flatly promising it is free.

## PART 3: Coverage gaps (the guide's highest-value sections)

These are things a Las Vegas new construction buyer genuinely needs and that **do not appear anywhere in the 110-post corpus.** Verified by keyword search across every post. These are where the guide earns its download.

### Tier 1: high-stakes money gaps with zero coverage

1. **Rate lock strategy across a 6 to 9 month build.** The corpus explains the preferred lender and when to refinance, but nothing explains extended locks, float-downs, lock extension fees, or what happens to your qualification if rates move while your house is being framed. Keyword `rate lock` appears only twice, in passing. **This is the single biggest gap.**

2. **The appraisal problem on new construction.** Zero coverage. Nothing on what happens when a home contracted at today's price appraises below that price eight months later, who eats the gap, whether builder incentives affect the appraised value, or how builder comps in a still-selling community distort the number. Keyword `appraisal gap`: zero hits.

3. **Selling your current home while you are under contract on a build.** Zero coverage. No bridge financing, no rent-back, no contingency discussion, no "what if my house does not sell". Keywords `bridge loan`, `sell your current`: zero hits.

4. **Homeowners insurance for a new build.** Zero coverage anywhere in 110 posts. No cost range, no builder's risk explanation, no note that the policy must be bound before closing. Keyword `homeowners insurance`: zero hits.

5. **Nevada's 3% versus 8% property tax cap and the new construction reset.** The corpus states the 3% primary-residence cap but never explains the 8% cap on other property, never explains that the cap election is address-specific and must be filed, and never warns that a brand-new home's first tax bill may be assessed on land only and jump the following year. Keywords `8 percent`, `abatement`, `3 percent cap`: zero hits. **This is a classic new-build surprise and it is completely uncovered.**

6. **Title insurance and who controls escrow on a builder deal.** One passing mention inside the closing costs post. Nothing on the builder choosing title and escrow, what that means for the buyer, or whether the buyer can shop it.

### Tier 2: lot and community selection gaps

7. **How to pick a lot, beyond the premium.** `lot-premiums-explained-las-vegas-new-construction` covers only what premiums cost. Nothing covers sun orientation in a 110-degree climate (west-facing backyards, afternoon glass load), backing to an arterial road, proximity to a retention basin or wash, power line easements, sound walls, or being downhill in a drainage path. Keywords `retention basin`, `power line`, `sound wall`, `arterial`, `west-facing`, `sun orientation`: all zero hits. **In this climate, lot orientation is a comfort and utility-bill decision, and nobody at the model home volunteers it.**

8. **Flood zone and desert drainage.** Zero coverage. Keyword `flood`: one incidental hit in a Toll Brothers post. Washes, detention basins, and FEMA zone status are real considerations in the valley and appear nowhere.

9. **Phase pricing and lot release mechanics.** Nothing explains how builders release lots in phases, why the price goes up between phases, why the best lots are gone before public release, or how to get on a release list. Keyword `phase release` returns only community-buildout mentions.

10. **Living inside an actively building community.** Nothing on construction dust, truck traffic, unpaved streets, unfinished amenities, or the multi-year gap between "amenity center coming" and the amenity center existing. Keyword `construction traffic`: zero hits.

11. **School zoning volatility in brand-new communities.** `best-school-zones-henderson-new-construction` covers Henderson zones as they stand. Nothing covers the actual new-build problem: the zoned school may not be built yet, boundaries get redrawn as the community fills, and the school your builder's flyer names may not be the school your child attends. This is one of the most common and most upsetting surprises for family buyers.

### Tier 3: process and protection gaps

12. **Vetting a builder before you buy.** `file-complaint-nevada-contractors-board` explains how to file a complaint after something goes wrong. Nothing explains how to look up a builder's NSCB license status and complaint history **before** signing. Keyword `NSCB license`: zero hits. This is a five-minute action that belongs in a protective guide.

13. **The actual questions to ask at the model home.** No post is a question list. The corpus tells the reader traps exist but never hands them a script. A one-page "ask these before you sign" list is the most screenshot-able, most shareable thing a guide like this can contain.

14. **Comparing two builders apples to apples.** Nothing gives the reader a worksheet for putting two base spec sheets side by side. The corpus compares builders editorially but never gives the reader a tool.

15. **Cash buyers.** Zero coverage. Keyword `cash buyer`: zero hits. A meaningful share of California relocators arrive with cash, and incentives are usually structured around financing, so cash buyers routinely lose the biggest concession without being told.

16. **What happens to your incentive if you switch lenders mid-build.** Referenced obliquely in the preferred-lender post, never answered directly.

17. **Out-of-state and remote buyers.** `moving-california-las-vegas-new-construction` covers the why, not the how. Nothing on remote contract signing, remote inspections, powers of attorney, or mail-away closings. Keyword `remote clos`: zero hits.

18. **Buying the model home, and leaseback.** Zero coverage. Keyword `model home leaseback`: zero hits.

19. **Short-term rental rules in Clark County for a new build.** Two glancing mentions only. Given how many buyers ask, and how restrictive local rules are, this deserves a plain answer.

20. **55+ age qualification mechanics.** Four posts cover 55+ communities. None explains how age verification actually works, how many occupants must meet the age minimum, or the rules on younger household members. Keywords `age verification`, `one occupant`: zero hits.

21. **Resale in year three, while the builder is still selling.** Several posts mention resale value in passing, but nothing addresses the core structural problem: when you sell, you are competing against the builder's own brand-new inventory with brand-new incentives, in your own neighborhood.

### Recommended guide architecture from these gaps

The corpus is strong on **what things cost** and weak on **what to do and when**. That points the guide at a sequenced, decision-by-decision structure rather than another list of prices:

1. Before you visit anything (agent registration, builder vetting, budget, lender)
2. Choosing the community and the lot (the gaps in Tier 2)
3. The contract (existing coverage, linked, plus the appraisal and rate-lock gaps)
4. The build (inspections, existing coverage, plus phase and delay reality)
5. Closing and the first year (existing coverage, plus insurance and the tax reset)
