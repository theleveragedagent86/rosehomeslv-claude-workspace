## 1. New home median closing price

## 4. Months of supply

"Months of supply" answers one question. If nothing new came on the market, how long would it take to sell everything that is for sale right now? Lower means tighter. Higher means more choice for buyers.

### Resale homes

**About 3.5 months of supply** in June 2026, based on the June sales pace. [source: https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/07/07/report-las-vegas-home-prices-hover-record-high-june-sales-climb/ | verified: 2026-07-25]

Source is Las Vegas REALTORS, using their Multiple Listing Service. [source: https://nevadabusiness.com/2026/07/lvr-reports-local-home-prices-hovering-at-record-high/ | verified: 2026-07-25]

Supporting inventory counts for June 2026:

- 7,147 single family homes were listed for sale with no offer on them, up 2.2 percent from a year earlier. [source: https://nevadabusiness.com/2026/07/lvr-reports-local-home-prices-hovering-at-record-high/ | verified: 2026-07-25]
- 2,690 condos and townhomes were listed with no offer, up 4.9 percent from a year earlier. [source: https://nevadabusiness.com/2026/07/lvr-reports-local-home-prices-hovering-at-record-high/ | verified: 2026-07-25]
- 2,823 existing homes, condos and townhomes sold in June 2026. Home sales were up 18.3 percent and condo and townhome sales were up 1.2 percent from June 2025. [source: https://nevadabusiness.com/2026/07/lvr-reports-local-home-prices-hovering-at-record-high/ | verified: 2026-07-25]

Note: other outlets have published different months of supply figures for the same period, ranging from about 2.6 to about 3.8 months. Those come from different brokerages slicing the data different ways. The 3.5 month figure above is the one in the Las Vegas REALTORS release itself, so that is the one used here.

## 6. The incentive climate

This section describes the general mood of the market. It is **not** a promise. No builder owes you any of this. Incentives change month to month, community to community, and sometimes house to house.

**What is happening.** Builders are selling fewer homes than last year, by a wide margin (see section 2). When builders sell fewer homes, they usually compete harder for the buyers who are still shopping. That is what is showing up now.

Reported across the valley in 2026: rate buydowns of about 1 to 1.5 points, design center credits in the range of $5,000 to $15,000, and homeowners association dues covered for 30 to 60 days. [source: https://vegasinc.lasvegassun.com/business/2026/may/12/las-vegas-builders-shrink-homes-slash-prices-as-af/ | verified: 2026-07-25]

Builders are also building smaller homes and using less expensive finishes to hold the price down. [source: https://vegasinc.lasvegassun.com/business/2026/may/12/las-vegas-builders-shrink-homes-slash-prices-as-af/ | verified: 2026-07-25]

Nationally, the chairman of the National Association of Home Builders said builders were offering sales incentives and cutting prices, and that many buyers were waiting on the sidelines for lower mortgage rates. [source: https://www.reviewjournal.com/business/housing/las-vegas-homebuilders-sales-keep-tumbling-this-year-3854606/ | verified: 2026-07-25]

**The four things builders usually offer.** Know the names so you can ask for them by name:

1. **Rate buydown.** The builder pays money up front to lower your interest rate. It can be temporary, like a rate that is lower for the first year or two and then steps up, or permanent for the life of the loan. A temporary buydown means your payment goes up later. Make sure you can afford the payment after it steps up, not just the first year payment.
2. **Closing cost credit.** The builder pays some of your closing costs. This is usually tied to using the builder's own lender.
3. **Design center credit.** Money toward upgrades like flooring, counters, and cabinets. Watch the pricing at the design center. Upgrade prices there are often well above what the same work costs elsewhere, so a credit is worth less than the number on it. [source: https://www.nevadarealestategroup.com/blog/new-construction-incentives-las-vegas-may-2026-builder-roundup/ | verified: 2026-07-25]
4. **Standing inventory discount.** A finished or nearly finished home with no buyer. These usually carry the biggest package, because the builder is paying to carry that house every month it sits.

**Two things to watch.**

Most of the largest incentives require you to use the builder's preferred lender. That lender's rate is not always the best rate available. Get a competing quote from an outside lender and compare the total cost, not just the incentive dollar amount. One published local comparison in May 2026 found builder lenders quoting rates above the best outside quotes. [source: https://www.nevadarealestategroup.com/blog/new-construction-incentives-las-vegas-may-2026-builder-roundup/ | verified: 2026-07-25]

Builders would usually rather give you a credit than cut the sticker price. A lower recorded sale price affects what they can charge the next buyer in that community. That is why you tend to see credits and buydowns instead of price cuts.

## 7. New versus resale price gap

There is more than one honest answer, because the two sides get counted by different groups. Both are shown here.

**Comparison A. Same month, June 2026.**

- New home median closing price: $525,000 [source: https://www.reviewjournal.com/business/housing/las-vegas-homebuilders-sales-keep-tumbling-this-year-3854606/ | verified: 2026-07-25]
- Existing single family median sale price: $490,000 [source: https://nevadabusiness.com/2026/07/lvr-reports-local-home-prices-hovering-at-record-high/ | verified: 2026-07-25]
- **Difference: $35,000, or about 7 percent more for new.** (This subtraction was done here, using the two figures above. Neither source publishes this gap.)

**Comparison B. A national study published June 22, 2026, by Clever Real Estate.**

- New home median: $504,460
- Existing home median: about $425,000
- **Difference: $79,460**
- Las Vegas ranked 45th in the country on the size of this gap. The national average gap was $52,565.
- New homes were about 18 percent of the Las Vegas market, against 15 percent nationally.

[source: https://www.reviewjournal.com/business/housing/want-a-brand-new-home-in-las-vegas-itll-cost-you-nearly-80k-more-3841482/ | verified: 2026-07-25]

**Why the two answers differ.** The Clever study puts the existing home median at about $425,000. Las Vegas REALTORS puts the June 2026 existing single family median at $490,000. Those are different because they are measuring different pools of homes. The Las Vegas REALTORS figure is single family homes only, sold through the local MLS. The Clever figure appears to use a broader mix. Neither is wrong. They answer different questions.

**What a buyer should take from this.** New costs more than used. In this valley the gap is somewhere between about $35,000 and about $79,000 at the middle of the market, depending on how you count. Some of that gap buys real things: a warranty, new systems, current code, nothing to repair for a while. Some of it is just the price of new.

Do not shop off medians. A median is the middle of thousands of homes across the whole valley. Compare the actual home you are looking at against actual resale homes in the same zip code, with the same bedroom count and roughly the same size. That comparison is the one that matters.

## 8. What these numbers describe about timing

These are observations about the numbers on this page as of 2026-07-25. They are not predictions. Nobody knows what prices or rates will do next, including anyone who tells you otherwise.

1. **Prices are flat, not falling and not climbing.** New home medians moved sideways all year, ending June 2026 up 0.3 percent from a year before. Resale was up 1.0 percent. Neither side of the market is moving fast right now.

2. **Builders are selling a lot less than last year.** Net sales down 15 percent, closings down 22 percent, permits down about 25 percent through June. That is the condition a buyer is walking into. A builder who is behind on sales has more reason to work with you than one who is sold out. That describes today, not tomorrow.

3. **Resale supply is still on the tight side, at about 3.5 months.** So the negotiating room is not the same everywhere. Right now there is generally more room to ask a builder for help than to ask a typical resale seller. Test this on the specific homes you are actually considering.

4. **Rates moved every week this month, inside a narrow band of 6.43 to 6.58 percent.** A rate you are quoted today is not the rate you get at closing unless it is locked. On a new build that may be months out, ask early and in writing how the lock works and what it costs to extend it.

5. **The best terms tend to sit on homes that are already built.** Builders pay to carry a finished house that nobody has bought. That is the most consistent pattern in this market. It also means less choice, since the colors and options are already picked.

## Geography and coverage notes

**What this snapshot covers.** Clark County, Nevada. That includes Las Vegas, North Las Vegas, Henderson, Summerlin, Spring Valley, Enterprise, Paradise, Centennial Hills, Skye Canyon, Mountain's Edge, Inspirada, Cadence, Union Village, and Sunrise Manor.

**Pahrump: excluded and confirmed excluded.** Pahrump is in Nye County. It is not in Clark County and it is not in the Las Vegas, Henderson and Paradise metro area. No source used on this page includes it. Nothing had to be stripped.

**Mesquite and Boulder City: a limitation, disclosed.** Both cities are inside Clark County. Every source on this page reports at the county or metro or "Southern Nevada" level, and none of them break out city level rows that could be removed. So Mesquite and Boulder City are inside these totals and cannot be separated out.

- Census series LASV832BP1FH covers the Las Vegas, Henderson and Paradise metro area, which is exactly Clark County, so it includes both cities.
- Las Vegas REALTORS reports "Southern Nevada" MLS activity, which includes both cities.
- Home Builders Research reports "Southern Nevada" builder activity, which includes both cities.

**Rows actually stripped: none.** No source produced a city level table containing Pahrump, Mesquite, or Boulder City rows. There was nothing to remove. The limitation above is disclosed instead of hidden. Both cities are small relative to the valley, so their effect on these valley wide medians and counts is minor, but it is not zero.
