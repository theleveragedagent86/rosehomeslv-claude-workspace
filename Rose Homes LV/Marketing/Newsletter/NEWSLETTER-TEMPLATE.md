# The Rose Report: Newsletter Template

Weekly consumer newsletter for Rose Homes LV. Built to ride on the weekly `/local-news` run, so the research is already done and verified before a single word gets written.

**Audience:** Clark County sphere, past clients, leads. Locals, not agents.
**Cadence:** Weekly, Thursday morning (weekend events are fresh, and it lands mid-week of the IG posting cycle).
**Length:** 700 to 900 words. About a 4 minute read.
**Source of content:** that week's `Content/Instagram/Local News/<date>/` folder.

---

## Part 1: What the inspiration newsletters have in common

Pulled from 9 issues across Beacon, Ellavate, The Chris List, ListingLeads (Jimmy Mackin), Elea, and Coffee & Contracts.

**Beacon, Ellavate and The Chris List are all the same team** (Chris Smith, with Jimmy Mackin on Beacon, under Knwn Local). That is why your two favorites feel alike. Their formula:

1. **Acrostic section names.** Beacon = B.I.T.S. (Best in Class, Ideas, Traffic, Seller Signals). Ellavate = E.L.L.A. (Engagement, List Growth, Launch Pad, Assignment). Chris List = C.H.R.I.S. (Content, How-To, Research, Inspiration, Stats). Same sections every issue, so readers learn where things live.
2. **Short hook intro.** 4 to 8 one-line paragraphs that set up a tension ("Seen is passive. Remembered is what turns a scroll into a showing.").
3. **Agenda block** listing each section with a one-line tease, then "Let's dive in 👇".
4. **Each section:** tag label, a big headline, short paragraphs, one real example with a source link, then a translation for the reader ("Why this matters for agents", "Realtor remix", "Steal this"), then a one-line Takeaway.
5. **Links woven into sentences.** Never "click here". Every claim links to the original source.
6. **One ask at the end.** A single CTA (webinar, trial), repeated in the P.S.
7. **Signed by a real person.** Name plus Instagram handle.

**Subject line patterns that repeat:**
- Emoji + big number + curiosity: "🏦 $400M home just hit Zillow. Here's what 140,000 views actually mean."
- Stop / start: "🛑 Stop forwarding showing feedback. Start doing this instead."
- Result + mechanism: "☕️ One coffee email. 17 replies, 2 listings."
- Unexpected pairing: "⛪️ The Pope vs. AI (and how it impacts Realtors)"
- Preheader always starts with "Plus," and teases the second story.

**Other steals:**
- **Ellavate, July 16 issue** literally tells agents to run a "This Weekend in [Town]" events section, because it is the section readers forward to their spouse. Ellavate also says community content earns opens and market content converts. That is your local news strategy, so the newsletter is an easy fit.
- **Ellavate, Sept 17:** add a timeline qualifier to the CTA ("If you're selling in the next 6 months, see what sold in Summerlin"). Clicks become hand-raisers.
- **Ellavate:** 3 to 5 meaningful links per issue, about 60% text and 40% images, never an all-image email. Key text (address, CTA) stays live text.
- **Ellavate (Tricia Lee):** "Sell the borough, not the building." A newsletter should read like a love letter to your zip code, not an MLS feed. Hers took 7 months to produce a lead.
- **Coffee & Contracts:** local pride and light roasts of neighboring areas get shared the most. Narrow and insider-y beats broad.
- **Jimmy Mackin:** very short issues, one idea, one script, done. Good model for a "quick hit" week.
- **Elea:** chatty, first person, emoji-heavy, one link. Lowest effort format. Good as a fallback.
- **Every one of them ends with a reply prompt or a single ask.** Replies help deliverability.

## Part 2: The format

### Name and structure: R.O.S.E.

Same acrostic trick as Beacon and Ellavate, and it spells your name.

| Section | Name | What goes in it | Pulled from local-news |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[R]** | **Right Now** | The #1 local story of the week and what it means for people who live here | Top story in `top-stories.md` (highest viral score, any category) |
| **[O]** | **Out and About** | "This Weekend in Las Vegas": 4 to 5 real events with dates and links, plus one line from Ryan | Local News and Events stories (events, openings, closings) |
| **[S]** | **Sold and Stats** | One market number, what it means for buyers and sellers, one neighborhood example | Real Estate Market stories, especially the national-to-local reframe |
| **[E]** | **Everything Else** | 3 to 5 one-line quick hits: roads, schools, development, Golden Knights | Gov/Dev, School Board, Hockey, road closures |

Close with **Ryan's Pick** (one personal recommendation) and a **reply question**.

### Rules
- No em-dashes. 6th-grade reading level. Short paragraphs, 1 to 3 sentences.
- Every fact comes from that week's verified local-news research, with the source linked inline. Anything unverified gets `[NOT VERIFIED]` and must be fixed before sending.
- 3 to 5 outbound links plus 1 CTA button. Link to credible local sources (Review-Journal, Clark County, CCSD, Redfin), not only rosehomeslv.com.
- Link the specific thing inside the sentence. Never "click here".
- Real text for every headline, address, and CTA. Images are extra, never the only place the info lives.
- One CTA per issue, with a timeline qualifier. Soft, never pushy.
- Link to the matching Instagram reel inside each section ("Watch the 45-second version"). The newsletter and IG feed each other.

## Part 5: Weekly workflow

1. Run `/local-news` as usual (Sunday or Monday).
2. Wednesday: open that week's `top-stories.md` and `weekly-summary.md`. Pick 1 for Right Now, 4 to 5 for Out and About, 1 for Sold and Stats, 3 to 5 for Everything Else.
3. Check that every Out and About event is still upcoming and every link works. (Ellavate's warning: one dead link or last year's festival hurts your credibility.)
4. Fill in the template. Add Ryan's take, Ryan's Pick, and the reply question in your own voice. That personal layer is what AI can't fake.
5. Swap in the matching IG reel links for any reels already live.
6. Thursday, before sending: update LAST week's issue. Swap every "Reel drops..." line for the real reel link (each one carries a hidden code comment naming its reel, like P06). Only the web version changes; sent emails can't. Instagram blocks Claude's lookups at times, so Ryan may need to paste the reel links.
7. Add the catch-up line (under "Hey #lead_first_name#,") linking to that updated web post.
8. Send Thursday morning. Reply to every response.

**Never send a second "updated" email for the same week.** It reads as spam and hurts deliverability. Late reels get caught up by step 6 and the catch-up line instead.

**beehiiv facts behind steps 6 and 7 (checked 2026-09-24):**
- Posts publish to both email and web by default (API `platform: "both"`), and each gets a web URL like `rosehomeslv.beehiiv.com/p/<slug>`. Link the catch-up line there.
- beehiiv allows editing a sent post's web version (content, title, thumbnail). The email already delivered does not change.
- The API can edit posts (`PATCH /publications/{id}/posts/{postId}` with `body_content`), but only on the **Max and Enterprise plans**, the same as creating posts. After the Max trial ends, on the free plan, do steps 6 and 7 in the beehiiv editor through Chrome instead.

**Note:** Thursday send is confirmed by Ryan (2026-09-24), even though about half the week's reels aren't live yet. Steps 6 and 7 cover the gap.
