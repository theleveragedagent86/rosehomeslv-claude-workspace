📍 [R] Right Now

Your Property Tax Cap Could Be 3 Percent or 8 Percent

Everyone in the valley has a theory about investors buying up the block. Now there is a real county number.

County tax records through 2025 show 332,040 of Clark County's 775,199 residential parcels are not lived in by their owner. That is about 43 percent, per the Review-Journal.

The count is countywide, with no split for Summerlin, Henderson, or North Las Vegas. A February study by Arbor ranked Las Vegas sixth in the country for share of renters, at 44.9 percent.

Here is the part most owners never check. Nevada has two caps on how fast your property tax bill can grow each year. If the home is where you live, the cap is 3 percent. If it is a second home, a vacation home, most rentals, or vacant land, it can go up to 8 percent.

Same street, very different tax bill over time. If the county has your home flagged wrong, you could be on the 8 percent side without knowing it.

What it means for you

If you own: Look up your parcel on the Clark County Assessor site and make sure it shows as your primary home.

If you're buying or renting: About 4 in 10 residential properties here belong to someone who lives elsewhere. If you buy, make sure your new home gets the 3 percent cap.

Freddie Mac says the 30-year fixed rate averaged 6.95 percent last week. A year ago it was 6.26 percent. It was the fourth straight weekly jump.

So I ran the math on a real local home. The Southern Nevada median single-family price was $475,000 in August. With 20 percent down, that is a $380,000 loan. At today's rate the payment is about $2,515 a month in principal and interest. At last year's rate it was about $2,342. That is roughly $173 more a month on the same house. (These are my own estimates, not published figures.)

Here is the good news for buyers. The typical Las Vegas home sold for 98.0 percent of its list price in August, so there is still room to negotiate.

What it means for you

If you're selling: Buyers are watching every dollar of that payment, so price it right from day one.

If you're buying: Ask about seller credits to buy down your rate, because that $173 can shrink.

⚡ [E] Everything Else

◆ Slower school zones: In the first 30 days of school, Clark County crews replaced 1,601 traffic signs and repainted or replaced 108 crosswalks. School-zone flashers now run 40 minutes before and after school, so plan on slower drives past campuses valley-wide.
◆ Data center tax breaks: Gov. Joe Lombardo signed an executive order making data center builders pay the full local school support sales tax to get a tax break. The Clark County Education Association says these breaks have cost K-12 schools $357 million since 2015.
◆ Ice Palace comeback: Clark County commissioners set aside $10 million for Insomniac, the company behind EDC, to redevelop the old Sahara Event Center at 800 Liberace Ave. Neighbors in Las Vegas Country Club Estates, about 150 feet away, asked commissioners to table it.
◆ Goodbye, Girl Scout house: The Downtown Henderson Girl Scout house at Pacific and Van Wagenen, open since 1943, closed Sept. 17 ahead of demolition for the city's redevelopment plans. More than 100 girls in 13 troops used it, and it will not be rebuilt.
◆ Reavo out: Former Golden Knights enforcer Ryan Reaves retired after 16 NHL seasons, finishing with 1,137 penalty minutes in 962 games. The good news for fans: GM Kelly McCrimmon says Reaves still makes Las Vegas his home.
