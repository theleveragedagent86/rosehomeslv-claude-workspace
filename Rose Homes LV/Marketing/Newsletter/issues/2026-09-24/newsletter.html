[R] ight Now

Nevada Just Put New Strings on Data Center Tax Breaks

Watch the 60-second version on Instagram
 

Data centers have been one of the biggest fights in the valley this year. Now the state is changing the rules.

On Sept. 18, Gov. Joe Lombardo signed an executive order on data center tax breaks. Any developer who wants one now has to sign a "Nevada Community Support Commitment" first.

The big one for families: developers must pay the local school support sales tax in full. The Clark County Education Association says these tax breaks have cost K-12 schools $357 million since 2015.

The state has until Nov. 6 to finish the terms. They cover water, power bills, the power grid, where projects can go, jobs, and transparency.

What it means for you

If you own a home: Data centers use the same water and power your household needs. Every project still in the pipeline now plays by these rules.

If you have kids in CCSD: These tax breaks pulled money from the same school budget that just got cut. New deals now have to pay the school tax in full.

Freddie Mac says the 30-year fixed rate averaged 6.95 percent last week. A year ago it was 6.26 percent. It was the fourth straight weekly jump.

So I ran the math on a real local home. The Southern Nevada median single-family price was $475,000 in August. With 20 percent down, that is a $380,000 loan. At today's rate the payment is about $2,515 a month in principal and interest. At last year's rate it was about $2,342. That is roughly $173 more a month on the same house. (These are my own estimates, not published figures.)

Here is the good news for buyers. The typical Las Vegas home sold for 98.0 percent of its list price in August, so there is still room to negotiate.

What it means for you

If you're selling: Buyers are watching every dollar of that payment, so price it right from day one.

If you're buying: Ask about seller credits to buy down your rate, because that $173 can shrink.

43 percent, no owner at home

Watch the reel on Instagram
County tax records show 332,040 of Clark County's 775,199 residential parcels, about 43 percent, are not lived in by their owner. Why it matters: a home you live in gets a 3 percent cap on yearly property tax growth. Most others can rise up to 8 percent. Check that the Clark County Assessor lists your home as your primary home.
◆

Slower school zones

Watch the 60-second version on Instagram
In the first 30 days of school, Clark County crews replaced 1,601 traffic signs and repainted or replaced 108 crosswalks. School-zone flashers now run 40 minutes before and after school, so plan on slower drives past campuses valley-wide.

Goodbye, Girl Scout house

Watch the 60-second version on Instagram
The Downtown Henderson Girl Scout house at Pacific and Van Wagenen, open since 1943, closed Sept. 17 ahead of demolition for the city's redevelopment plans. More than 100 girls in 13 troops used it, and it will not be rebuilt.
