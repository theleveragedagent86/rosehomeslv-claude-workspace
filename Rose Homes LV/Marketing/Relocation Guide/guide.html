Why the valley

Why People Move to Las Vegas

No State Income Tax

Nevada has no personal state income tax, so more of your paycheck stays with you. It is one of the biggest reasons people relocate here.

290+ Days of Sun

The valley sees more than 290 sunny days a year. Winters are mild, and the outdoor lifestyle runs nearly year round.

Real Estate Value

Your dollar stretches further here than in California and many coastal metros, with newer construction and bigger lots.

A Growing Economy

Sports, tech, healthcare, and logistics keep expanding, bringing new jobs and steady demand to the region.

World-Class Everything

Dining, live shows, concerts, and pro sports are minutes away. This is the entertainment capital of the world.

Easy to Get Around

Commutes are short compared with big coastal cities, and McCarran, now Harry Reid International, is a quick hop for travel.

The numbers

Cost of Living

Las Vegas sits near the national average overall, but the tax picture and housing value are where newcomers feel the biggest difference, especially those arriving from the coast.

Housing

The valley median is around $490,000 (mid-2026). Condos, townhomes, and outlying areas run less; guard-gated and new-build communities run more.

Income Tax

Zero. Nevada has no state income tax on wages, retirement, or investment income. For many movers this is the headline savings.

Sales Tax

The combined sales tax in Clark County is 8.375%. Groceries and prescriptions are exempt.

Property Tax

Among the lower effective rates in the country, roughly 0.5% to 0.85% of value, with a 3% annual increase cap on your primary residence.

Utilities & Extras

Summer cooling is the real budget line. Expect higher electric bills from June to September, offset by low winter heating.

$0
Nevada charges no state income tax. For many families moving from California or other high-tax states, that single line is the biggest month-to-month difference they feel after the move.

Figures are estimates for general planning and vary by home, district, and provider. Verify current rates before you budget. Nothing here is tax advice.

Why Nevada Is a
Tax-Friendly State

Nevada built its economy to be welcoming to residents and businesses. For families relocating from higher-tax states, the difference can add up fast.

No personal income tax

Wages, retirement income, and capital gains are not taxed at the state level.

A cap on property tax hikes

Your primary residence is protected by a 3% annual cap on tax increases, which keeps costs predictable as values rise.

No estate or inheritance tax

Nevada does not levy a state estate or inheritance tax, another draw for retirees and families planning ahead.

Business-friendly rules

No corporate income tax and a straightforward setup make Nevada popular with entrepreneurs and remote workers.

The climate

Weather & the Outdoors

The valley is high desert, so the air is dry and the sun shows up almost every day. Once you learn the rhythm of the seasons, it is easy to love.

Summer

Hot and dry, often over 100°F from June to August. Homes are built for it with efficient cooling and shaded patios. Mornings and evenings are the sweet spot.

Winter

Mild and sunny, with daytime highs often in the 50s and 60s. Snow is rare in the valley but sits on the nearby peaks, so you can ski and golf in the same week.

Monsoon season

Late summer can bring short, dramatic storms. They pass quickly, but avoid washes and low crossings when it rains.

Spring & fall

The best of the year. Warm days, cool nights, and perfect weather for hiking Red Rock or lingering on a patio downtown.

Beyond the Strip

Life in the Valley

People are surprised by how much of Las Vegas life happens outdoors and off the Strip. Red Rock Canyon is a 20-minute drive for hiking and climbing, Lake Mead brings boating and paddling, and Mount Charleston offers pine forests and winter snow just up the road. When you want the bright lights, world-class dining and live shows are always close.

  • Red Rock Canyon scenic loop, 20 minutes away
  • Lake Mead & the Colorado River for boating
  • Mount Charleston hiking & winter snow
  • Dozens of golf courses, playable year round
  • Award-winning dining & celebrity chefs
  • Day trips to Zion, the Grand Canyon & Death Valley
