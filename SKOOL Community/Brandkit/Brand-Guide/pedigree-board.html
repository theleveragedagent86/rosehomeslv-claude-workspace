02

Seven things they all do

  1. The base is achromatic. Always.

    Nine of ten build on pure white, pure black, or a near-black. Not one uses a tinted cream or beige as the primary surface. Corcoran’s #FEF1E1 is the only cream in the set and it is a minor accent block, not the page.

  2. One accent, covering almost no area.

    Agency red, Sotheby’s navy, Altman gold, Corcoran rose, Real pink, Serhant navy. Compass, Elliman and Eklund Gomes ship zero chromatic accent at all. Nobody runs two decorative colors on top of a tinted base.

  3. Ink is a near-black with a color cast, not pure black.

    #171717, #141C29, #100B28, #050E3D, #002349, #212529. The cast is almost always blue or navy. This one detail separates the expensive-looking sites from the cheap ones.

  4. Two fonts. Never one, never three.

    A display face and a neutral text face, with a hard division of labor. Every single brand in the set.

  5. Display type splits into exactly two camps.

    Camp A sells houses to consumers: high-contrast serif at light weight, 325 to 400, large, near-zero tracking. Camp B sells the operator: heavy sans, geometric or condensed, 700 to 800, negative tracking, often uppercase.

  6. Labels are tiny, uppercase, wide-tracked.

    Camp A pairs a light serif headline with all-caps sans labels at +1.28px to +2px. That tension, huge soft serif over small hard caps, is most of the luxury effect.

  7. The wordmark is the logo.

    Serhant, Eklund | Gomes, Compass, Corcoran, Elliman: the name in the display face, all caps, wide tracking. No icon, no house, no key, no roof. SERHANT.’s period is the entire mark.

The punchline

Your gold was never the problem. The Altman Brothers run #D2AD41. Your canonical gold is #E8A33D and your classroom-cover gold is #D9A441. That is the same color, and it is the pedigree gold.

The cream is the problem. No high-pedigree US brand uses a tinted cream as its primary surface, and the cream is exactly what forces every accent to stay dark and low-chroma, which is why nothing pops.

Minimum change, maximum credibility: pure white and black base, keep #08343E as ink, keep the gold as the single accent used at small area, drop sage entirely, push Poppins to 700/800, and set labels in wide-tracked uppercase Inter. That is Altman’s formula with your ink, it keeps your existing logo files valid on white, and it costs nothing. Voltage stays on the table if you want the brand to read tech-forward rather than broker-forward.
