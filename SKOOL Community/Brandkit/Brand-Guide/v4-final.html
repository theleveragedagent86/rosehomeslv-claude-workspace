Why the orange did not pop, and what brighter would actually cost

Your read was right, and the reason is mechanical, not aesthetic. Every accent in this set was darkened until white text sits on it at 4.5:1 or better. For blue and red that happens while the color is still vivid. For orange it does not. By the time orange is dark enough to carry white text, it has lost most of its chroma and starts reading brown. That is the #BB4C0A you saw.

Going brighter would not be too loud. It would just force dark text on the fill instead of white, which is the exact two-value problem that killed Rosewood and that the gold had in v1. That is the real cost, not volume.

#F26F21 + whiteTom Ferry's actual orange, white text on it. 2.97:1 FAILS
#F26F21 + near-blackSame bright orange, dark text. This works, but now the accent needs a second value for links. 6.18:1 passes
#BB4C0A + whiteThe corrected orange. One value, works everywhere, and reads muddy next to a blue. 5.06:1 passes
#1768E5 + whiteBlue clears the same bar while staying fully saturated. This is the whole argument. 5.06:1 passes

On the blue bias: it is not a bias worth correcting. Serhant, Sotheby's, Real Broker and Tom Ferry all land on navy or blue, and it is the only hue that reads authoritative without raising its voice. The genuine risk with blue is looking generic, and that risk is handled by structure, the true-black surface, not by switching hue.
