#### In this video, Ryan covers:

Nobody loses a deal on the listing presentation. They lose it in the thirty days after it goes under contract, because a disclosure went out two days late, or the loan contingency date was in someone's head, or the inspection response sat for a day and a half while a ninety page PDF waited to be read. The work is not hard. It is relentless, it is date-driven, and it does not care that you were at a showing.

The system is **the AI Transaction Coordinator**, a folder per deal that holds the contract, the parties, the dates, the documents and the correspondence, and a set of commands that run against it. Fifteen minutes a day, not because the work got smaller but because the tracking, drafting and chasing stopped being manual.

The practical steps are building the transaction folder structure, running intake from a real executed contract, generating the timeline, drafting an inspection response from a real report, reviewing disclosures for flags, producing the weekly status update, tracking escrow, title and lender items, wiring it into one command, and setting up the 90-day post-close sequence.

#### Who this is for:

- **Agents running their own transactions** who are the TC, and know exactly which part of that job is going to bite them eventually.

- **Agents who already pay a TC** who want the drafting and tracking half handled so their coordinator is doing judgment work instead of chasing signatures.

## Production notes (not for Skool)

**Densest technical level in the course, and the one with real liability attached.** Every date is a deadline somebody can sue over. The tone should be more careful here than anywhere except Level 9.

**Use a real closed transaction with the names and addresses scrubbed on camera.** Show the scrubbing, briefly. It models the behavior and it doubles as a privacy lesson before Level 9 arrives.

**The win is at roughly 14:30:** a real inspection report PDF in, a drafted repair request out, under a minute, on screen. That is the moment an agent forwards this course to another agent.

**The dates section is the most important two minutes in the level.** Nevada and every other state differ, so teach the move rather than the dates: have it read the contract and extract every date-bearing clause, then have it show its work with the clause reference next to each date. Never let it infer a deadline it cannot cite.

> "Make it show you where it got the date. Clause and page. If it can't point at it, it made it up, and that's the one place in this whole course where making it up costs you real money."

**The line you do not cross, said clearly and without hedging.** This is what makes the level defensible:

> "It reads the report. It drafts the response. It does not decide what you ask for, it does not advise your client, and it does not send anything. Those three are yours, they're what the license is for, and there is no version of this where you hand them over."

**Show a flag that is wrong.** Have it flag something in a disclosure that turns out to be nothing, and say so:

> "See, that one's a nothing. It flagged it because it doesn't know that's standard for this HOA. That's fine. I'd rather it over-flag and I spend nine seconds dismissing it."

**Everything client-facing goes through the critic loop from Level 3.** Say it explicitly here, because this is the level where output goes to a client under a deadline.

**Homework:**

> "Take your next deal, or your last closed one, and just do the intake. Contract in, dates out. Ten minutes. If the dates are right, you'll trust the rest of it."

**Close on the Level 7 question:**

> "Here's what I can't do. I can't build a lesson for every situation you're gonna run into. So Level 7 isn't a tool. It's how you build the thing I never taught you."
