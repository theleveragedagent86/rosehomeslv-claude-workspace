#### Hey, in this video, you're gonna learn:

🧭 **Build Workflows Nobody Taught You** by applying a repeatable five-step method to any problem in your business, so you stop waiting for a lesson that covers your exact situation.

❓ **Question Every Requirement Before You Automate It** by insisting every step have a person's name attached to why it exists, so you stop automating a process that a brokerage stopped requiring in 2019.

🗑️ **Delete the Step Instead of Speeding It Up** using the rule that if you do not add ten percent of the deleted steps back later, you did not delete enough, so you are not building an elegant version of unnecessary work.

⚙️ **Simplify Only What Survived Deletion** so effort goes into the steps that actually earned their place.

🚀 **Automate Last, Not First** by understanding why automation is step five and never step one, so you stop building things you then have to maintain forever.

🔨 **Watch a Real Build From a Real Request** as something gets built live that has not appeared anywhere in this course, so you see the method work rather than just hearing it.

🛑 **Recognize When Not to Automate** by learning the three signals that a task should stay human, so the method has an off switch.

💬 **Use the Bottleneck Prompt** that makes the AI interrogate your process before it builds anything, so you get a solution to the real problem instead of a fast version of the wrong one.

#### In this video, Ryan covers:

Six levels in, there is a trap waiting. A student can follow every lesson perfectly and still be stuck the first time they hit something that was not covered, because what they actually learned was how to follow along. Every business is different, every brokerage has its own requirements, and no course can enumerate them. If the only thing this course gives you is workflows, it expires the moment your situation changes.

The system is **the Five-Step Build Method**: question every requirement, delete the step, simplify what survived, speed it up, and only then automate. In that order, always, and the order is the whole point. Most people start at step five, which is how you end up with a beautifully automated process that should never have existed.

The practical steps are learning to attach a person's name to every requirement, applying the deletion rule, running the bottleneck prompt that forces the AI to interrogate your process before building, watching a live build of something not covered anywhere else in this course, and learning the three signals that mean a task should stay human.

#### Who this is for:

- **Agents whose business does not look like the examples** with a niche, a team structure or a brokerage requirement that no course is ever going to cover specifically.

- **Agents who want to stop buying courses** and would rather own the method than rent the next set of workflows.

## Production notes (not for Skool)

**Deliberate cool-down after Transactions.** Mostly talking, one build. Shortest level so far and that is correct.

**The live build is the whole level.** Take a real request, ideally a genuine one from the community, and build it on camera without preparing it first. It should visibly be the first time Ryan has done this. If it stumbles, that is the point.

Good candidates: a rental-property cash flow analyzer, an expired-listing outreach system, a builder-incentive tracker, a relocation-client welcome packet, a probate lead workflow.

**The deletion rule needs a real estate example or it stays abstract.** Something like a step in a listing checklist that exists because a previous brokerage required it, carried for three years, never questioned.

**When not to automate, name three signals plainly:**
1. It happens rarely and it is different every time.
2. The relationship is the product, not the output.
3. Getting it wrong is expensive and you would have to check every result anyway.

> "If you're gonna proofread every single one, you didn't automate it. You just added a step."

**Assign the reading and frame the ROI**, the way Jack does. One article or chapter, and tell them it is the highest-return fifteen minutes of reading they will do this year.

**Homework, and make it a community action:**

> "Post your problem in the community. Not 'how do I use AI,' the actual thing that ate your Tuesday. I'll build a few of them on camera."

**Close by teasing Level 8 visually.** Show something built earlier in the course next to a properly designed version, and let the gap do the talking.

> "This is a listing graphic. So is this. Same information, same listing. One of them looks like an agent made it and one of them looks like a brokerage made it. That's Level 8, and it's not about taste, it's about writing the rules down."
