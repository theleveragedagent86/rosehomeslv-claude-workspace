Sacramento REALTOR, eXp Realty, DRE 01977518. Founder of The REDD Group. 11.5K followers,
2,533 following, 1,337 posts, verified. Bio link goes to stan.store/KPrinceHomes.

Sample analysed: **216 posts, 24 Jan 2025 to 9 Sep 2026**, 1,821 human comment rows, 1,136
unique commenters, 13,205 liker rows, 5,291 unique likers. Capture limits are in the README.
Read them before you quote a number.

## 1. The one mechanic: one property, one made-up keyword, DM the details

She does not run a coaching funnel on Instagram. She runs a **listing-lead machine**. The
engine is a reel about a single luxury property with a keyword CTA that is the name of that
property or its town. Not "comment BUY". Comment **NEWCASTLE**. Comment **PENRYN**. Comment
**BARN**.

151 of 216 posts (70%) carry a keyword CTA. Split those keywords into two groups and the
whole account explains itself.

| keyword type | posts | % of posts | median comments | mean comments | total comments | % of all comments |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| unique / property specific (NEWCASTLE, PENRYN, BARN, VERNON, HILLTOP, LILJA, MCM) | 37 | 17% | **12** | 19.9 | 735 | **40%** |
| generic (LOCAL, REDD, BUY, SELL, HIRE, SEARCH, WAIT, TRY) | 114 | 53% | 3.0 | 5.6 | 635 | 35% |
| no keyword at all | 65 | 30% | 4 | 6.9 | 451 | 25% |

17% of her posts earn 40% of her comments. A unique keyword is worth **4x** a generic one on
the same account, same face, same production quality.

And the generic keyword is worth **less than no keyword at all** (3.0 vs 4). That is the
finding that matters. Asking people to comment a word does nothing on its own. Asking them to
comment a word that names a specific thing they just watched and want is the whole trick.

The top of the list is not close:

| date | keyword | comments | plays | what it was |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-01-31 | NEWCASTLE | 162 | 38,278 | 2.3 acres, pool, 3 car garage |
| 2026-03-20 | LUXURY | 65 | 37,258 | estate tour |
| 2025-06-20 | LOOMIS | 50 | 57,399 | 7 acres, gated, vineyards |
| 2025-06-19 | ADU OR DM | 50 | 19,854 | new construction with ADU |
| 2026-03-10 | PENRYN | 40 | 12,719 | 3,600 sq ft on 2.6 acres |
| 2026-02-21 | BARN | 37 | 5,735 | Granite Bay barn property |

Note the plays column. NEWCASTLE got 162 comments on 38K plays. A different reel that month
did 181K plays and 66 comments. **Reach and comments are not the same business.** She wins on
the reel that reaches a smaller, hotter group and gives them a reason to raise a hand.

**Copy this verbatim:**
- Every post gets ONE asset and ONE keyword, and the keyword names the asset, never the action.
- Put the ask on line 2, directly under the hook, and repeat it once at the bottom of the caption. She does both on her best posts.
- Keyword CTAs go on reels, never on carousels. See section 2.

## 2. Format: reels carry the account, and the CTA belongs on them only

| format | n | % posts | % comments | median likers sampled | median comments | median plays |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| reel | 171 | 79% | 81% | 55 | 4 | 3,409 |
| carousel | 44 | 20% | 17% | 71.0 | 4.0 | n/a |
| single image | 1 | 0% | 2% | 99 | 40 | n/a |

Reels are the account. But the CTA lift splits hard by format:

| format | posts | median comments WITH keyword | WITHOUT | multiple |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| reel | 171 | 4.5 (n=134) | 2 (n=37) | **2.2x** |
| carousel | 44 | 3 (n=17) | 5 (n=27) | **0.6x** |
| all | 216 | 4 (n=151) | 4 (n=65) | 1.0x |

A keyword CTA more than doubles comments on a reel and actively **hurts** a carousel. Her
carousels are recognition posts, gratitude posts and city guides. People comment on those
because they want to, and bolting an ask onto them suppresses that.

This corrects the obvious assumption. On Peyson Robertson the carousels were the business
engine. Here they are not. Here the reel is the engine and the carousel is the relationship.

## 4. Hooks

Top 20 posts by comments versus bottom 20:

| | top 20 | bottom 20 |
|---|---|---|
| median hook length | 64 chars | 75 chars |
| hook ends in a question mark | 5% | 0% |
| hook contains a number in the first 40 chars | 25% | 20% |

The winners are short, flat, declarative statements of fact about a specific place:

- "2.3 acres in Newcastle with a pool and a 3-car garage."
- "3,600 sq ft on 2.6 private acres in a gated community."
- "7 private acres in an exclusive 88-home gated community, surrounded by vineyards."
- "Gated community on a hilltop with sweeping views in Loomis."
- "Not a replica. A tribute."

No verb, no question, no hype adjective. A spec sheet read out loud. The reader supplies the
desire.

The losers are announcements and setups that make a promise the viewer has to wait for:

- "REGISTRATION IS OPEN"
- "HERE IT IS…"
- "Let's break this down:"
- "Trust me, you won't regret it."
- "If you're new here, hi I'm Kimberly"

Every one of those is about her. Every winner is about the thing.

**Three hooks for Ryan in the same shape:**

1. "Fourteen CMAs, one prompt, 11 minutes."
2. "A folder on my laptop that knows every client I have ever had."
3. "Not a chatbot. A file that runs my listing launch."

Same rule: name the object, state the spec, no question mark, no "here's why".

## 5. Captions

Median caption 656 characters. Posts with a keyword CTA run longer, 816 characters median,
versus 642 without. These are long captions by Instagram standards, 8 to 15 short paragraphs.

Structure on the best ones, in order:
1. Hook line, one sentence, the spec.
2. Blank line, then **the ask**: "Comment VIEW for full listing details."
3. Four to eight one-sentence paragraphs of detail. Rooms, acreage, neighborhood, what daily life is like.
4. The ask again, verbatim, sometimes with the keyword in quotes: `Comment "VIEW" and I'll send you the full listing details`.
5. A fixed signature block: `Marketed by Kimberly Prince / Selling real estate in OC + Sacramento / eXp Realty | DRE 01977518 / kim@kprincehomes.com`.

The ask sits at position 2, not at the end. Somebody who bails after two lines has already
seen the keyword.

## 9. What to steal, in priority order

1. **The unique keyword.** One asset, one word, and the word names the thing. 4x on her own account against generic keywords, and generic keywords underperform having no CTA at all.
2. **The ask on line 2.** Hook, blank line, the ask, then the detail, then the ask again at the bottom.
3. **Reels get keywords, carousels do not.** 2.2x on reels, 0.6x on carousels.
4. **Spec-sheet hooks.** Short, declarative, concrete, no question mark. Her top 20 hooks median 64 characters.
5. **The tripwire pair.** A $17 operational checklist and a $34 playbook that both sell the same identity. Cheap, instant, and they qualify a buyer before any call.
6. **Local authority as a moat.** She hosts "In Your Neighborhood" on @gooddaysac, a real TV segment. Ryan has the Las Vegas equivalent available through his local news work.
7. **The free interactive tool.** "Relocate to Sacramento" is a neighborhood picker, not a PDF. It captures the lead and it is the only asset on her store nobody else can copy in an afternoon.

## 10. Where she is beatable

**She created AI demand and does not fulfil it.** Seven posts of 216 mention AI. They are among
her weakest, median 3 comments. But look at what she wrote in one of them: "Inside Powerhouse,
agents are connecting AI to their Google Drive, email, calendar, and CRM, building systems" and
"that is the difference between staying stuck at $4M and scaling to $8 to $10M+." She names the
outcome and then sends people to a $97 mastermind that is somebody else's product. There is no
teaching, no artifact, no ladder. **That gap is the entire opening for The Leveraged Agent.**

**Her top rung requires a brokerage move.** Everything under $97 is a warm-up for joining eXp
under her. That is the biggest ask in this industry: license transfer, cap reset, pipeline
disruption, telling your broker. Ryan's Skool asks an agent to open a laptop. He is not
competing with her offer, he is competing for attention from the same people, and his
conversion friction is a tenth of hers.

**She is not the top of her own funnel.** Powerhouse belongs to Amy Gregory. REDD sits under it.
Anyone who buys the $97 challenge becomes Powerhouse's relationship as much as Kimberly's.
Ryan owns his community outright.

**She is geographically anchored twice over.** Sacramento and Placer County, and now Newport
Beach. Three quarters of her content only works on somebody moving to those places. A Las Vegas
agent, a Tampa agent and a Boise agent can watch every listing tour she posts and never have a
reason to buy anything. Ryan's product is location independent, which means her own audience is
addressable to him and his is not addressable to her.

**She is over-invested in luxury listing tours.** They are her best performing content and they
pull consumers, not agents. 19% of her commenters carry a realtor name signal. On her
agent-facing posts it is 26%. She is spending her best production on the wrong audience for the
business she says she wants to build.

**She does not show up in her own comments.** Only 17 top-level comment rows in the capture are
hers, against 1,821 human rows. Fulfilment happens silently in DM. That is efficient and it is
also cold. Nobody watching the post sees a person answer. Ryan can win the same keyword game and
be visibly present in the thread, which is worth more than the automation saves.

**The realtors are already there and already warm.** 367 accounts commented on her agent-facing
posts, 97 of them with an explicit realtor signal in the handle or display name. 219 of her
1,136 commenters are identifiable agents. They are watching agent-facing content from a woman
who tells them the answer is AI plus systems, and then does not teach it. See
TOP-300-DM-TARGETS.csv.
