# @kristijencks, full strategy teardown

Captured 9 Sep 2026. Account: Kristi Jencks, 13.4K followers, 3,087 following, 3,407 posts,
verified. Bio: "Coach / I help agents build businesses that don't run them. / Step 1: Follow /
Step 2: Grab the free newsletter". Links to pop.at/onlykristijencks and kristijencks.com.

Tom Ferry senior coach since 2018, licensed agent since 2014, Phoenix area (Ahwatukee, Florence,
Gilbert listings appear on the grid). Builds "Claude for Agents" with her husband Merrill Jencks
(@merrill.jencks). Speaks from the Tom Ferry Success Summit stage.

**Her audience is Ryan's avatar almost exactly:** working agents who want AI systems, not buyers
or sellers. She is teaching Claude to realtors, in public, right now. She is the closest direct
competitor The Leveraged Agent has.

**Sample:** 238 posts from 29 Jan 2026 to 9 Sep 2026, harvested with engagement metrics and full
captions. Commenter and liker identities could not be harvested this run, see the README. Every
number below comes from the 238-post sample.

## 1. The one mechanic: comment-keyword to an automated DM, on a very small stage

She runs the same engine Peyson Robertson runs. Post a build, tell people to comment one word, a
bot DMs the file. She says so out loud: **"Grab my notes. Comment notes and my robot will send
them over."**

| Format | n with CTA | median comments with CTA | n without | median without | multiple |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carousel | 42 | **14** | 33 | 1 | **14x** |
| Reel | 36 | **18.5** | 56 | 2 | **9.2x** |
| Single image | 24 | 2 | 47 | 0 | n/a |
| All formats | 102 | 9.5 | 136 | 1 | 9.5x |

Median likes barely move (11 vs 10 on carousels, 24 vs 18 on reels). Same as Peyson: the keyword
does not make the post better, it converts the same attention into a DM thread.

**The difference is scale, and it is the whole story of this account.** Peyson's carousels with a
CTA run 194 median comments. Kristi's run 14. She is executing the same playbook at roughly
one fourteenth the yield.

Her numbers, plainly:

- 238 posts in 2026 produced **7,559 comments and 8,496 likes**.
- Median post: **3 comments, 14 likes**. On 13.4K followers that is about 0.1% engaging.
- **76 posts (32%) got zero comments. 116 posts (49%) got two or fewer.**
- Her top 5 posts hold **49%** of all 2026 comments. Top 10 hold **63%**.

So the account is not an engine running steadily. It is a few hundred posts of noise with about
ten real hits inside it.

reaction. The asset was a synthesized recap of every Summit session, made with Claude, released
while the event was still in everyone's feed. Timely, genuinely hard to get elsewhere, and free.

### What Ryan should copy verbatim

- **One asset, one word, one line.** Her best-converting caption in nine months is fourteen
  words long. Captions of 0 to 80 characters median **26 comments**. Captions of 201 to 400
  characters median **zero**. Say the thing, ask for the word, stop typing.
- **Say "my robot will send it."** Naming the automation did not hurt her, it is on her two
  highest-converting captions. Agents find it proof, not spam.
- **Ride an event.** The Summit recap post out-earned her entire preceding six months. Ryan's
  equivalents: a new Claude release, a NAR ruling, a Las Vegas market report, an MLS rule change.
  Publish the synthesis inside 48 hours while everyone is still talking about it.

## 2. Format: reels for reach, carousels for the ask, single images for nothing

| Format | n | share of posts | comments | share of comments | median comments | median likes | median comment:like |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reel | 92 | 39% | 5,377 | **71%** | 4 | 22 | 0.1 |
| Carousel | 75 | 32% | 1,853 | 25% | 6 | 11 | 0.4 |
| Single image | 71 | 30% | 329 | **4%** | 1 | 7 | 0.0 |

**Correction to the Peyson-shaped assumption:** on Kristi's account reels carry the comments, not
carousels. 71% of everything. Her four biggest posts are all reels. The carousel still shows the
better comment-to-like ratio (0.4 versus 0.1), so per unit of attention the carousel converts
harder, but reels bring the attention in the first place. She has 171,455 total reel plays in
2026 against a 13.4K following, so reach is coming from non-followers.

**30% of her output is single images that produce 4% of her comments.** That is 71 posts of pure
cost. Most are quote cards and branded text slides. This is the single biggest waste on the
account.

## 4. Hooks: receipts win, "Stop doing X" is dead, and her own branded sign-off is poison

Median comments on 2026 posts, sliced by what the caption does:

| Hook feature | n | median comments | median likes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mentions the robot / automation** | 2 | **443.5** | 115 |
| **First-person receipt ("I built", "I made", "we made")** | 26 | **12.5** | 11.5 |
| Has a comment-keyword CTA | 102 | 9.5 | 12.5 |
| Starts "Most agents..." | 12 | 8 | 22.5 |
| Names Claude or AI | 97 | 5 | 15 |
| Contains a number | 120 | 4 | 15.5 |
| First line ends in a question mark | 33 | 4 | 18 |
| Contains a dollar amount | 18 | 3 | 20.5 |
| Contrarian word (not / never / stop / wrong) | 112 | 2 | 10 |
| **Starts with "Stop "** | 12 | **0** | 6.5 |
| **Contains "Take It and Run"** | 61 | **0** | 9 |
| Baseline, all 2026 posts | 238 | 3 | 14 |

Three findings here, and two of them are counterintuitive.

1. **The receipt beats the lecture, 4x.** "I built a dashboard that emails me my social media
   numbers every morning" (101 comments) works. "Stop opening 5 apps to check your numbers"
   (same build, lecture framing) does not. Twelve posts open with "Stop " and their median is
   zero comments. Agents do not want to be told to stop. They want to see the thing you built.
2. **Her own brand phrase is on her worst posts.** "Take It and Run" appears in 61 captions with
   a median of **zero comments**. That is not the phrase's fault, it is a marker: the posts
   carrying it are the templated, mass-produced branded ones. When she is actually building
   something she forgets to say it.
3. **The contrarian hook, which is Peyson's best weapon, does nothing for her.** 112 posts use
   not/never/stop/wrong and median 2 comments. Peyson's "prove me wrong" posts hit 814. The
   difference is that his states a checkable operational opinion and invites a fight. Hers state
   a generic scold. Same word class, opposite mechanics.

### Three hooks for Ryan in the winning shape

- "I built a Claude agent that writes my listing description from the MLS sheet in nine seconds.
  Comment LISTING and my robot sends you the file."
- "We pulled every expired in Henderson last month and had Claude write all three letters. 41
  went out Tuesday. Comment EXPIRED for the build."
- "My CMA used to take ninety minutes. I taught Claude my format once. Comment CMA and I will
  send the setup."

All three are first-person, name a finished artifact, carry a number, and end in one word.

## 5. Captions: shorter is dramatically better, and she writes long

| Caption length | n | median comments | median likes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 to 80 chars** | 6 | **26** | 26.5 |
| 81 to 200 chars | 19 | 16 | 30 |
| 201 to 400 chars | 68 | **0** | 14.5 |
| 401 to 800 chars | 108 | 4 | 11.5 |
| 801+ chars | 37 | 3 | 12 |

**61% of her posts sit in the 201 to 800 character band that performs worst.** The six shortest
captions she wrote all year out-converted everything else by 8x. Structure of the winners: one
line of what it is, one line of the ask. No story, no setup, no hashtag block.

The ask, when it exists, sits at the very end, in caps, one word, usually with an emoji ahead of
it. That placement is right. The problem is the four hundred characters in front of it.

## 8. Month over month: growing output, flat conversion, one lucky August

Covered in section 6. The summary: **she is working extremely hard and the account is flat.**
Posting volume is up 5x, keyword discipline is up 5x, and median engagement per post is
unchanged at 2 to 4 comments. Her follower count of 13.4K against 3,407 lifetime posts is the
long-run version of the same fact.

The one genuine growth signal is reach, not engagement. Reel plays hold at roughly 800 to 1,400
median through the ramp, and she now clears 6,000 to 18,000 on the good ones. Non-followers are
finding her. They are just not converting into anything measurable on the post itself.

## 9. What to steal, in priority order

1. **The asset-per-post discipline.** Nearly every one of her wins is a named, finished, free
   build with a one-word keyword. Not a tip. A file. Ryan's Business Brain course material is
   already this shape and is not being cut into keyword drops.
2. **The short caption.** Fourteen words beat four hundred characters by 8x on her own account.
   This is free to copy and costs nothing to test.
3. **The automated DM, said out loud.** "Comment X and my robot will send it." Her two
   highest-converting captions both do this.
4. **Event-timed synthesis.** Her single best post of the year is a same-week recap of an event
   her audience was already watching. This is repeatable on a calendar.
5. **The first-person receipt hook.** "I built", "we made", "I taught Claude", plus the artifact.
   12.5 median versus 3 baseline on her own data.
6. **Free build cadence with a name.** "Claude for Agents, a new build every week" is a promise
   a newsletter can be sold on. The name does the work.

Do not copy: her posting volume, her single-image quote cards, her "Stop doing X" hooks, or her
habit of putting a branded sign-off on content that has nothing in it.

## 10. Where she is beatable

**She is spread across four businesses and Instagram is the one getting the leftovers.** Tom
Ferry coaching, speaking, an active Phoenix real estate business with listings, and Claude for
Agents. The grid shows all four fighting for the same feed. The listings posts get 1 to 7 likes
from an agent audience that does not care about a Florence, Arizona townhome, and the coaching
posts get scrolled past by whatever local audience the listings attract. **Ryan's account can be
one thing.**

**Her teaching is shallow in public by design.** Every build is described, then gated behind a
keyword and a DM. Nothing on the grid shows the actual prompt, the actual file structure, or the
actual failure modes. An agent who wants to understand rather than receive has nowhere to go.
That is the opening: **teach the build in public, in full, and sell the implementation.**

**There is a hole in the middle of her ladder.** Free newsletter, then a $500 or $750 one-off
working session, then Tom Ferry coaching at an unpublished price. There is no community, no
cohort, no recurring low-ticket rung. **That gap is exactly the shape of The Leveraged Agent
Skool.** Everyone who comments SOP and gets a file has raised their hand for the thing she does
not sell.

**She is over-invested in output and under-invested in depth.** 238 posts in nine months to
produce ten posts that mattered. A competitor publishing four genuinely deep builds a month
would out-produce her results on a fraction of the labor.

**Her audience likes her, not her systems.** 108 median likes on family posts against 14 on
everything else. She has built parasocial affection, not a systems following. Affection does not
transfer to a Skool checkout. A creator who builds a systems following from the start converts
better on the same list size.

**The demand she is creating and not fulfilling:** agents who commented a keyword, got a file,
and now have a file they do not know how to run. Nothing on her grid or her site catches that
person. No implementation community, no "I got the file and I am stuck" path, no accountability
around the build. She hands out artifacts and walks away. **That person is Ryan's ideal member,
and she is manufacturing them at a rate of several hundred a month.**
