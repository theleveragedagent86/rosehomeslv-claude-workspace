## Thesis
A local service business should be rebuilt around one project management system as the single source of truth, because once every job lives in one record, every other function (booking, quoting, invoicing, reviews, follow-up) becomes an automation off that record rather than a person's job.

## System demoed
No live build. A 15-node mind map walked node by node. ClickUp as the spine (statuses, dates, assignee, time tracking, email integration). Then: booking via cenly [sic?] or cal.com, link sent by SMS during the call; custom-worded reminders a week out, a day before, and a couple hours before; lead gen via PPC, SEO, and bark.com lead aggregation; a required intake form on every call capturing address, name, phone, email, problem description; a pandadoc [sic?] quote fired to SMS mid-call for thumb signature; Stripe invoice auto-sent when status flips to job done; payroll via QuickBooks, a deal [sic? Deel], ripling [sic? Rippling], Gusto, or wage Point [sic? Wagepoint]; 30 AI-written social posts a month scheduled in Buffer; review request on job completion, gated by the tech's own report; forms as the only write path into the system; sortly [sic?] or service Titan [sic?] for inventory; an X-days-later follow-up SMS that references a specific detail the tech logged; dashboards in ClickUp or Looker Studio for revenue, cash collected, gross and net margin, profit, turnaround time, call conversion rate; Google Alerts on the brand and staff names; seasonal promo blasts to the past-customer list. He explicitly excludes AI voice agents and AI callers as not yet worth it.

## Transferable to realtors?
- **Verdict:** ADAPTABLE
- **Why:** The architecture is exactly a real estate business: intake, appointment, quote, service delivery, invoice, review, reactivation. But the rebuild is real. The spine becomes a real estate CRM (Lofty, Follow Up Boss) not ClickUp, jobs become transactions with milestone statuses, Stripe invoicing does not apply since commission flows through the brokerage, and the review and follow-up automations have to respect state advertising rules. The single-source-of-truth thesis and the "you are a business architect, not a plumber" reframe are the parts worth lifting whole.
