## Thesis
Video-to-video editing of footage you already shot beats text-to-video generation, and the pipeline only survives contact with real content if you hide the 720p quality seam behind a scene cut.

## System demoed
Record a real source clip of roughly 10 seconds containing a deliberate physical trigger (a finger snap, a reach, a spoken word) with nothing happening at the trigger moment. Write a hyper-specific prompt naming the trigger and the change, either by exact timestamp or by described action, e.g. "Right after the man says this at exactly 2.9 seconds, change his outfit to a cool-looking hoodie with a chain." Feed the clip plus prompt into a video-to-video model, he uses Gemini Omni, and names Kling as an alternative, accessed either directly at deepmind.google or through an aggregator (Higgsfield). Generate five variants simultaneously rather than serially, because the effect lands maybe one time in five. Pick the best, then in an editor (Premiere Pro) align the AI clip against the original, which will have drifted in length and audio waveform, and cut out of the 720p AI shot into a completely different shot type (a screen share, b-roll) rather than back to another talking head, so the compression change is invisible. Optionally drive the whole loop via the Higgsfield MCP from Claude Code or Codex to generate ten variants and select.

## Economic argument
He is unusually candid that this costs money and pre-empts the cheap-guru framing. Verbatim: "one of these things might realistically cost you 50 cents in order to generate." Then: "this costs 15 tokens. I don't know exactly what the token to dollar conversion is here, but you know, if you do this four or five times, that's like 60, 70 tokens. That can actually add up a fair amount. You should expect to spend maybe 50 cents to a dollar for this." On the hit rate: "This is going to work like 20% of the time, which means on average you're probably going to need to rerun this thing five times or so if you really want to get the smoothest effect possible. Nobody's telling you this right now because they want you to think that this is way cheaper than it actually is." And on tooling generally: "if you want to work with AI video, you know, it's not going to be cheap. It's going to cost you quite a pretty penny." The return side is asserted, never quantified: "you can arbitrage the credits that you spent on this to like massively improve, you know, your ad CPCs, you know, the watch time and retention of your content." Volume proof is borrowed: "I have a friend who is worth many tens of millions of dollars that's currently generating over 2,000 AI videos a week with something like this." ## Transferable to realtors?
- **Verdict:** DIRECT
- **Why:** The pipeline is content-agnostic. A realtor records a talking-head hook, snaps, changes outfit or lighting, and cuts to listing b-roll, which is exactly the seam-hiding move he prescribes. Nothing about the mechanism depends on B2B, and the cost is a few dollars per reel. One caveat worth flagging to students: AI-altering footage of the property itself, as opposed to the agent, is a misrepresentation problem, so the effect belongs on the person and never on the house.
