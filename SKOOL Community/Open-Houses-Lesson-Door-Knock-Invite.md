## Post Body (paste under the video)

The best traffic at your open house is not coming from Zillow. It lives on the same street as your listing.

The top agents know this. Before an open house they knock the neighbors' doors and personally invite them. Tom Ferry teaches a 4-step version of this that does two jobs at once: it fills your open house, and it quietly surfaces your next listing. Andy Tse, one of the highest-volume agents in the country, personally invites neighbors the same way. A mailed postcard cannot do what a face at the door does.

So why do most agents skip it? They freeze. They walk up to the door and have no idea what to say, so they mumble something apologetic and leave. The tactic is not the problem. The script is.

In this lesson you have AI write the whole thing for the exact neighborhood: the spoken invite, a door-hanger for the houses where nobody answers, and a follow-up text for the neighbors who give you their number.

**What you'll build in this one:**

- Why the neighbors are your highest-ROI open house traffic (Ferry and Tse both work the street in person)
- Tom Ferry's 4-step invite: introduce yourself and the open house, ask who they know who might want to move in, reiterate the invite, and softly ask if they have thought about selling
- The prompt that writes your version, tuned to this neighborhood so it sounds natural
- The soft listing question that turns an invite into a seller conversation
- A door-hanger card for the doors nobody answers
- A follow-up text for a neighbor who hands you their number
- How to practice it so it sounds like you talking, not you reading

**The reusable prompt** (copy this, paste it into Claude or ChatGPT, fill in the blanks):

> You are my real estate coach. I have an open house this weekend and I want to personally invite the neighbors on the listing's street, but I freeze at the door and do not know what to say. Write me a natural door-knock invite based on Tom Ferry's four-step approach: introduce myself and the open house, ask who they know who might want to move into the neighborhood, reiterate the invite, and then softly ask if they have had any thoughts about selling their own home.
>
> Here is what you need:
> - My name, brokerage, and phone:
> - The listing address and the open house date and time:
> - The neighborhood name and one or two things people love about living there:
>
> Give me:
> 1. The spoken door-knock invite, short and natural enough that I can say it from memory instead of reading it
> 2. A door-hanger card for houses where nobody answers
> 3. A follow-up text for a neighbor who gives me their number
>
> Keep it warm and human, not salesy. No pressure language. Short sentences. If you need anything before you write, ask me first.

**Example output** (what it wrote for a listing on a quiet cul-de-sac):

> **Spoken invite:** Hi, I'm [Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm hosting an open house this Saturday at [address], right on your street, and I promised the owner I'd personally invite the neighbors. I'd love for you to stop by, see the house, and see what's happening with values on the street. Quick question, who do you know who has talked about moving into the neighborhood? And while I'm here, have you had any thoughts about selling your own place in the next year or so?
>
> **Door-hanger:** Sorry I missed you. I'm [Name] with [Brokerage], hosting an open house this Saturday from 12 to 3 at [address] on your street. Come see the house and find out what it means for your own home's value. Hope to meet you Saturday. [phone]
>
> **Follow-up text:** Hi [Name], it's [Your Name], we met at your door about the open house at [address] Saturday. Great meeting you. If it helps, I can send over what homes on your street have been selling for lately, just say the word. See you Saturday.

**Your turn:** run the prompt for your next open house, then knock at least 20 doors on the street with your invite in your back pocket.
