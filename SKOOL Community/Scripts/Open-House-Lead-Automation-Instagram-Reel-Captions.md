### Reel 2 | Open-House-Reel-02-Stop-Typing-Your-Prompts.mp4
**Length:** 67s
**Clip:** the microphone button in Claude Code and why blurting beats typing

Stop Typing Your Prompts. Start Talking To AI Instead

One of the best things Claude Code added is a microphone button.

Thank God.

Here's why that matters so much.

When most people sit down and type a prompt, they try to sound grammatically correct. They want every sentence to make sense. They want it to sound perfect.

That doesn't help you.

When you type, you filter. You take what's in your head and trim it down before it ever reaches the screen.

You leave things out. Little details. Side thoughts. The "oh, and also" stuff that actually matters.

And that is never a good thing when you're trying to build something.

Sometimes you just need to blurt it out. Blab. Get every single thought out until the whole picture is on the table.

That's what the microphone lets you do.

So when I built my open house lead system, I didn't type a neat little paragraph. I hit the mic and talked it out. What's on the sign-in sheet. What I want done with it. Who I want the follow-up to sound like. What the end goal is.

All of it, messy and out loud.

Here's the pivot most agents miss.

AI is not grading your grammar. It doesn't care if your sentences are clean. It cares whether it has enough information to do the job right.

A short, polished prompt gives it very little to work with. A long, messy one gives it everything.

And agents are already good at this. You talk all day. You explain things to buyers and sellers out loud all the time. You're better at talking through a process than typing one.

So use that.

Next time you open Claude or ChatGPT, don't type. Hit the microphone and talk for two minutes like you're explaining the task to a new assistant on their first day.

Then see how much better the result is.

The people who walked through are the prize. Most of them won't buy that house. Some of them will buy a house. Some of them have one to sell.

Decide what you want from them before you ever follow up.

The relationship started at an open house. They walked in because they were looking at a house. That's the conversation they're already in.

Talk about what they're looking for. What they liked. What they didn't. What they're hoping to find.

One Video For Every Open House Visitor

One of the optional high-impact touches in my open house follow-up plan is a personal 30-second video.

Just you, on your phone, talking to one person.

A personal video, one per visitor, is 100 percent better. "Hey, we just met. Thank you so much for stopping by." Their name, your face, a real thank you.

It's ultimately going to be better because they recognize who you are. They see your face again. They remember the conversation.

But what if 30 people showed up? What if 100 did?

Recording a hundred personal videos isn't going to happen.

So here's another way to do it.

Record one video for everyone. A general one.

"Hey, we just met. I appreciate you stopping by. If you have any questions about buying, or anything else, reach out."

Then send that same video text to every visitor.

It's not as strong as a personal one. But it's a lot stronger than nothing, which is what most open house visitors get.

This is the pivot I want agents to think about.

People skip video because they think it has to be perfect, or personal, or edited. So they don't do it at all.

But the bar is low. Most agents never send a video after an open house. The one who does already stands out, even with a simple, general one.

Pick the version that fits the day. Small open house, a handful of visitors? Do personal videos. Busy weekend with a packed house? Record one and send it to everyone.

Either way, your face shows up on their phone and they remember who you are.
