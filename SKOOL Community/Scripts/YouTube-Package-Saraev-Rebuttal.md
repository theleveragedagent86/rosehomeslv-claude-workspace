# SCRIPT

## Hook, 0:00 to 0:20

> There's a guy named Nick Saraev. 488,000 subscribers, one of the biggest AI automation channels out there. And he made a video explaining why he refuses to sell AI to businesses like mine.
>
> He's right about three things. He's wrong about one. And the one he's wrong about is the whole reason I built what I built.

**Note:** state the consensus, negate it, then earn the negation. Do not open with a credential you have not shown yet. Your credential is the screen recording later, not a number.

## Preview the three, 0:20 to 1:30

> His argument has three legs, and I want to give you all three up front so you can decide if you even need the rest of this video.
>
> One. The lead pool is too small. Two. The sales call turns into unpaid education. Three. The margins are too thin to pay for anything.
>
> Two of those do not apply to us at all. One of them is dead accurate, and it is the reason most agents who try this quit in the first week.

## Reason 1, the lead pool, 1:30 to 3:30

His actual words, quote them:

> "How many HVAC businesses are there in Chattanooga?"

His math: 1,500 potential clients, 1,500 emails, 10 responses, 3 calls, 1 client. Then you are out of city and you have to move.

Your answer:

> That math is completely correct, and it has nothing to do with us. He is describing the problem of *selling to* a local business. I am not selling to one. I am one.
>
> I don't have to find 1,500 HVAC companies in Chattanooga. I have to close the deals already in front of me faster than the agent down the street.

**Whiteboard or Excalidraw here.** Two funnels side by side. His: find business, pitch, educate, close, run out of city. Yours: lead comes in, respond, convert. Same tools, no acquisition problem, because the acquisition problem was already yours before AI existed.

## Reason 2, thin margins, 3:30 to 5:30

His anecdote, and it is a good one, tell it straight:

> He bought a business off Craigslist doing over $2 million a year. Asked the owner what the margin was. Six to ten percent. So $120,000 in profit, split with a partner, on $2 million of revenue.

Your answer:

> Now do that math on a real estate deal. One transaction. [YOUR AVERAGE COMMISSION, NOT FOUND, fill this in] on a Vegas median-priced home.
>
> His argument is that a local business can't pay for software because the margin isn't there. Our marginal deal isn't a six percent margin on a widget. It's the whole commission. One extra closing a year pays for every tool I use, several times over.

**Do not** state a specific commission number until you have checked it. Do not use a market-wide average as if it were yours.

## Reason 3, the one he's right about, 5:30 to 8:00

This is the honest center of the video. Do not soften it.

> Here's the leg of his argument that lands.
>
> He says the sales call becomes unpaid education. That you spend the whole call teaching somebody what the technology even is, and you never get to the part where they buy.
>
> He is completely right, and I'll go further than he did. It isn't just the sales call. It is the first two weeks. Most agents who try to use AI don't fail because the tools don't work. They fail because nobody ever showed them one thing that worked on day one, so they spent four days configuring something and then went back to what they were already doing.

Then the personal failure. **This is the persuasion, not the argument.**

> [YOUR OWN STORY HERE, NOT FOUND. The strongest version is a specific thing you tried, spent real time on, and abandoned. Saraev's version is a dental seminar he ran that flopped. Yours needs to be equally specific and equally unflattering, or the whole video reads as defensive.]

Then the resolution:

> The fix isn't a better argument. It's a shorter runway to the first thing that works.

## The disclosure, 8:00 to 9:00

Do this. It is what separates an honest video from a cheap one, and the comments will find it if you don't.

> One more thing, and I want to be fair to him here, because I think he's a genuinely good operator and I've learned a lot from his stuff.
>
> His revenue doesn't come from selling AI to local businesses. It comes from Maker School, teaching people how to do this. Which is, structurally, exactly what I'm doing right now.
>
> So take my rebuttal with that in mind, and take his argument with it too. He's not wrong about his own experience. He's describing a business I'm not in.

## Proof, 9:00 to 11:00

Screen recording. This is the part nobody else in this space can film.

> Let me show you what this actually looks like instead of arguing about it.

Show, blurred addresses:

- The three live transactions running through the coordination system
- One escrow email chain that generated itself
- The daily checklist pulling actions off those deals

> Three deals in escrow right now. Every email those clients get for the next thirty days is already written. I didn't write them this morning.

**Compliance:** blur addresses and client names. Do not show anything with an MLS number or client contact info on screen.

## Close, 11:00 to 11:30

Soft. Match the workspace CTA rule.

> If you're an agent and you've bounced off this stuff before, I'd genuinely rather hear about it than not. Tell me what you tried and where it fell apart.
>
> And if you want the day-one version, the one thing that works before you configure anything, that's what Module 0 is.
