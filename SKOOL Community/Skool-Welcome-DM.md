# The first DM to a new Leveraged Agent member

**Part 1 is the evidence: what Ryan received and why he ignored all of it.**
**Part 2 is the system: the DM, the post ask, the pinned thread, the timing.** Skip to it.

Written 2026-09-01 from a read of every welcome DM Ryan has personally received after
joining a Skool community. Eight threads, six different operators, April through August 2026.
Source is Ryan's own Skool inbox. Nothing here is copied from those messages, the patterns
were extracted and the copy below is original.

**Ryan replied to zero of the eight.** That is the single most useful fact in this document.

## What that adds up to

**Six of the eight were link dumps.** Route, route, route, done. Every one of them got
silence. A link is not a conversation starter, it is a chore, and a new member who has
just joined already has a Start Here tile telling them the same thing.

**Only two asked a question.** Florian's and Tommi's are the only two messages in the
inbox that made replying feel possible. Tommi's is the better one mechanically: "are you
here just for curiosity, or do you want to start your AI eBook business?" Two options,
answerable in three words, and the answer segments the member. Florian's was warmer but
open-ended, which is harder to answer than it looks.

**Three upsold in the first message.** Chase, Nate, and Lanie's link. Asking for more
money from someone who joined ninety seconds ago reads as a funnel, not a welcome.

**Zero referenced anything about Ryan.** Not his market, not his job, not his profile.
Every single one was visibly templated. He clocked all eight as automated and answered none.

**One had a personality.** Jack's, because it ended in a GIF. That is the only message in
the inbox that read like a person typed it.

**Tommi's thread is the cautionary tale.** Six messages over five weeks into total silence,
finishing with "Ryan ?" There is no recovering a conversation that never started. The fix
is not more follow-ups, it is a first message worth answering.

## The one advantage none of them had

Every one of those operators was writing to a general audience: entrepreneurs, automators,
"AI people." They could not ask a specific question because they did not know who they were
talking to.

**Every member of The Leveraged Agent is a licensed real estate agent.** Ryan can ask a
question that only makes sense to a realtor, and that alone proves a human wrote it. That
is the whole edge, and it costs nothing to use.

# Part 2: the goal is a post in the community, not a reply in the DM

Revised 2026-09-01 after Ryan set the real objective: get the new member to **post publicly**
(who they are, their market, and a workflow they want), and to tell him which of their tasks
are repetitive enough to hate. This section replaces the earlier "one question, done" version.

## The two ideas, graded

**"Ask them in the DM what tasks are repetitive." KEEP, and promote it to message one.**
It is the strongest question available. Concrete, about their actual work, no social risk,
and the answer is directly routable to a level. More important: **the answer to it IS the
post.** That is the whole mechanism below.

**"Send them to post an intro with market plus a pain point." RIGHT DESTINATION, WRONG STEP ONE.**
Three problems:

1. **It is a public performance ask to a stranger.** Posting is permanent, visible, and
   status-loaded. Agents are status-sensitive in a way that automation hobbyists are not.
   An agent who is behind on AI does not want to announce that to a room of other agents.
2. **"Go do a thing somewhere else" DMs do not convert, and Ryan is the proof.** Six of the
   eight messages in his inbox asked him to go click something or go post something. He did
   none of it. Assume his members behave the way he did.
3. **It is three asks stacked.** Intro, plus market, plus a pain point. Stacked asks get a
   one-liner ("Hi I'm Bob, Phoenix") or nothing, and a one-liner intro is worth nothing to
   anybody.

**"Pain point" is the wrong frame, independent of where it is asked.** It reads as "tell the
room what you are bad at." Reframe it to repetition and it becomes a complaint about
busywork, which agents will make loudly and publicly all day. Same data, zero social risk.

> Do not say: what are you struggling with, where are you stuck, what's your biggest pain point.
> Say: what's the thing you do every week that you'd hand off tomorrow.

**The daily / weekly / monthly scaffold is good and is in the wrong place.** It makes a vague
question answerable, but three time buckets is too much work for a DM. It belongs in the post
template, where a longer answer is the point.

---

## The mechanism: the DM writes the post for them

This is the part worth reading twice. Do not ask for a post. Ask the question, get the
answer, then hand their own words back to them and tell them where to paste them.

```
DM asks one question   ->   they answer in private, low risk, 20 seconds
Ryan replies           ->   "paste exactly that into Intros, plus your market"
```

The post is now transcription, not composition. They are not facing a blank box trying to
sound competent, they are copying a sentence they already wrote. That single move is worth
more than any amount of better post-prompt copy, because the reason intro posts do not get
written is the blank page, not the topic.

It also fixes the ordering problem: the private conversation earns the right to ask for the
public one. Message one asks nothing of the room.

---

## MESSAGE 1, on join. DM.

> Hey [First name], Ryan here. Thanks for joining.
>
> One question before you dig in: what's the task you do the most that you'd never miss if
> it just did itself? Listing descriptions, follow-up texts, transaction paperwork, CMAs,
> whatever it is.
>
> Tell me the one and I'll point you at exactly where to start in here.

Fifty-six words. No links. One question. The four examples are load-bearing, they turn an
open question into a multiple choice, and they signal that Ryan knows the job. Payoff for
replying is stated.

If their Skool profile shows a city or a brokerage, spend ten seconds and put one true
detail in the first line. One specific reference kills the template read completely, and
none of the eight messages Ryan received had one.

## MESSAGE 2, the second they reply. This is the post ask.

> [One real sentence about their specific answer. Not a template line.]
>
> Do me a favor. Post exactly what you just told me in the community, plus what market
> you're in and whether you're full time. Two reasons: other agents in here have the same
> one and they'll find you, and every week I pick one of these and build it on video. Yours
> can't get picked sitting in my DMs.

Why this converts when a cold post ask does not: the words already exist, the reason is
concrete, and the incentive is real. "Yours can't get picked sitting in my DMs" is the line
doing the work.

Two honest constraints on it:

- **Do not use the "other agents have the same one" line until it is true.** At low member
  count it is a fabrication and an agent will smell it. Cut that clause and keep the rest.
- **Do not promise weekly unless it is weekly.** A build cadence Ryan misses twice is worse
  than never having offered one. If weekly is not safe, say "I build these" with no interval.

## MESSAGE 3, day 3, only if no reply. Give, do not ask.

> No rush on that one. Either way, here's the thing most agents fix first: AI writes generic
> listing copy because nobody ever told it who you are or what your market is. Fifteen minute
> fix, and it's the first thing in the classroom.

Still no link, still no ask. This message exists to prove a person is on the other end.

## MESSAGE 4, day 7, only if still no reply. One action, then stop.

> Last one from me, I won't keep pinging. If you drop a two-line intro in the community the
> other agents will find you, and that's usually where the useful conversations start. Door's
> open whenever.

Then stop. Six unanswered follow-ups is Tommi Pedruzzi's mistake and it does not recover.

## The post template, because "post an intro" is a blank page

Pin this. Link it in message 2. A bracket-fill format gets multiples of what a topic gets,
and it standardizes the data so the posts are comparable.

```
Where I sell:
What I mostly do (listings, buyers, both, investors, property management):
Full time or alongside something else:
The thing I do every WEEK that I'd hand off tomorrow:
The thing I do every MONTH that eats a whole day:
What I want out of this community:
```

Six lines, all short, no paragraph required. This is where the daily / weekly / monthly
scaffold pays off: two buckets, not three, because monthly and weekly are the two an agent
can actually recall. Drop "daily," it produces "check my email" from everybody.

**Seed it before asking anyone.** Nobody posts into an empty category, and nobody posts a
long intro under three one-line intros. Put three or four real filled-in examples in there
first, Ryan's own plus any existing members he can ask directly. This is an operational
prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.

## The lower-friction version, and why it may beat the post

A **comment on a pinned thread** is a fraction of the effort of a post, and it collects far
more responses. Evidence from Ryan's own inbox: Jack Roberts runs a 3.7k member community
and his onboarding checklist's social step is **"find a post you like and leave a comment,"**
not "make a post." An operator at that scale chose the comment.

Pinned thread copy:

> **What's the one task you'd hand off tomorrow?**
>
> Drop it below. Doesn't have to be clever, the boring ones are the ones worth killing.
> Weekly or monthly, whatever eats the most time.
>
> I read every one of these and I build them. If yours is here, it's in the running.

Ryan replies to every single comment, publicly, with something specific. That is the real
asset: one public reply is seen by everyone who scrolls past, so it does the work of twenty
DMs at once.

**The tradeoff, decide it deliberately:**

| Want | Ask for |
|---|---|
| activity, retention, and data on what to build | the pinned comment |
| member-to-member connection and content raw material | the intro post |
| both | comment on day 0, post after their first win |

Recommendation: **run the comment thread as the standing ask and the intro post as the
day-0 DM ask.** They do not compete because they live in different places, and a member who
comments has already broken the seal on posting.

---

## The timing point nobody gets right

The worst moment to ask for a public post is the minute someone joins. They have no context,
no result, and no reason to care what the room thinks of them.

The best moment is **immediately after their first win.** Level 0's paste block works, the
listing copy stops sounding like a robot, and right then they want to tell somebody. That is
when a "post what just happened" ask converts at a multiple of the join-day rate.

So there are two post asks, not one, and they are different posts:

- **Join day:** the intro. Low bar, six bracket lines, purely so the room knows who they are.
- **After Level 0:** the win. "Post the before and after." This is the one that produces
  content Ryan can actually use, and social proof for everyone else.

Ryan's original plan only had the first one.

---

## The engine this is actually building

Members post repetitive tasks. Ryan picks one, builds it on video, and ships it into the
classroom. Then the intro post stops being an onboarding chore and becomes a lottery ticket,
and the community's roadmap is written by its own members.

Three things that fall out of it for free:

1. **Level order validation.** If forty agents name follow-up and nobody names landing pages,
   the ladder is wrong and he finds out in week two instead of month six.
2. **Content calendar.** Every named task is a reel, a YouTube cut, and a lesson.
3. **Case studies.** Ask permission when the answer is good: "mind if I build this one on
   video and tag you?" That converts a member into proof.

This is the highest-value idea in this document and it costs one build a week.

## Ideas worth testing, ranked by upside over effort

1. **Voice note instead of text for message 1.** Twenty seconds to record, and it is
   unfakeable proof of a human. Nobody in Ryan's inbox of eight did it. Downside: some
   people never play them, and it does not scale past a certain join rate. Test it on the
   next twenty and compare reply rate.
2. **One true detail from their profile in line one.** Ten seconds, destroys the template read.
3. **Same-day replies or the premise dies.** A reply that lands two days later reads exactly
   like the automation he was trying not to be. If he cannot reply the same day, do not send
   the DM that day.
4. **Do not send message 1 the instant they join.** Two to four hours later reads like a
   person noticed, zero seconds reads like a webhook.
5. **Ask permission to build their task on video.** Free case study, and it flatters.
6. **Verify what Skool's onboarding checklist lets him customize** before designing around
   it. Jack's shows three steps (intro video, leave a comment, download app). Whether those
   items are editable is `NOT FOUND, verify in admin settings`. If they are, one of them
   should be the pinned task thread.

---

## Do not

- Do not ask for the post in message one. Ask the question, then convert the answer.
- Do not say pain point, stuck, or struggling. Say repetitive, or every week.
- Do not stack asks. One per message.
- Do not ask for posts into an unseeded category.
- Do not gate anything artificially. Lanie's "roadmaps unlock 24 hours after your first post"
  is a fake reward, and Ryan has a real one available, which a fake one only cheapens.
- Do not automate the reply to a post or a comment. The public, specific reply is the entire
  product. Automating it deletes the value and everyone can tell.
- Do not send a link in message one.
- Do not upsell. They already joined.
- Do not send six unanswered follow-ups.
