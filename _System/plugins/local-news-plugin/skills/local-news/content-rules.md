# Content Rules - Local News

These rules apply to ALL output: video transcripts, blog posts, Instagram captions, YouTube descriptions, Reddit posts, and spreadsheet summaries. Every sub-agent must follow these without exception.

---

## Writing Style

- **No em-dashes.** Never use the em-dash character. Use commas, periods, semicolons, or the word "and" instead. This is the most important formatting rule.
- **6th-grade reading level.** Short sentences. Simple words. Talk like you are explaining to a friend over coffee.
- **Professional but warm tone.** Knowledgeable neighbor, not newscaster. Not salesy, not stiff.
- **Active voice preferred.** "The city approved the project" not "The project was approved by the city."
- **Short paragraphs.** 3-4 sentences max per paragraph.

## Factual Standards

- **Factual only.** Never fabricate news stories, dates, names, quotes, statistics, or details.
- **Mark gaps.** If a detail cannot be verified, mark it as `[NOT VERIFIED]` so a human can fill it in.
- **Every story must have a verifiable source URL.** No source URL means do not include the story.
- **Recency.** Only include stories from the past 7-10 days unless the story has ongoing significance.
- **No rumors or unconfirmed reports.** If a story is speculation, skip it.
- **Verify the link resolves.** Before a story is accepted, fetch the source URL and confirm it loads with real content. SEO aggregator and content-farm pages frequently die or carry wrong numbers. When an aggregator and an official source disagree, the official source wins.
- **Prefer the primary source.** If a number originates with a government agency, an MLS/REALTOR association, or a federal data release, cite that body directly rather than a blog or aggregator that repeated it.

## BLOCKED TOPICS (NEVER COVER)

Ryan is a local real estate agent. These topics put his brand in the middle of a fight he does not want on a profile that sits next to his listings. This overrides story quality and viral score.

**Never build a story around:**
- Racial, ethnic, or identity-based disparity or conflict, including disparity in policing, school discipline, arrests, or use of force
- Accusations of racism, discrimination, or bias against a person, school, agency, or business
- Protests or public fights organized around race, ethnicity, religion, immigration status, gender identity, or sexual orientation
- Immigration enforcement actions, raids, or status disputes
- Partisan political conflict, election fights, candidate scandals, culture-war flashpoints, book bans, curriculum fights

**Research agents:** do not return these stories at all. Note them in your data quality section as "excluded, identity-conflict rule" and move on. Do not let a strong source or a strong hook talk you into returning one.

**The civic topic underneath is usually still fair game.** School safety, police budgets and staffing, discipline policy, CCSD spending, crime trends, traffic enforcement: all normal coverage. Cover the non-identity angle when one exists and is independently sourced. When the identity conflict is the whole story, drop it.

This is a content-selection rule for Ryan's videos, blogs, and captions. It does not touch fair housing obligations, which are unaffected.

## Source Attribution (REQUIRED)

Every story carries its source all the way through the pipeline so it can be credited publicly on Instagram.

**Research agents must capture and return, for every story:**
- `Source:` the publication name exactly as it brands itself (Redfin, Las Vegas Review-Journal, FOX5 Vegas, KTNV 13, Clark County, LVMPD, GLVAR, Freddie Mac)
- `Article Title:` the exact headline of the source article, transcribed word for word
- `URL:` the working link

**The Article Title is not optional.** It is what appears in quotes in the Instagram caption so followers can go find the piece themselves. Do not paraphrase it, do not clean it up, do not title-case it differently than the original. Copy it exactly.

If a story is built from more than one source, capture the single strongest one as the primary source and article title. That is the one that gets credited.

The viral strategist carries `Source` and `Article Title` through to its output. The social media writer uses them to build the source line on every Instagram caption.

**Instagram source line format** (last line of every caption, after the contact block):

```
Source: Redfin - "How to Buy a House"
```

Publication name, space hyphen space, then the exact article title in straight double quotes. Nothing else on that line.

**Never cite an Instagram or TikTok account as a source.** Social accounts are leads only. Trace the story back to the primary or news source and credit that.

## Hockey-Specific Rules

Hockey ships as one weekly roundup, `HK-WEEK`, not as individual stories. These rules apply to the roundup script, caption, description and blog, and to any hockey item that ends up as a bonus blog.

- Use full team names on first reference: "Vegas Golden Knights" and "Henderson Silver Knights." After first reference, "VGK" and "Silver Knights" are fine.
- Include venue names (T-Mobile Arena, Lifeguard Arena, City National Arena) for discoverability.
- For local rink stories, name the specific rink and neighborhood. "The Fiesta Rancho ice rink in North Las Vegas" not "a local ice rink."
- Treat youth hockey, travel hockey, and house league events as community news. These stories resonate with parents and families.

## Local News and Events Rules

- **Residents first, tourists never.** If a Las Vegas local would never go or never care, it is not a story.
- **Time-sensitive events need the date, time, location, and cost stated early.** A post about a festival that already happened is worthless.
- **Say when something is free.** Free events are the highest-performing item on this beat.
- **Name the neighborhood.** "The AMP at Craig Ranch in North Las Vegas" not "a local park."
- **Openings and closings:** give the address or the cross streets, and say how long a closing business had been open. Longevity is what makes a closure hurt.
- **Public safety:** cover it only when it affects a community or drives a real conversation. No gore, no victim exploitation, no chasing individual tragedies.
- Tone on this beat is conversational and a little bit "did you hear about this," not the measured expert tone used for market data.

## Video-Specific Rules

- Transcripts are spoken aloud. Write for the ear, not the eye.
- Avoid complex sentence structures or subordinate clauses.
- No filler phrases like "in today's video" or "hey guys."
- Open with a hook: question, surprising fact, or "did you hear about..."

## Blog-Specific Rules

No `.category-label`, `.dateline`, or `.article-meta` CSS. The blog content starts immediately after the H1 title, with no elements between them.

- Use `"@type": "NewsArticle"` schema (not generic "Article").
- Include source attribution at the bottom of every blog.
- Include contact page hyperlink and 702-747-5921 CTA in every blog.

- Blog URL pattern: `https://www.rosehomeslv.com/blog/[slug]` (singular /blog/, not /blogs/).
