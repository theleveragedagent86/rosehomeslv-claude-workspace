Why people come

What actually pulls people to Las Vegas

I hear the same handful of reasons over and over. None of them are the casinos. Here is what people tell me when I ask why they are looking at Nevada.

Nevada has no state income tax

There is no state income tax here, and no state estate or inheritance tax either. For people coming from California, Oregon, or New York, that alone changes the math on what they can afford every month.

Your housing dollar goes further

Buyers coming from the coasts are usually surprised by what the same budget buys here. I will not quote you a number on a web page, because it depends entirely on the area and the house. Tell me your budget and I will show you what it really gets.

It is a genuinely easy place to fly out of

Harry Reid International sits right in the middle of the valley. If you travel for work or have family in another state, that convenience turns out to matter more than people expect.

You are outside faster than you think

Red Rock Canyon, Lake Mead, and Mount Charleston are all short drives. Plenty of people move here picturing the Strip and end up spending their weekends on a trail.

Pick the commute first, then the house

This is the mistake I see most from out of state buyers. They fall in love with a house online, buy it, and then find out they are driving 45 minutes across the valley twice a day. Las Vegas looks small on a map and does not always drive small.

So we start the other way around. Tell me where you or your spouse will be working, whether you need to be near the airport, and what your weekends look like. That narrows the whole valley down to two or three areas before we ever open a listing.

If you cannot fly out to look, that is normal. A lot of my relocation clients see everything by video first. I walk the homes, record them honestly including the parts you would not put in a listing, and we go from there.

The trade-off

Rent first, or buy right away?

There is no single right answer, and anyone who tells you there is has not asked you enough questions. Here is the honest version of both.

Rent for 6 to 12 months

  • You learn the valley before you commit to a zip code
  • You can test the real commute in real traffic
  • You are not buying and selling twice if the job changes
  • Easier if you have not sold your current home yet
  • You pay rent and you build no equity while you wait
  • You may move twice, which is expensive and exhausting
  • Rentals in good school zones go fast and are not always nice

Buy right away

  • You move once, unpack once, and you are done
  • Your payment is fixed instead of renewing every year
  • Kids start the school year in the school they will stay in
  • You start building equity from month one
  • You are choosing an area you have not lived in yet
  • It is harder to pull off if your old home has not sold
  • Buying under time pressure rarely gets you the best house

Roughly half my relocation clients rent first and half buy right away, and both groups end up happy. What matters is that you chose on purpose instead of by default.

The fine print

What changes when you become a Nevadan

These are the things that catch people off guard in the first year. None of them are dealbreakers. They are just easier when somebody tells you ahead of time.

There is a property tax card most new owners miss

Nevada limits how much your property tax bill can go up each year, and the limit is lower for a home you actually live in than for a rental or a second home. You have to tell the Clark County Assessor which one yours is. New owners forget, get billed at the higher cap, and do not notice for a year. Ask me and I will point you to the form.

Your license and plates are on a clock

Nevada gives new residents a short window to switch their driver's license over and register their vehicles. Check the current deadline on the Nevada DMV site before you move, and get an appointment on the calendar early, because they book out.

Almost everything is one school district

Clark County School District covers the whole county, including Henderson, Summerlin, and North Las Vegas. Your school is assigned by your home address, so the house you pick decides the school. Zoning changes, so confirm the current assignment for a specific address with the district before you write an offer.

Most newer neighborhoods have an HOA

And in the master planned communities you can have two, one for the master plan and one for your own sub-neighborhood. Get the dues and the rules in front of you early, not the week before closing.

Summer is the part to plan for

July and August are the test, and the power bill goes with it. When we look at homes I pay attention to which way the house faces, how old the AC units are, and whether there is real shade in the back yard. It shows up on your bill every summer you own it.

You will want to see it in person once

Even if we do everything by video, try to get out here for a weekend before you close. Drive the commute at the actual hour you would drive it. That one trip has changed a few of my clients' minds about an area, and always for the better.

FAQ

Relocation questions, answered

Does Nevada have a state income tax?

No. Nevada has no state income tax, and no state estate or inheritance tax either. That is the single biggest financial reason people give me for looking at Nevada. Nevada does make up revenue in other ways, including sales tax, so ask your own CPA how the whole picture changes for your situation before you count on a specific number.

How much does it cost to live in Las Vegas?

It depends heavily on the area and the house, so I am not going to put a number on a web page that will be wrong by the time you read it. The costs that surprise people the most are the summer power bill, HOA dues, and car insurance. Tell me your budget and the area you are considering and I will build you a real monthly number for an actual house, not an average.

Which part of Las Vegas should I live in?

Start with the commute. Summerlin is the west valley and is master planned and trail heavy. Henderson is its own city in the southeast, quieter, closest to the airport. Mountain's Edge is southwest with mountain views and a lot of young families. Skye Canyon and Centennial Hills are northwest and newer. North Las Vegas usually has the most attainable pricing. Tell me where you will be working and I can narrow it to two or three.

Should I rent first or buy right away when I move to Las Vegas?

Both work. Renting for six to twelve months lets you learn the valley and test the real commute before you commit to a zip code, but you pay rent, build no equity, and may end up moving twice. Buying right away means one move, a fixed payment, and kids starting in the school they will stay in, but you are choosing an area you have not lived in yet. If your current home has not sold, that usually decides it for you.

How do schools work in Las Vegas?

Clark County School District covers the entire county, including Henderson, Summerlin, and North Las Vegas. Schools are assigned by your home address, so the house you choose determines the school. Zoning can change from year to year, so always confirm the current assignment for the specific address with the district before you write an offer rather than trusting a listing or a third party website.

Do I need a Nevada driver's license and plates when I move here?

Yes. Nevada gives new residents a limited window after you establish residency to transfer your driver's license and register your vehicles. Check the current deadline and document list on the Nevada DMV website before your move, and book an appointment early, because they fill up well in advance.

How hot does it actually get in the summer in Las Vegas?

Hot enough that July and August are the real test, and the power bill goes up with it. It is a dry heat, which genuinely does feel different from humid heat, but it is still heat. Late summer brings monsoon storms that can drop a lot of water fast. When we tour homes I look at which way the house faces, the age of the AC units, and whether the back yard has any real shade, because those three things show up on your bill every summer you own the place.

Can you help me buy in Las Vegas if I cannot fly out to look at homes?

Yes, and it is a normal part of what I do. I walk homes on video and record them honestly, including the things a listing would never show you, like the neighbor's yard or the noise from the road. We shortlist from there. I do still recommend one trip out before you close so you can drive the commute at the hour you would actually be driving it.

What is the property tax situation in Clark County?

Nevada caps how much your property tax bill can increase each year, and the cap is lower for a home you live in as your primary residence than it is for a rental or a second home. You have to declare which one your home is with the Clark County Assessor. New owners regularly miss this and get billed at the higher cap for a year without realizing it. Ask me and I will point you to the right form.

How far ahead should I start planning my move to Las Vegas?

Earlier than you think, and the conversation costs nothing. The two things that set your real timeline are whether your current home needs to sell first and how long your lender takes to get you fully underwritten. Both take longer than people expect. Getting those started early is what keeps you from having to take whatever house is available the week you need to move.
